# Antipodal depth shell and simultaneous quotient atoms — V1

Status: **proved prime-uniform structural theorem for support-four maximal corridors**. This sharpens the representation-depth criterion by classifying its shallowest antipodal shell and turns the two saturated maximal-atom directions into simultaneous rank-two atom projections of the companion. No generalized Davenport value or novelty/priority claim is made.

## 1. Setup

Let `p>=5` be prime and let the maximal atom have canonical support-four form

`U=e1^(p-1)e2^(p-1)e3^a g4^(p-a)`,

where

`g4=e3-a^{-1}(e1+e2)`, `1<=a<=(p-1)/2`.

For `x in C_p^3`, let

`rho_U(x)=min{|T|: T|U, sigma(T)=x}`

with `rho_U(0)=0`, and define the antipodal depth

`delta_U(x)=rho_U(x)+rho_U(-x)`.

The four support vectors form a projective circuit: every three are linearly independent, and the kernel relation is generated by the multiplicity vector

`(p-1,p-1,a,p-a)`.

## 2. Universal antipodal lower bound

> **Lemma.** For every nonzero `x`,
>
> `boxed{delta_U(x)>=p.}`

### Proof

Choose minimum U-subsequences `T,S` with

`sigma(T)=x`, `sigma(S)=-x`.

Their multiset product `TS` is zero-sum and is supported on the four support values of `U`. Its length is `delta_U(x)`.

Assume for contradiction that `delta_U(x)<p`. Then every coordinate multiplicity of `TS` is strictly smaller than `p`. Because any three support vectors are independent, every nonzero zero-sum vector with all four coordinates in `{0,...,p-1}` is a nonzero scalar multiple of the unique circuit relation.

For scalar `q in {1,...,p-1}`, the least positive residue vector of

`q(p-1,p-1,a,p-a)`

has length

`(p-q)+(p-q)+[qa]_p+(p-[qa]_p)=3p-2q`.

Its minimum over nonzero `q` is attained at `q=p-1` and equals

`p+2`.

Thus there is no nonempty zero-sum supported on the circuit with all coordinate counts below `p` and total length below `p`, contradiction.

## 3. Exact equality shell `delta_U=p`

The same argument classifies equality.

Suppose `delta_U(x)=p`. If every coordinate count of the zero-sum product `TS` were below `p`, the circuit calculation would force length at least `p+2`. Hence one coordinate count is at least `p`. Since the total length is exactly `p`, the product must be exactly

`g_i^p`

for one support value `g_i` of `U`.

Write `x=r g_i`, `1<=r<=p-1`. Then the two minimum representations use `r` and `p-r` copies of `g_i`. If `w_i=v_{g_i}(U)`, both fit precisely when

`r<=w_i` and `p-r<=w_i`,

or equivalently

`p-w_i<=r<=w_i`.

Conversely, whenever this interval condition holds, the two one-direction representations have total length `p`, and the universal lower bound forces equality.

Therefore:

> **Exact shallow-shell classification.** For nonzero `x`,
>
> `delta_U(x)=p`
>
> if and only if
>
> `x=r g_i`
>
> for a support value of multiplicity `w_i>=(p+1)/2` and a scalar
>
> `p-w_i<=r<=w_i`.

For the canonical multiplicities this becomes:

- on `<e1>`: every nonzero scalar multiple has `delta_U=p`;
- on `<e2>`: every nonzero scalar multiple has `delta_U=p`;
- on `<e3>`: none do, since `a<=(p-1)/2`;
- on `<g4>`: exactly the scalar interval `a<=r<=p-a` has `delta_U=p`.

When `a=1`, `g4` is also saturated and its entire nonzero projective line belongs to the equality shell.

## 4. Companion proper subsums avoid the shallow shell

Now place `U` in a prime-uniform maximal corridor and let the longer companion atom `V` have length

`m=p+b>p`.

The graded representation-depth criterion gives, for every nonempty proper `Y|V`,

`rho_U(sigma(Y))>=|Y|`,

`rho_U(-sigma(Y))>=m-|Y|`.

Hence

> `boxed{delta_U(sigma(Y))>=m>p.}`

for every nonempty proper companion subsequence.

Therefore no proper companion subsum can lie in the equality shell `delta_U=p`.

In particular:

> **Saturated-line avoidance.** For every nonempty proper `Y|V`,
>
> `sigma(Y) notin <e1>^bullet union <e2>^bullet`.

Taking `Y` to be a singleton shows that `V` contains no term on either saturated projective direction at all, not merely no copies of the actual values `e1,e2`.

For `a=1`, the same conclusion holds for the whole projective direction `<g4>`.

More generally, no proper companion subsum may equal `r g4` for any `a<=r<=p-a`.

## 5. Simultaneous quotient-atom theorem

Let

`pi_i : C_p^3 -> C_p^3/<e_i> ~= C_p^2`, `i=1,2`.

The full companion has sum zero, so each projected sequence `pi_i(V)` is zero-sum.

No term of `V` lies in `<e_i>`, by singleton saturated-line avoidance, so the projection has no zero terms and preserves the full length `m`.

If a nonempty proper subsequence `Y|V` projected to zero, then

`sigma(Y) in <e_i>`.

It cannot be zero because `V` is an atom, and it cannot be nonzero by saturated-line avoidance. Contradiction.

Thus:

> **Simultaneous quotient-atom theorem.** The two projected sequences
>
> `boxed{pi_1(V), pi_2(V)}`
>
> are both atoms of length `m` over `C_p^2`.

When `a=1`, projection modulo the third saturated direction `<g4>` is an atom as well.

This is stronger than projective separation or one/two-term subsum avoidance: **every** proper companion subsequence remains nonzero in two independent rank-two quotients.

## 6. Interface with the support-six equality normal form

If additionally `|supp(UV)|=6`, the previous equality theorem says the companion has two new actual values and support three or four.

The present theorem upgrades that face:

- neither new value can lie on `<e1>` or `<e2>`;
- no proper sum of the two-new-value/overlap configuration may enter either saturated line;
- the entire companion must be minimal zero-sum after quotienting by either saturated direction.

Thus the rank-three support-four equality branch is not an arbitrary four-point circuit: it is a **bi-minimal** circuit whose prescribed multiplicity vector remains atomic under two distinct rank-two quotient maps.

This is the next finite/algebraic classification target.
