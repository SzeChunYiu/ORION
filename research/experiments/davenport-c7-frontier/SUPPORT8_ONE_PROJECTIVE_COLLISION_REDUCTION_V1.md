# Support-eight, one-projective-collision reduction for `C_7^3` — V1

Status: **analytic reduction**. No exhaustive closure claim yet. Novelty/priority: **CANNOT_CHECK**.

Let `B` be a zero-sum sequence over `C_7^3` with

- `|B|=37`,
- zero-sum packing number `z(B)<=3`, and
- `|supp(B)|=8`.

Existing results in this campaign imply that `B` is 7-short-zero-free.

## Projective-direction count

The total occupancy of any one-dimensional subgroup is at most six. Otherwise seven terms in that copy of `C_7` contain a nonempty zero-sum subsequence of length at most seven, contradicting 7-short-zero-freeness.

Therefore 37 terms require at least

`ceil(37/6)=7`

projective directions.

With eight actual support elements, only two projective support patterns are possible:

1. eight distinct projective directions;
2. exactly seven projective directions, with exactly one direction containing two distinct actual support elements.

This note closes the geometry of case 2 to the already-enumerated seven-point arc universe.

## The one-collision branch

Assume there are exactly seven projective directions. Let their total occupancies be

`t_1,...,t_7`,

so

`1<=t_i<=6`, `sum t_i=37`.

Define direction deficits

`d_i=6-t_i`.

Then

`d_i>=0`, `sum d_i=42-37=5`.

Because `B` is 7-short-zero-free, any two-dimensional subgroup contains at most 18 terms: donor equality `eta(C_7^2)=19` would otherwise produce a zero-sum subsequence of length at most seven.

Suppose four of the seven projective directions were collinear, so their vectors lie in a common two-dimensional subgroup. Their total occupancy would be

`24 - (d_i+d_j+d_k+d_l)`.

But the four selected deficits sum to at most the global deficit 5, so that plane would contain at least

`24-5=19`

terms, contradicting the plane-occupancy cap 18.

Therefore **no four of the seven projective directions are collinear**.

Hence the projectivized direction set is a seven-point `(7,3)`-arc in `PG(2,7)` and lies in the same complete 54-class projective cover already generated by `generate_support7_projective_arcs_v1.py`.

## Consequence for exact search

The support-eight / seven-projective-direction branch is no longer a search over arbitrary eight vectors of `F_7^3`. It reduces to:

- one of the 54 frozen seven-point projective classes;
- a choice of which projective direction is doubled;
- two distinct scalar representatives on that doubled line;
- positive multiplicity split on those two actual values;
- six ordinary scalar lifts on the remaining directions;
- direction deficits summing to five;
- total-zero and 7-short-zero-free constraints;
- exact four-pack verification.

The remaining support-eight geometry with **eight distinct projective directions** is not closed by this note and should remain a separate branch.
