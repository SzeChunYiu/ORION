# Type two: the entire rank-two overlap c=H-1 is empty — V1

Status: **proved prime-uniform elimination of a complete high-overlap layer**. For every prime `p>=7`, the canonical type-two first-corridor pair has no rank-two light-share companion with `c=H-1`, regardless of the two new multiplicities. The argument atomizes a cyclic quotient, forces a rigid square, and reduces it to the already eliminated two-occurrence endpoint. No scalar sweep supplies the proof.

## 1. Setup and graded depth

Write `p=2H+1>=7`, `m=3H+1`, and

\[
U=e_1^{p-1}e_2^{p-1}g^{p-2}s^2,
\qquad e_1+e_2=2(s-g),
\]

where `(e_1,e_2,g)` is a basis. Suppose

\[
V=s^c x^r y^t,\qquad c+r+t=m,
\]

is a rank-two companion, with distinct new values `x,y` and positive multiplicities at most `p-1`. Assume `UV` has no nonempty zero-sum subsequence of length below `m`. In particular `V` is an atom. Its support spans a plane `K`.

For a target `v`, let `rho_U(v)` be the minimum length of a subsequence of `U` summing to `v`. Every nonempty proper `Y|V` satisfies

\[
\rho_U(\sigma(Y))\ge |Y|,\qquad
\rho_U(-\sigma(Y))\ge m-|Y|.
\tag{1}
\]

These are the established graded depth inequalities, obtained by completing `Y` or its complement using actual occurrences of `U`.

On the light line the exact original-donor depth is

\[
\boxed{\rho_U(qs)=2q-2+\epsilon(q),\quad 1\le q\le p-1,}
\tag{2}
\]

where `epsilon(q)` is one for odd integers and zero for even integers. This is the type-two radial staircase with no extra shared occurrences. Directly, a representation has `z` copies of `s` and `v` copies of `g`, with `z+v==q (mod p)`, `0<=z<=2`, and equal axis counts `[v/2]_p`. Among nonwrapped even `v`, choose the least feasible one: `v=q-2` for even `q`, and `v=q-1` for odd `q` (including `v=0` at `q=1`). This gives (2). Odd `v` adds a full `p` to the axis cost, and a wrapped choice cannot improve the displayed representation.

## 2. The general shifted quotient-depth window

Project `K` modulo `<s>` onto `C_p` and put `R=x^r y^t`. The projected values of `x` and `y` are nonzero: if one vanished, the zero-sum relation and the nonzero multiplicity of the other would force both to vanish, contradicting rank two.

Let `Y` be a nonempty proper subsequence of `R` whose projection has sum zero. Write

\[
\sigma(Y)=qs,\qquad 1\le q\le p-1,
\qquad \ell=|Y|.
\]

The sum cannot be zero because `V` is an atom. Nor can `p-q<=c`, since `Y s^{p-q}` would then be a proper zero-sum subsequence of `V`. Hence

\[
1\le q\le p-c-1.
\tag{3}
\]

Both `Y` and `Y s^c` are proper in `V`, so (1) and (2) apply without a modular wrap to `q` and `q+c`. They give

\[
\ell\le 2q-2+\epsilon(q),
\]

\[
m-\ell-c\le 2(p-q-c)-2+\epsilon(p-q-c).
\]

Since `p` is odd, `epsilon(p-q-c)=1-epsilon(q+c)`. Therefore the exact necessary window is

> **Shifted-depth window.**
>
> \[
> \boxed{2-\epsilon(q)\le 2q-\ell
> \le H-c-\epsilon(q+c).}
> \tag{4}

This holds for every proper projected-zero part, not just for one chosen relation multiplier or one quotient factorization.

## 3. At c=H-1 the quotient must be a rigid square

Now set `c=H-1`, so `|R|=p+1`. The projected zero-sum sequence can be atomized in `C_p`, whose atoms have length at most `p`. Hence every atom dividing it is proper in `R` and is subject to (4).

The right side of (4) is at most one, and the left side is at least one. Equality forces

\[
2q-\ell=1,\qquad q\text{ odd},\qquad q+c\text{ even}.
\tag{5}
\]

Thus `c` is odd, `H` is even, and `p==1 (mod 4)`. This already excludes the complete layer when `p==3 (mod 4)`.

Let a projected atom have actual multiplicities `A,B` on `x,y`. The rank-two relation space on the three values `s,x,y` is one-dimensional and generated by `(c,r,t)`. Consequently, for some scalar `lambda`,

\[
(-q,A,B)=\lambda(c,r,t)\quad\text{in }\mathbb F_p^3.
\]

As `r+t=p+1`, its length satisfies `ell==lambda (mod p)`. Also `-2c==3 (mod p)`. Using (5),

\[
\ell+1=2q\equiv 3\lambda\equiv3\ell\pmod p.
\]

Every projected atom therefore has the unique length in `[1,p]` satisfying `2ell==1 (mod p)`:

\[
\boxed{\ell=H+1,\qquad q=(H+2)/2.}
\tag{6}

The required oddness of `q` forces `H` divisible by four, or

\[
p\equiv1\pmod8.
\]

Moreover `A=[(H+1)r]_p<=r`. If `r` were odd, this residue would be `(p+r)/2>r`, impossible. Thus `r` is even and `A=r/2`. Similarly `t` is even and `B=t/2`.

The projected values are distinct, since equality of the two nonzero values would make the sum of `p+1` projected terms nonzero. We have proved that **every** projected atom dividing `R` is the same two-value atom

\[
Q=\bar x^{r/2}\bar y^{t/2},\qquad |Q|=H+1,
\]

and that

\[
\boxed{\pi(R)=Q^2,\qquad
Q\text{ is the only atom dividing }Q^2.}
\tag{7}

The last assertion is stronger than merely finding a factorization into two identical factors.

## 4. Two-value cyclic atoms at this length have index one

We use two established structural inputs, with their ownership retained:

- Xia--Yuan's two-support splitting lemma: every cyclic prime-order atom with exactly two distinct values admits a one-term split that remains an atom. It is recorded as Lemma 2.5, citing Xia--Yuan Lemma 2.15, in [Peng--Sun, arXiv:1409.1970v1](https://arxiv.org/html/1409.1970v1).
- The long-atom index theorem gives index one at length `(p+3)/2`. See [Savchev--Chen, Section 5 and Proposition 10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0602568), independently obtained by Yuan; note that Savchev--Chen's unnormalized index is `p`, whereas normalized index is one.

Apply the splitting lemma to the two-value atom `Q` of length `(p+1)/2`. Its split is an atom of length `(p+3)/2`, so has positive representatives summing to `p` under some generator. Recombining the split terms retains this sum: their positive representatives add to less than `p`, since other positive terms remain. Thus `Q` itself has index one.

The blanket long-atom theorem cannot be applied directly one term below its threshold. The splitting lemma is the necessary extra step here. The condition `p>155` on Peng--Sun's later main theorems is not a hypothesis of the cited preliminary splitting lemma. The surviving congruence class in Section 3 has `p>=17`.

## 5. A two-value atom with a rigid square has a singleton

We prove the needed integer step explicitly. Suppose

\[
Q=(j h)^a h^b,\qquad a+b=H+1,
\qquad ja+b=p,
\]

is the index-one representation. One coefficient must be one because `2|Q|=p+1>p`; the other, `j`, is at least two because the values are distinct. The two equations give

\[
a(j-1)=H.
\]

Assume both multiplicities are at least two. Put `d=j-1`; then `a,d>=2` and `H=ad`, while

\[
b-j=H-a-d=(a-1)(d-1)-1\ge0.
\]

Equality would require `a=d=2`, hence `p=9`, which is not prime. Therefore `b-j>=1`. The sequence

\[
Q'=(j h)^{a+1}h^{b-j}
\]

is an actual subsequence of `Q^2`, since `a+1<=2a` and `0<b-j<=2b`. Its positive representatives sum to

\[
j(a+1)+(b-j)=ja+b=p,
\]

so `Q'` is an atom. But

\[
|Q'|=|Q|-(j-1)<|Q|,
\]

contradicting (7). Hence one multiplicity of `Q` is one.

It follows that, after swapping `x,y` if necessary,

\[
\boxed{r=2,\qquad t=p-1.}
\tag{8}

The already proved `A2_RANK2_TWO_NEW_OCCURRENCES_ENDPOINT_ELIMINATION_V1.md` excludes exactly this row for every prime `p>=7`. This supplies the final contradiction.

## 6. Complete-layer theorem and exact boundary

> For every prime `p>=7`, a type-two canonical first-corridor maximal pair has no rank-two light-share companion
>
> \[
> \boxed{V=s^{H-1}x^r y^t,\qquad r+t=p+1.}
> \]
>
> The conclusion holds for all positive allowed `r,t`, including unsaturated new values.

When `p==3 (mod 4)`, `H-1` is the largest allowed light overlap, so this removes that entire top layer. When `p==1 (mod 4)`, it removes the entire layer immediately below `H`. The `c=H` layer and lower high-overlap layers are not eliminated by this theorem.

The proof authority is quotient atomization, the exact shifted-depth inequalities, the two cited cyclic inputs, and the previously proved endpoint. That endpoint's final prime class imports the published Bhowmik--Halupczok--Schlage-Puchta rank-two multiplicity theorem, with its donor-owned bounded computational inputs already disclosed there. No new enumeration or prime sweep is used here.

## 7. Review and preserved failed routes

The coordinator checked the proof in two forms: the window (4), and the equivalent sum bound `rho_U(qs)+rho_U((p-c-q)s)>=m-c`. Both give the same parity obstruction. The audit also checked properness of every quotient atom, nonzero and distinct quotient values, the rank-two coefficient relation, unique least-residue lengths, all occurrence bounds in `Q'`, and the exact endpoint dependency.

Two stronger shortcuts were rejected during this derivation. A factorization `Q^2` alone does not imply its atomic divisors are all `Q`; that rigidity is proved by applying the depth window to **every** quotient atom. Also, exchanging the whole new-value sequence into the maximal atom does not license the saturated-quotient theorem when its new multiplicities are below `p-1`. The cyclic square argument avoids that missing hypothesis and reduces to actual saturation only at (8).
