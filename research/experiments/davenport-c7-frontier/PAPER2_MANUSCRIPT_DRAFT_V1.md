# Representation-depth rigidity and support growth in the first multiwise Davenport corridors of elementary rank-three groups

**Manuscript draft V1 — 2026-09-04**

**Claim boundary.** This draft develops exact representation-depth theorems and bounded support-growth results. It does **not** prove `D_3(C_7^3)=36`, does not classify all length-37 obstructions, and does not claim external novelty, priority, or venue acceptance.

## Abstract

Let `G=C_p^3` and let `D_k(G)` denote the least length forcing `k` pairwise disjoint nonempty zero-sum subsequences. We study the first nontrivial multiwise Davenport corridors through a maximal zero-sum atom `U` and the representation-depth function

`rho_U(z)=min{|T|:T|U, sigma(T)=z}`.

For the canonical support-four maximal type with light multiplicity two, we determine the exact reusable light-overlap ceiling, the exact radial lifting cost, and a complete piecewise-linear maximum of `rho_U` on every affine coordinate-sum fiber. For arbitrary canonical type, we also determine the exact cost of every target in the plane generated by the two unsaturated support values. These formulas turn support sharing into explicit residue inequalities.

They yield several prime-uniform consequences. The complete light-multiplicity-three support-three rank-two face is empty for every prime `p>=7`. For canonical types `a>=4`, simultaneous overlap is sharply bounded; doubling forces every rank-three survivor onto a thin boundary; and scalar three removes the central part of that boundary, leaving four edge regimes. On the top type-two overlap face for primes `p==1 (mod 4)`, an inverse-quarter rotation selector eliminates both standard high-multiplicity families.

At `p=7`, the remaining type-two rank-three support-four equality face reduces to six multiplicity rows and fourteen ordered parameter pairs. Each admits an explicit mixed zero-sum certificate of length at most eight. Combining this first-corridor closure with two structurally independent exhaustive verifiers for the `(9,9,19)` corridor proves that, in both maximal `C_7^3` corridors, a hypothetical length-37 packing obstruction containing a support-four length-19 atom cannot remain in the support-six maximal-pair face. The result is a support-growth theorem and a reproducible proof architecture, not an exact determination of `D_3(C_7^3)`.

## 1. Introduction

For a finite abelian group `G`, the classical Davenport constant asks for the least sequence length forcing one nonempty zero-sum subsequence. Its multiwise analogue `D_k(G)` asks for `k` pairwise disjoint zero-sum subsequences. Even in elementary abelian rank-three groups, exact small-`k` values expose a difficult interaction between additive geometry, multiplicity, and factorization length.

The direct formulation is global: one must rule out every long sequence whose zero-sum packing number is too small. Our approach is local and asymmetric. A first-failure reduction isolates a longest zero-sum atom `U` and companion atoms whose products inherit progressively shorter zero-sum prohibitions. The decisive object is then not the original sequence but the bounded representation problem inside `U`.

Given an atom `U`, define

`rho_U(z)=min{|T|: T|U, sigma(T)=z}`,

with `rho_U(z)=infinity` when `z` is not representable. If `W` is a subsequence of a companion atom, then `TW` is zero-sum exactly when `sigma(T)=-sigma(W)`. Thus a short-zero prohibition for `UW` becomes the graded inequality

`|W|+rho_U(-sigma(W)) >= threshold`.

When `rho_U` is explicit, a high-dimensional sequence problem becomes a family of modular inequalities. This paper develops that calculus for canonical support-four maximal atoms and uses it to force support growth in the two maximal `C_7^3` corridors.

### 1.1. Canonical maximal atoms

For prime `p`, a support-four maximal atom in `C_p^3` has the canonical form

`U_a=e1^(p-1)e2^(p-1)s^a g^(p-a)`,

where

`g=s-a^(-1)(e1+e2)`

and `1<=a<=p-1`. Its length is

`|U_a|=3p-2`.

At `p=7`, this is the length-19 atom occurring in both maximal first-failure corridors

`(8,10,19)` and `(9,9,19)`.

The two values `s` and `g` are unsaturated; the basis values `e1,e2` occur `p-1` times. A support-six companion can share one unsaturated value in a rank-two support-three configuration or both unsaturated values in a rank-three support-four configuration. This dichotomy organizes the proof.

### 1.2. Contributions

The paper has four mathematical layers.

First, we derive exact representation-depth formulas: the type-two radial staircase, the complete type-two fiber envelope, and the arbitrary-type overlap-plane lifting cost.

Second, we use those formulas to prove prime-uniform rigidity: complete type-three rank-two elimination, a simultaneous-overlap bound, a doubling boundary theorem, a scalar-three central-boundary theorem, and an inverse-quarter selector for the standard type-two top-overlap families.

Third, we close the last support-six first-corridor face at `p=7` by an explicit fourteen-entry certificate table.

Fourth, we combine that closure with a dual-verifier exhaustive theorem in the `(9,9,19)` corridor to obtain a two-corridor support-growth result.

The analytic and computational claims are separated throughout. Every finite terminal case has either a human-readable zero-sum certificate or two structurally independent exact verifiers.

## 2. Sequences, atoms, and inherited short-zero conditions

A sequence over `G` is a finite multiset, written multiplicatively. Its length is `|S|`, its sum is `sigma(S)`, and its support is `supp(S)`. A nonempty zero-sum sequence is an **atom** if it has no proper nonempty zero-sum subsequence.

Let `z(S)` be the maximum number of pairwise disjoint nonempty zero-sum subsequences of `S`. Then `D_k(G)` is the least `ell` such that every sequence of length at least `ell` has `z(S)>=k`.

The first-failure framework used here starts from a hypothetical total-zero sequence at the first unresolved length and chooses an atom factorization with a maximal factor. Hereditary packing tightness forces strong short-zero conditions on products of initial factors. At `p=7`, a support-four maximal factor has length 19, and the only maximal length triples are

`(8,10,19)` and `(9,9,19)`.

In the first corridor, the product of the length-19 and length-10 atoms is 9-short-zero-free. In the second corridor, the product of the length-19 and either length-9 atom is 8-short-zero-free, and the full length-37 product is 7-short-zero-free.

These thresholds are the source of every depth inequality below.

## 3. Exact representation depth for canonical support-four atoms

Choose coordinates adapted to `U_a`. In the basis `(e1,e2,g)`, one has

`s=(a^(-1),a^(-1),1)`.

For a target `z=(P,Q,C)`, choosing `k` copies of `s` forces the numbers of `e1,e2,g` used in any representation. Consequently `rho_U(z)` is the minimum of a small bounded family of least-residue sums.

The general formula is already enough for exact computation. The main analytic gain comes from collapsing it further in the exceptional type `a=2` and in the overlap plane `span{s,g}`.

## 4. The exact type-two radial staircase

Put `p=2H+1` and `u=2^(-1)=H+1`. For `a=2`,

`U_2=e1^(p-1)e2^(p-1)s^2g^(p-2)`.

Suppose a companion contributes `c` additional literal copies of `s`. Let `lambda_{2,c}(D)` be the minimum number of terms in the combined radial resources whose sum is `D s`.

### Theorem 4.1 — exact light overlap and radial cost

The exact reusable light multiplicity is

`c_light=2 floor(H/2)=2 floor((p-1)/4)`.

For every allowed `c` and every `1<=D<=p-1`,

`lambda_{2,c}(D)=D+2 ceil(max(D-c-2,0)/2)`.

#### Proof idea

A representation using `q` copies of `g` and `z` literal copies of `s` has cost

`z+q+2[uq]_p`

subject to `z+q==D (mod p)`. Wrapped representations are too expensive. For nonwrapped representations, even `q=2j` gives `[uq]_p=j`, while odd `q=2j+1` gives `2[uq]_p=p+q`. Hence every optimizer uses the first admissible even `q`, producing the parity staircase

`0,0,...,0,2,2,4,4,6,6,...`.

A separate full-capacity hostile replay reconstructs the bounded-resource oracle directly and rejects the tempting floor-rounding mutation.

## 5. The exact type-two depth-fiber envelope

For `a=2`, still in `(e1,e2,g)` coordinates, let the target be `(P,Q,C)` and put

`w=[P+Q]_p`.

Only `k=0,1,2` copies of `s` are possible. For each `k`, the remaining resources are forced, so

`rho_U(P,Q,C)`

`=min_k ([P-ku]_p+[Q-ku]_p+[C-k]_p+k)`,

with the natural `g`-capacity restriction.

Define the fiber maximum

`M_p(w,C)=max_{[P+Q]_p=w} rho_U(P,Q,C)`.

### Theorem 5.1 — exact fiber envelope

Let `p=2H+1`. Then:

**Low fibers, `0<=w<=H-1`:**

- `M_p(w,C)=p+w` for `C=0,1,2`;
- `M_p(w,C)=p+w+C-2` for `3<=C<=p-2`;
- `M_p(0,p-1)=2p-2`, while `M_p(w,p-1)=2p+w-3` for `w>=1`.

**Middle fiber, `w=H`:**

- `M_p(H,0)=p+H`;
- `M_p(H,C)=H+C` for `1<=C<=p-2`;
- `M_p(H,p-1)=2p+H-3`.

**High fibers, `H+1<=w<=p-2`:**

- `M_p(w,0)=p+w`;
- `M_p(w,C)=w+1` for `C=1,2`;
- `M_p(w,C)=w+C-1` for `3<=C<=p-1`.

**Endpoint fiber, `w=p-1`:**

- `M_p(p-1,0)=p-1`;
- `M_p(p-1,C)=p` for `C=1,2`;
- `M_p(p-1,C)=p+C-2` for `3<=C<=p-1`.

Every maximum has an explicit sharp pair `(P,Q)`.

#### Significance

The three-dimensional depth problem has been compressed to two residues. The middle fiber `w=H` exhibits a large depth drop on `1<=C<=p-2`; the endpoint fiber `w=p-1` has separate shallow values at `C=0,1,2`. These singular fibers drive the later power obstructions.

The theorem is checked at 5,726,716 direct depth points through prime 101, 14,283 independently maximized pair-sum fibers through prime 401, and 192,577 explicit sharp witnesses through prime 1009. Mutations erasing the middle-fiber collapse or the double-zero endpoint are rejected.

## 6. Exact lifting in the overlap plane

A rank-three support-four companion shares both `s` and `g`. Its first scalar multiple lies in `span{s,g}`, independently of the two new values.

### Theorem 6.1 — overlap-plane cost

For arbitrary canonical type `a`, the exact `U_a`-cost of a target `C s+D g` is

`nu_a(C,D)=min (z+q+2[a^(-1)(q-D)]_p)`

over

`0<=z<=a`, `0<=q<=p-a`, `z+q==C+D (mod p)`.

This formula is an exact rank-three certificate interface. It separates the overlap arithmetic from the free geometry of the new values and supports broad scalar scans without treating computation as theorem authority.

## 7. Prime-uniform rank-two rigidity

For a support-three rank-two companion sharing one unsaturated value, all interior multiplicity rows are eliminated by a universal radial-doubling argument. Heavy sharing is empty for every canonical type. The remaining difficulty is the light-share boundary.

### Theorem 7.1 — complete type-three rank-two closure

For every prime `p>=7`, no exact-support-six first-corridor support-three rank-two companion survives for canonical type `a=3`.

The proof has three ingredients.

1. The exact inverse-three radial staircase computes every light lifting cost.
2. A non-upper boundary row produces a length-four minimal zero-sum sequence over `C_p`; the classical index-one theorem supplies a multiplier, after which only capacity and radial inequalities remain.
3. Two explicit constructions eliminate the two boundary halves `e<=f` and `e>f`.

The cyclic index-one input is donor-owned. The new content is the capacity-aware embedding and the explicit radial-compatible multipliers.

Thus every surviving rank-two equality face is forced into the exceptional light types `a=1,2`. At `p=7`, their complete reusable overlap ranges are already eliminated by the layer theorems, which is one input to the first-corridor closure below.

## 8. Prime-uniform rank-three reductions

Let a rank-three support-four companion have the form

`V=s^c g^d x^r y^t`.

### Theorem 8.1 — simultaneous overlap

For canonical types `a>=4` with both overlap directions available,

`c+d<=a-2`.

This sharply limits the overlap box.

### Theorem 8.2 — doubling boundary

If the smaller new-value multiplicity exceeds `H`, doubling the companion relation yields a zero-sum of length exactly `p-1`. Hence every survivor lies on a thin boundary

`r=H-k`, `t=p-(c+d)+k`, `0<=k<=c+d-1`.

### Theorem 8.3 — scalar-three central boundary

On that thin boundary, scalar three eliminates every box satisfying the four central capacity inequalities. Only four explicit edge regimes remain.

These theorems do not close all `a>=4` rank-three companions. Their role is to replace a four-dimensional box by a finite menu of boundary mechanisms suitable for a sequel or a final all-prime closure.

## 9. Inverse-quarter rotation on the top type-two overlap face

Assume `p>=13`, `p==1 (mod 4)`, and top light overlap `c=H`. A boundary companion has

`V=s^H x^r y^(p-r)`, `1<=r<=H`,

with

`x=y-delta s`, `delta=Hr^(-1)`.

Suppose the high-multiplicity value lies in one of the two standard families

`y=(A,-A,kappa)`, `kappa in {1,2}`.

For `-jx`, the fiber coordinates are

`w=[j delta]_p`, `C=[w-kappa j]_p`.

The fiber theorem reduces the problem to finding `1<=j<=r` with

`j+M_p(w,C)<m`, `m=3H+1`.

### Theorem 9.1 — standard families are empty

No top-overlap companion survives in either standard family.

The lower half is handled by `j=r` or `j=1`. In the upper half put

`b=p-2r`,

so `delta^(-1)=b`, and write `p=qb+a`. Three explicit selectors suffice:

- a half-step when `q=2`, `b==1 (mod 4)`;
- a quarter-step when `q=2`, `b==3 (mod 4)`;
- a complemented quarter-step when `q>=3`.

Each selector lands in a controlled fiber and leaves a positive integer margin. The broad replay covers 632,434 coprime odd-modulus rows through 5001; the exact all-parameter depth replay covers 1,977,484 standard-family instances through prime 401. Three nearby selector mutations are rejected.

The remaining top-overlap gap is a classification theorem: the separate power-depth inequalities appear to force the high-multiplicity value into these standard families, apart from three bounded central exceptions. That statement remains open and is not used below.

## 10. The first `C_7^3` corridor

Set `p=7`. Then

`U=e1^6e2^6s^2g^5`, `g=(3,3,1)`

is the canonical type-two length-19 atom. In corridor `(8,10,19)`, a length-10 rank-three support-four companion with pair support six has the form

`V=s^c g x^r y^t`.

Exact overlap and capacity leave only six rows:

`(c,d,r,t)` in

`{(1,1,2,6),(1,1,3,5),(1,1,4,4),`

`  (2,1,1,6),(2,1,2,5),(2,1,3,4)}`.

An occurrence-level dynamic program over the actual 19 terms of `U` computes all 343 depth states. For each row, the atom relation determines `y` from `x`. After support and rank conditions, exactly 290 ordered structural candidates remain per row, or 1740 total.

Separate `x`- and `y`-power tests leave

`(2,0,4,4,4,0)`

candidates across the six rows: fourteen ordered pairs.

### Theorem 10.1 — the rank-three equality face is empty

Every one of the fourteen ordered pairs admits an explicit mixed zero-sum certificate of length at most eight. Up to coordinate exchange and, when `r=t`, exchange of `x,y`, representatives are:

| row `(c,d,r,t)` | representative `(x,y)` | companion part `W` | maximal-atom part `T` | length |
|---|---|---|---|---:|
| `(1,1,2,6)` | `((1,2,0),(5,0,2))` | `x^2y^3` | `e1 g` | 7 |
| `(1,1,4,4)` | `((0,4,1),(1,4,2))` | `xy` | `s^2g^2` | 6 |
| `(2,1,1,6)` | `((1,5,6),(4,1,2))` | `xy^3` | `e1^2g^2` | 8 |
| `(2,1,1,6)` | `((2,5,5),(5,1,1))` | `xy` | `e2s` | 4 |
| `(2,1,2,5)` | `((0,2,4),(5,0,2))` | `x^2y^2` | `e1sg` | 7 |
| `(2,1,2,5)` | `((4,6,3),(2,4,1))` | `xy^2` | `e2g^2` | 6 |

The checker verifies all fourteen ordered identities and capacities directly. Deleting any one certificate leaves exactly its corresponding candidate unresolved.

The support-three rank-two branch is already empty for all canonical types at `p=7`: type one has four possible light-overlap layers, type two has two, and type three is closed uniformly. The other rank-three canonical types have zero simultaneous overlap at `p=7`.

### Corollary 10.2 — first-corridor support growth

In corridor `(8,10,19)`, any maximal pair containing a support-four length-19 atom has total support at least seven.

This is a statement about the `19+10` pair before the length-8 atom is adjoined.

## 11. The second `C_7^3` corridor

In corridor `(9,9,19)`, pair support six is not empty. Exact normal-form generation and the condition `z(UV)=2` leave 26 maximal-pair candidates:

| canonical type | light-share rank two | heavy-share rank two | rank-three support four | total |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `a=1` | 12 | 0 | 0 | 12 |
| `a=2` | 4 | 0 | 6 | 10 |
| `a=3` | 2 | 2 | 0 | 4 |
| total | 18 | 2 | 6 | 26 |

Extending by the second length-9 atom under the full 7-short-zero-free condition yields exactly 1634 factor triples. Every one four-packs.

### Theorem 11.1 — second-corridor support-six closure

No length-37 zero-sum sequence over `C_7^3` with packing number at most three and corridor `(9,9,19)` can have both a support-four length-19 atom and a maximal pair of total support six.

Two exact implementations independently reproduce the 26-pair and 1634-completion censuses. Their decisive internal methods differ:

| layer | primary verifier | independent verifier |
|---|---|---|
| pair short-freeness | exact representation-depth formula | direct bounded count-vector enumeration |
| third-atom state | cardinality-indexed subset sums | minimum base depth plus occurrence masks |
| four-pack test | compatible small blocks plus residual | pair-union/complement matching |

Agreement at all three layers substantially reduces the risk of shared implementation error.

## 12. Main bounded theorem

### Theorem 12.1 — two-corridor support-six exclusion in `C_7^3`

Let `B` be a hypothetical length-37 zero-sum sequence over `C_7^3` with zero-sum packing number at most three. Suppose a maximal atom `U|B` has length 19 and support four. Then `B` cannot remain in the support-six maximal-pair face in either maximal length corridor.

- In `(8,10,19)`, pair support six is empty already at the maximal-pair stage.
- In `(9,9,19)`, support-six maximal pairs exist, but every admissible completion four-packs.

This theorem forces the obstruction search beyond the smallest support-four maximal-pair geometry. It does **not** eliminate pair support at least seven, maximal atoms of support at least five, or the remaining support-eight Type-A packing face. Therefore it does not establish `D_3(C_7^3)=36`.

## 13. Reproducibility and proof authority

The analytic theorem files contain complete residue arguments and explicit witnesses. Their checkers are regression evidence, not substitutes for proof.

The finite theorems follow a stricter certificate standard.

1. The search space is derived from proved normal forms and exact capacity bounds.
2. Every terminal first-corridor survivor has a direct vector-sum certificate.
3. The larger second-corridor closure is reproduced by two structurally different exact algorithms.
4. Deterministic transcripts and exact censuses are frozen.
5. Hostile mutations target load-bearing rounding, fiber, selector, certificate, and claim-authority assumptions.
6. A machine-readable claim ledger prevents open exact-value, novelty, or venue claims from being promoted silently.

The branch-scoped workflow compiles and runs the complete theorem stack, both second-corridor executables, and the claim validator from a clean checkout.

## 14. Related work and subtraction

Freeze and Schmid introduced and developed the multiwise Davenport constants and proved eventual linearity with difference `exp(G)`. Weighted multiwise variants connect the subject to coding theory and projective geometry. Those frameworks are donor-owned.

The type-three boundary proof invokes the length-four cyclic index-one theorem. The prime-power case needed here was proved by Li, Plyley, Yuan and Zeng. A 2026 preprint by Li, Yuan, Yuan and Zhang gives a multiplicative-Fourier proof of the full order-coprime-to-six conjecture. Our contribution is not the index theorem, but its capacity-aware integration with exact radial lifting.

Inverse problems for sequences without short zero-sums, restricted sumsets, Property B, and unsplittable minimal zero-sum sequences form the closest structural neighborhood. The present method is distinguished by its maximal-pair inheritance, bounded representation depth, and multiwise packing target.

A targeted public-source audit has not located the exact type-two radial staircase, the full depth-fiber envelope, the overlap-plane lifting formula, the inverse-quarter selector, or the two-corridor `C_7^3` support-six exclusion. This is not a novelty certificate. MathSciNet, zbMATH, thesis, conference, and expert subtraction remain required before submission.

## 15. Remaining frontier

The next all-prime steps are sharply localized:

1. classify power-compatible high-multiplicity values on the top type-two overlap face;
2. eliminate light-share rank-two type one and type two for overlap `c>=5`;
3. close the four `a>=4` rank-three edge regimes left by scalar three;
4. close exceptional rank-three types `a=2,3` uniformly;
5. derive a support-seven augmentation theorem spanning both `C_7^3` corridors;
6. treat maximal atoms of support at least five;
7. resolve the support-eight one-projective-collision Type-A face.

Only after those tasks should the exact value `D_3(C_7^3)=36` be reconsidered.

## 16. Conclusion

Representation depth provides a tractable interface between maximal zero-sum atoms and multiwise packing. In canonical support-four atoms it admits exact arithmetic formulas strong enough to drive prime-uniform eliminations and to reduce finite terminal faces to transparent certificates. For `C_7^3`, the resulting analytic and computational chain excludes support-six maximal-pair obstructions in both maximal corridors.

The present endpoint is deliberately bounded: a support-growth theorem with a reproducible proof architecture. Its mathematical value lies in the exact depth calculus and the conversion of global packing obstructions into explicit low-dimensional residue geometry.
