# Type two: an exact quotient defect budget at every submaximal overlap — V1

Status: **proved prime-uniform structural theorem**. In the rank-two light-share problem, every cyclic quotient atomization has the same positive defect budget, with no modular carry. This holds throughout `1<=c<H`, not just for a bounded number of layers near `H`. It does not eliminate every such layer.

## 1. Hypotheses and the proper-part window

Let `p=2H+1>=7` be prime, `m=3H+1`, and

\[
U=e_1^{p-1}e_2^{p-1}g^{p-2}s^2,
\qquad e_1+e_2=2(s-g),
\]

where `(e_1,e_2,g)` is a basis. Suppose

\[
V=s^c x^r y^t,\qquad c+r+t=m,
\qquad 1\le c<H,\quad 1\le r,t\le p-1,
\]

has rank two and distinct new values `x,y`, and `UV` has no nonempty zero-sum shorter than `m`. Thus `V` is an atom. Put

\[
d=H-c,\qquad R=x^r y^t,\qquad |R|=p+d.
\]

Project the support plane modulo `<s>` to `C_p`. Both projected values are nonzero, since the companion relation would otherwise force rank one. They are distinct: equality would give `sigma(pi(R))=d pi(x)!=0`.

For every nonempty proper projected-zero occurrence subsequence `Y|R`, write

\[
\sigma(Y)=qs,\quad \ell=|Y|,\quad
1\le q\le p-c-1,
\qquad D(Y)=2q-\ell.
\]

Here `q=0` would contradict atomicity of `V`, as would `p-q<=c`. Sections 1–2 of `A2_RANK2_PENULTIMATE_OVERLAP_FULL_ELIMINATION_V1.md` prove, using the original donor depth `rho_U(qs)=2q-2+epsilon(q)`, that

\[
\boxed{2-\epsilon(q)\le D(Y)\le d-\epsilon(q+c).}
\tag{1}
\]

The parity function `epsilon` is one on odd integers and zero on even integers. In particular, every such proper part has `1<=D(Y)<=d`. The quantifier over **every** proper part, including unions of atoms, is essential.

## 2. The exact budget and absence of carry

Take any occurrence-level atomic factorization

\[
\pi(R)=Q_1\cdots Q_k
\]

and lift its factors to disjoint occurrence subsequences `Y_i|R`. Write their lengths, canonical light sums and defects as `ell_i,q_i,D_i`. A cyclic prime-order atom has length at most `p`, whereas `|R|=p+d>p`; thus every `Y_i` is proper, and (1) applies.

> **Exact quotient budget.** Every such factorization satisfies
>
> \[
> \boxed{\sum_{i=1}^k q_i=p-c=H+1+d,
> \qquad \sum_{i=1}^k D_i=d+1.}
> \tag{2}
> \]
>
> In particular, `2<=k<=d+1`.

**Proof.** Since `sigma(R)=-cs`, the positive ordinary sum `Q=sum q_i` has the form

\[
Q=p-c+ap,\qquad a\ge0.
\]

Consequently

\[
\sum_i D_i=2Q-(p+d)=d+1+2ap\ge d+1.
\tag{3}
\]

Order the factors arbitrarily and take the first prefix whose ordinary defect sum `S` exceeds `d`. Each summand is in `[1,d]`, so

\[
d+1\le S\le2d<2p.
\]

If the prefix is proper, its union `Y` is subject to (1). Let `h=floor((sum_prefix q_i)/p)`. Canonical reduction of its light sum gives

\[
D(Y)=S-2ph.
\]

For `h=0` this exceeds `d`; for `h>=1` it is negative. Both contradict (1). Therefore the first crossing is the whole factorization. Equation (3) and `S<=2d<2p` now force `a=0` and `S=d+1`. Positivity of the summands proves the factor-count bound. There are at least two factors because `|R|>p`. QED.

No asymptotic condition such as `d^2<p` was used. The whole allowed range `1<=d<=H-1` is covered.

For any proper subcollection of this factorization, the canonical light sum and defect are their ordinary sums: all `q_i` are positive and their total is below `p`. In particular, any complementary nonempty projected-zero pair `Y,R/Y` obeys

\[
q(Y)+q(R/Y)=p-c,\qquad D(Y)+D(R/Y)=d+1.
\tag{4}
\]

This follows by atomizing the two parts and applying (2).

## 3. A finite defect alphabet, uniform in the prime

Let an atom have counts `A,B` on `x,y`. The rank-two relation space on `s,x,y` is one-dimensional, generated by `(c,r,t)`. Thus

\[
(-q,A,B)=\lambda(c,r,t)\quad\text{in }\mathbb F_p^3.
\]

As `r+t=p+d` and `-2c=2d+1` modulo `p`,

\[
\ell\equiv d\lambda,\qquad
2q\equiv(2d+1)\lambda,\qquad
D\equiv(d+1)\lambda\pmod p.
\]

Hence, with `[z]_p` denoting the least nonnegative residue,

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\lambda&=D(d+1)^{-1},\\
A_D&=[rD(d+1)^{-1}]_p,\\
B_D&=[tD(d+1)^{-1}]_p,\\
\ell_D&=[dD(d+1)^{-1}]_p,\qquad
q_D=(\ell_D+D)/2.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{5}
\]

Here `1<=D<=d`. The length formula uses the cyclic atom bound `ell<=p` and the fact that its displayed residue is nonzero. All atoms are mixed, since fewer than `p` copies of either nonzero projected value cannot sum to zero.

A defect is allowed only if the counts fit, `A_D+B_D=ell_D`, `ell_D+D` is even, `q_D` satisfies (1), and the resulting projected sequence is an atom. Thus there are at most `d` distinct atom types. Every factorization is a partition of the integer `d+1` into these allowed defects, with the exact occurrence vector fixed by each part.

Formula (5) is not an existence assertion for all defects. Also, the least-residue length formula is stated for atoms, not for arbitrary proper projected-zero parts, whose length has not been bounded by `p`.

## 4. General first-crossing mechanism

The proof of (2) is an instance of the following elementary principle. Suppose a zero-sum object is partitioned into atoms, a cyclic aggregate has modulus `n`, and for a positive integer `b` the defect of a part is `b` times its canonical aggregate minus an additive integer size. Suppose every proper nonempty admissible part has defect in `[1,D]`, and the full object's canonical defect is `D+1`. If `2D<bn`, then every atomization has ordinary defect sum `D+1`, with no aggregate carry.

Indeed, a first prefix crossing `D` has defect sum in `[D+1,2D]`. Canonical reduction changes this by a multiple of `bn`, so it cannot become a number in `[1,D]`. The crossing is therefore the whole object, and its total is the specified canonical defect. This formulation requires atoms themselves to be proper and the aggregate sum of a nonempty proper admissible part to have a valid canonical representative. Both hypotheses were checked above.

## 5. Audit and boundary

The proof was checked against the original shifted-depth derivation, including the complementary part, positivity of canonical sums, the sign of every modular carry, the first-crossing endpoint, and the atom-length qualification in (5). No finite enumeration or prime sweep is proof authority.

The `c=H` layer has `|R|=p` and need not have a proper quotient atom. It is outside this theorem. The budget is necessary; it does not supply the remaining mixed donor geometry or establish the full first corridor, `D_3(C_7^3)`, or the generalized Davenport formula.
