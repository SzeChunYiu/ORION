# Type two: an exact quotient budget for every unsaturated rank-three face — V1

Status: **proved prime-uniform structural reduction**. The generalized budget, exact `b=2` alternatives, ordinary-proportion rigid-power corollary, and `b=3` consequence have received a separately tasked internal proof audit and coordinating-researcher review. They do not eliminate every face.

## 1. Setup

Let `p=2H+1>=7` be prime, `m=3H+1`, and

`U=e1^(p-1)e2^(p-1)g^(p-2)s^2`, `2s=e1+e2+2g`.

Suppose that

`V=s^c g x^r y^(p-b)`

is zero-sum, has rank three on its four distinct support values, and that `UV` has no nonempty zero-sum shorter than `m`. We use the already proved interior reduction and saturated-boundary closure, so

`2<=b<=c+1`, `1<=c<=2 floor(H/2)<=H`,

`r=H+b-1-c`, `1<=r<=H`, `|V|=m`.

The actual shared old donor has `p-1` copies of each of `e1,e2,g` and `c+2` copies of `s`.

The relation on `s,g,x,y` is unique up to scalar, because their rank is three. All four coefficients `(c,1,r,-b)` are nonzero in the field. Consequently any three support vectors are independent: a relation on three extended by a zero coefficient would contradict the uniqueness and full support of this relation.

## 2. Saturated shared direction forces a rank-two atom

Put `R=s^c x^r y^(p-b)`, so `sigma(R)=-g` and `|R|=m-1`. Project modulo `L=<g>`. The resulting sequence `bar R` is a zero-sum sequence over `G/L`, of rank two, and is an atom.

For proof, if a proper nonempty part `Y|R` has zero projected sum, write `sigma(Y)=qg`. A lift with `q=0` is itself a short zero-sum, as is the complement when `q=-1`. Therefore the canonical residue lies in `1<=q<=p-2`; the complementary lift is `(p-1-q)g`.

The two sequences

`Y g^(p-q)` and `(R/Y) g^(q+1)`

are separately available zero-sums in `UV`. Each `g` exponent lies in `[2,p-1]`, so each individually fits the actual saturated donor. Their lengths sum to

`|R|+p+1=m+p<2m`.

At least one is shorter than `m`, contradiction. These two certificates need not be disjoint or simultaneously available; only their individual availability is used.

## 3. Every second quotient atomization has zero carry

Within `G/L`, project further modulo `<bar y>`. Denote this second, cyclic quotient by `C_p`. Set

`S=bar s^c bar x^r`, `|S|=L_0=H+b-1<=p-1`.

Its sum is `b bar y`, so the projected sequence `pi(S)` is zero-sum. The two projected values are nonzero, because every pair among `bar s,bar x,bar y` is independent. They are distinct: equality would give a nonzero common value repeated `L_0<p` times with zero sum.

Let `Y|S` be nonempty and proper with zero projected sum, and write its lift as `q bar y`, using a canonical residue. The value `q=0` contradicts atomicity of `bar R`. If `b<=q<=p-1`, then

`Y bar y^(p-q)`

is a proper zero-sum divisor of `bar R`, because `p-q<=p-b` and `Y` is proper in `S`. Therefore every such part has

`1<=q<=b-1`.                                                   (1)

Consider any atomic factorization of `pi(S)`. If it consists of the single atom `pi(S)`, label its lift by `q=b`. Otherwise every lifted atom is proper and has a label in (1). The sum of its positive labels is congruent to `b` modulo `p`, hence is at least `b`.

Order the factors arbitrarily and take the first prefix whose ordinary label sum reaches `b`. Its sum is in

`[b,2b-2] subset [b,p-1]`,

because `b<=H+1`. If this prefix were proper in `S`, its label would be its ordinary sum, contrary to (1). Hence the prefix is the whole factorization. Its total lies in `[b,p-1]` and is congruent to `b`; therefore

\[
\boxed{\sum_i q_i=b.}
\tag{2}
\]

This holds for every quotient atomization. In particular the number of factors is at most `b`. It is not an assertion that every partition of `b` occurs.

## 4. Exact occurrence alphabet

If a quotient atom has `a_i` copies of `bar s` and `d_i` copies of `bar x` and lifted sum `q_i bar y`, the coefficient vector `(a_i,d_i,-q_i)` belongs to the one-dimensional relation space generated by `(c,r,-b)`. Consequently

\[
a_i=[c q_i b^{-1}]_p,\qquad d_i=[r q_i b^{-1}]_p.
\tag{3}
\]

All inverses and least residues in (3) are modulo `p`. The actual integer bounds `a_i<=c`, `d_i<=r` remain necessary. There is at most one occurrence-count vector for each label `q_i`; equal values of a label do not by themselves prove that its vector is atomic.

For a proper atom both entries in (3) are positive, since all of `c,r,b,q_i` are nonzero modulo `p`. Its actual length is the sum of these entries, which is below `p` because it is a part of `S`.

## 5. The atomic branch has an elementary divisor normal form

If `pi(S)` is an atom, it has exactly two distinct values and length `L_0=H+b-1>p/2`. It has normalized index one. For `b>=3`, this follows directly from the long cyclic-atom index theorem at length greater than `p/2+1`, [Savchev--Chen, Section 5 and Proposition 10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0602568). At `b=2`, use the two-value splitting lemma of Xia--Yuan, stated as Lemma 2.5 in [Peng--Sun](https://arxiv.org/html/1409.1970v1). Its split atom has length `H+2` and hence index one. Recombine its two split positive representatives: their sum is less than `p` since other positive terms remain, so the original atom also has positive representatives summing to `p`. The `p>155` condition on Peng--Sun's later main theorems is not a condition of the preliminary splitting lemma. Both cited statements were reopened in their primary papers during this continuation.

Normalize by an index-one generator. The two positive integer coefficients have weighted sum `p`. They cannot both be at least two because `2L_0>p`. Since the values are distinct, exactly one coefficient is one and the other is an integer `j>=2`. Thus one of the following holds:

`c(j-1)=p-L_0=H-b+2`,

`r(j-1)=p-L_0=H-b+2`.                                     (4)

Equations (2)--(4) are necessary structural restrictions; they do not assert full companion realizability.

## 6. The first unsaturated face has exactly two alternatives

Take `b=2`, so `r=H+1-c` and `|S|=H+1`.

If `pi(S)` is atomic, (4) reads `c(j-1)=H` or `r(j-1)=H`.

If it is not atomic, (1)--(2) force exactly two atoms, both with label one. The counts in (3) are `[c/2]_p` and `[r/2]_p`. If `c` were odd, `[c/2]_p=(c+p)/2>c`, impossible; similarly `r` is even. Hence

`c,r are even`, `Q=pi(bar s)^(c/2) pi(bar x)^(r/2)`,

`pi(S)=Q^2`,

and `Q` is the only atomic divisor of `pi(S)`. The last claim follows because any atomic divisor is proper (the whole is reducible), so it has label one and the same uniquely forced counts.

In particular this second alternative requires `H+1` even, or `p=3 (mod 4)`. Its existence is not asserted. The rigid-power bound for this short `Q` alone does not exclude it.

The parity distinction gives the following exact necessary pruning. If `p=1 (mod 4)`, then `c+r=H+1` is odd, so the atomic branch is forced, and `c|H` or `r|H`. If `p=3 (mod 4)` and `c` is odd, both counts are odd and the same atomic branch and divisor restriction hold. If `p=3 (mod 4)` and `c` is even, both counts are even; the atomic branch would require an even count to divide the odd integer `H`, impossible. Hence precisely the rigid-square branch is forced in that parity case. These necessary conditions do not assert the existence of any original companion.

## 7. A general no-wrap criterion forces a rigid power

Suppose additionally that

`(b-1) max(c,r)<p`.                                           (5)

For any proper projected-zero part with label `q`, its actual counts `A,D` satisfy

`bA=cq+np`, `bD=rq+vp`

for integers `n,v`. We have `0<cq<p`, and `1<=A<=c`. Thus `n<=-1` would give a negative `bA`. If `n>=1`, then

`p<=bA-cq<=bc-cq=(b-q)c< p`,

also impossible. Hence `n=0`, and likewise `v=0`. The count relations become ordinary rational proportions:

`A=cq/b`, `D=rq/b`.                                           (6)

Put `h=gcd(b,c,r)`. The two divisibilities `b|cq` and `b|rq` hold precisely when `b/h|q`. If `h=1`, no proper label in `[1,b-1]` is possible, so `pi(S)` itself is an atom.

If `h>=2`, the occurrence sequence

`Q=pi(bar s)^(c/h) pi(bar x)^(r/h)`

has zero projected sum: its lift is `(b/h) bar y`, since dividing the original rank-two relation by `h` is legitimate modulo `p`. It is an atom. Any nonempty proper zero projected part of it would, by (6), have a positive label at least `b/h`, so would contain at least all the displayed occurrences of `Q`, contradiction.

Every proper projected-zero part of `S` is an ordinary power of `Q` by (6). Thus

\[
\boxed{\pi(S)=Q^h,\qquad Q\text{ is its only atomic count-vector type}.}
\]

This statement includes `h=1` with `Q=pi(S)`. It is proved from actual capacities and the lift window, not from a mere displayed power factorization.

## 8. The second unsaturated face has an atomic quotient

For `b=3`, condition (5) always holds because `2 max(c,r)<=2H=p-1`. Also

`c+r=H+2`, `2(H+2)=p+3`.

Since the prime `p>=7` is not three, `gcd(3,H+2)=1`, so `h=gcd(3,c,r)=1`. Section 7 therefore proves that the quotient sequence `pi(S)` is an atom throughout this whole face. Its length is `H+2`, so the strict long-atom index theorem applies directly. Equation (4) becomes

\[
\boxed{c(j-1)=H-1\quad\text{or}\quad r(j-1)=H-1,\qquad j\ge2.}
\]

This is an inverse reduction of every rank-three face with `t=p-3`, not a proof that the face is empty.

## 9. Exact arithmetic separation of the first unsaturated face

At `b=2` one has `c+r=H+1`. If `p==1 (mod 4)`, then `H` is even and `c+r` is odd, so the even-even rigid-square alternative is impossible. Every surviving row must satisfy `c|H` or `r|H`.

If `p==3 (mod 4)`, then `H` is odd. For odd `c`, both `c,r` are odd and the rigid-square alternative is impossible, so the same divisor condition holds. For even `c`, both counts are even; neither can divide odd `H`, so the atomic alternative is impossible and the quotient must be the rigid square of Section 6. These are forced alternatives, not constructions of surviving companions.

## 10. Preserved boundary and failed shortcut

The saturated-value donor classification uses all `p-1` copies of that value. It has not been applied to `p-b` copies here. For `b=2`, the missing complementary pure-power index `p-1` is a genuine gap in that older proof; assuming the saturated inverse normal form would be invalid.

The second quotient budget controls every atomization, but does not select one uniquely in general. Its `b=2` rigid-square alternative still needs additional mixed geometry for elimination. The original rank-three problem is not replaced by an assertion that every formal cyclic budget is realizable.
