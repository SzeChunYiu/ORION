## 1. Purpose

The current ORION papers provide large protected empirical separations and exact bounded formal laws, but they do not yet establish a universal theory of autonomous science. This Grand Challenge defines the independent cross-domain programme required to test whether those paper-local results are instances of one deeper principle.

The aspiration is deliberately field-level: a genuine breakthrough would have to compress several previously separate failure modes into one falsifiable law, make new predictions before outcome access, survive independent implementations and naturalistic scientific artifacts, and improve consequential scientific decisions. Prestige cannot be produced by wording. The programme earns wider authority only through prospective evidence.

## 2. Canonical hypothesis

### SDSP.G1 — cross-domain scientific-discharge hypothesis

> **Across heterogeneous real scientific workflows, adding an explicit target-bound scientific-discharge relation to donor-complete systems will reduce unauthorized scientific-status transitions while preserving non-inferior clean valid transitions. An information-equivalent implementation carrying the same discharge objects and rules will reproduce the decisions.**

The canonical programme principle is the **Scientific Discharge Sufficiency Principle (SDSP)**:

> Processing, aggregating, translating, repeating, optimizing, or composing locally valid scientific artifacts does not by itself enlarge the set of different scientific obligations they are entitled to discharge. A broader target becomes dischargeable only through a registered sound inference or transport bridge, new authorized evidence, or protected revalidation that supplies the missing target-bound premise.

This principle is compatible with legitimate scientific inference. A new conclusion may be established when a sound rule and its premises authorize it. The claim is only that computation or local success cannot substitute for the missing authority-bearing relation.

## 3. Current authority

`SDSP.G1` is a `PROGRAMME_CONJECTURE`, not an established universal law.

Existing bounded support includes:

- P1 protected reformulation and revision-responsibility separations;
- P2 material-route closure and evaluation-transport failures;
- P3 claim-relative identity and integration separations;
- P4 scientific-promotion and evaluator-identifiability results;
- P6 necessary-and-sufficient restoration in a finite certificate-lifting model;
- P7 necessary-and-sufficient closure restoration and exact bridge composition;
- P8 complete finite target-bound composition across the registered thirteen-family donor product;
- repeated information-equivalent product ties supporting representation independence.

Those results motivate the conjecture. They cannot confirm it on their own because the synthesis was formulated after the paper-local outcomes were known.

## 4. Breakthrough criteria

A field-level claim requires all of the following.

1. **Conceptual compression.** One typed discharge relation explains reformulation, closure, identity, promotion, revalidation, navigation, and authority composition without paper-specific decision code.
2. **Prospective prediction.** The calculus predicts new failures, ties, or repairs before hidden outcomes are accessed.
3. **Causal intervention.** Adding the discharge layer changes target scientific-status decisions while holding donor-native mechanisms and visible information fixed.
4. **Clean-utility preservation.** The safety gain is not produced by blanket refusal or systematic under-claiming.
5. **Cross-domain invariance.** The effect survives different disciplines, institutions, implementations, artifact types, and scientific epochs.
6. **Independent reconstruction.** Separate teams reproduce the formal judgments, case bindings, and headline effects without importing the primary decision implementation.
7. **Consequential benefit.** The intervention reduces invalid scientific conclusions, avoidable human correction, unsafe experiment/release decisions, or wasted scientific work in settings where errors matter.

Failure on any criterion narrows the programme claim.

## 5. Participating scientific domains

The first execution should include at least five independently owned domains with native scientific artifacts rather than one reskinned synthetic benchmark.

1. **Biomedical evidence synthesis and claim release.** Examples include systematic-review inclusion, evidence grading, clinical-claim promotion, and update after evidence revocation.
2. **Experimental physics or autonomous instrumentation.** Examples include calibration validity, measurement interpretation, anomaly escalation, and authority to revise a model or release a result.
3. **Materials or chemistry closed-loop experimentation.** Examples include objective revision, experiment-selection authority, provenance-bound measurements, and transfer across instruments or recipes.
4. **Computational discovery, theorem proving, or machine-learning experimentation.** Examples include proof/result certificates, benchmark promotion, model/evaluator changes, and reuse after code or data drift.
5. **Environmental, public-health, or policy evidence synthesis.** Examples include heterogeneous source transport, population/epoch changes, conflicting support families, and decision release.

A sixth domain should be added when feasible as a held-out transfer domain selected after the calculus and interfaces are frozen but before outcome access.

## 6. Unit of analysis

Each case contains:

- one or more donor-native artifacts or judgments with independently verifiable local validity;
- a different target scientific object whose status may or may not change;
- the exact target obligation, scope, content identity, epoch, support requirements, and blocker state;
- candidate-visible information that excludes protected target labels;
- a gold target transition produced by domain experts under a predeclared adjudication protocol;
- a case-level content and provenance binding.

Cases must include both easy and hostile configurations. The decisive cases are those where every local donor mechanism succeeds but the target obligation is not discharged, and matched clean cases where the full target relation is satisfied.

## 7. Experimental arms

All primary arms receive the same donor-native information and resource envelope.

### A0 — strongest domain-native system

The best available native workflow or mechanism for the domain, evaluated on its own declared object and then mapped to the target decision through the domain's ordinary practice.

### A1 — donor-complete product without explicit target discharge

A deliberately strong integrated product containing the mature local mechanisms relevant to the domain, such as retrieval, verification, provenance, certificates, authorization, custody, uncertainty, transport metadata, or replanning. It lacks only the complete target-bound discharge relation under test.

### A2 — SDSP discharge layer

The same donor-complete product plus the generic typed object, obligation, bridge, support, blocker, revalidation, and revocation rules declared in the foundational protocol. Domain customization is limited to type and rule declarations. Paper-specific or case-family-specific decision code is prohibited.

### A3 — information-equivalent ideal product

An independently implemented product carrying the same scientific objects and sound discharge rules as A2. It tests representation independence and guards against an ORION-centralization claim.

### Controls

- **compensatory scalar product:** allows strong local signals to compensate for failed target prerequisites;
- **blanket fail-closed control:** refuses every nontrivial transition, testing whether A2's safety comes from indiscriminate abstention;
- **target-agnostic aggregation control:** combines all locally valid artifacts without target-bound bridge semantics;
- **registered ablations:** remove one load-bearing discharge coordinate or rule at a time.

## 8. Primary outcomes

### Primary safety outcome

**False scientific-status transition rate** over cases whose gold target obligation does not permit the proposed transition.

Examples include false reformulation, false task closure, false identity merge, false claim promotion, stale standing preservation, false closure inheritance, and unauthorized cross-domain discharge.

### Co-primary clean outcome

**Correct clean scientific-status transition rate** over cases in which every target obligation is discharged.

The programme succeeds only if the safety improvement is accompanied by clean performance above a predeclared non-inferiority margin determined through outcome-blind power analysis.

### Exact-decision outcome

**Exact target terminal accuracy** over the full registered terminal set, preserving distinctions such as `DISCHARGE`, `CONTINUE/REOPEN`, `CANNOT_CHECK`, and `BLOCK`.

## 9. Secondary outcomes

- target/subject/content/scope/epoch attribution accuracy;
- selective reopening or revalidation cost;
- amount of unaffected valid assurance retained;
- false-null and false-superiority rate under invalid evaluation transport;
- independent replay and audit time;
- robustness to evidence correlation, provider failure, evaluator corruption, representation change, revocation, and epoch drift;
- downstream expert correction burden;
- scientific decision latency and resource use;
- domain-expert assessment of whether the final status transition is actionable and correctly justified.

No secondary outcome may replace a failed primary endpoint after outcome access.

## 10. Prospectively frozen predictions

Before hidden-case generation, the programme should freeze at least these predictions.

### PRED-1 — local-success separation

A1 will match A2 on donor-native local judgments but make more false target-status transitions on cases where the missing information is specifically the target-bound discharge relation.

### PRED-2 — clean preservation

A2 will retain high clean transition accuracy and materially outperform the blanket fail-closed control.

### PRED-3 — portability

A3 will be decision-identical or statistically indistinguishable from A2 within a predeclared exact or practical equivalence criterion.

### PRED-4 — coordinate specificity

Each registered ablation will fail primarily on cases whose gold decision depends on the removed coordinate or rule, rather than producing undifferentiated degradation.

### PRED-5 — valid novel inference

A2 will permit genuinely new conclusions when a registered sound inference rule and its premises are present. It will therefore outperform a trivial rule that never allows authority to grow.

### PRED-6 — invalid transport firewall

A2 will refuse to mint either a positive or valid null comparison when the evaluation/provider transport contract is invalid, while retaining power on valid transports.

### PRED-7 — prospective benchmark diagnosis

The calculus will identify at least one previously unexecuted benchmark construction whose score is predicted to be non-identifying for its intended competence; a frozen audit will confirm or refute that prediction.

### PRED-8 — sufficient-interface tie

The calculus will prospectively identify at least one strong reduced interface predicted to tie a richer ORION representation on target decisions while differing in audit or provenance richness.

## 11. Case-generation and custody

- The object/obligation/bridge schemas, arm interfaces, scoring rules, exclusions, and analysis plan are frozen before hidden outcomes.
- Development cases are public and excluded from confirmatory inference.
- Protected cases are generated or selected by an independent host lane using a committed secret or externally held sampling seed.
- Candidate-visible packets exclude gold labels, family names, custody classes, and fields that trivially reveal the target terminal.
- Scored execution is separated from protected evaluation.
- Network, file, environment, and protected-label access are recorded where technically possible.
- The evaluator becomes eligible to access protected gold only after all primary arms finish.
- Independent reconstruction uses separately written decision code and recomputes the canonical case and result digests.

## 12. Statistical and formal analysis

The final plan must be frozen after outcome-blind power analysis and before protected execution.

At minimum:

- paired effects for A2 versus A1 on false transitions and exact target decisions;
- domain-stratified uncertainty intervals;
- clean non-inferiority analysis;
- per-domain and per-failure-family direction checks;
- exact accounting of all failures, abstentions, timeouts, and candidate-caused errors;
- multiplicity control or a declared hierarchy for multiple co-primary claims;
- separate confirmatory and exploratory tables;
- equivalence or exact-match analysis for A2 versus A3;
- model checking of the registered formal fragment;
- independent reproduction of every headline count and digest.

The study should not select margins from observed hidden effects.

## 13. Primary falsifiers

`SDSP.G1` is rejected, narrowed, or absorbed if any of the following occurs.

1. A1 matches A2 on the decisive target-status cases despite lacking an explicit or equivalent discharge relation.
2. A proposed discharge coordinate is inert under intervention.
3. A2 reduces false transitions only by suppressing clean valid transitions.
4. A2 blocks legitimate registered novel inference because it mistakes all scientific authority growth for laundering.
5. The generic calculus requires paper-specific algorithmic branches or ad hoc exceptions.
6. An information-equivalent A3 implementation disagrees materially with A2.
7. The effect disappears on naturalistic artifacts, independent institutions, or held-out domains.
8. A prior formalism already entails an equivalent target-bound scientific-discharge calculus at equal or broader scope.
9. Independent teams cannot reproduce the case bindings, judgments, or effects.
10. The intervention produces no meaningful improvement in consequential scientific or human decision outcomes.

A falsifier is a scientific result, not a reason to conceal the campaign.

## 14. Human-benefit evaluation

A broad-impact claim requires direct outcomes rather than analogy. Domain teams should predeclare one consequential measure, such as:

- invalid clinical or scientific claim releases prevented;
- unsafe or scientifically unauthorized experiments blocked without loss of valid experiments;
- expert-hours saved in audit, revalidation, or correction;
- stale conclusions correctly reopened after evidence, model, instrument, or evaluator change;
- false scientific identity merges prevented;
- resource waste avoided by preserving unaffected valid certificates or closure;
- downstream decisions improved under blinded expert adjudication.

ORION should not claim benefit to humankind merely because the formalism is elegant or an agent benchmark improves.

## 15. Governance and independence

- Each domain has a native scientific owner and an independent claim-authority auditor.
- At least one external institution owns a protected domain and gold adjudication.
- The main authors do not tune candidate logic after protected outcomes.
- The ideal-product implementation is written by a separate team.
- Negative, null, tie, contamination, invalid-transport, and incomplete-execution records remain in the archive.
- All changes after freeze are classified as execution repair, protocol amendment, or new study; none silently alters the confirmatory subject.

## 16. Success ladder

### Level 1 — generic formal compatibility

One calculus recovers the existing registered paper decisions without paper-specific decision branches.

### Level 2 — prospective prediction

The calculus correctly predicts new hostile failures, ties, or repairs before outcome access.

### Level 3 — cross-domain causal effect

A2 reduces false target transitions versus A1 while preserving clean valid transitions across independently owned domains.

### Level 4 — independent and naturalistic transfer

External institutions and information-equivalent implementations reproduce the law on native artifacts and held-out domains.

### Level 5 — consequential scientific benefit

The intervention measurably improves real scientific decisions, safety, audit burden, or resource use.

Only after Levels 1–5 should ORION present SDSP as a candidate foundational principle of autonomous science. Even then, priority and long-term significance are judged by the scientific community and subsequent history, not by the project itself.
