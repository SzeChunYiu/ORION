## Intended successor-paper statement

The higher-order ORION implementation should **not** be presented as a newly invented universal calculus of scientific reasoning.

A parent-formalism saturation pass identifies mature foundations for the principal ingredients: model-based diagnosis and preferred hypotheses; AGM/minimal and iterated belief revision; Blackwell/state-abstraction sufficiency; rational metareasoning/value of computation; viability/safe-set control; common-knowledge/dependent-information aggregation; and minimal-repair/persistence theory.

> a typed, versioned, non-authorizing scientific-admissibility interface that composes these components with explicit read/write footprints, preservation/reopening obligations, hard evidence/authority requirements, fail-closed unresolved states, and separation between diagnosis, proposal, validation, authorization and adoption.

## Why the implementation remains valuable

The negative result does not require deleting T1–T8. Their value is that incompatible authority semantics can otherwise be lost when mature components are composed in an autonomous-science system.

The finite checker therefore pressures implementation invariants, not novelty:

- minimal registered revisions do not lose to broader ones;
- narrower unresolved alternatives block escalation;
- incomparable minima stay plural;
- authority cannot be purchased by low cost/high utility;
- mandatory scientific verification can outrank optional compute value;
- local compute stopping does not close an unresolved scientific task;
- containment cannot certify truth;
- correlated evidence cannot be relabelled independent;
- framework composition remains non-authorizing.
