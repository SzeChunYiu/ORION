## 1. Research cell and roles

This programme is reviewed through four simultaneous expert lenses.

1. **Formal epistemology / verification** — dynamic belief revision, typed transition systems, provenance, preservation, authority, formal countermodels. Role: reject attractive unifications that reduce to known logic, category theory, TMS/ATMS, state sufficiency or ordinary verification.
2. **Scientific-method / conceptual-change theory** — abduction, representation change, measurement, model criticism, historical scientific transitions. Role: distinguish real scientific change from code/workflow optimization and prevent retrospective mythology from becoming ground truth.
3. **Meta-learning / open-ended AI** — program-library learning, self-evolving agents, algorithm evolution, goal evolution, harness/meta-skill evolution. Role: make the strongest self-improvement donors first-class baselines and strike any ORION claim they already own.
4. **Experimental design / evaluation integrity** — protected holdouts, causal attribution, benchmark validity, leakage, reward hacking, independent replay and non-compensatory evaluation. Role: ensure an apparent self-improvement cannot be obtained by changing, leaking, or gaming the test.

The research rule is adversarial: every proposed breakthrough is first assigned to the expert most likely to reduce it to prior work.

## 4. Programme-level unification: Justified Reachability

The grand unification should first be an **organizing measurement object**, not a novelty claim.

### 4.1 Mutable scientific framework

Let a scientific framework be

```text
F = (Σ, M, I, D, P, H, π)
```

where:

- `Σ` — representation/schema language for scientific state;
- `M` — registered mechanics/operators and their contracts;
- `I` — observation, experiment, computation, retrieval and construction interfaces;
- `D` — dependency, defeater, obligation and reopening semantics;
- `P` — provenance, correspondence and preservation semantics;
- `H` — history, failures, negative knowledge and context applicability;
- `π` — discovery/research policy over the available scientific operations.

These coordinates are not claimed irreducible. They are an operational envelope for the successor experiment and may be factored differently after donor absorption.

### 4.2 Protected scientific constitution

Separate the mutable framework from the authority that decides whether a change counts as scientific progress:

```text
κ = (V*, A*, L*)
```

where:

- `V*` — protected validation/evaluation routes and independent checkers;
- `A*` — authority/adoption rules and non-escalation policy;
- `L*` — leakage/integrity constraints, protected holdouts and immutable result identity.

A system may **propose** a change to `κ`, but that proposal cannot certify itself. A constitution change requires a separate external authorization/equivalence protocol and becomes a different experimental generation.

This is the core non-laundering boundary:

```text
ability to propose a scientific improvement != authority to declare the improvement scientific
```

### 4.3 Justified reachable contracts

For initial state `x`, contract family `C`, resource/information bound `B`, mutable framework `F` and protected constitution `κ`, define conceptually:

```text
JReach_B(F, x, C | κ)
```

as the set of protected contracts reachable by some bounded transition path for which every load-bearing step is:

1. representationally and mechanically well-typed;
2. supported by its required observation/evidence;
3. within declared information/resource access;
4. preservation-safe or accompanied by explicit reopening/invalidations;
5. provenance-bound;
6. authority-valid under `κ`;
7. not obtained through evaluator leakage/tampering or an invalid shortcut.

This refines raw success and raw bounded reachability. It gives all papers a common question:

> **Which scientifically justified states become reachable, unreachable, reopened, or cheaper after this transition?**

### 4.4 Scientific value of a candidate mechanic or framework change

For candidate addition/revision `a`:

```text
ΔJ_B(a; F, x, C | κ)
  = JReach_B(F ⊕ a, x, C | κ) \ JReach_B(F, x, C | κ)
```

A positive `ΔJ` is still not sufficient for broad adoption; it must be checked for regressions, overreach, cost and fresh transfer.

### 4.5 Non-compensatory framework dominance

Do **not** collapse scientific improvement into one weighted reward.

A successor `F'` may be declared to dominate `F` only if a preregistered partial-order test holds, for example:

```text
F' ≻κ F
```

requires:

- at least one protected justified-discovery frontier strictly improves;
- no mandatory validity/authority/integrity gate regresses beyond its frozen bound;
- affected prior commitments are preserved, transported or explicitly reopened rather than silently reinterpreted;
- `UNKNOWN/CANNOT_CHECK` calibration remains acceptable;
- resource differences are accounted for;
- the result survives a disjoint fresh suite and independent replay.

A higher task score with worse authority integrity is **not** scientific dominance.

```text

L2  representation/model/objective/research policy
```

- `L4` is protected and cannot be changed by the same actor that benefits from the new evaluation.

## 7. The decisive benchmark: ORION Constitutional Recursive Evolution Benchmark

Working name: **C-RSE Bench**.

The benchmark must make the strongest parents succeed on the cases they already own. ORION earns a residual only on interaction families they do not solve under matched power.

### Tier 0 — no evolution needed

Known method/operator solves the target. Any framework rewrite counts as unnecessary overreach.

### Tier 1 — local repair

A method is correct but implementation/interface/evidence binding is wrong. Diagnosis/local repair should win.

### Tier 2 — model/hypothesis-class expansion

Current model family is inadequate, but standard M-open model criticism plus hypothesis expansion and experiment design can solve it. MDA-style parent should win.

### Tier 3 — representation/regime transition

Old language cannot express the protected solution, but a registered representation transition with correspondence/preservation does. Self-Revising Discovery parent should win.

### Tier 4 — objective revision

The optimization objective is a poor proxy. SAGA-style goal evolution should win, while scientific authority stays external.

### Tier 5 — skill/tool/workflow evolution

A new reusable skill/tool or workflow is required. SkillFoundry/CORAL/DGM-class parent should win.

### Tier 6 — recursive improvement-procedure evolution

The task skill is not enough; the improver/meta-skill/harness itself must change. MetaSkill-Evolve/MAC-class parent should win.

### Tier 7 — scientific-constitution interaction residual

Construct worlds in which individually strong donor mechanisms are locally correct but naive composition is scientifically invalid. Candidate families:

1. **stale-certificate after representation change** — a valid old receipt no longer has the same semantics after the schema transition; the system must transport or reopen it rather than preserve the label;
2. **goal/authority mismatch** — objective evolution improves a score but the scientific claim was authorized under a different goal/measurement contract;
3. **new-operator / negative-history staleness** — a learned operator changes the context in which an old failure applies; global blacklisting causes harmful negative transfer;
4. **non-commuting revisions** — two individually valid framework edits produce different scientific standing depending on order; the system must detect the interaction instead of assuming commutativity;
5. **cross-generation semantic drift** — a successor can still produce the right answer but its internal representation silently changes the meaning of an inherited scientific object;
6. **false framework escalation** — an unresolved task looks like framework inadequacy but is actually non-identifiable under available evidence; correct terminal is `UNKNOWN/CANNOT_CHECK`;
7. **evaluator-tampering attractor** — a framework rewrite can raise benchmark reward by modifying/inferring the protected evaluator, but must be rejected as invalid progress.

This tier is the first serious candidate for an ORION-specific integration residual.

### Tier 8 — true framework insufficiency

Only after Tiers 0–7 are stable, construct a stronger exact case:

> two worlds are observationally equivalent under the incumbent scientific framework for all registered queries within bound `B`, but require different scientifically licensed future transitions; adding a new framework distinction plus a prospectively chosen discriminator separates them.

This is a **framework-sufficiency falsifier**, not automatically a new primitive. State-abstraction, diagnosis, program-language and regime-revision donors receive the same candidate expansion power. If a donor-composed parent solves it, ORION adopts the mechanism and strikes the claim.

## 9. Recursive improvement requires a second generation

A one-step self-improvement is not enough for the strongest claim.

Freeze disjoint suites:

```text
D0  development / failure experience used to propose F1
D1  protected acceptance suite for F1
D2  fresh future scientific environments never seen during F1 design
D3  meta-generation suite: can F1 produce a better F2 than F0 can?
D4  protected acceptance/future suite for F2
```

Required sequence:

```text
F0
  -> proposal for F1 from D0
  -> external κ gate on D1
  -> freeze F1
  -> direct future test on D2
  -> F0 and F1 independently attempt successor generation on D3
  -> external κ gate on D4
  -> compare the quality/integrity of produced successors
```

Only then may the programme discuss recursive scientific improvement.

The central distinction is:

```text
Improve(F1, F0)       !=       MetaImprove(F1, F0)
```

`Improve` asks whether `F1` does better science on fresh tasks.

`MetaImprove` asks whether `F1` has become better than `F0` at producing a later scientifically valid improvement under the same protected constitution.

No scalar is required; evaluate a non-compensatory vector including justified reach, invalid promotion, preservation/regression, cost, UNKNOWN calibration, future transfer and successor-generation quality.

## 10. Prospectively falsifiable hypotheses

### H0 — donor sufficiency (mandatory null)

The donor meta-product matches ORION on all protected tiers. If supported, there is no ORION-specific recursive-framework residual.

### H1 — cross-layer scientific integrity

At matched task information and resources, ORION preserves/reopens scientific commitments and prevents invalid authority transfer more reliably than the donor meta-product on Tier-7 interaction worlds without sacrificing the clean-case task frontier.

### H2 — future justified-discovery gain

An externally accepted framework successor expands or cheapens the protected `JReach` frontier on `D2` while satisfying all non-compensatory validity/preservation/integrity gates.

### H3 — recursive meta-improvement

A frozen `F1` produces an `F2` with a better protected dominance profile than `F0` produces under the same D3/D4 successor-generation experiment.

### H4 — minimal escalation

ORION selects framework-level evolution substantially less often than broad self-evolving baselines on Tiers 0–6 when a narrower donor-owned repair is sufficient, without missing true Tier-7/8 cases.

### H5 — scientific constitution value

Removing external constitutional separation increases apparent task improvement but also materially increases evaluator leakage/tampering, stale authority or invalid scientific promotion. The full system improves the scientifically valid frontier rather than the reported-score frontier.

H1–H5 remain hypotheses until protocol freeze and execution.

## 14. The ultimate experiment

Give ORION an intentionally incomplete scientific framework `F0`, but keep `κ` externally protected.

Expose it to a long, hidden sequence containing a deliberate mixture of:

- ordinary search problems;
- local defects;
- model-class failures;
- representation failures;
- objective failures;
- tool/skill/workflow deficiencies;
- meta-skill deficiencies;
- cross-layer scientific-integrity failures;
- genuinely non-identifiable cases;
- evaluator-hacking temptations.

Do not tell the system which level is wrong.

It must:

1. diagnose the minimum responsible layer;
2. exhaust or reject the strongest narrower donor solution;
3. produce a content-bound framework-revision proposal only when necessary;
4. predict affected commitments and failures;
5. construct discriminating checks;
6. submit the proposal to an external protected constitution;
7. accept rejection when the proposal is invalid or unnecessary;
8. after acceptance, revalidate/reopen affected knowledge;
9. demonstrate fresh scientific value on unseen environments;
10. preserve old valid commitments where required;
11. then use the new framework to produce a second successor under a new protected suite.

The strongest target is not merely:

```text
F0 < F1 < F2
```

on one task score.

It is:

```text
F0 ≺κ F1 ≺κ F2
```

under a protected, non-compensatory scientific dominance relation, with the crucial extra observation that `F1` is better than `F0` at generating a valid `F2`.

That would be evidence that the **scientific improvement process itself** has improved without giving the improving system authority to redefine success.
