# P1 — Minimal responsibility-conditioned epistemic mutation

## P1.1 Formal object

Let `(F,≤)` be a finite or complete formulation poset. A formulation `f∈F` exposes an observational partition `Π_f` of possible scientific worlds. A responsibility `r` induces the target partition `Π_r`, where two worlds share a cell exactly when they require the same admissible terminal for `r`.

Define adequacy:

\[
Adeq_r(f) \iff \Pi_f \preceq \Pi_r,
\]

where `Π_f≼Π_r` means `Π_f` refines `Π_r`. A mutation is a monotone move `f≤g`. Its cost is a declared vector `c(f,g)`.

## P1-T1 — formulation insufficiency

If one cell of `Π_f` intersects two cells of `Π_r`, no policy observing only formulation `f` can be correct for `r` on both worlds.

**Proof.** OSTC-T2.

## P1-T2 — minimal repair theorem

Let

\[
Rep_r(f)=\{g\in F:f\le g\land Adeq_r(g)\}.
\]

Every inclusion-minimal element of `Rep_r(f)` is a responsibility-complete minimal mutation. Every strict predecessor between `f` and such a `g` remains inadequate.

If adequacy is generated by a closure operator `cl_r:F→F` satisfying extensivity, monotonicity, idempotence, and

\[
Adeq_r(g)\iff cl_r(f)\le g\quad(f\le g),
\]

then `cl_r(f)` is the unique least repair.

## P1-T3 — attribution identifiability

Let candidate failure causes be rows of an intervention-response matrix. The responsible layer is identifiable exactly when the selected interventions separate every candidate cause. Minimum diagnostic design is a minimum separating-column problem.

**Converse.** Colliding rows force set-valued diagnosis or `CANNOT_CHECK`.

## P1-T4 — recursive reconstruction termination

Let `rank(Σ)` be a well-founded lexicographic tuple of unresolved responsibility ambiguity, open obstruction count, and protected change budget. If every adopted reconstruction strictly lowers `rank`, no infinite adopted reconstruction sequence exists.

## P1-T5 — protected adoption

A diagnosis or mutation proposal carries zero adoption authority. Adoption requires a P5 evolution certificate with fresh-transfer and external-adoption witnesses.

## P1 falsifiers

- a strictly smaller mutation is adequate;
- a transcript-only policy distinguishes colliding causes without new information;
- an accepted repair does not reduce the declared rank;
- RR1 implementation constraints are used to redefine adequacy after outcomes.

## Frozen predictions

- matched worlds with the same failure transcript but different causes defeat transcript-only attribution;
- a discriminating intervention resolves exactly the collided cause pair;
- the minimal mutation preserves all distinctions unrelated to `r`;
- over-broad reframing incurs unnecessary cost without target-decision benefit.

# P2 — Assumption-indexed open-world scientific closure

## P2.1 Formal object

A world is `(U,M,A)` where `U` is a possibly extensible route/source universe, `M⊆U` the material obligations, and `A_t⊆U` the acquired/processed items by history `t`. The observer receives `h_t=Obs(U,A_t)` rather than `U` itself.

Define distinct predicates:

```text
RouteStop(route)
SourceFamilyStop(family)
TaskClosure(M subseteq discharged(A_t))
PopulationCompleteness(A_t = U)
```

## P2-T1 — open-world closure impossibility

For every finite history-only rule, if the workflow class contains two worlds with identical `h_t` but different task-closure truth, the rule is unsound on at least one. Under equal prior, error is at least `1/2`.

## P2-T2 — bounded-universe positive closure

If `U` is finite, fully enumerated by a protected registry, every materiality predicate is decidable, and every registered material item has a discharged processing obligation, then exact task closure is decidable by finite conjunction.

## P2-T3 — capture-model closure certificate

Under a frozen probabilistic acquisition model `P_θ` with valid identifiability and calibration assumptions, a closure certificate may bound residual material mass or premature-stop risk. The certificate authority is conditional on `θ`, censoring, dependence, and transport validity; invalid model transport yields `CANNOT_CHECK`, not closure.

## P2-T4 — stopping hierarchy

Task closure implies every mandatory route is stopped or discharged, but route stop does not imply task closure. Population completeness implies task closure only when every population member’s materiality/processing obligations are covered.

## P2 falsifiers

- a hidden material route exists under the same visible history;
- route dependence invalidates a capture estimator;
- provider transport changes admissible exposure;
- local stop is promoted to global closure.

## Frozen predictions

- history-identical complete/incomplete pairs force divergence only after a coverage witness is supplied;
- blanket continuation wastes work on bounded-complete worlds;
- value-only stopping false-closes worlds with unresolved processing obligations.

# P3 — Scientific gluing, identity, and obstruction

## P3.1 Formal object

Let `C` be a finite overlap category of sources and `D:C→S` a diagram of local scientific portraits, where `S` carries typed referent, construct, measurement, context, modality, attribution, and epoch coordinates. A global portrait is a cone `(G,{η_c})` satisfying every overlap compatibility equation.

## P3-T1 — gluing existence

A global portrait exists exactly when the system of compatibility equations induced by all overlap maps has a solution in `S`.

## P3-T2 — uniqueness up to isomorphism

If the overlap projections are jointly monic on the registered object class, any two global portraits inducing the same local projections are isomorphic.

## P3-T3 — pairwise compatibility is not global compatibility

There are three-source cycles whose pairwise overlap equations are satisfiable but whose composite cycle equation is inconsistent. Hence pairwise matching cannot certify global scientific identity.

## P3-T4 — obstruction preservation

When the cone set is empty, a sound integrator must emit a typed gluing obstruction or `CANNOT_CHECK`; forced canonicalization creates a false merge. When several non-isomorphic cones remain, the correct object is a plural portrait set rather than a fabricated unique identity.

## P3-T5 — representation invariance

Information-equivalent typed implementations carrying the same diagram and identity equations return the same cone/obstruction judgments by OSTC-T9.

## P3 falsifiers

- raw extraction errors are counted as canonicalization failures;
- a simpler typed graph solves every proposed sheaf/local-map discriminator;
- a claimed unique portrait has a second non-isomorphic cone;
- unavailable mapping coordinates are coerced into identity.

## Frozen predictions

- cycle-hostile cases separate pairwise matchers from global consistency;
- exact-coordinate controls tie the ideal product;
- missing maps cause `CANNOT_CHECK`, not merge or split by default.

# P4 — Authorized scientific full abstraction and promotion

## P4.1 Formal object

Let `Ω` be scientific cases, `Φ` the evaluator/verification interface, and `T_r` the correct promotion terminal for responsibility `r`. Promotion also consumes a typed bridge, authority, support family, and blocker state.

## P4-T1 — exact evaluator attainability

Zero-error target decision from `Φ` is possible iff `T_r` is constant on every fibre of `Φ`.

## P4-T2 — irreducible target risk

Under finite mass `μ`, the optimal 0–1 error is the sum, over fibres, of total fibre mass minus modal target mass. More optimization cannot beat this representation-induced lower bound.

## P4-T3 — data processing

For any post-processing `G`, the partition of `G∘Φ` is no finer than that of `Φ`; therefore target risk cannot improve unless `G` acquires new target-relevant information.

## P4-T4 — promotion normal form

For an available result, scientific promotion requires native validity, target sufficiency, typed entitlement, and blocker/support clearance. Verification, provenance, custody, or generic action permission may be premises but are not individually sufficient.

## P4-T5 — nuisance identifiability

If a target-irrelevant construction variable determines the benchmark label within every target-competence class, benchmark score does not identify the claimed competence. A nuisance-resistant benchmark must contain matched nuisance worlds with different competence terminals.

## P4 falsifiers

- a mixed fibre is nevertheless solved without extra information;
- a native comparator can emit required terminals but was artificially restricted;
- the promotion policy reads protected gold;
- blanket refusal is scored as safety without useful-promotion retention.

## Frozen predictions

- a reduced sufficient interface ties a richer audit record on decisions;
- adding one hidden load-bearing coordinate causes exactly the predicted divergence;
- a saturated benchmark cannot order systems on the intended competence.

# P5 — Protected recursive scientific improvement

## P5.1 Formal object

A self-change cycle is

\[
Σ_i \xrightarrow{propose} c_i \xrightarrow{isolate/replay/fresh} E_i
\xrightarrow{external\ adopt} Σ_{i+1}.
\]

Proposal, evidence, and adoption are distinct modalities. Candidate write authority excludes protected evaluator identity, fresh tasks, constitutional invariants, and final adoption.

## P5-T1 — reflexive custody impossibility

If the candidate controls both evaluator predicate and all evidence read by it, there exist a genuine-improvement world and a gaming world with identical candidate-visible PASS transcripts but different protected truth. No internal gate is sound and nontrivial on both.

## P5-T2 — protected adoption sufficiency

Within the declared evaluation class, adoption is sound when the evolution certificate binds candidate identity, pre-candidate evaluator, isolated execution, replay, fresh transfer, protected assurance, negative-history update, and external adoption authority, and every required predicate passes.

## P5-T3 — sequential false-adoption control

When each adaptive candidate is tested by a valid anytime rule or a predeclared multiplicity procedure, the programme inherits that donor’s false-adoption guarantee. ORION novelty is the custody and scientific-adoption semantics, not the statistical martingale or correction.

## P5-T4 — causal credit

A performance change is attributable to an intervention only when the comparison identifies the intervention under matched subject, evaluator, resources, and task distribution. Without intervention separation, the correct result is an attribution set.

## P5-T5 — termination and negative history

If adopted changes strictly decrease a well-founded unresolved rank and rejected/harmful changes become constraints in append-only history, adopted recursion terminates within the finite protected budget.

## P5 falsifiers

- evaluator identity changes after candidate generation;
- fresh tasks are exposed or reused;
- same-owner CI grants adoption;
- harmful regressions are omitted;
- a proposal merges itself.

## Frozen predictions

- direct self-editing gains apparent acceptance under evaluator-control attacks;
- external custody blocks the gaming twin while admitting genuine improvement;
- negative-history retention prevents recurrence of a previously rejected failure class.

# P6 — Conservative certificate lifting and selective revalidation

## P6.1 Formal object

Let scientific coordinates form vertices `V`, local artifacts and bridges form a directed support hypergraph, and each target judgment have alternative complete support families `F_1,…,F_m`. A regime change alters coordinate set `Δ⊆V`.

## P6-T1 — exact certificate survival

A target certificate survives iff at least one complete support family remains valid and contains no invalidated load-bearing bridge coordinate.

## P6-T2 — selective revalidation

For a single support family, revalidating the backward dependency slice of `Δ` is sufficient, and every strict subset omitting a load-bearing affected node fails on a compatible semantics.

## P6-T3 — alternative-support repair

With multiple support families, restoration requires making at least one complete family valid. Minimum-cost restoration is a weighted family/repair selection problem; it is not necessarily full reset or unique backward closure.

## P6-T4 — repair optimization complexity

Minimum revalidation with alternative supports is NP-hard by reduction from set cover/hitting set: repair actions cover invalidated obligations, and a complete restored family corresponds to a cover.

## P6-T5 — mechanic commutation

Two deterministic mechanics commute on the scientific-state projection when their read/write frames are noninterfering and their history effects are equivalent under swapping independent events. Disjoint writes alone are insufficient when one reads the other’s write.

## P6 falsifiers

- an affected dependency is absent from the graph;
- an independent support family is unnecessarily revoked;
- overlapping frames are claimed necessarily noncommuting;
- donor-native validity is erased because scientific standing changes.

## Frozen predictions

- selective repair retains all unaffected native certificates;
- full reset is sound but strictly more expensive on sparse changes;
- omitted-edge mutants create stale standing.

# P7 — Closure-carrying scientific regime transport

## P7.1 Formal object

A regime is `ρ=(ontology,objective,measurement,evidence semantics,coverage,epoch)`. A transport morphism maps objects, responsibilities, evidence meaning, authority, and closure contracts.

## P7-T1 — transport soundness

A closed obligation transports only when every load-bearing coordinate is preserved or connected by a registered sound bridge. Native transform validity does not imply closure transport.

## P7-T2 — coherence and composition

For composable regime morphisms `f,g`, transported certificates compose when the intermediate contracts match. Path independence holds exactly on coordinates whose transport squares commute.

## P7-T3 — path dependence

A noncommuting ontology/objective/evidence square yields a witness whose final responsibility or terminal differs by path; the old closure must reopen or become `CANNOT_CHECK`.

## P7-T4 — maximal sound abstract licensing

Given an abstraction `α` from concrete support states to opaque signatures, the maximal sound closure rule accepts exactly abstract cells contained wholly in the concrete-closed set. Mixed cells must refuse. This is the best-correct-abstraction result; richer evidence can reduce unnecessary reopening by splitting mixed cells.

## P7-T5 — price of opacity

The unavoidable false-refusal mass of an abstract rule is the probability mass of concrete-closed states lying in mixed abstract cells. No rule using only the abstraction can improve this without false closure.

## P7 falsifiers

- a value-preserving transform changes obligation meaning;
- an opaque signature is treated as universal closure evidence;
- a path-dependent loop is reported as compositional;
- always-reopen is compared without cost.

## Frozen predictions

- witness-aware transport ties on exact bridge cases and separates on hidden-support cases;
- ontology, objective, and evidence-interface changes produce qualitatively different reopen reasons;
- exact containment removes unnecessary reopens only where the abstract cell becomes pure.

# P8 — Typed scientific authority algebra

## P8.1 Formal object

Authority types include `(domain,kind,scope,content,responsibility,epoch)`, ordered by attenuation. Scientific discharge is the least closure under native judgments, sound inference, protected coercion, complete support, blocker clearance, and revocation.

## P8-T1 — no amplification

Ordinary derivation and delegation preserve or narrow authority. A broader target type cannot be discharged without an explicit protected coercion carrying the missing scientific premise.

## P8-T2 — permission/discharge separation

Action permission and scientific proposition support are non-inverse relations. There exist action-authorized but scientifically unsupported cases and action-denied cases whose proposition is independently supported.

## P8-T3 — coercion composition

Protected coercions compose only when target/source types and authority premises match; composed authority remains bounded by the protected root and declared target.

## P8-T4 — exact revocation

A judgment survives revocation exactly when one independent complete support family remains unbroken.

## P8-T5 — decentralized equivalence

A shared authority service and a product of decentralized gates are extensionally equal when they expose the same types, bridges, blocker semantics, and support families. Centralization may change operational cost or audit consistency, not expressive authority by itself.

## P8-T6 — cycle safety

Authority cycles are safe only when every cycle is non-widening and has a well-founded or fixed-point semantics that cannot introduce unsupported judgments. Otherwise the cycle is rejected or remains unresolved.

## P8 falsifiers

- donor identity loops are counted as heterogeneous evidence;
- confidence compensates for an absolute blocker;
- domain-only typing omits content/scope/epoch collisions;
- shared service secretly receives stronger information.

## Frozen predictions

- strong local authorization products false-discharge matched cross-domain cases;
- ideal fully typed products tie exactly;
- revocation removes only dependence-bearing conclusions.

# P9 — Interventional capability-failure diagnosis

## P9.1 Formal object

Let causes be

```text
missing semantic information,
inaccessible representation,
insufficient computation,
insufficient model/decoder capacity,
method-language obstruction,
incorrect formulation.
```

Each intervention has a response signature under the declared resource ledger.

## P9-T1 — diagnosis identifiability

A cause is exactly identifiable iff its response row differs from every competing cause on the selected intervention set.

## P9-T2 — minimum diagnostic design

Selecting the minimum intervention set separating every cause pair is a minimum hitting/set-cover problem and is NP-hard in general.

## P9-T3 — access deficiency

For target family `R`, define interface deficiency as the worst increase in optimal target risk relative to a richer reference interface. Zero deficiency is responsibility-relative sufficiency; positive deficiency is an irreducible interface loss under the declared decoder class.

## P9-T4 — compute non-substitution

When two target worlds collapse under the accessible interface, additional computation that receives no new information cannot separate them. Compute escalation can help only when the target lies inside the information/method closure and the binding constraint is computational.

## P9-T5 — vector accounting invariance

A diagnostic comparison is valid only when acquisition, transformation, state, model, fitting, inference, explicit tools, and registered external resources are separately charged. No scalar exchange rate is implied without prices.

## P9 falsifiers

- interventions leak cause labels;
- thresholds are transported after observing failure;
- serializer symbol reminting changes conclusions;
- larger compute is declared useless from a selected finite grid.

## Frozen predictions

- same-performance failures respond differently to accessibility and compute interventions;
- a held-out colliding signature remains `CANNOT_CHECK`;
- generic compute escalation wastes resources on information/method obstructions.

# P10 — Obstruction-certified method-language expansion

## P10.1 Formal object

A method language `L` consists of seed methods and legal composition rules. `Cl(L)` is its least behavioral closure under a frozen information/access/resource model.

## P10-T1 — exact obstruction

If `t∉Cl(L)`, no search, repair, synthesis, or evolution restricted to `L` can reach `t`, regardless of search effort.

## P10-T2 — genuine semantic expansion

An edit `e` is a genuine expansion for target family `T` when `e∉Cl(L)` and at least one held-out `t∈T` lies in `Cl(L∪{e})\Cl(L)`.

## P10-T3 — macro rejection

If `e∈Cl(L)`, adding its name or implementation does not expand behavioral reach; it may improve cost only, which belongs to optimization/compilation rather than invention.

## P10-T4 — minimal expansion

An expansion is inclusion-minimal when removing any new primitive loses at least one protected new-reach target. Cost-minimality is separately price-vector relative.

## P10-T5 — transfer condition

Origin-only selection supports a family-level method claim only when the selected semantic primitive solves prospectively hidden targets sharing the certified obstruction without access widening.

## P10-T6 — synthesis/checking separation

Checking a supplied finite derivation is polynomial in certificate size; finding a derivation is NP-hard by SAT encoding and may be undecidable in unrestricted languages.

## P10 falsifiers

- timeout is called an obstruction;
- candidate grammar encodes the answer;
- stronger retrieval/representation closes the target without expansion;
- edit changes task semantics or gains hidden oracle access.

## Frozen predictions

- complete old-closure donors all fail the exact obstruction;
- a real outside-closure edit creates held-out reach;
- known-method controls produce zero false expansion.

# P11 — Responsibility-relative state/computation/optionality theory

## P11.1 Formal object

A state encoder `φ:X→Z` has compilation cost, memory, update cost, and induced partition. A query/responsibility family has downstream decoder costs, quality constraints, horizon, drift, and recovery cost.

## P11-T1 — responsibility state lower bound

Any exact state for family `R` must have at least as many distinguishable values as the common-refinement partition `∨_{r∈R}Π_r`; therefore its information content is at least the log of the number of cells. Stronger rank/communication bounds may apply for restricted decoders.

## P11-T2 — break-even law

For one-time cost `K`, direct per-query cost `d`, compiled cost `c<d`, compilation dominates after

\[
U^*=\lfloor K/(d-c)\rfloor+1.
\]

Vector resources yield a Pareto region rather than a universal scalar threshold.

## P11-T3 — optionality

The value of retaining raw/universal state equals expected future responsibility benefit plus avoided reconstruction under drift, minus memory/update cost. A responsibility-specific state may dominate now while destroying future optionality.

## P11-T4 — decoder-capacity boundary

A compiled-state advantage is conditional on the allowed downstream access class. If a stronger same-information decoder realizes the target at matched total cost, the state advantage narrows to placement/cost rather than information.

## P11-T5 — phase diagram

The optimal policy lies among retain raw, compile, cache, hybrid retain+compile, or reconstruct, determined by responsibility set, horizon, width, decoder, drift, and price vector.

## P11 falsifiers

- compiler work is free;
- answer is encoded in state;
- selected responsibilities are treated as arbitrary family support;
- negative narrow-width or family-scale results are hidden.

## Frozen predictions

- phase boundaries move predictably with width and responsibility count;
- small responsibility sets and long horizons favor compilation;
- hostile stronger decoders collapse some but not all placement gains.

# P12 — Online vector scientific metareasoning

## P12.1 Formal object

At each time, choose among evidence acquisition, state construction, reasoning, verification, recovery, or abstention under vector budget and typed responsibility. Actions have case-dependent cost vectors and exact-output constraints.

## P12-T1 — exact-certificate optimality

Given exact pre-action costs and a frozen objective/constraint set, choosing the minimum feasible action has zero hindsight location regret.

## P12-T2 — coarsened-certificate impossibility

If two cases share the visible signal but have different unique optimal actions, every deterministic signal-only allocator incurs positive regret on at least one; under equal prior, expected regret is bounded below by half the smaller cross-action loss gap.

## P12-T3 — value of certificate acquisition

Purchasing a refined certificate is rational when its expected reduction in optimal downstream loss exceeds its acquisition cost under the supplied price vector. Without a price vector, the result is a Pareto comparison.

## P12-T4 — robust selection

For uncertainty set `Θ` over prices/costs, an action is robustly optimal only if it minimizes the objective for every `θ∈Θ`; otherwise the correct result is a set of admissible actions or further information acquisition.

## P12-T5 — online regret

Under standard bounded-loss and feedback assumptions, donor online-learning algorithms provide regret bounds. ORION’s residual is the typed action/state/responsibility interface and fail-closed certificate semantics, not generic bandit or metareasoning mathematics.

## P12 falsifiers

- exact hindsight certificates are treated as forward-time observables;
- action menus differ across policies;
- heterogeneous resources are scalarized post hoc;
- robustness failures are retuned away.

## Frozen predictions

- coarsened signals fail on constructed witness pairs;
- certificate acquisition is selected near phase boundaries;
- unchanged allocators transfer only where the visible state remains decision-sufficient.

# P13 — Responsibility lattice and safe scientific reuse

## P13.1 Formal object

Responsibilities induce partitions `Π_r` and an information order

\[
r_1\preceq r_2 \iff \Pi_{r_2}\preceq\Pi_{r_1},
\]

meaning `r_2` requires at least the distinctions of `r_1`. The family need not be a total order.

## P13-T1 — joint sufficient state

The coarsest exact state for responsibility family `R` is the common refinement `∨_{r∈R}Π_r`.

## P13-T2 — safe reuse

Stored state `Z` is reusable for new responsibility `r'` iff `Π_Z` refines `Π_{r'}` and the regime/authority transport is valid. Provenance freshness alone is neither necessary nor sufficient.

## P13-T3 — drift transport

Redundant drift preserves a certificate when all target-relevant distinctions and support remain. Conflicting drift invalidates at least one required distinction or premise and forces revalidation/recovery.

## P13-T4 — conditional reissue optimality

Always transport is unsound on conflicting drift; always reissue is unnecessarily costly on redundant drift. A sound discriminator between the strata dominates both when its own cost is charged and its classification is correct.

## P13-T5 — revocation locality

Revoking a responsibility-support premise removes exactly support families depending on it; independent families survive.

## P13 falsifiers

- exact support sets are assumed available in deployment;
- responsibility is confused with confidence or provenance;
- incomparable responsibilities are forced into one ladder;
- safety is scored from the controller’s own support bit.

## Frozen predictions

- current provenance can coexist with unsupported reuse after responsibility change;
- responsibility-aware recovery preserves correctness at lower raw-read cost;
- redundant and conflicting drift require different actions.

# P14 — Causal governance and identifiable evaluation

## P14.1 Formal object

A governance policy maps a scientific packet/history to promote, retain, reopen, block, or `CANNOT_CHECK`. The causal estimand compares policies under matched subject capability and information, measuring false transitions, valid-transition retention, useful discovery, cost, and later correction burden.

## P14-T1 — gate attainability

A threshold gate is empirically testable only when both pass and fail regions intersect the protocol’s admissible support. If one terminal is unreachable, the experiment cannot test comparative superiority at that gate.

## P14-T2 — policy/gold separation

A policy evaluation is implementation-independent only when protected gold and rationales are withheld from policy inputs and the evaluated policy is not the same function used to generate gold.

## P14-T3 — blanket-abstention guard

A governance policy cannot establish useful superiority solely by reducing false promotions; it must satisfy a predeclared valid-promotion/useful-discovery noninferiority condition and report abstention/review cost.

## P14-T4 — negative-history value

Under a recurrence model where past failure classes predict future risk and history-aware policy uses no target leakage, negative-history retention has positive expected value exactly when its avoided expected loss exceeds storage, review, and false-blocking cost.

## P14-T5 — longitudinal reopening

When evidence, evaluator, responsibility, or regime changes, a previously valid status survives only through the corresponding P6/P7/P13 transport certificate. Governance that ignores the change creates stale standing.

## P14 falsifiers

- thresholds are outside reachable statistic range;
- full policy reads the gold decision function;
- all policies satisfy the same gate;
- governance wins by refusing everything;
- internally authored cases are called external validity.

## Frozen predictions

- partial governance contracts fail on the component they omit;
- negative history helps only in recurrent failure regimes and can hurt under distribution shift;
- independent blinded packets separate conformance from real scientific value.

# P15 — Layered scientific execution integrity

## P15.1 Formal object

Scientific processing is a product of layers:

```text
execution occurrence,
attribution/content/environment/chronology,
replay,
cross-implementation agreement,
attestation and custody,
scientific validity,
scientific authority.
```

Each layer has an explicit bridge to any higher layer; no silent implication exists.

## P15-T1 — execution/science noninterference

Any transformation acting only on execution-integrity coordinates preserves scientific-validity and authority coordinates. A change in those coordinates requires an explicit registered bridge.

## P15-T2 — receipt indistinguishability

There exist valid and invalid scientific payloads with identical complete execution receipts. Therefore receipt completeness cannot identify scientific validity without target scientific premises.

## P15-T3 — attestation boundary

A valid signature proves the signed statement under key-possession assumptions. Under full key compromise, forged false facts can carry valid signatures; signature validity therefore does not imply key custody or fact truth.

## P15-T4 — threshold trust

A threshold attestation system resists at most the compromise pattern assumed by its threshold/custody model. Scientific authority additionally requires independent scientific premises; more signers do not substitute for them.

## P15-T5 — publication linearizability

A publication is occurrence-linearizable when execution, environment, output, validation, authority, and publication records bind one content identity and one ordered occurrence, and finalization occurs after all required execution and cleanup facts. Cross-occurrence splicing, stale replay, or pre-reap finalization violates linearizability.

## P15-T6 — provenance representation invariance

Transport through W3C PROV, RO-Crate, or another donor representation preserves P15 decisions exactly when the normalized execution facts and identities are losslessly carried. Scientific fields remain outside donor provenance unless explicitly represented and authorized.

## P15 falsifiers

- agreement is called correctness;
- a signature is called truth;
- occurrence identity is omitted;
- cleanup/finalization chronology can be laundered;
- production superiority is inferred from a small hostile corpus.

## Frozen predictions

- rich receipts outperform plain logs on execution faults but still false-promote science if used as authority;
- full key compromise defeats signature-layer detection;
- a separate scientific-admission layer remains invariant across provenance encodings.

# Cross-paper closure

The paper theories form four coupled strata:

1. **Status transitions:** P1–P8.
2. **Capability and computational placement:** P9–P13.
3. **Governance:** P14.
4. **Occurrence integrity:** P15.

OSTC-T23 states that scientific advance requires both availability and admissibility. No stratum subsumes another. This is the fixed theoretical architecture that later execution agents must test.
