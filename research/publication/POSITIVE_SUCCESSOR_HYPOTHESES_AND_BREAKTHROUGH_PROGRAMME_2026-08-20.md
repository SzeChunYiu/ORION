# ORION positive successor hypotheses and breakthrough programme

**Date:** 2026-08-20  
**Branch:** `codex/papers-1-8-academic-refine`  
**Papers:** P1, P2, P3, P4, P6, P7, P8  
**Method:** evidence-first writing, mutually blind hostile review, formal claim/evidence binding, and transparent post-result hypothesis generation

## Purpose

This third pass does more than reframe adverse outcomes. It reconstructs each failed, null, saturated, tied, or blocked hypothesis into the strongest **positive successor hypothesis** that the evidence motivates, while keeping the original hypothesis and outcome immutable.

The integrity rule is strict:

1. an original confirmatory hypothesis is never retroactively rewritten as if it predicted the observed result;
2. a positive hypothesis learned from observed results is labelled `RETROSPECTIVE_SUCCESSOR` until it receives an independent prospective test;
3. a theorem or finite-model law already entailed by a frozen protocol may be labelled `ESTABLISHED_BOUNDED` at its registered scope;
4. a cross-paper generalization is labelled `PROGRAMME_CONJECTURE` until it survives a separately frozen cross-domain study;
5. no positive hypothesis may cite a failed or invalid campaign as performance evidence for a different endpoint.

This is transparent hypothesis reconstruction rather than secret HARKing. It preserves discovery while protecting confirmatory authority.

## Expert panel

The pass uses five independent roles before synthesis.

- **Discovery theorist and philosopher of science:** identifies whether an adverse result supports a new mechanism, impossibility, invariance, or unifying law rather than a weaker performance story.
- **Registered-report and statistical-methods reviewer:** separates planned inference, retrospective explanation, and future confirmation; defines the decisive independent test.
- **Formal-methods and type-theory reviewer:** checks necessity, sufficiency, monotonicity, compositionality, countermodels, and universal-quantifier leakage.
- **Autonomous-science systems reviewer:** asks whether the law matters for real scientific workflows, instruments, evidence synthesis, and claim release.
- **Hostile breakthrough reviewer:** applies the hardest standard: does the work create a sudden and significant increase in knowledge, survive the strongest donor mechanisms, and expose a plausible route to broad benefit rather than merely improving prose?

## Breakthrough standard

The Nobel Foundation's official language emphasizes discoveries or improvements that confer the greatest benefit to humankind. Its historical discussion of scientific prizes describes a discovery as a sudden and significant increase in new knowledge rather than a steady accumulation. ORION cannot create that status by declaration. It can, however, organize its research programme around the properties that a genuine field-level breakthrough would need:

1. **Conceptual compression:** one new principle explains several previously separate scientific failure modes.
2. **Decisive falsifiability:** the principle states exact conditions under which it fails, ties, or is absorbed by a stronger donor.
3. **Causal intervention:** adding the proposed relation changes scientific-status decisions while preserving clean valid decisions.
4. **Cross-domain invariance:** the relation survives independent implementations, domains, institutions, and naturalistic data.
5. **Human benefit:** the relation prevents invalid scientific conclusions or enables safe autonomous discovery in consequential domains.

The current papers establish strong bounded instances of items 1–3. Items 4–5 define the next programme rather than a present claim.

## ORION Scientific Discharge Principle

### Programme conjecture OSDP.1

> **A locally valid scientific result may change the status of a different scientific object only through an explicit target-bound discharge relation. Without that relation, local validity may be preserved or narrowed, but it cannot silently widen into authority over a different obligation.**

Let a local judgment be

`J = (subject, local_type, local_verdict, content, scope, epoch, evidence, authority)`

and a target scientific obligation be

`O = (target, target_type, required_content, required_scope, required_epoch, support_requirements, blocker_state)`.

A discharge relation `D(J,O,B)` is admissible only when:

1. the local verdict is valid for its native subject;
2. subject/content/scope/epoch match the target or an explicit protected bridge `B` establishes the transformation;
3. authority is preserved or narrowed across the chain unless `B` supplies the additional target authority;
4. at least one complete support family survives;
5. every load-bearing blocker is refuted, while unresolved blockers remain `CANNOT_CHECK`;
6. any affected scientific continuity coordinate has been revalidated;
7. the resulting status transition is recorded against the exact target obligation.

The conjecture predicts a common failure pattern: a system can be locally correct yet globally unauthorized because it lacks `D`. The seven papers instantiate different target objects:

- P1: formulation/revision status;
- P2: task-global closure;
- P3: scientific identity/integration;
- P4: claim promotion and benchmark-competence inference;
- P6: preservation of scientific standing after change;
- P7: inheritance and composition of task closure;
- P8: cross-domain scientific authority.

### Existing bounded support

The current programme does **not** prove OSDP.1 universally. It provides convergent bounded evidence:

- large protected empirical separations in P1, P2, P3, and P4;
- exact repair, composition, and portability laws in P6, P7, and P8;
- matched countermodels showing what happens when the discharge relation, bridge, or complete affected contract is absent;
- ideal-product ties showing that the principle concerns semantics rather than ORION centralization.

## Paper-by-paper successor hypotheses

### P1 — protected scientific reformulation

**Original adverse hypothesis:** the historical broad H1 claimed external superiority from an underpowered 48-case study. It remains `NOT_SUPPORTED`.

**P1.H+1 — protected necessity dual-benefit hypothesis**  
**Status:** `ESTABLISHED_BOUNDED` on the powered mechanical and P1-X exact-contract families.

> When the formulation is genuinely responsible for failure, target-bound protected necessity evidence authorizes high-level scientific reformulation and increases recovery; when lower-level repair is sufficient, the same contract suppresses unnecessary high-level change.

**Decision proof:** powered v2.2.4 reaches hidden-shift success 1.000 in primary and disjoint replication versus 0.494/0.483 for the strongest frozen parents, while recording zero unnecessary high-level reframes, protected-sibling regressions, and forbidden mutations. P1-X reaches 400/400 exact revision decisions versus 275/400 for the donor-complete greedy product with zero false high-level reframes and protected-invariant violations.

**Positive use of the failed H1:** the historical failure is the falsifier for the unsafe rule `failure -> authority to reformulate`. It identifies the need for the protected necessity relation that the successor then tests prospectively.

**P1.H+2 — naturalistic responsibility-transfer hypothesis**  
**Status:** `PROSPECTIVE_CONFIRMATORY`.

> In noisy naturalistic research-agent trajectories, explicit responsibility-to-authority coupling will reduce false high-level reframes without reducing recovery from genuine formulation error relative to donor-complete diagnosis/repair systems.

**Decisive test:** freeze naturalistic cross-domain trajectories before outcome access; use independent responsibility annotation, hidden protected invariants, a donor-complete comparator, and separate false-reframe and genuine-recovery endpoints.

### P2 — material-route scientific closure

**Original adverse hypothesis:** broad external Wide/Deep retrieval superiority remains `CANNOT_CHECK` because the Wide transport gate failed and the Deep route was an inadequate keyless probe.

**P2.H+1 — material-route discharge hypothesis**  
**Status:** `ESTABLISHED_BOUNDED` on P2-X.

> Task-global scientific closure requires discharge of every material acquisition and question-conditioned processing obligation; route-local success cannot authorize task stop while a material route remains unavailable, censored, provider-invalid, or scientifically unprocessed.

**Decision proof:** P2-X reaches 400/400 exact closure decisions versus 250/400 for the donor-complete available-route product, a +0.375 effect with 95% CI [0.3275,0.4225], zero false task closures, perfect clean closure, and positive effects in all five acquisition families.

**P2.H+2 — stage-separable evidence-loss hypothesis**  
**Status:** `RETROSPECTIVE_SUCCESSOR`.

> Acquisition pipelines contain scientifically distinct loss-bearing stages: retrieving relevant evidence does not imply that screening, question-conditioned processing, or final inclusion has discharged its obligation.

**Current support:** the MetaSyn probe records retrieval recall 0.7485, inclusion recall 0.5651, and measured post-retrieval loss 0.1834 across all 86 released reviews.

**Decisive test:** prospectively randomize stage-specific repairs and measure whether retrieval, screening, processing, and closure errors respond selectively rather than moving as one scalar competence.

**P2.H+3 — transport-authority hypothesis**  
**Status:** `RETROSPECTIVE_SUCCESSOR`.

> Benchmark transport is itself a scientific obligation: valid candidate execution cannot authorize either superiority or a valid null when provider identity and transport validity fail.

**Current support:** the Wide campaign passes candidate hashes, caps, common-capture and request-count checks but fails logical provider validity, yielding `CANNOT_CHECK` rather than a fabricated conclusion.

**Decisive test:** preregister multiple independent providers and deliberate transport corruptions, then test whether the governance layer rejects invalid comparisons while retaining power on valid transports.

## Breakthrough synthesis

The strongest present programme statement is not that every original hypothesis succeeded. It is:

> **Seven apparently different scientific-agent problems are bounded instances of one status-transition problem: when may a locally valid result discharge a different scientific obligation? ORION supplies target-bound discharge relations, exact repair laws, protected bridges, blocker semantics, and portability results that prevent local success from becoming unauthorized global scientific status.**

This is a stronger and more durable contribution than paper-specific performance rhetoric because it survives donor engulfment. Better retrieval, better verification, better certificates, better navigation, and better authorization all remain useful. ORION asks the relation that allows their valid outputs to change a different scientific object.

## ORION Grand Challenge: independent test of OSDP.1

A field-level breakthrough claim requires a new outcome-blind campaign rather than further rewriting of existing papers.

### Domains

Use independent teams and native artifacts from at least five consequential domains:

1. biomedical evidence synthesis and clinical-claim release;
2. experimental physics or autonomous-instrument operation;
3. materials/chemistry closed-loop experimentation;
4. computational discovery, theorem proving, or machine-learning experimentation;
5. environmental, public-health, or policy evidence synthesis.

### Arms

- strongest domain-native donor system;
- donor-complete product without explicit target-bound scientific discharge;
- ORION discharge layer over the same donor mechanisms;
- information-equivalent ideal product carrying the complete discharge relation.

### Primary endpoint

False scientific-status transition rate over cases where the local mechanism succeeds but the target obligation is not discharged.

### Co-primary clean endpoint

Correct status-transition rate when every target obligation is discharged. A breakthrough result must improve safety without winning by blanket refusal.

### Secondary endpoints

- exact target/subject/content/epoch attribution;
- selective reopening or revalidation cost;
- independent replay and claim-evidence audit time;
- robustness to provider failure, evaluator corruption, correlated evidence, and representation change;
- transfer across institutions and implementations;
- downstream human correction burden and scientific decision quality.

### Prospective programme hypothesis

**OSDP.G1 — cross-domain causal hypothesis**  
**Status:** `PROGRAMME_CONJECTURE`.

> Across heterogeneous real scientific workflows, adding an explicit target-bound discharge relation to donor-complete systems will reduce false scientific-status transitions while preserving non-inferior clean valid transitions, and an information-equivalent implementation will reproduce the effect.

### Falsifiers

OSDP.G1 must be weakened, absorbed, or rejected if any of the following occurs:

- a donor-complete system without an explicit or equivalent discharge relation matches ORION across hostile and clean cases;
- a proposed discharge coordinate is inert under intervention;
- ORION reduces false transitions only by suppressing clean valid transitions;
- the law fails systematically outside exact synthetic contracts;
- an existing formalism already entails the same target-bound status relation at equal or broader scope;
- independent teams cannot reproduce the decision rule or evidence bindings;
- the intervention does not improve any consequential scientific or human decision endpoint.

## Manuscript positioning rule

Each paper should use three explicit labels:

- **Original hypothesis:** immutable wording and verdict;
- **Positive successor hypothesis:** the strongest mechanism or law learned from the result, labelled retrospective or bounded-established;
- **Prospective confirmation:** the independent test that would authorize wider generalization.

This structure turns adverse results into a credible discovery programme without pretending they were predicted. It also gives editors a cleaner decision proof: the manuscript shows what was expected, what happened, what law was learned, what is already established, and what remains to be tested.

## Final claim ceiling

The current ORION programme can strongly claim protected empirical effects, exact finite repair/composition laws, benchmark non-identifiability, transport-authority failure, and implementation portability at their registered scopes. It cannot yet claim a universal law of science, deployed cross-domain superiority, or broad benefit to humankind.

The route to a genuinely breakthrough-level contribution is therefore concrete rather than rhetorical: freeze OSDP.G1, execute the cross-domain causal campaign with independent institutions and native scientific artifacts, demonstrate a large reduction in unauthorized scientific-status transitions without clean-utility loss, and show that the result materially improves consequential scientific decisions.
