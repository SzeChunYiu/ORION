# P1-U R6 with role-free identifiers — result

Protocol: `FREEZE_2026-08-21_ROLE_FREE_IDENTIFIERS_V4.md`. V1–V3 stand beside it
with the three precondition aborts they produced; none of them scored an arm.
Artifacts: `ROLE_FREE_RERUN_V{1,2,3,4}.json`.

## Outcome

**All fifteen checks pass. Terminal:
`P1_R6_NATIVE_PRIMARY_PASS_PENDING_2019_REPLICATION`,** up from
`P1_R6_NATIVE_PRIMARY_NOT_SUPPORTED` in `claude_t3/REPAIRED_GUARD_RUN_V1.json`.

The only check that changed is `no_candidate_metadata_leakage`, which went from
failing on 96 of 96 episode-arms to passing. The audit itself is unchanged —
same function, same forbidden-token set, built from the **real** episode ids. It
passes because the tokens are no longer in the payload, not because it was asked
an easier question.

This corroborates the bound `claude_t3` measured directly (anonymising the
identifier changes 0 of 44 ARD choices) and extends it to every scored
statistic rather than to choices alone.

**It is also the weakest part of the claim, and that must not be glossed.** The
host is a frozen deterministic provider. It has no capacity to exploit a leaked
label, so "removing the leak changed nothing" is close to true by construction
here. What this run establishes is not that the leak was harmless in general —
it is that the identifier boundary is now clean, so a later run on a semantic
host starts from a boundary that does not hand it the answer. On such a host the
identical-scores finding would be a real result; here it is a precondition for
one.

`R5-` appears **zero** times in the candidate-visible payload values. The 96
occurrences in the raw capture file are the sink's own keys, which are
evaluator-side bookkeeping and are what let the audit know which episode a
payload belongs to.

The English words `adverse` and `control` do occur in dossier text — the source
notes describe things like "an adverse-versus-corrected artifact pair". Measured

| role | n | contains "adverse" | contains "control" |
|---|---|---|---|
| adverse | 44 | 1 | 1 |
| control | 44 | 1 | 4 |

In-sample informedness: `adverse` 0.000, `control` 0.072, on 5 payloads of 88.
This is incidental vocabulary in the scientific content the candidate is
supposed to read, not an encoded label, and the freeze said in advance that a
dossier-level leak would be a different defect this repair does not touch. It is
recorded here rather than repaired, because repairing corpus content during an
identifier repair would be a change to the arena in the middle of a run.

## Four freeze versions, and why each was allowed

Each precondition defect was found by running the precondition, and **no arm was
scored under any of the first three**:

1. **V1** fitted and scored the role classifier on the same 48 rows. With near
   unique handle prefixes a majority vote memorises its training labels, so it
   reported informedness 1.0 for a cryptographic hash. Any injective cue scores
   1.0 under that instrument.
2. **V2** held the classifier out but split by sorted episode id, alternating.
   Episode ids are `R5-<QUERY>-A` and `-C`, so pair members sort adjacent and
   the split came out nearly anti-correlated with the role: fit carried
   `{adverse, unresolved}`, score carried `{control, unresolved}`. V2's own
   stratification guard caught it.
3. **V3** stratified by role and reported 0.4196 at a 1-character prefix and
   0.0 at every longer one. A longer prefix strictly refines a shorter one, so a
   real signal cannot vanish under refinement; this is the noise floor of a
   sixteen-bucket majority vote fitted on ~24 episodes. V3's ceiling of exactly
   0.0 was unattainable there for any handle scheme, which is the
   `handicapped-baseline-unattainable-margin` shape.
4. **V4** replaced the fixed ceiling with a 1000-permutation null at the 99th
   percentile, measuring the noise floor instead of assuming it. All twelve
   prefix lengths pass.

Three wiring defects were also found by measurement rather than by reading:
patching the DR1 repaired-root context manager reached nothing (0 handles minted,
96 failures unchanged); patching `native_orion`'s re-export reached nothing,
because `run_native_ard` resolves the global in `native_orion_core_v1`; and
patching the root alone left the probe path leaking (48 handles minted, failures
halved from 96 to 48). Substituting the episode's own `id` — the single upstream
source both paths read — covers every surface.

## What this licenses, and what it does not

**Licenses**, and only this: on the frozen deterministic host, the R6 primary and
its attribution ablation survive removal of the pair-role identifier leak, with
every scored statistic unchanged, under an audit whose token set was not
weakened.

**Does not license**:

- any superiority claim on a semantic model;
- discharge of `P1-U-T1`, whose terminal requires prospective protected
  evidence under **external** evaluator custody; this evaluator is
  self-authored and its custody is internal;
- discharge of `P1-U-T2`'s 2019 replication, separately blocked by the
  evaluator hardcoding `source_year == 2020`;
- any claim that a leaked pair role is harmless in general. The measurement
  behind that phrase is a property of a provider that cannot read.
