\section{Failure as knowledge}
ORION stores failed, blocked, partial and cannot-check episodes as immutable experience rather than treating them as disposable execution noise.  A failure description separates the observed mode, propagated effect, competing cause hypotheses, detection method, recovery actions and falsifier.  Recurrence is evidence that a pattern persists; it is not by itself evidence of cause.

Repeated failures across explicit task variations may create a lesson/guard candidate.  Reuse authority remains gated by replay, fresh transfer and protected verification bound to the exact candidate content and support episodes.  Negative history remains addressable after later success.

The Shadow development process applies the same principle to ORION development.  For example, a CI test incorrectly predicted that every mechanic would reopen its dependency coordinate.  The observed graph showed only the root dependency was missing.  The forecast error was preserved as a development failure, and the repair changed dependency modeling rather than distorting the graph to satisfy the test.
