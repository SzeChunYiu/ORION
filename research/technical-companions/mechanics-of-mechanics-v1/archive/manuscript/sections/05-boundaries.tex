\section{Claim boundary}
The Shadow mechanics substrate is an implementation/formalization result.  It demonstrates that ORION can represent a recursive mechanic graph, generate typed open questions without an LLM planner, attach modular specification contracts, schedule cross-mechanic research, and preserve failure-learning evidence under protected promotion rules.

It does not establish that every candidate mathematical family is the correct one, that the parent-domain registry is complete, that search routes achieve high live recall, that step-specific metrics measure their intended constructs, or that the resulting mechanics improve real scientific problem solving.  Those are empirical coordinates to be tested in known-answer, hostile, live-provider and fresh-transfer benchmarks.

Likewise, structural readiness for benchmarking does not grant Self-ORION development authority.  Self-development remains in Shadow mode until ORION demonstrates fresh failure localization, open-world knowledge acquisition, repair construction, matched evaluation and protected assurance without rewriting its evaluator.
