## S1 — extension dichotomy

For nonzero `x in G`, appending x to S creates a new short zero-sum iff some subsequence `T|S` satisfies

`1 <= |T| <= 4`, `sigma(T)=-x`.

Hence exactly one of:

1. `EXTENDABLE`: some nonzero x has `-x notin Sigma_{<=4}(S)`, so `S*x` is still short-free of length 32;
2. `SATURATED`: every nonzero x satisfies `-x in Sigma_{<=4}(S)`.

Because `eta(C_5^3)=33` and Property C is donor-known, every length-32 short-free extension is `U^4` for an 8-point support U.

## S2 — saturation-defect lemma

Assume S is SATURATED and let `x in supp(S)` have multiplicity `m=m_x(S)<4`.

Saturation provides `T|S` with `|T|<=4` and `sigma(T)=-x`.  If T used at most `m-1` copies of x, then the same T together with one of the remaining copies of x already present in S would be a zero-sum subsequence of S of length at most 5, contradiction.

Therefore **T must use all m copies of x**.

Write

`T = x^m R`, with `x notin supp(R)` and `|R|<=4-m`.

Then

`x^{m+1} R`

is a zero-sum sequence of length at most 5 that appears immediately after adding one more x.  Equivalently,

`sigma(R)=-(m+1)x`.

This is the local defect certificate forced at every support point whose multiplicity is below 4.

### Multiplicity 3 is impossible

If `m=3`, then `|R|<=1` and `sigma(R)=-(4)x=x` in `C_5^3`.

- `R` cannot be empty because x is nonzero;
- if `R=y`, then y=x, contradicting `x notin supp(R)` / the fact that T already used all three available copies of x.

Therefore:

> **SATURATION-DEFECT LEMMA.** A saturated short-free sequence over `C_5^3` has no support element of multiplicity 3.

### Remaining local certificates

For later theorem synthesis:

- `m=2`: `R` has size at most 2 and `sigma(R)=2x`; adding x creates `x^3R`;
- `m=1`: `R` has size at most 3 and `sigma(R)=3x`; adding x creates `x^2R`;
- `m=4`: saturation is automatic locally, since four existing copies already sum to `-x` and adding the fifth gives `x^5`.

The low-multiplicity points are therefore not arbitrary: each is algebraically generated by a subset of at most two/three other source terms.
