## ORION saturation principle: learn, absorb, then test the residual

ORION does not treat nearest work as a citation perimeter. The purpose of
saturation is to make the papers and framework stronger by incorporating the
best structures already discovered. For every serious nearest-work donor this
packet therefore requires four receipts:

1. **Structure extraction:** state the donor's strongest reusable mechanism,
   evaluation discipline, failure boundary or representation—not merely its
   topic or conclusion.
2. **Assimilation:** identify the exact ORION code, formal object, protocol,
   benchmark, claim boundary or paper owner that adopts the structure. A
   citation without an adoption decision is incomplete.
3. **Strength check:** record how the assimilation makes ORION more capable,
   more reproducible, more falsifiable or more honest. If adoption is deferred,
   name the incompatible assumption and a concrete reopen trigger.
4. **Residual test:** assess a standalone contribution only after assimilation.
   The residual must be an object that the strengthened donor-aware system
   still lacks; vocabulary changes and paper-count targets do not qualify.

This is **saturation before residual**, not novelty before reading. `ABSORBED`,
`ALREADY_PRESENT`, and `DEFERRED_WITH_TRIGGER` are valid donor dispositions.
`CITED_ONLY` is not. Saturation remains bounded and dated: it can support a
peer-review-ready search record and hostile already-solved test, never a claim
that no relevant knowledge exists outside the search.

No fixed paper count is an admissible reason to select a formulation.

## Challenge to the saturation basis

- Branch-name searches can miss equivalent work on unrelated names; tree-level
  comparisons and `git branch --contains` are therefore required, not just
  commit-message matching.
- Squash merges make commit-ahead counts overstate missing content; decisions
  use tree diffs and content checks.
- A green deterministic test can preserve a false claim if it only snapshots a
  hard-coded metric. Every headline must be traced to raw per-task outcomes or
  labelled unmeasured.
- Synthetic exact-ground-truth tasks can demonstrate a mechanism but cannot by
  themselves support general superiority or real-task transfer.
- A larger Lean corpus can make a p-value look more decisive while preserving
  donor dependence or repository leakage; source-level resampling and
  repository-stratified nulls are required.
- Content hashes prove identity, not Lean acceptance, theorem truth, novelty or
  scientific authority.

- clean `git merge-tree` result and post-merge equality checks for normative P8
  files;
- P6–P8 peer-review-ready checker, claim-authority checker and citation audit;
- PR #387 manifest verification and deterministic result replays;

- mutation of archived P9/P10 result inputs must fail regeneration checks;
- content/source/revision substitution must invalidate P10 receipts;
- native verifier rejection must never be converted into `PASS`;
- paper gates must fail if novelty, availability, checksum, generated-artifact
  or independent-replay evidence is absent.
- donor ledgers must fail closure if a nearest work is only cited: every donor
  requires structure extraction plus `ABSORBED`, `ALREADY_PRESENT`, or
  `DEFERRED_WITH_TRIGGER` evidence before the residual decision.

## Reopen triggers

Reopen the corresponding fiber if any of the following occurs:

- a relevant remote ref moves after the recorded precondition check;
- merge or cherry-pick changes the expected candidate tree;
- current-main P8 formal semantics and submission prose are not equivalent;
- a metric cannot be re-derived from archived task-level records;
- a strong baseline removes the claimed P9/P10 distinction;
- corpus expansion changes the sign or interpretation of the P10 null;
- a closest full-text donor absorbs the proposed residual;
- native Lean replay disagrees with a stored verifier receipt;
- a generated figure/table or claim-ledger sentence drifts;
- final PDF audit finds an unsupported claim, citation error or layout defect.
