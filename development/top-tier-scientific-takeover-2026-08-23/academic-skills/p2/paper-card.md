## 02 One-Sentence Summary

[Paper: PDF p. 1, Abstract] The paper defines the attainable envelope of acquisition and closure controllers, proves exact sufficiency and lower-bound results, and uses controlled and public-development studies to show why better downstream ranking cannot repair missing acquisition information.

## 03 Research Question

[Paper: PDF pp. 1-4] Under a common source-access and resource contract, when may a discovery controller close a scientific task, and what new information is necessary when observable states remain compatible with opposite closure truths?

## 06 Core Idea

1. **Surface method.** Compose acquisition mechanisms under a common contract and separate route state from task-level authority.
2. **Core insight.** Safe closure is the maximally permissive sound rule over compatible worlds, and strict ascent requires new state rather than cosmetic representation search.
3. **General lesson.** [Analysis] Search quality and closure authority should be reported on separate axes.

## 07 Method Overview

[Paper: PDF pp. 4-19] Inputs are tasks, routes, evidence records, typed obligations and budgets. Donor acquisition components update evidence and optionality; a non-compensatory authority layer determines stop, open or undetermined state. Outputs are acquisition trajectories and closure terminals.

## 11 Correct Interpretation of the Conclusions

[Paper: PDF pp. 1-2, Abstract] The theory identifies when acquisition state is sufficient for closure. Empirical studies provide controlled, one-pool and public-development evidence with different authority. They do not establish protected open-world superiority.
