# Retained failed strengthenings and successor research identities

Every negative result remains a negative result. The exact receipt checks finite
witness arithmetic only; it does not make any empirical or historical-novelty
claim positive.

## S1. A state label does not enforce action legality

**Failed strengthening.** The stationary frontier remains valid for
history-dependent availability if the current state is merely mentioned in
prose.

**Smallest witness.** Two equiprobable opposite target worlds; an initially
unavailable zero-cost perfect test. The stationary action set gives value 0,
but the legal initial state has no acquisition and value \(1/2\).

**Cause.** The old recurrence quantifies over the same \(E\) at every node.

**Correct repair.** Use \(E(s)\), \(c(s,e)\), joint
\(Q_{s,e}(o,s'\mid w)\), and continuation frontiers
\(\Gamma_{n-1,s'}\).

**Successor identity.** Controlled legality as a first-class scientific state.

**Next discriminator.** Exhaustively enumerate legal policy trees and require
frontier equality at every state/horizon.

## S2. A destructive test cannot be repeated merely because the horizon remains

**Failed strengthening.** If a test is defined once, every later policy node
may invoke it.

**Finite witness.** A binary test has outcome-1 probabilities \(1/10\) and
\(3/5\) in opposite worlds. One legal destructive use has Bayes error \(1/4\)
and transitions from `fresh` to `spent`. The legal value remains \(1/4\) at
horizon two. Illegally returning to `fresh` and repeating it gives \(7/40\).

**Cause.** Horizon is a resource bound, not an availability license.

**Successor identity.** Destructive, replenishing, and inventory-constrained
experimental design.

**Next discriminator.** Freeze the laboratory transition graph and compare the
controlled frontier to a repeatable-test ablation.

## S3. An observed stochastic next state is not an irrelevant bookkeeping field

**Failed strengthening.** Only the named outcome can carry information, so a
stochastic next state may be averaged away before the decision.

**Finite witness.** A cost-\(1/20\) action transitions to `good` with
probability \(3/4\) versus \(1/4\) in the two target worlds and otherwise to
`bad`. State-contingent terminal decisions give risk vector
\((3/10,3/10)\).

**Cause.** The observed next state is part of the transcript and posterior
conditioning event.

**Successor identity.** Informative laboratory-state transitions.

**Next discriminator.** Compare the joint \((o,s')\) kernel against a model
that censors \(s'\); require any claimed equivalence to preserve Bayes risk.

## S4. Deterministic Bayes attainment does not imply deterministic robust attainment is optimal

**Failed strengthening.** Because each singleton-prior Bellman problem has a
deterministic optimum, arbitrary-credal minimax also has a deterministic
optimum of the same value.

**Finite witness.** With no observations, two opposite point-mass priors and
zero--one loss, deterministic robust risk is 1; a fair world-independent
randomization has risk \(1/2\).

**Cause.** Supremum over priors makes the objective convex rather than linear
in the policy risk vector.

**Successor identity.** Auditable randomized control at unresolved frontiers.

**Next discriminator.** Report deterministic and randomized values separately
and specify whether a private seed and randomized terminal action are licensed.

## S5. Policy attainment does not imply an attained worst licensed prior

**Failed strengthening.** Finiteness of the policy tree guarantees a
least-favourable member of any credal set.

**Finite witness.** For
\(\Pi=\{(\theta,1-\theta):0<\theta<1\}\) and risk vector \((1,0)\), the robust
supremum is 1 but no licensed \(\theta\) attains it.

**Cause.** The policy risk set is compact; the licensed credal set need not be.

**Successor identity.** Provenance-preserving epsilon-worst priors.

**Next discriminator.** Either prove \(\Pi\) compact or report an approaching
sequence without naming its unlicensed limit as the worst prior.

## S6. Posterior-local minimax does not solve arbitrary fixed-prior ambiguity

**Failed strengthening.** One may condition each licensed prior, choose the
worst posterior independently at every history, and still claim the fixed
ex-ante \(\Pi\) value.

**Finite witness.** Two priors on \((Z,Y)\) give fixed ex-ante minimax error
\(1/10\). At both \(Z\)-histories, the posterior family contains both
deterministic labels; independent local random minimax gives \(1/2\).

**Cause.** Historywise posterior pasting adds path laws not generated by any
one licensed ex-ante prior.

**Successor identity.** Rectangularity and dynamic-provenance certificates.

**Next discriminator.** Test path-law stability under every positive-history
pasting. If it fails, use the root risk-vector scalarization or explicitly
license the rectangular hull.

## S7. A positive acquisition-cost obligation cannot survive a separating zero-cost closure

**Failed strengthening.** A KL-per-cost lower bound can be applied unchanged
when informative zero-cost actions are present.

**Finite witness.** Two complementary free tests reveal the two bits whose
parity is the target. Bayes error is \(1/2\) with zero or one acquisition and 0
after two acquisitions, while total charged acquisition cost stays 0.

**Cause.** Division by a zero resource charge is not a positive lower bound;
free outcomes also cannot be discarded when collapsing the preprocessing path.

**Successor identity.** Multi-resource acquisition and zero-cost reachability
frontiers.

**Next discriminator.** Charge a strictly positive resource, or condition the
paid problem on the full free-history state and prove the resulting reduction.

## S8. A bounded-horizon zero-cost theorem is not an infinite-horizon theorem

**Failed strengthening.** Because every \(\Gamma_{n,s}\) is finite and
attained, the union over all horizons automatically has an attained optimal
proper policy.

**Explicit witness.** In two opposite worlds, a repeatable zero-cost Bernoulli
test has success probabilities \(1/4\) and \(3/4\). Every finite transcript has
positive probability in both worlds, so every finite-horizon exact error is
positive. Majority/Bayes error nevertheless converges to 0 as the horizon
grows. Hence the infimum over finite horizons is 0 but no finite policy attains
exact error 0. A separate free cycle can also avoid terminal loss forever if
nontermination is not assigned a loss or excluded by properness.

**Cause.** Finite-horizon compactness does not provide compactness of the union
of all policy depths or exclude improper policies.

**Successor identity.** Proper infinite-horizon active identification with
zero-cost cycles.

**Next discriminator.** Supply discounting, contraction, positive stage cost,
or a proper-policy/transience condition and prove value existence separately.

## S9. A recorded laboratory state is not automatically sufficient

**Failed strengthening.** Any chosen finite state label makes the joint kernel
Markov and validates the recurrence.

**Counterexample mechanism.** If specimen degradation depends on an omitted
past exposure, two histories with the same recorded \(s\) can have different
legal actions or future kernels. One \(Q_{s,e}\) cannot represent both.

**Cause.** State sufficiency is an assumption to verify, not a consequence of
notation.

**Successor identity.** Minimal sufficient controlled scientific state.

**Next discriminator.** Search for two same-state histories with different
conditional next-branch laws, costs, or legality. Split the state whenever one
is found.
