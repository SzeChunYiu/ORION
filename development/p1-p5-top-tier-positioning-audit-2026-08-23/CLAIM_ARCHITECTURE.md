# P1--P5 claim architecture: unique ownership versus donor theory

## Governing architecture

The papers should share one inherited information lemma and differ in the
scientific object to which it is applied. The shared lemma is: a target is
recoverable from an interface exactly when it factors through that interface;
under a law, mixed fibres induce conditional Bayes risk. P1 states this at
`papers/orion-11-recursive-epistemic-reconstruction/manuscript/sections/02a-minimal-escalation-theory.tex:90-185`; P2 at
`papers/orion-12-open-world-scientific-discovery/manuscript/sections/acquisition_authority-envelope.tex:179-261`; P3 at
`papers/orion-13-global-knowledge-portrait/manuscript/sections/36-partial-identification-theory.tex:91-169`; P4 at
`papers/orion-14-verified-scientific-discovery/manuscript/sections/02a-verification-axis-identifiability.tex:11-126`; and P5 at
`papers/orion-15-self-orion/manuscript/sections/02a-minimal-method-revision-theory.tex:41-134`.

That repeated lemma is programme substrate, not five independent novelty claims.
The current umbrella synthesis already says so explicitly at
`development/top-tier-scientific-takeover-2026-08-23/P1_P5_UMBRELLA_SYNTHESIS.md:118-131`.

## Inheritance between papers

The scientific dependency should be stated rather than hidden:

1. **P2 supplies observations and unresolved acquisition obligations.**
2. **P3 turns those observations into source-compatible query sets.**
3. **P4 asks whether an evaluation terminal is identifiable and fairly
   attainable from the compared systems' interfaces.**
4. **P1 chooses a minimal admissible scientific transition from a declared
   transition order and gate set.**
5. **P5 actively acquires discriminators for method-revision collisions and
   leaves final promotion with the host.**

This division matches the current programme map at
`development/top-tier-scientific-takeover-2026-08-23/P1_P5_UMBRELLA_SYNTHESIS.md:6-41`, but the manuscripts need short cross-paper boundary paragraphs so that a
reviewer does not infer five renamings of the same fibre theorem.

## Active-identification donor packet

The risk-vector Bellman frontier is a useful common audit representation, not a
new P3 or P5 theorem claim. A same-repository adversarial review found no
counterexample to the finite stationary core but concluded
`PASS_FINITE_CORE / BLOCK_NOVEL_THEORY_CLAIM / REQUIRE_SCOPE_REPAIRS`
(`development/active-identification-theory-review-2026-08-23/ADVERSARIAL_REVIEW.md:1-20`).
Its nearest-work audit identifies finite POMDP alpha vectors, robust-POMDP
formulations, multiple-prior rectangularity, controlled sensing, and adaptive
submodularity as direct donors, leaving only a careful synthesis/audit interface
(`development/active-identification-theory-review-2026-08-23/ADVERSARIAL_REVIEW.md:63-77`).

The scope repairs have been applied: the recurrence is explicitly stationary;
behavioral randomization requires world-independent private randomness and
perfect recall; policy attainment is separated from prior attainment; positive
cost is explicit; and the risk-vector/KL components are donor-owned
(`development/active-identification-theory-review-2026-08-23/SCOPE_REPAIR_DISPOSITION.md:7-30`).
The safe architecture is therefore:

- integrate the support-envelope/credal distinction and risk-vector frontier
  only as donor-derived common representation;
- do not advertise a new Bellman frontier or adaptive-KL theorem;
- preserve N1--N8 and A1--A3 as negative research identities; and
- pursue a state-indexed nonrectangular frontier, composite-target matching
  bound, credal-specific complexity result, or joint world/kernel coverage
  theorem only under a new identity and another literature/proof audit
  (`development/active-identification-theory-review-2026-08-23/ADVERSARIAL_REVIEW.md:79-106`).
