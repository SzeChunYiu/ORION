# Integration candidates for P3/P5 and umbrella theory

No manuscript has been edited by this lane. A same-repository adversarial
review found the finite stationary core sound, added three scope
counterexamples, and blocked a distinct-theorem novelty claim. These are
candidates only as a donor-derived synthesis and audit interface; they are not
new Bellman, alpha-vector, robust-POMDP or adaptive-KL theorems.

## Candidate A — active epistemic envelope

At history \(h\), pair the support envelope
\(\mathcal Y(h)=\{g(w):p(w)\lambda_h(w)>0\text{ for some }p\in\Pi\}\) with the
conditioned credal family \(\Pi_h\). The set answers exact identification; the
credal family answers licensed risk. This extends P3 without pretending that
support alone supplies probabilities.

## Candidate B — adaptive transcript-purity theorem

A finite stochastic discriminator programme identifies the target exactly iff
the complete transcript laws of distinct target classes are mutually singular.
For deterministic tests this reduces to P5's pure-leaf condition. Strictly
positive noise blocks finite exactness even when positive KL permits arbitrarily
small asymptotic error.

## Candidate C — risk-vector Bellman frontier

The finite recursion \(\Gamma_n\) exactly enumerates deterministic policy-tree
risk vectors. Bayes evaluation is a linear scalarization. Ex-ante credal
minimax is support-function minimization over \(\Gamma_n\) or its convex hull
when randomized policies are allowed. This is the strongest bridge
representation in the packet because it remains correct for arbitrary
nonrectangular licensed prior sets. It is a static-hidden-state specialization
of donor POMDP policy-vector theory plus finite minimax scalarization, not a
historically new frontier theorem.

## Candidate D — information-cost lower bound

Any uniformly \(\delta\)-correct adaptive policy must spend, in every oriented
cross-target pair, expected cost at least

\[
\operatorname{kl}(1-\delta\Vert\delta)
/\max_e[D_{\rm KL}(K_e^w\Vert K_e^{w'})/c_e].
\]

This converts an unresolved pair into a quantitative acquisition obligation
without promising that the bound is attainable.

## Candidate E — narrow safe claim for greedy information

Greedy target mutual information is exact for one compulsory equal-cost test
followed by Bayes log-loss prediction. It is not generally exact for zero--one
loss, multistep complementarity, hard budgets, nonrectangular credal minimax,
or world information containing nuisance coordinates.

2. Check whether the transcript-singularity equivalence has a more standard
   target-class experiment-comparison formulation.
3. Add exact citations for adaptive KL chain rules and sequential lower bounds.
4. Decide whether P3/P5 operational protocols license a singleton prior, a
   fixed credal set, or only support constraints.
5. Decide whether randomized terminal revisions are admissible. If not, retain
   the deterministic value and mark the convexified value as a theoretical
   comparator only.
6. Validate every real acquisition kernel independently; the finite theory
   does not certify empirical kernels.
7. Keep the displayed recurrence stationary. State-dependent availability,
   destructive tests and no-repeat constraints require \(\Gamma_{n,s}\) and a
   separately proved controlled-state theorem.
8. Do not name a least-favourable prior for nonclosed \(\Pi\), and do not apply
   the positive-cost lower bound when a zero-cost separating path exists.
