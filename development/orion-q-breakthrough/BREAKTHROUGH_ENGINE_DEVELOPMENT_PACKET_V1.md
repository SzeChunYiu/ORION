## 1. Atomic development questions

### A1 — breakthrough level

Can ORION distinguish exact finite progress from reusable structural progress without confusing a scheduling coordinate with truth/novelty authority?

Discriminator: B0–B5 classification changes research priority on at least one live frontier while a hostile control confirms it cannot promote proof/novelty state.

### A2 — compression gate

Can a research cycle be forced to state how new computation reduces the unknown rather than merely enlarges an atlas?

Discriminator: on a live exact census, the gate yields a strictly smaller sufficient/hypothesis-discriminating state or refuses a low-information computation.

### A3 — obstruction-to-invariant

Can failed theorem attempts be converted into replayable counterexamples, minimal obstructions, required state coordinates, or impossibility theorems instead of generic failure?

Discriminator: a failed attack produces a successor hypothesis whose grammar is smaller/more explanatory than blind search widening.

### A4 — two-sided theorem lab

Does isolating proof construction from adversarial counterexample construction expose a missing lemma that neither packet states when run alone?

Discriminator: synthesis identifies a premise/invariant with an independently checkable role. Agreement alone does not pass.

### A5 — symbolic lift

Does immediately parameterizing a B2/B3 finite result expose a reusable theorem, a donor-owned generalization, or a precise failure boundary before publication scope is frozen?

Discriminator: at least one live result changes its scientific ceiling/next experiment after lift.

### A6 — hard stratum

When a proof handles a strict subset of an exact census, can ORION freeze and study only the complement with the exact reason the proof fails?

Discriminator: solved-region recomputation is avoided and at least one new state coordinate is tested on the complement.

### A7 — portfolio

Does limiting live breakthrough work to exploit/structure/boundary roles improve research allocation without creating a pseudo-objective that replaces expert judgment?

Discriminator: a killed/absorbed lane is explicitly reallocated; scoring never grants claim authority.

## 5. Explicit challenge to the saturation basis

The selected vocabulary may simply rename ordinary mathematical research behavior. Therefore V1 passes only if it changes a prospective decision or produces a smaller theorem-bearing state compared with the incumbent cycle.

The B-level taxonomy may create prestige gaming. Therefore B-level is explicitly non-authoritative and should not appear in native mathematics manuscripts unless studying ORION itself.

Compression may discard a load-bearing coordinate. Therefore every compression requires hostile ablation/counterexample testing; finite empirical sufficiency is not all-instance semantic sufficiency.

Symbolic lifting may manufacture false generality. Therefore every lift has a donor-first prior-art check and a first-failure-boundary requirement.

A proof/counterexample split may only duplicate effort. Therefore it must expose a synthesis increment on a live calibration to survive.

## 6. Why prior searches could have missed useful knowledge

- exact-result work may terminate once the constant/classification is closed;
- publication work may optimize framing after rather than before theorem generalization;
- large obstruction atlases make low-dimensional invariants hard to see without explicit ablation;
- multiple research agents can converge on the same donor theorem, creating apparent independent support;
- a failed exchange grammar can invite larger brute force before the trapping invariant is named;
- fixed numerical proofs can hide parameter independence in bookkeeping constants.

These are hypotheses, not established causal explanations.
