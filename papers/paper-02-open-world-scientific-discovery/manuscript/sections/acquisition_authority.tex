\section{Acquisition is not closure}
\label{sec:acquisition-authority}

The first design move is to separate two problems that are often collapsed into
one policy.  An \emph{acquisition policy} decides what to search, retrieve,
inspect, expand or reread next.  A \emph{closure authority} decides whether the
evidence state is allowed to support route-local stopping or scientific task
completion.  The larger theory then reconnects these layers through a joint
frontier: better acquisition may improve discovery, utility and optionality,
but it improves scientifically sound closure only when it discharges an
obligation or exposes a state distinction that closure needs.  This makes strong
external retrieval and stopping mechanisms composable rather than adversarial
to the contribution.

Let $x_t$ denote the observable search state at step $t$.  We write an
acquisition mechanism as
\[
  a_t \sim A(x_t),
\]
where $a_t$ may be a lexical query, a newly generated source route, a
reasoning-aware query, a fielded Boolean filter, a graph-expansion action, a
selective fetch, a reranking operation, a state-preservation/recovery action, or
a request for another retrieval round.  No novelty is claimed here for generic
members of that action family.  Instead, acquired evidence updates both an
evidence state $E_t$ and a typed obligation state $O_t$.  Closure is a distinct map
\[
  C(E_t,O_t) \in \{\textsc{continue},\textsc{route-stop},
  \textsc{open},\textsc{cannot-check},\textsc{task-close}\}.
\]
A utility, relevance, confidence, sufficiency or coverage signal may influence
$A$ and may recommend a local stop, but it cannot by itself delete an unresolved
obligation in $O_t$.  This interface is where the residual contribution of this
paper sits.

\subsection{Absorbing strong acquisition mechanisms}

The acquisition layer is intentionally donor-composable.  SAGE shows that
keyword-oriented agent queries can make strong lexical retrieval difficult to
beat and that corpus-side metadata/keyword augmentation can further improve
retrieval \cite{sage2026}.  AgentIR shows that an agent's reasoning trace can be
used jointly with its query as a retrieval signal \cite{agentir2026}.  SIEVE
shows that fielded Boolean admission, structure-rich inspection and selective
section fetching can improve the accuracy--context tradeoff
\cite{sieve2026}.  Breadth-first bibliography expansion can recover literature
missed by API-only retrieval, while also demonstrating why citation-list recall
cannot be the only evaluation axis \cite{rethinkinglitsearch2026}.  Lexical,
metadata-augmented, reasoning-aware, field-aware and citation-expansion
retrieval are therefore treated as candidate strengths to compose, not as
inventions of this work.

The same rule applies to continuation and stopping signals.  DeepControl uses
marginal information utility to regulate continued retrieval
\cite{deepcontrol2026}; decision-theoretic policies optimize screening stops
against explicit costs and payoffs \cite{decisionstop2026}; HALT uses
claim--evidence coverage to verify whether expected evidence has been obtained
\cite{halt2026}; MiCP applies conformal prediction to adaptive multi-turn
reasoning to maintain a target coverage guarantee while permitting early stopping
\cite{micp2026}; and structured Search-R1 stopping judgments explicitly encode
sufficiency and remaining gaps \cite{multiragstop2026}.  These signals and
guarantees can all be useful inputs to acquisition or route-local stopping.  The
question asked here is higher-order rather than a competition over any one
signal: what joint discovery frontier does their composition attain, and is the
resulting state sufficient to certify closure when routes are dependent,
unavailable, censored or only locally exhausted?

\subsection{The authority residual}

This decomposition yields four interface requirements and one wider comparison
claim.  Route independence must be earned from backend, query-derivation and
capture identity rather than from tool names; route-local stopping and
task-global closure occupy different authority levels; unavailable or censored
routes remain typed obligations rather than empty results; and content identity
stays distinct from question-conditioned processing state.  Above those
requirements, the scientific object is the acquisition--authority envelope:
the attainable joint frontier over discovery, utility, optionality, closure
safety, valid closure yield and charged cost.  Section~\ref{sec:envelope}
derives when a donor-complete product can reach and authorize a point on that
frontier.

The resulting design is deliberately asymmetric.  It is permissive about
\emph{how} evidence is acquired: a stronger retriever, query generator,
expansion rule or verification signal can replace a weaker one.  It is
conservative about \emph{what acquisition outcomes are allowed to mean}: local
flatness, high estimated coverage, high confidence, apparent sufficiency or a
provider failure cannot silently become proof of global completeness.  This
asymmetry is what permits the paper to absorb progress in retrieval without
making its claimed authority boundary obsolete whenever a stronger retriever is
published.

\subsection{Empirical consequence}

The decomposition also fixes the widening experiment.  Candidate-generation
methods must be compared and composed under matched \emph{admissible exposure},
not merely equal request counts.  Only after the acquisition ceiling is high
enough to make processing and stopping observable should the state and closure
layers be challenged with direct ablations and strong donor products.  A
positive external result must therefore improve the guarded joint frontier; a
cheaper stop that hides reachable evidence or an attractive report assembled
from a truncated candidate pool is not a positive result.  Conversely, a
stronger donor retriever that improves acquisition while leaving the closure
constraints intact strengthens the composed system and raises the comparison
standard rather than weakening the theory.
