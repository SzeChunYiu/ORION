\begin{abstract}
Deep-research agents increasingly combine iterative retrieval, planning, citation, and evidence-aware termination. The ORION Paper II successor therefore does not claim novelty for multi-hop search or stopping rules. It asks a stricter question: under matched models, tools, information access, and total resources, can an agent discover more decision-relevant scientific evidence while making fewer false task-global closure decisions than strong deep-research and evidence-aware stopping systems? The protocol separates six obligations that prior adverse ORION probes showed can fail independently: route generation, retrieval, inclusion, processing, state representation, and task-global closure. Protected tasks contain hidden distributed evidence, contradictory and duplicated sources, provider-invalid routes, retrieved-but-unprocessed evidence, apparently saturated routes, and evidence whose future relevance is destroyed by over-aggressive state compression. Candidate systems must emit route-level and task-level receipts with an explicit unresolved state. State compilation, caching, and summarization are charged against the same total resource budget as retrieval and reasoning. Primary outcomes include decision-relevant recall, precision, false closure, unresolved calibration, final scientific utility, optionality loss, and time/cost to valid closure. Strong comparators receive modern deep-research retrieval, evidence-aware termination, coverage estimation, and state-management mechanics. A superiority claim is authorized only if scientific utility or decision-relevant recall improves prospectively while false closure satisfies a non-inferiority or superiority guard, and the effect replicates across domains, a systematic-review-like setting, a disjoint hidden split, and an independent route-accounting implementation. Existing negative and \textsc{CannotCheck} results remain intact and seed responsibility-specific successor experiments rather than post-hoc score repair.
\end{abstract}

\section{Residual after donor subtraction}
DeepResearch Bench evaluates research-report quality and retrieval/citation behavior on expert-authored tasks \cite{deepresearchbench2026}. Evidence-aware termination has also been proposed explicitly to reduce premature stopping in deep research \cite{dontstopearly2026}. SAGE shows that reasoning-intensive scientific retrieval remains difficult for current deep-research agents and that stronger-looking learned retrievers do not automatically dominate classical retrieval in this setting \cite{sage2026}. These are donor-owned capabilities and findings.

P2-U therefore targets the residual relation
\[
\text{search coverage} + \text{processing state} + \text{preserved optionality} \Longrightarrow \text{valid global closure authority}.
\]
A system can retrieve a relevant source and still fail to process it; it can process many sources while missing a material route; and it can answer the current question while compressing away evidence needed to detect that a future route remains open. No one stage is allowed to stand in for the others.

\section{Immutable adverse evidence}
The predecessor P2 external probes include non-positive outcomes and provider-invalid cases. They are retained as stage-localizing evidence, not rewritten as performance wins or losses. The successor preserves these distinctions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item evidence never retrieved;
    \item evidence retrieved but excluded incorrectly;
    \item evidence included but not processed into a decision-usable state;
    \item a material route never generated or revisited;
    \item provider/tool output invalid or unavailable;
    \item local route closure incorrectly promoted to task-global closure.
\end{enumerate}
A provider-invalid run is \textsc{CannotCheck}. It is never scored as a comparator failure.

\section{Task model}
Each protected task contains a hidden evidence universe partitioned into material routes $r\in R$. A route may contain supportive, contradictory, redundant, superseded, or non-decision-relevant evidence. Candidate systems do not receive $R$ or the gold materiality labels.

At time $t$, the system maintains:
\[
S_t=(Q_t,E_t,P_t,U_t,C_t),
\]
where $Q_t$ is the active query/route frontier, $E_t$ is acquired raw evidence, $P_t$ is processed evidence state, $U_t$ is unresolved/optionality state, and $C_t$ is the candidate closure status. The protected evaluator separately knows whether material evidence or routes remain open.

\section{Closure states}
The only task-level terminals are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textsc{Closed}: all frozen closure obligations are discharged;
    \item \textsc{Open}: at least one identified material obligation remains actionable;
    \item \textsc{Unresolved}: available evidence cannot establish closure or a specific remaining route under the budget;
    \item \textsc{CannotCheck}: required provider, evidence-custody, or evaluator authority is unavailable.
\end{itemize}
\textsc{Unresolved} is a calibrated epistemic state. \textsc{CannotCheck} is an authority/evidence-availability state. Neither is silently converted into \textsc{Closed}.

\section{State optionality as a charged resource}
P2-U compares four evidence-state policies under matched total cost:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item raw accumulated history;
    \item universal evidence index;
    \item query-conditioned compiled evidence state;
    \item compile+cache/materialize policy that may preserve selected raw evidence for future routes.
\end{enumerate}
Compilation is never free preprocessing. Its token, compute, storage, tool, and latency costs are charged to the same budget as retrieval and reasoning.

Future query/route changes are frozen before the current state is built. This permits a direct optionality test: a state can preserve current answer quality yet be harmful if it deletes the only coordinate needed to detect a later material route.

\section{Baselines}
Comparators receive equivalent model/tool access and total resource budgets. The mandatory set includes:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item fixed-budget search+RAG;
    \item iterative deep-research agent;
    \item evidence-aware termination system in the style of modern EDR \cite{dontstopearly2026};
    \item strong retrieval-focused deep-research system evaluated on scientific retrieval, informed by SAGE \cite{sage2026};
    \item coverage estimator / capture-recapture augmented system, treated as donor mechanics;
    \item donor-complete product with route tracking, evidence sufficiency, contradiction handling, and state compilation but without ORION task-global discharge semantics;
    \item an oracle route ceiling used only for analysis.
\end{enumerate}
Official unavailable systems are not fabricated. Mechanism-matched reimplementations are labelled as such.

\section{Primary hypotheses}
\paragraph{H1, discovery utility.}
At matched total resources, ORION improves decision-relevant evidence recall or protected final scientific utility over the strongest runnable donor-complete comparator by a prospectively frozen margin.

\paragraph{H2, closure safety.}
ORION is non-inferior or superior on false task-global closure, with the margin frozen before protected outcomes.

\paragraph{H3, non-vacuous clean coverage.}
Any reduction in false closure must not be purchased by excessive abstention on truly closed tasks.

\paragraph{H4, optionality preservation.}
State compilation reduces repeated work or cost without increasing false closure or destroying the frozen future-route evidence coordinate.

\paragraph{H5, transfer.}
The H1--H4 pattern replicates on disjoint hidden tasks, across domains, and under an independent route-accounting implementation.

\section{Outcomes and statistical plan}
Every task is an independent experimental unit unless a hierarchy is explicitly frozen. Repeated model samples within one task are technical repeats and are not counted as independent $n$.

Primary outcomes are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item decision-relevant evidence recall and precision;
    \item false closure rate;
    \item true clean-closure coverage;
    \item calibrated \textsc{Unresolved} decisions;
    \item protected final answer/scientific utility;
    \item route-level missed-obligation count;
    \item optionality loss under frozen future queries;
    \item tokens, retrieval calls, state-compilation cost, wall time, and time-to-valid-closure.
\end{enumerate}

Before protected access, freeze superiority/non-inferiority margins, family weights, confidence-interval method, multiplicity policy, missing/provider-invalid handling, and catastrophic false-closure rules. Report effect sizes and uncertainty, not significance language alone.

\section{Route and task closure receipts}
A route receipt records route identity, queries attempted, evidence identities, processing status, unresolved obligations, and stop reason. A task receipt records which route obligations support global closure and which remain open. A route may be locally saturated without authorizing task closure.

A source citation count, context length, number of search calls, or repeated duplicate retrieval never constitutes a closure proof.

\section{Hostile controls}
Mandatory attacks include:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item a high citation count generated entirely from redundant evidence;
    \item a decisive document retrieved early but omitted from processed state;
    \item a late contradictory source reachable only through a distinct route;
    \item state compression that preserves the current answer but deletes a future-route cue;
    \item an unavailable provider that would otherwise contain material evidence;
    \item a clean closed case to defeat deny-all/never-stop policies;
    \item evidence-label or route-name leakage into candidate-visible metadata;
    \item a deeper-search arm showing whether extra retrieval alone closes the apparent ORION advantage.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Negative-result research recursion}
Every false closure, missed material source, harmful state compilation, and tied/negative family is retained. Each is assigned one primary causal responsibility from
\[
\{\text{route generation},\text{retrieval},\text{inclusion},\text{processing},\text{state optionality},\text{stopping},\text{provider},\text{global authority},\text{benchmark}\}.
\]
The recovery programme freezes a one-factor discriminator before rerunning. For example, a retrieval failure may justify a new route generator; a processing failure does not. A state-optionality failure requires a compile/cache policy test, not a larger retrieval budget by default. A provider failure remains \textsc{CannotCheck} unless authority is restored.

ORION issue \#650 is the authority-bearing P2-U research programme. A child issue is warranted only for a newly observed causal stage/mechanic that requires its own frozen discriminator. Outcome deletion, margin relaxation, baseline weakening, or scoring provider failures as comparator losses are prohibited.

\section{Claim ladder}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Protocol frozen}: no performance claim.
    \item \textbf{Stage-identification supported}: failures can be causally localized under the benchmark.
    \item \textbf{Protected search/closure superiority supported}: H1 and H2 pass with H3 safety.
    \item \textbf{Replicated open-world superiority}: H1--H5 pass across domains and independent route accounting.
    \item \textbf{Bounded terminal}: final claims are rewritten to the strongest state actually earned; negative, tied, and \textsc{CannotCheck} families remain visible.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Current status}
This is a prospective TeX successor. It does not modify the predecessor P2 results and does not assert end-to-end open-world superiority before the frozen protected campaign is executed.
