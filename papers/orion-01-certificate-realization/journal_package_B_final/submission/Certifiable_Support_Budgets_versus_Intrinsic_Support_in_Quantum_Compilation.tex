\title{Certifiable Support Budgets versus Intrinsic Support in Quantum Compilation}
\author{Sze Chun Yiu}
\date{}
\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
A support bound can be exact for a restricted proof system and still be
loose as a description of the compiler it certifies. We make that
distinction explicit in two quantum-compilation families. The generic
zero-sum language is donor mathematics: for a finite abelian group
\texttt{H} and fixed alphabet \texttt{A}, let
\(\operatorname{zsf}(H;A)\) be the maximum length of a zero-sum-free
sequence over \texttt{A}. In the registered rank-only deletion proof
system, \(\operatorname{zsf}(H;A)\) is the exact terminal support budget
when a maximum zero-sum-free word is realizable and no additional rule
is available.

The compiler quantity is different. Define intrinsic support
\(kappa(F,C)\) as the least \texttt{k} for which every instance of
compiler family \texttt{F} under objective \texttt{C} has an exact
optimum of support at most \texttt{k}. Evidence that \texttt{k-1} cannot
suffice is required to establish an exact numerical value, but it is not
part of the definition. This makes \(kappa\) a mathematical property of
the compiler rather than a statement about current knowledge.

The main result is the matched tight/loose pair. In frozen one-Tag R6M,
the rank-only certifiable support budget is two and independent compiler
upper/lower evidence gives \(kappa_R6M=2\). In frozen R6I under the same
declared unit-objective interpretation used by its parent theorem, the
rank-only system has exact budget five, while a stronger whole-system
Tag-relocation theorem gives \(kappa_R6I=1\). Thus

\(beta_rank-only(R6I)=5 > 1=kappa_R6I\).

The separation is deliberately proof-system-relative: it does not prove
that every local, syndrome-preserving, or unrestricted proof system
needs support five. Registered direct products amplify the same
mechanism to budgets \texttt{5t} versus \texttt{t} because the product
definition imposes additive support and forbids cross-component moves;
the corresponding \(\Theta(n^(4t))\) direct-enumeration ratio is a
corollary of that declared search model, not an algorithm-independent
lower bound. The contribution is therefore the production tight/loose
control pair and the identification of the missing operation in the
looser certificate language, not a new generic zero-sum or
proof-complexity theory.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} quantum compilation; certifiable support; intrinsic support; proof systems; Pauli strings

\hypertarget{scientific-question-and-novelty-boundary}{%
\section{1. Scientific question and novelty
boundary}\label{scientific-question-and-novelty-boundary}}

Three quantities are often collapsed in compiler papers:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\item
  the smallest support the compiler intrinsically needs;
\item
  the support reached by a named normalization;
\item
  the support a restricted proof system can certify.
\end{enumerate}

This paper asks when those quantities coincide and when they do not.

The generic ingredients---zero-sum thresholds, rank obstructions,
proof-system-relative lower bounds, and direct products---are prior
mathematics. The paper-level residual is:

\begin{itemize}
\item
  a tight production control in R6M;
\item
  a strict production separation in R6I under an explicitly named
  rank-only proof system;
\item
  the compiler operation absent from that proof system: whole-system Tag
  relocation/reconstruction.
\end{itemize}

Product amplification is reported only as a transparent corollary of the
registered product construction.

\hypertarget{three-support-quantities}{%
\section{2. Three support quantities}\label{three-support-quantities}}

Fix a compiler family \texttt{F} and objective \texttt{C}.

\hypertarget{intrinsic-support}{%
\subsection{2.1 Intrinsic support}\label{intrinsic-support}}

Define

\(kappa(F,C) = min { k : every instance in F has an exact optimum of \operatorname{support} \le k }\),

whenever such a uniform finite bound exists.

This is a mathematical property of \texttt{(F,C)}. To prove
\(kappa(F,C)=k\), one needs both an upper theorem at \texttt{k} and a
lower witness showing that \texttt{k-1} fails for at least one admitted
instance. The witness is evidence for the value; it is not part of the
definition.

\hypertarget{normalization-ceiling}{%
\subsection{2.2 Normalization ceiling}\label{normalization-ceiling}}

A transformation \texttt{N} may show that every feasible configuration
has a no-more-expensive representative of support at most \texttt{B\_N}.
Unless a matching intrinsic lower witness is known, \texttt{B\_N}
belongs to \texttt{(F,C,N)} rather than to the compiler alone.

\hypertarget{certifiable-support-budget}{%
\subsection{2.3 Certifiable support
budget}\label{certifiable-support-budget}}

Let \texttt{P} be an explicitly registered proof system with a fixed
observation language and legal rules. Define \(beta_P(F,C)\) as the
least uniform support budget that \texttt{P} can certify over the
production scope for \texttt{(F,C)}.

This paper uses \textbf{certifiable support budget} rather than
unqualified ``certificate complexity'' to avoid collision with the
established Boolean-function quantity usually denoted \texttt{C(f)}.

Soundness of \texttt{P} gives

\[kappa(F,C) \le  beta_P(F,C)\]

when both quantities refer to the same compiler family and the same
objective. Equality requires additional mathematics.

\hypertarget{donor-zero-sum-scaffold}{%
\section{3. Donor zero-sum scaffold}\label{donor-zero-sum-scaffold}}

Let \texttt{H} be a finite abelian group and let \texttt{A\ subseteq\ H}
be fixed. A subsequence means an arbitrary nonempty subset of positions,
not necessarily a contiguous factor. A sequence is zero-sum-free when no
nonempty subsequence sums to zero. Define

\(\operatorname{zsf}(H;A)=max({0} \cup {|W| : W is zero-sum-free over A})\).

Consider the abstract deletion language whose states are nonzero-total
sequences over \texttt{A} and whose only legal shortening removes a
nonempty \textbf{proper} zero-sum subsequence.

\textbf{Lemma 1 (terminal length of the registered deletion language).}
The maximum terminal length is \(\operatorname{zsf}(H;A)\).

\textbf{Proof.} Any sequence longer than \(\operatorname{zsf}\) contains
a nonempty zero-sum subsequence. Because the state's total sum is
nonzero, that subsequence cannot be the whole sequence, so the deletion
is legal. Conversely, a maximum zero-sum-free sequence has nonzero
total: if its total were zero, the whole sequence itself would be a
forbidden nonempty zero-sum subsequence. It therefore belongs to the
state language and has no legal deletion. ∎

This lemma is generic zero-sum mathematics and is not claimed as a new
theorem of this paper.

A production compiler obtains an exact \(\operatorname{zsf}\)-based
lower witness only when it realizes such a terminal word \textbf{and}
the named proof system has no other rule that can reduce it.

\hypertarget{tight-control-r6m}{%
\section{4. Tight control: R6M}\label{tight-control-r6m}}

All quantities in this section use the frozen R6M unit objective bound
by the parent evidence.

The one-Tag R6M production signature alphabet contains a basis of
\texttt{F\_2\^{}2}. In the registered rank-only proof system, the basis
word is terminal and the binary dependence upper bound is two, hence

\(beta_rank-only(R6M)=2\).

Separately, the all-size compiler normalization supplies support at most
two and the committed lower witness rules out support one for an
admitted instance. Therefore

\(kappa_R6M=2\).

Thus R6M is a genuine tight control:

\(beta_rank-only(R6M)=kappa_R6M=2\).

The proof-system certificate is not automatically loose; tightness
depends on whether the certificate obstruction is also a compiler
obstruction.

\hypertarget{strict-separation-r6i}{%
\section{5. Strict separation: R6I}\label{strict-separation-r6i}}

All quantities in this section use the frozen R6I unit objective named
in the live claim ledger.

The R6I production block-deletion alphabets realize basis obstructions
in a five-dimensional binary quotient. The explicitly registered
\textbf{rank-only} proof system contains only the zero-sum/rank deletion
rule for this bound. Therefore the production upper and realized
terminal witness give

\(beta_rank-only(R6I)=5\).

This exactness is intentionally relative to that proof system. The paper
does not assert that rank-only is the strongest proof language available
to practitioners or that every local proof system has the same lower
bound.

A stronger compiler transformation changes the auxiliary structure that
rank-only freezes: it localizes each block to an anticommuting core and
then relocates/reconstructs the shared Tag at whole-system scope. The
committed all-size theorem gives support at most one, while support zero
is infeasible. Therefore

\(kappa_R6I=1\).

The exact production separation is

\(beta_rank-only(R6I)-kappa_R6I=4\).

This identifies the missing proof operation rather than merely observing
a smaller bound.

\hypertarget{registered-product-amplification}{%
\section{6. Registered product
amplification}\label{registered-product-amplification}}

Let \texttt{F\_R6I\^{}t} be the registered direct product of \texttt{t}
independent R6I components on disjoint coordinates. By definition:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\item
  support budgets add across components; and
\item
  the registered product proof system permits no cross-component move.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Theorem 2 (declared-product budgets).} For every \(t\ge 1\),

\(beta_rank-only(F_R6I^t)=5t\),

\(kappa(F_R6I^t)=t\).

\textbf{Proof.} Componentwise upper bounds give \(beta\le 5t\) and
\(kappa\le t\). For the rank-only lower bound, each component realizes
its support-five terminal obstruction; because the product proof system
has no cross-component move and the budget is additive, these \texttt{t}
component lower bounds compose to \(beta\ge 5t\). For intrinsic support,
support zero is infeasible in every component, so any exact product
solution needs at least one supported coordinate per component, giving
\(kappa\ge t\). The componentwise support-one compiler normalization
attains \texttt{t}. ∎

The additive gap is \texttt{4t}. This is amplification of one registered
mechanism, not a second independent compiler phenomenon.

\hypertarget{direct-enumeration-corollary}{%
\subsection{6.1 Direct enumeration
corollary}\label{direct-enumeration-corollary}}

For a direct support enumerator over \texttt{n} coordinates with fixed
local alphabet and fixed support budget \texttt{B}, the leading search
volume is \(\Theta(n^B)\). Under the declared product model the
rank-only and intrinsic budgets therefore induce

\(\Theta(n^(5t))\) and \(\Theta(n^t)\)

for fixed \texttt{t}, with ratio \(\Theta(n^(4t))\) as
\texttt{n\ -\textgreater{}\ infinity}.

The separate statement that the additive budget gap grows without bound
is a \texttt{t\ -\textgreater{}\ infinity} observation. The two limits
are not conflated, and neither statement is an algorithm-independent
complexity-class lower bound.

\hypertarget{relation-to-prior-work}{%
\section{7. Relation to prior work}\label{relation-to-prior-work}}

Davenport and restricted zero-sum theory own the generic sequence
thresholds. Sparse integer optimization owns general support bounds and
lower constructions. Proof-complexity and formal-methods literature own
the distinction between an object's property and what a restricted
calculus can prove.

The substantive residual here is the \textbf{matched production pair}:
R6M, where the rank-only certificate is tight, and R6I, where the same
style of certificate is loose by five-to-one because the compiler admits
a stronger global Tag reconstruction that the proof language freezes
out.

The abstract zero-sum lemma and the declared-product search exponent are
supporting scaffolds, not standalone novelty claims.

\hypertarget{reproducibility-and-evidence-authority}{%
\section{8. Reproducibility and evidence
authority}\label{reproducibility-and-evidence-authority}}

The R6I production alphabets, basis witnesses, source/generic/native
agreement and support-one parent theorem are commit-bound in the
existing parent evidence package. The R6M support-two upper theorem and
support-one obstruction are commit-bound in the parent evidence package.
Finite verifiers check representative alphabet and product identities;
all-size authority for the compiler values comes from the corresponding
parent theorems and witnesses.

Internal independent implementations are not external specialist
replication.

\hypertarget{limitations}{%
\section{9. Limitations}\label{limitations}}

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\item
  \(beta_rank-only\) is exact only for the explicitly registered
  rank-only proof system.
\item
  No lower bound is proved for every local, syndrome-preserving or
  unrestricted proof system.
\item
  The product theorem uses additive support and no cross-component move
  by definition.
\item
  The search exponent concerns a direct support enumerator, not
  arbitrary algorithms.
\item
  Structural support is not a hardware speedup or physical
  quantum-resource advantage.
\item
  The generic zero-sum and proof-system concepts are donor-owned.
\item
  Submission-date overlap review and independent specialist proof attack
  remain external scientific checks.
\end{enumerate}

\hypertarget{conclusion}{%
\section{10. Conclusion}\label{conclusion}}

A support ceiling should be assigned to the layer that proves it. In
R6M, the rank-only ceiling and intrinsic compiler support coincide at
two. In R6I, the rank-only budget is five while the compiler's intrinsic
support is one because a stronger whole-system Tag transformation leaves
the certificate language. The paper's contribution is this production
tight/loose control pair and the identified missing proof operation.
Generic zero-sum mathematics and direct-product amplification are
supporting tools, not the novelty claim.
