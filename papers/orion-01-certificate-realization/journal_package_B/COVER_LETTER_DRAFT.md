**Manuscript:** *Exact Certificate Complexity versus Intrinsic Support in Quantum Compilation*

Dear Editors,

Please consider our manuscript, *Exact Certificate Complexity versus Intrinsic Support in Quantum Compilation*, for publication in **Quantum**.

The paper studies a reporting and proof-language question that arises in exact quantum compilation: when does a sharp support bound belong to the compiler itself, and when does it belong only to the normalization or certificate language used to prove it? We formalize the distinction using the alphabet-restricted zero-sum invariant `zsf(H;A)`, prove that it is the exact terminal complexity of the stated zero-sum deletion language, and make the production-realization requirement explicit.

Two compiler families give opposite controls. In R6M the rank-only certificate and intrinsic compiler support coincide at two. In R6I the rank-only certificate complexity is five while an independently bound whole-system Tag-relocation theorem gives intrinsic support one. Registered independent products amplify the support-budget gap from `5` versus `1` to `5t` versus `t`, with the stated consequence for a direct support enumerator. The manuscript does not promote this to an algorithm-independent complexity lower bound or a physical quantum-resource advantage.

The generic zero-sum, sparse-support, and proof-language ideas are donor-owned. The residual contribution is the exact quantum-compilation instantiation: a certificate that is tight in one production family, provably loose in another, and whose looseness is traced to a concrete proof operation omitted by the restricted certificate language.

Analytic proofs carry the all-size claims. Finite programs bind the production alphabets and registered controls. External independent proof review, final archive deposition, and filing-time author declarations remain explicitly open rather than being inferred from internal verification.
