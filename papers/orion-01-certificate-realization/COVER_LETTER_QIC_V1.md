Please consider our manuscript, "Restore-Sensitive Certificate Realization: Normal Forms and
Intrinsic Support in Quantum Compilation" ([PLACEHOLDER: manuscript ID]), for publication in
[PLACEHOLDER: journal name] as a research article.

The manuscript studies when a local deletion certificate for quantum-compiler support
describes the compiler it certifies and when it does not. Tag-and-Restore Encoding (TARE)
compilers [Schillo, Sturm and Quay, arXiv:2601.05740] expose auxiliary Pauli frames whose
support could in principle grow with system size, and we ask which support ceilings are
intrinsic to the compiler objective rather than artifacts of the certificate language used
to derive them.

The paper's contributions, as bounded by its claim ledger, are:

1. **A whole-instance deletion contract.** Under explicit assumptions (whole-instance
   deletion soundness, objective dominance, support monotonicity), a terminating global
   descent yields one exact optimum that simultaneously satisfies every constrained
   generator's zero-sum-free alphabet ceiling. For the phase-quotiented MultiTag grammar
   studied here, the binary signature ceiling equals the rank of the generated span, the
   exact upward Restore sensitivity is \(b-1\), and the objective condition is the explicit
   cone \(\mu \ge (b-1)t_R\).

2. **A matched control and a separation, on fixed objectives and fixed support statistics.**
   In a matched one-Tag, three-block family, the rank-only certifiable-support budget and
   the intrinsic optimal support both equal two (the sharpness witness is an exact
   enumeration over the complete support-one family). In a dependent-triple family, the
   rank-only joint active-column budget is five while whole-system Tag relocation reaches
   intrinsic support one.

3. **A scoped interpretation of that separation.** The certifiable-support calculus is
   defined operationally and contains exactly one rule, so the exactness of its budget holds
   by construction within that one-rule system rather than as a bound over alternative
   certificate rules. The manuscript accordingly claims no lower bound for local,
   syndrome-preserving, or unrestricted proof systems, no transfer to production compilers,
   and no physical-resource or quantum-advantage statement. Two adverse records — a rejected
   finite production-realization gate and a pinned PyZX transfer attempt that stopped
   inconclusively — are reported as part of the result, and the reproducibility section
   describes the deterministic checkers and their finite coverage.

We believe this fits [PLACEHOLDER: journal name] because the question — what a restricted
