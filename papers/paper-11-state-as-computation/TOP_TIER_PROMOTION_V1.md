## Maximum claim to earn

> **A representation is a computational placement decision.** Structural/search work can be paid during state construction, during downstream access/reasoning, or later through reconstruction after specialization destroys future optionality. P11 characterizes this trade-off theoretically and empirically, and predicts when task-specific compilation, caching, raw retention or universal materialization is resource-optimal.

Task-conditioned retrieval/memory, feature selection, partial evaluation, knowledge compilation, compression, usable information, sparse access and nonlinear decoders are donor-owned components.

## Theory expansion

### T11.1 — General query-family rank/access law

Generalize the exact parity/rank substrate to arbitrary finite linearly independent query families and an approximate low-rank/noisy extension. Explicitly separate:

- semantic information rank;
- representation/access rank;
- decoder/access-mechanism complexity;
- compiler complexity.

No unrestricted nonlinear/time lower bound may be implied.

### T11.2 — No-answer-laundering condition

Formalize a sufficient condition under which compiled state is informative/access-enhancing without containing or trivially encoding the final protected answer. Include hostile constructions showing why query identity or latent-feature selection can otherwise leak answer authority.

### T11.3 — Optionality law

For a future-query process with horizon/diversity/drift parameters, derive exact or bounded conditions governing when to:

- compile per query;
- compile union-rank state;
- cache compiled state;
- retain raw state;
- materialize a universal state;
- retain reversible raw+summary state.

Current-task efficiency and future optionality must remain separate coordinates.

## Real-system evidence

### E11.1 — learned non-oracle compiler

Train a compiler using development source state and task/query descriptions only. Protected query families/identities are unseen. Compare:

- oracle compiler;
- learned compiler;
- universal/raw state;
- random/feature-selection donor;
- strong sparse decoder/access baseline;
- deterministic nonlinear decoder/access baseline.

Charge compiler training and inference work.

### E11.2 — end-to-end resource surface

Measure the vector:

`R = (compiler/preprocessing work, state size/memory, downstream model capacity, inference/search, tool/verifier calls, latency, cache/reuse, recovery)`

Report Pareto frontiers rather than hiding transfers in one scalar.

### E11.3 — real-system replication

Require two qualitatively different settings unless one is impossible for reasons frozen before outcomes:

1. open-weight/procedural agent tasks;
2. verifier-backed formal/search tasks or a long-horizon agent-memory workflow with delayed future queries.

The stronger headline requires a smaller/cheaper downstream reasoner over learned compiled state to meet a frozen quality target that a materially larger universal-state system misses at matched end-to-end resources.

## Phase-diagram target

Prospectively freeze query horizon, overlap/diversity, drift and memory/compute grids. Predict the best state policy before protected evaluation. The contribution is a law/crossover map, not universal compilation superiority.

## Primary endpoints

- critical resource required to hit frozen quality target;
- total Pareto dominance/non-dominance;
- compiler leakage/answer-laundering rate;
- learned-vs-oracle compiler gap;
- future-query service probability;
- rare critical-query loss;
- recovery/recompilation cost under drift;
- phase-diagram prediction accuracy.

## Strongest hostile attacks

- stronger decoder erases the effect;
- compiler performs the final answer computation;
- query identity leaks protected target;
- preprocessing/training cost is ignored;
- universal state loses only because of serialization/context length;
- compilation appears better only for the tuned query distribution;
- future optionality losses are hidden;
- claimed phase law fails under drift or rare critical queries.
