Dear Action Editor,

Please consider the enclosed research paper, **“Diagnosing Learning-System Failures Before Escalating Compute,”** for Transactions on Machine Learning Research.

The paper asks a bounded methodological question: before increasing model capacity or inference time, can a prospective intervention test distinguish missing information, inaccessible representation, and insufficient computation? Across five fixed task families, the diagnostic agrees with four protected dispositions, versus one for generic compute escalation, while avoiding four false compute escalations. The unmatched family is retained as indeterminate because its registered threshold does not transport.

The manuscript is deliberately symmetric about unfavorable evidence. It retains a real-data null, a protected model-size negative, a hostile symbol-reminting result that withdraws an earlier contrast, and a follow-up analysis that fails its own stability condition. Corrected vector-valued resource accounting preserves the primary count but prevents the registered intervention costs from being interpreted as a universal physical exchange rate.

We believe the paper will interest readers studying evaluation, test-time computation, structured learning, selective decision-making, and reproducible methodology. It claims neither a universal diagnostic nor a general argument against scaling.
