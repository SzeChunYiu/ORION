# ORION-01–25 publication execution board

**Purpose:** convert the science-gap audit into owned, falsifiable publication work.  
**Authority delta:** `NONE`. This board schedules work; it does not create evidence.  
**Operating rule:** a paper advances only when its strongest claim, negative boundary, decisive test, artifacts and rendered manuscript agree byte-for-byte in meaning.

## Cross-disciplinary review cell

Every paper is reviewed through seven lanes. A lane may return `GREEN`, `RED` or `CANNOT_CHECK`; silence is never approval.

| Code | Background | Primary responsibility | Mandatory adversarial question |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | senior scientific editor and journal reviewer | novelty, contribution shape, claim hierarchy, narrative discipline | Does every headline claim have one identifiable result and an explicit failure boundary? |
| E2 | formal methods, theorem proving and semantics | definitions, theorem scope, proof objects, counterexamples, implementation correspondence | Is the theorem exactly the theorem tested, and is every premise operationally checkable? |
| E3 | statistics, causal inference and experimental design | estimands, paired structure, uncertainty, multiplicity, power, missingness | Could the reported direction survive while the magnitude claim remains unidentified or underpowered? |
| E4 | machine learning, information retrieval and benchmark evaluation | data splits, comparator fidelity, shift, calibration, action support | Is the comparator genuinely competitive and given the same information, actions and stopping rights? |
| E5 | systems, security and software assurance | threat models, fault injection, runtime/resource accounting, production transfer | What realistic failure or compromise makes the system appear correct while the scientific claim is false? |
| E6 | research governance, scientific authority and institutional independence | custody, conflicts, authorization, external adjudication, non-laundering | Is this independent authority, or merely a second artifact/model controlled by the same programme? |
| E7 | reproducibility, data governance and scholarly artifacts | manifests, hashes, provenance, replay, tables, figures, PDF/source binding | Can a reviewer regenerate the exact claim surface, including null and adverse outcomes, from frozen inputs? |

### Decision rule

A submission candidate requires:

1. E1 and the domain lead to agree on one paper-shaped contribution rather than a portfolio manifesto.
2. E2/E3/E4/E5 to mark all applicable scientific and technical gates `GREEN`; an inapplicable lane must state why.
3. E6 to identify the highest authority actually earned: internal, independent implementation, external data, independent evaluator or institutional custody.
4. E7 to verify source-to-artifact-to-PDF identity and the presence of every null, adverse, retracted, `CANNOT_CHECK`, `NOT_EXECUTED` and `NOT_COMPUTED_BY_PROTOCOL` result.
5. Any unresolved result to remain a named adverse terminal, not a missing row or optimistic discussion sentence.

## Work classes

- **L0 — claim and package repair:** can be completed from existing authoritative artifacts without generating a new scientific result.
- **L1 — internal scientific closure:** requires a new theorem, proof object, simulation or same-programme experiment. It may improve internal support but not external authority.
- **L2 — external empirical transfer:** requires new external data, systems or independently implemented comparators.
- **L3 — external scientific authority:** additionally requires independent evaluator or institutional custody capable of issuing an adverse outcome.

A paper may be publishable at a top level with a bounded or negative result, but only when its claimed level matches the level actually completed.

## Portfolio sequence

### Sequence A — repair before experiment

Complete Wave 0 first. These changes have high confidence because they reconcile existing authority and prevent known overclaim, stale rendering, path ambiguity or missing sentinels. They should not wait for new science.

### Sequence B — theory and proof-object closure

Run ORION-01, ORION-04, ORION-05, ORION-09, ORION-10, ORION-16 and ORION-18 through E2-led statement, premise, counterexample and proof-object audits. A proof claim cannot advance on solver success alone; an impossibility or bounded negative may be the stronger paper.

### Sequence C — smallest decisive external campaigns

Prioritize external studies whose outcomes discriminate between live explanations rather than merely adding benchmark volume:

- ORION-02: predicted value/no-value strata.
- ORION-07: agreement, disagreement and uninformative-instrument cases.
- ORION-11: faithful ordered search versus reconstruction under matched cost.
- ORION-12: matched retrieval actions and resource frontier.
- ORION-13: non-degenerate coordinate strata and downstream utility.
- ORION-14/18/24: independent authority and common-mode controls.
- ORION-15: sealed longitudinal transfer without self-access.
- ORION-20/22: one-shot registered campaigns with adverse publication terminals.
- ORION-25: independent production fault matrix.

### Sequence D — mature bounded papers

ORION-05, ORION-09, ORION-10, ORION-21 and ORION-23 may be strongest as carefully bounded theorem/boundary papers rather than by postponing submission until universal transfer is claimed. E1 must ensure the title, abstract and conclusion match that narrower contribution.

## Submission packet invariant

For every paper, the release candidate must contain:

- a claim-to-evidence table with one row per abstract/result/conclusion claim;
- frozen data/task/model/comparator identities and access dates;
- exact analysis code plus environment lock;
- primary, secondary, negative-control and adverse-terminal outputs;
- resource and evaluator-access accounting;
- independent-check records and declared authority level;
- source, tables, figures, supplementary objects and final PDF bound by hashes;
- a clean-clone reproduction log;
- a machine-readable statement that editorial repair has `science_authority_delta=NONE`.

The release checker must fail when a required item is absent, stale, contradictory or replaced by a favorable secondary analysis.
