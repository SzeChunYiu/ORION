Dear Editors of Artificial Intelligence,

Please consider the regular paper **“Epistemic Navigation in Open Worlds: Closure Transport under Representation and Objective Change.”**

The paper studies a formal AI problem that arises when an autonomous research system cannot assume that the representation defining its states, routes, objectives, and stopping conditions remains fixed. It deliberately absorbs strong prior mechanisms—graph and belief-space navigation, planning abstraction, schema/ontology transport, recent categorical and sheaf-theoretic scientific-regime transport, goal evolution, world-model revision, and open-world stopping—and does not claim those mechanisms as new.

The manuscript isolates the scientific closure interface that remains after those mechanisms are included. Its main results are: (i) a stopping impossibility theorem conditioned on explicit observational extension ambiguity; (ii) a fixed-latent construction showing that representation refinement can change worst-case solvability without new sensing or latent states, together with a harmful-coarsening control; (iii) a separation theorem showing that evidence may remain valid while a transformed scientific obligation is no longer discharged; and (iv) positive and ambiguity-conditioned negative closure-transport theorems based on a complete support/obligation/coverage witness. Route stop, task stop, continuation, and inability-to-check remain separate terminals throughout.

A fresh literature pass immediately before preparation of this submission explicitly added Self-Revising Discovery Systems for Science, sheaf-theoretic theory-shift transport, and current closure-gap work as close donors. This narrowed the manuscript’s publication claim: it does not claim representational-regime transport or obstruction. The contribution is the stronger scientific closure/obligation-transport contract across representation and objective change, coupled to fail-closed open/censored stopping.

The repository contains deterministic finite theorem/countermodel checks and eight frozen reference contracts, including a harmful-reframe control and a non-retrieval experimental-design transfer case. These artifacts support the formal boundaries; they are not represented as deployed-agent performance results. The strongest baseline is an ideal integrated donor product, and the paper explicitly allows that product to tie the proposed interface when it implements the same semantics.

The submission package includes editable LaTeX source, in-text citations and references, reproducibility paths, highlights, claim ledgers, and a transparent generative-AI disclosure.
