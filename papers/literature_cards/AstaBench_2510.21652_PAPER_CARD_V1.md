## 01. Bibliographic position
Jonathan Bragg et al. **AstaBench: Rigorous Benchmarking of AI Agents with a Scientific Research Suite.** arXiv:2510.21652v2; ICLR 2026 conference paper.

## 02. Research question
How can scientific-research agents be benchmarked under controlled, reproducible tools, costs, interfaces, tasks and strong baseline agents?

## 03. Background route
The paper responds to rapid growth of general and specialized research agents whose evaluations confound model, tool access, cost and task realism.

## 04. Prior-work / field context
It positions existing scientific-agent benchmarks as insufficiently reproducible/controlled and provides a large multi-domain suite spanning the research process.

## 05. Pain point
Benchmark scores are hard to interpret when tools, cost/access, interfaces, baseline quality or task realism differ across systems.

## 06. Core insight
Build a standardized scientific research environment and task suite, evaluate many agent classes under controlled tooling/costs, and use production-informed scientific tasks rather than isolated toy capabilities.

## 07. Method / module logic
The abstract reports:
- 2,400+ problems spanning scientific-discovery activities/domains;
- a production-grade controlled search/tool environment;
- nine science-optimized agent classes plus baselines;
- evaluation of 57 agents across 22 agent classes.

## 09. Experiment-to-claim evidence
Large controlled benchmark comparison. The main conclusion is that progress is uneven and current AI remains far from fully solving scientific research assistance.

## 10. Main conclusions
Scientific-agent evaluation requires controlled environment/tooling, strong baselines, cost awareness and task breadth; performance should be evaluated on standardized problems rather than anecdotal agent demos.
