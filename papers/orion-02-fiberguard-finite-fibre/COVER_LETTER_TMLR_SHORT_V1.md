Dear Editors,

Please consider *When a Representation Can Certify: A Fail-Closed Calculus for Finite Representation Fibres*, a short theory note, for Transactions on Machine Learning Research.

The manuscript's contribution is a joint fail-closed calculus for deterministic certificates on finite representation fibres, governed by a single quantity, the target diameter hidden by a representation state. Five linked results follow from that one quantity: the exact minimax certificate radius, the exact certifiability threshold at a declared tolerance, the minimum unconstrained refinement as a greedy interval cover, an exact realizability characterization under a restricted separator family, and the exact whole-fibre coverage available without refinement. The proofs are short, self-contained, and assume no sampling, smoothness, or computational hypotheses.

The submission deliberately retains its adverse boundary as its measured identity. Seven preregistered application studies, compressed to one table with evidence pointers, failed at decision value, useful coverage, or held-out validity; none is pooled, and none is reinterpreted as a near success. The one statistically notable paired comparison (exact McNemar p=0.03125) is reported for what it is: six one-way discordant pairs at n=44, with both compared certificates failing their frozen validity gate, supporting no superiority claim in either direction.

TMLR is the intended venue because its acceptance criterion evaluates correctness and clarity rather than perceived significance, which matches a bounded exact calculus presented together with honest negative results under preregistered gates. The work is not a transfer or production claim, and we believe stating that plainly is part of the contribution.

The source, executable review materials, atomic claim inventory, and checksums are supplied with the package. The manuscript does not treat an in-repository replay as external replication and does not enlarge the active claim authority. The work is not being submitted in parallel; the author must confirm that fact again in the live portal immediately before filing.
