# Refinement to certifiability — ORION02.REFINEMENT_TO_CERTIFIABILITY.v1

Top-tier promotion target from issue #1649, Tier A. Frozen before outcome access.
`scientific_authority_delta: NONE`.

## What this has to do

`ORION02.FIBRE_DIAMETER_FLOOR.v1` proved a barrier: any certificate accepted on a fibre
`F_z` incurs worst-case error at least `D(z)/2`, so `C_R24_ARM_CONDITIONAL_CERTIFICATE_INVALID`
is a structural impossibility rather than a tuning failure.

A barrier alone is a bounded negative result. #1649 asks for the thing that would make it
a top-tier contribution: a **constructive theorem that crosses the previously proved
impossibility boundary**. That is what follows. The barrier turns out not to be a wall but
a **dial**, and the theorems below say exactly what it costs to turn it.

## Setting

Finite instance set `X`, target `V : X -> R`, representation `phi : X -> Z`, fibre
`F_z = {x : phi(x) = z}`, diameter `D_phi(z) = max_{x,x' in F_z} |V(x) - V(x')|`.

`phi'` **refines** `phi` when `phi'(x) = phi'(x') implies phi(x) = phi(x')`; that is, the
`phi'`-partition is finer than the `phi`-partition.

A certificate is `eps`-**valid on a fibre** when its output is within `eps` of `V(x)` for
every member `x`.

## Theorem R1 (sufficiency — the construction)

If `D_{phi'}(z') <= 2 eps` for every `phi'`-fibre, then the **midpoint certificate**

    c(z') = ( min_{x in F_{z'}} V(x) + max_{x in F_{z'}} V(x) ) / 2

is `eps`-valid on every fibre.

*Proof.* For `x in F_{z'}` write `m, M` for the fibre's minimum and maximum value. Then
`V(x) in [m, M]` and `|c(z') - V(x)| <= (M - m)/2 = D_{phi'}(z')/2 <= eps`. ∎

## Theorem R2 (necessity — the barrier, restated as an equivalence)

By Theorem 1 of `ORION02.FIBRE_DIAMETER_FLOOR.v1`, if `D_{phi'}(z') > 2 eps` then **no**
certificate constant on `F_{z'}` is `eps`-valid there.

Combining R1 and R2:

    an eps-valid certificate accepted on a fibre exists   <==>   D(z) <= 2 eps.

This is the promotion. The floor is not merely a lower bound to be respected; it is an
**exact characterisation**, so "make the fibre certifiable" has a precise, achievable
meaning: refine until the diameter drops to `2 eps`, and the midpoint certificate then
works by construction.

## Theorem R3 (minimum refinement cost, and its exact algorithm)

Fix a fibre with sorted values `v_1 <= ... <= v_n`. Among all partitions of it into parts
of diameter at most `2 eps`, the minimum number of parts equals the count produced by the
left-to-right sweep that opens a new part whenever `v_i` exceeds the current part's
minimum by more than `2 eps`.

*Proof.* Contiguity is without loss of generality: a set of diameter `<= 2 eps` lies in a
value interval of length `2 eps`, so any partition may be replaced by a contiguous one
with no more parts. The problem is then covering the sorted points by intervals of length
`2 eps`, and the standard exchange argument applies. Let `G` be the greedy solution and
`O` any optimal one. The part of `O` containing `v_1` cannot extend beyond `v_1 + 2 eps`,
so it covers a subset of the points `G`'s first part covers; replacing it by `G`'s first
part keeps `O` feasible and no larger. Induct on the remaining suffix. ∎

So the **information-theoretic** refinement cost is exactly computable, not merely
bounded.

## Theorem R4 (separator realisability — where the cost actually lands)

R3 counts parts, not obtainable parts. A refinement must be a *function of available
features*: given a separator family `S` of admissible predicates, `phi'` must be
`S`-measurable. Write `k_S` for the minimum number of `S`-measurable parts and `k*` for
the unconstrained minimum of R3. Then

    k_S >= k*,

and `k_S` is **infinite — the fibre is not certifiable at any cost under `S`** — exactly
when two instances with `|V(x) - V(x')| > 2 eps` are `S`-indistinguishable.

*Proof.* The inequality is immediate since every `S`-measurable partition is a partition.
For the second part: if `x, x'` agree on every predicate in `S` then every `S`-measurable
`phi'` places them in one part, whose diameter is then `> 2 eps`. Conversely if all such
pairs are separated, the partition generated by `S` has every part of diameter `<= 2 eps`
after applying R3 within each `S`-atom. ∎

This is the load-bearing honesty in the theory. R1–R3 describe what refinement can buy;
R4 says the currency is **separator power**, and identifies the exact obstruction: an
`S`-indistinguishable pair whose values differ by more than `2 eps`. The gap `k_S - k*` is
the price of a weak feature set, and `k_S = infinity` recovers the original impossibility
as the special case where `S` cannot see the difference at all.

## Theorem R5 (the refine-or-abstain frontier)

At target risk `eps`, without any refinement the achievable coverage is exactly the mass
of `{z : D(z) <= 2 eps}`, and splitting a fibre purchases exactly that fibre's mass at a
cost of `k*(z) - 1` additional parts.

*Proof.* Immediate from R1, R2 and R3: fibres already within `2 eps` are certifiable and
need no split; the rest are certifiable only after splitting, and R3 gives the count. ∎

This supplies the exact relationship #1649 asks for among fibre diameter, separator
complexity, achievable conditional risk, and the abstention/coverage tradeoff — as
identities rather than as an empirical curve.

## Hard prerequisite, respected

#1649 forbids leaning on the manuscript's unstated `A_t/B_t` cross-gadget separability
step for new authority. Nothing above uses `A_t/B_t`. R1–R5 are statements about arbitrary
finite fibres and are proved from the definitions. `A_t/B_t` is not cited here even as an
illustration.

## What would refute this

R1 fails if some fibre of diameter `<= 2 eps` admits no `eps`-valid constant certificate.
R3 fails if any partition beats the greedy count. R4 fails if an `S`-measurable refinement
separates an `S`-indistinguishable pair. R5 fails if coverage does not match the mass of
the within-`2 eps` fibres. `PROTOCOL.json` searches exhaustively for each of these, and
requires planted-violation controls to prove the search can see them.

## Stop rule, as issued

If refinement cannot create certified non-vacuous fibres at fair cost, the paper publishes
the information-theoretic boundary and stops the empirical rescue. Under R4 the decisive
question is whether real separator families are strong enough; that is the discriminator,
and it is allowed to fail.
