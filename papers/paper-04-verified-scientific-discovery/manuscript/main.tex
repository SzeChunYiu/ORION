\begin{abstract}
A benchmark score can fail to support a scientific-competence claim because the target is not identifiable from the intended evidence, because a compared system cannot emit the target terminal, or because the panel is saturated.  We give a verification-axis theory that separates these failures.  For finite benchmark distributions, the optimal exact-terminal risk is the fibrewise Bayes error of the representation available to the decision rule.  Zero risk is attainable if and only if the gold terminal is constant on every positive-probability representation fibre and belongs to the rule's output alphabet.  The same result yields a donor-product factorization theorem: a composition of provenance, verification, custody and authorization mechanisms can reproduce a scientific-promotion relation exactly if and only if its exposed state is target-sufficient; otherwise its mixed fibres impose an irreducible error bound.  A complementary total-variation bound shows when the observable record cannot distinguish two opposite interpretations of a competence result.

We apply this theory to three prospectively separated evidence layers.  In protected V2, a non-compensatory authority pipeline produced 0/360 false promotions versus 180/360 for the strongest frozen mechanism proxy while both promoted 60/60 clean positives.  The registered H3 abstention contrast remains \textsc{NotSupported}: every system scored 30/30 because an empty evidence list made the family trivial.  A distinct V3 repair cleared the frozen fourteen-probe register on the exact \textsc{CannotCheck} axis and produced 30/30 versus 0/30 for the V2-selected comparator and 15/30 for the one comparator able to escalate.  Because nine of ten comparators cannot emit \textsc{CannotCheck}, that result establishes terminal expressiveness, not general epistemic judgement.  Finally, the separate P4-X successor grants a strong product donor-complete provenance, verification, artifact/version, custody, epoch and generic-authorization mechanisms.  Across 400 frozen exact contracts, the non-compensatory scientific-promotion relation was correct on 400/400 cases versus 250/400 for that product and 50/400 for a compensatory product; an information-equivalent typed product tied at 400/400.  The paired improvement over the strong product was $0.375$ (domain-stratified bootstrap 95\% CI $[0.3275,0.4225]$), with zero false promotions and complete clean promotion.  These results support the factorization boundary and a scientific-promotion interface on the exact contracts, not naturalistic, provider-level, or universal superiority.  A frozen paired 768-cluster naturalistic successor is reported as a prospective design only; no external-panel outcome is claimed.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} scientific verification; benchmark identifiability; attainable risk; provenance; authorization; protected evaluation; abstention.
