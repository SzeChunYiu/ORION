\begin{abstract}
Verification, provenance, attribution, abstention, confidence estimation, benchmark auditing, and authorization are now standard ingredients of trustworthy scientific-agent systems. ORION Paper IV therefore targets the strongest upward claim rather than a narrower verification residual: across heterogeneous scientific domains, evidence structures, state representations, and adversarial benchmark constructions, can ORION outperform a donor-complete system at deciding when evidence actually licenses a scientific conclusion, while preserving valid conclusions and avoiding excessive abstention under matched resources? The predecessor protected V2 result remains immutable, including the saturated H3 slice whose benchmark could not identify the intended competence. P4-U turns that null into a benchmark-science programme: identifiability is a prerequisite for interpreting any promotion score, not a reason to retreat from the larger claim. The protected benchmark includes partial and conflicting evidence, dependence, invalid statistics, unverifiable code, contamination, persuasive unsupported claims, scope mismatch, temporal invalidation, evaluator defects, and cases where the final answer is correct but the state used is insufficient for promotion responsibility because provenance, dependency, uncertainty, or custody coordinates are missing. Strong comparators receive modern verification, provenance, abstention, confidence, generic authorization, chain-of-evidence, and responsibility-aware state mechanisms. Primary outcomes include false scientific promotion, false rejection, clean coverage, calibration, responsibility-rung misuse, benchmark shortcut susceptibility, downstream utility, transfer, and resource cost. The intended terminal is replicated general scientific authorization superiority over the strongest donor-complete system, ideally with at least one new promotion obligation or benchmark-audit mechanic discovered from a prior null/failure and validated prospectively across fresh constructions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Largest scientific question}
P4-U asks:
\begin{quote}
Across arbitrary scientific evidence situations, can ORION decide more accurately than the strongest donor-complete system whether a candidate scientific conclusion is actually licensed, and can it preserve that advantage when benchmark shortcuts, representation changes, and evaluator attacks are removed?
\end{quote}

This is broader than factual verification, provenance, abstention, or generic authorization. Those capabilities are absorbed into the comparator. ORION must win at the higher-order relation between evidence, responsibility, and scientific conclusion authority.

\section{Upward-generalization principle}
Nearest-work pressure expands the donor-complete comparator. If a donor solves one failure class, ORION does not retreat to a smaller wording. The target moves upward to whether ORION can coordinate and generalize over those mechanisms across regimes. A contribution is earned through one or more of:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item lower false scientific promotion at matched clean coverage;
    \item lower false rejection at matched safety;
    \item correct routing among answer, verify, promote, repair, and unresolved responsibilities;
    \item robustness to benchmark construction changes and adversarial shortcut removal;
    \item transfer across scientific domains and evidence modalities;
    \item discovery of a new promotion obligation or benchmark-identifiability mechanic that expands protected reach beyond the frozen donor basis.
\end{enumerate}
The intended terminal is \textbf{GENERAL_SCIENTIFIC_AUTHORIZATION_SUPERIORITY}.

\section{Scientific authority as a responsibility relation}
For evidence state $E$, candidate claim $C$, context $X$, and requested responsibility $R$, define
\[
A(E,C,X,R)\in\{\textsc{Authorized},\textsc{Blocked},\textsc{Unresolved},\textsc{CannotCheck}\}.
\]
Responsibilities are not collapsed. At minimum distinguish:
\[
R\in\{\textsc{Answer},\textsc{Verify},\textsc{Promote},\textsc{Repair}\}.
\]
A state sufficient to answer may be insufficient to promote. Correct final-answer accuracy is therefore not sufficient evidence of scientific authorization competence.

\section{Responsibility-carrying state stress test}
Compare confidence-only summaries, provenance summaries, full raw evidence, and responsibility-carrying states. Hold final-answer correctness equal when possible. The decisive test is whether the system distinguishes lower-rung sufficiency from higher-rung authorization.

After evidence or representation change, authorization state must be explicitly transported, reopened, or revoked. Stale authority cannot survive merely because the final answer text remains unchanged.

\section{Primary hypotheses}
\paragraph{H1, authorization superiority.}
Across the heterogeneous protected benchmark, ORION reduces false scientific promotion versus the strongest runnable donor-complete comparator by a prospectively frozen margin.

\paragraph{H2, coverage preservation.}
The H1 gain is invalid if clean valid promotion falls below a frozen non-inferiority margin or false rejection rises unacceptably.

\paragraph{H3, benchmark-identifiability robustness.}
The advantage survives shortcut audits, balanced reconstructions, and independently generated benchmark variants.

\paragraph{H4, responsibility-rung superiority.}
ORION more accurately distinguishes \textsc{Answer}, \textsc{Verify}, \textsc{Promote}, and \textsc{Repair} sufficiency, reducing false promotion caused by reuse of lower-responsibility state.

\paragraph{H5, mechanism expansion.}
At least one old null/failure yields a new authority obligation or benchmark-identifiability mechanic that expands protected reach beyond the pre-freeze donor-complete basis and transfers to fresh domains/constructions.

\section{Statistics}
The protected case, not repeated model generations, is the default independent unit. Freeze task/construction sampling, superiority and non-inferiority margins, multiplicity policy, confidence-interval method, catastrophic false-promotion rule, and handling of \textsc{CannotCheck} before protected outcomes.

Report false promotion, false rejection, clean coverage, calibration, and responsibility-rung errors separately. A single aggregate score cannot compensate a scientific-authority violation with an unrelated gain.

\section{Identifiability audit}
No headline result is interpretable until an independent audit establishes that candidate-visible fields do not predict labels above the frozen shortcut ceiling through trivial metadata or construction artifacts. Required attacks include length-only, source-count-only, missingness-only, template-only, metadata-only, and randomized-content controls.

If a shortcut predicts the target, the benchmark is repaired prospectively with a new construction and the old result remains a non-identifying historical artifact.

\section{Adversarial controls}
Mandatory attacks include:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item correct answer with unauthorized evidence;
    \item persuasive but unsupported explanation;
    \item valid evidence from the wrong scope or time;
    \item hidden dependence among apparently independent sources;
    \item self-authored or stale evaluator;
    \item candidate access to protected labels or contamination artifacts;
    \item answer-sufficient state reused for promotion without provenance/custody;
    \item always-abstain and always-open-raw controls, penalized by clean coverage and resource cost;
    \item donor-complete dynamic routing among verification, provenance, abstention, and authorization mechanisms.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Negative-result recursion as upward search}
Every false promotion, false rejection, null, or benchmark failure is preserved and attributed to one primary responsibility hypothesis:
\[
\{\text{evidence sufficiency},\text{provenance},\text{dependence},\text{statistics},\text{scope},\text{time},\text{state responsibility},\text{evaluator},\text{benchmark identifiability},\text{authority rule}\}.
\]
The first discriminator localizes the cause. The research programme then searches materially different mechanisms and parent disciplines for a more general authority rule or evaluation design. Recurrent failures across multiple obligations may justify a new higher-order promotion obligation rather than an ad hoc patch. Any proposed mechanic must survive a fresh construction and domain transfer before it can enter the main claim.
