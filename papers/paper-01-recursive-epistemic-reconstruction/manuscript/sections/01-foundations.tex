\section{Foundations}

ORION treats research as recursive epistemic reconstruction rather than a single model invocation or a fixed retrieval pipeline. For a scoped problem, its evolving state is represented as
\[
X_t=(K_t,W_t,M_t),
\]
where $K_t$ is the provenance-preserving portrait of the target object, $W_t$ is the current model of what domains, representations and search routes may be relevant, and $M_t$ is the governed research method.

This separation is load-bearing. New evidence may change an answer without changing the search universe; a newly discovered parent discipline may change $W_t$ without changing the method; a repeated, well-attributed research failure may eventually justify a challenger to $M_t$. These transitions require different evidence and governance.

The Apple Principle treats source statements as contextual projections rather than whole-object answers. Compatible projections may be aligned through GLUE; structurally informative but distant domains may be explored through JUMP. Neither operation grants scientific authority by itself.
