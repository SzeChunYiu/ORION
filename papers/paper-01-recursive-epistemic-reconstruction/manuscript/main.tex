\begin{abstract}
Many research-agent architectures iterate over search, reasoning, experimentation, and revision while keeping the task formulation itself largely implicit. ORION Paper I studies a narrower failure mode: a research process can become locally competent yet remain trapped when new evidence or failure shows that its representation, decomposition, or model of the relevant search universe is wrong. ORION therefore represents object knowledge $K_t$, the current relevance/search-universe model $W_t$, and governed method state $M_t$ as explicit mutable state. Typed residuals are diagnosed before any high-impact revision; supported formulation or search-universe changes trigger dependency-directed reopening of prior closure and force renewed research under the changed state. The same reconstruction discipline is recursively applied to the research workflow through inspectable mechanic cells. A frozen deterministic falsifier suite exercises hidden-domain, hidden-representation, missing-evidence, and execution-only worlds. The local suite passed after exposing and repairing an over-broad reframe gate that had allowed evidence/execution failures to rewrite the formulation. This establishes an implemented and locally falsified mechanism, not external novelty or superiority. Matched prospective comparisons on fresh hidden-shift tasks remain \texttt{CANNOT\_CHECK}.
\end{abstract}
