\begin{abstract}
Scientific systems can inherit strong mechanisms for diagnosis, repair, belief
revision, model discovery, and dependency maintenance while still lacking a
principle that determines which scientific transition their combined evidence
authorizes. We define an \emph{epistemic transition envelope}: the minimal
admissible front of a well-founded partial order over changes to questions,
representations, measurements, model classes, experiments, search policies, or
methods. We prove that an arbitrary product of donor mechanisms can reproduce
an envelope decision exactly if and only if its interface is constant on the
decision fibres. When an interface is lossy, its optimal transition error is
the Bayes impurity of those fibres. We also give necessary and sufficient
conditions for a stronger donor theory to extend an envelope conservatively,
and show that every antichain-valued transition policy has an envelope
representation. Minimal scientific escalation is one five-gate instance: a
transition must be evidence-licensed, task-preserving, invariant-preserving,
impact-matched, and budget-admissible. A frozen mechanical study evaluates nine
systems and five ablations on 2,882 worlds per run; on 480 hidden shifts the
instance attains protected success 1.000 versus 0.494 and 0.483 for the
strongest parent in the primary and disjoint replication, with no unnecessary
high-level change on 2,402 controls. A separate 400-case contract battery yields
400/400 decisions versus 275/400 for a donor-complete interface; an
information-equivalent product ties at 400/400, as the factorization theorem
requires. The theory applies beyond these generators, but the empirical
evidence does not yet establish naturalistic or model-general superiority.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} epistemic transitions; scientific reformulation; information sufficiency; belief revision; autonomous science; research agents.
