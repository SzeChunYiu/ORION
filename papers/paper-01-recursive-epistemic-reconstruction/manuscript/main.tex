\begin{abstract}
ORION is an evidence-governed research architecture in which search, knowledge integration, problem representation, and the research method itself may be revised by the results of research. The central state is separated into object knowledge $K_t$, a model of the potentially relevant knowledge/search universe $W_t$, and method state $M_t$. The core process repeatedly frames a scoped problem, searches heterogeneous routes, absorbs source-grounded contributions, reconstructs a global portrait, detects residuals, diagnoses responsibility, performs scoped reframing, reopens dependent closure, and recurses. In the Shadow architecture, each atomic problem-solving step is additionally represented as a recursively auditable mechanic cell whose missing observability, mathematical, metric, handoff, failure, storage, verification, engineering, parent-domain and saturation coordinates generate explicit machine-readable research questions. Failed executions are preserved as immutable episodes and may support candidate cross-variation failure patterns, but recurrence alone cannot promote a reusable method lesson. Finalization is governed by bounded saturation rather than a claim of open-world completeness. This manuscript specifies the architecture; it does not establish empirical superiority of ORION over alternative research workflows.
\end{abstract}
