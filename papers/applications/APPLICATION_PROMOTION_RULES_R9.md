# Application Promotion Rules R9

**Date:** 2026-08-26

These rules determine how an application moves from mathematical motivation into manuscript evidence. They are fail-closed and apply to every paper in the five-paper portfolio.

# 1. Promotion levels

## Level A0 — mathematical interpretation

Permitted language:

- “can be interpreted as”;
- “suggests a possible use in”;
- “defines a certificate for”;
- “motivates the following experiment.”

Required evidence: an exact theorem-to-system map with no hidden change of semantics.

Not permitted: performance, usefulness, deployment, prevalence, safety, or transfer claims.

## Level A1 — internal executable application

Required:

- deterministic implementation;
- positive and hostile negative controls;
- content-bound inputs and outputs;
- exact replay in a clean environment;
- explicit authority ceiling.

Permitted language: “implemented and internally validated on the declared panel.”

Not permitted: external validity, production impact, independent replication, or broad domain language.

## Level A2 — finite exact application

Required:

- complete finite instance domain;
- exact target or policy semantics;
- independent target/checker formulation where feasible;
- every collision, failure, or exceptional case retained;
- complete manifests and endpoint witnesses.

Permitted language: “exact for the declared finite domain.”

Not permitted: all-size sufficiency, production prevalence, or average-case utility.

## Level A3 — public benchmark evidence

Required:

- frozen public or production-derived corpus before aggregate outcomes;
- source revision, license, task IDs, exclusions, and redistribution rules;
- strongest runnable comparators under matched budgets;
- predeclared estimands and gates;
- raw outputs and failure ledger;
- inference unit at the task/family/domain level, not generated rows;
- null and harmful effects preserved.

Permitted language: “demonstrated on the named public benchmark under the registered protocol.”

Not permitted: independent custody or production deployment unless separately established.

## Level A4 — domain-reviewed operational evidence

Required:

- domain or policy maintained outside ORION;
- at least two independent encoders/adjudicators where semantic judgment is material;
- disagreement and cannot-encode cases reported;
- blinded or otherwise protected evaluation where feasible;
- one consequential target/action error or a strong null equivalence result;
- no silent forcing of richer semantics into a weaker model.

Permitted language: “prevented/detected the named reviewed error in the declared domain.”

Not permitted: universal legal, scientific, or governance correctness.

## Level A5 — independent transfer

Required:

- structurally independent implementation or external replay;
- at least two non-isomorphic external domains;
- frozen matched-cost controls;
- consistent direction or a precisely characterized boundary;
- no benchmark/task reuse that double-counts one external result across papers.

Permitted language: “transfer supported across the declared domains.”

Not permitted: universal applicability.

## Level A6 — standalone top-tier application candidate

All of the following are required:

1. a unique application contribution not reducible to the mathematical theorem alone;
2. clear subtraction from the closest current systems and theory;
3. an external or production-derived discriminator;
4. matched strong baselines;
5. exact or statistically valid evaluation with negative results retained;
6. reproducible software and content-bound receipts;
7. one independent specialist review appropriate to the domain;
8. a result broad enough for the intended venue but no broader than the evidence.

A6 means “defensible candidate for top-tier review,” not acceptance.

# 2. Paper-specific promotion gates

## Q1

- A0: map support coordinates to a proposed compiler resource.
- A1/A2: internal compiler realization with exact equivalence.
- A3: maintained external stack and public workloads.
- A5/A6: production-faithful move registry, material resource effect, external specialist review.

Hard blocker: abstraction or move-registry incompleteness.

## Integrated A+B

- A0: proof-carrying exact-search architecture.
- A1/A2: XOR and finite interaction controls.
- A3: one external exact optimizer with complete move registry.
- A5/A6: material search effect in at least two domains and a realized certificate-versus-production distinction that survives hostile review.

Hard blocker: abstract terminal numbers written as intrinsic production complexity.

## Paper C / FiberGuard

- A0: exact representation-audit gate.
- A2: complete finite collision atlas and exact repairs.
- A3: architecture-bound learned-optimizer representation plus public/production-derived corpus.
- A4/A5: matched-cost enrich/abstain/route benefit and independent replay.
- A6: unique exact-fibre/cost-aware pipeline contribution beyond generic expressivity and selective prediction.

Hard blocker: using a hand summary while claiming to audit the actual model representation.

## Paper D

- A0: typed authority for agent/evidence workflows.
- A1/A2: executable positive-rule system and hostile finite merge audit.
- A3: public external policy corpus.
- A4: independent policy adjudication and a reviewed error or strong null result.
- A5/A6: transfer across distinct policy/evidence domains and comparison with lineage/provenance/action-certificate systems.

Hard blocker: same-author policy or a domain needing negation/defaults/probability treated as positive Horn.

## Nonquantum paper

- A0: disjoint binary-kernel interpretation.
- A1/A2: proof-producing solver benchmark on exact threshold controls.
- A3: public solver benchmark package and transfer parameters.
- A5/A6: independent replay and a reproducible search-to-proof contribution beyond one exact constant computation.

Hard blocker: benchmark results used to self-certify mathematical constants or novelty.

# 3. Quantitative reporting rules

Every performance statement must report:

- denominator and inference unit;
- exact task/domain count;
- comparator and budget matching;
- central estimate and uncertainty where sampling is involved;
- worst-domain and catastrophic-failure outcomes;
- compute, memory, tool/model calls, and feature/certificate cost;
- invalid/abstained/cannot-check cases;
- the registered terminal.

A mean improvement cannot hide a domain regression, false authorization, invalid cap, or exact collision failure.

# 4. Negative and null results

The following are publishable boundaries when honestly frozen:

- production moves collapse an abstract certificate gap;
- a sound cap has no material search value;
- exact representation collisions exist but are rare in the public corpus;
- refinement cost erases FiberGuard’s operational benefit;
- lineage-only filtering matches typed authority on the chosen domain;
- a policy requires richer semantics;
- AI-proposed cuts add no value or are frequently unsound;
- the production resource does not correspond to the theorem support object.

Negative terminals must update the manuscript, claim ledger, application atlas, and venue plan. They may not be hidden by switching domains or metrics after inspection.

# 5. Manuscript phrasing templates

## A0/A1

“Within the declared formal model, the theorem yields a machine-checkable certificate that can serve as the basis of [system]. We provide an internal implementation on [panel]; external utility remains untested.”

## A2

“On the complete finite domain [X], the registered application statement holds exactly. This does not establish prevalence or transfer beyond [X].”

## A3

“On the frozen public benchmark [X], under matched [budget], the method [result]. The evidence is benchmark-specific and remains under same-owner execution unless otherwise stated.”

## A4/A5

“Under independently maintained [policy/domain] and the registered adjudication protocol, the method [prevented/detected/failed to improve] [target]. The result transfers to [named domains] and is bounded by [limitations].”

# 6. Standalone application-paper stop/go rule

The cross-paper certificate control plane may be submitted separately only when:

- at least two component application lanes reach A4 or A5;
- the integrated system has a unique endpoint;
- full-system versus component-ablation gains survive matched budgets;
- the result is not merely a packaging of already reported component experiments;
- current-source review finds a residual systems contribution;
- the application family has its own independent title, abstract, claim ledger, and task partition.

Otherwise, application results remain sections or companion artifacts of the five mathematical papers.
