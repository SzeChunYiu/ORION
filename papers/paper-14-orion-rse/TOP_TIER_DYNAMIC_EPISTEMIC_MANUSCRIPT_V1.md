## Abstract

The value of scientific governance is causal, not rhetorical. P14 treats governance as an intervention on acquisition, diagnosis, experiment selection, reopening, promotion, and stopping decisions over a dynamic epistemic state. It absorbs AI-for-science benchmarks, sequential testing, online error control, metascience, safety cases, and research evaluation. We prove governance-identifiability conditions, false-promotion control under declared assumptions, positive value of retained negative history when it changes later decisions, specification separation, and the need for prospective unseen incidents. A blinded externally held trial will compare confidence/provenance/always-act baselines, generic research agents, Discovery V1, and dynamic ORION.

## Theory

- `P14-T1`: policy value is identifiable only with independent tasks, gold, thresholds, and adjudication.
- `P14-T2`: non-compensatory dynamic-state policies can bound false promotion under declared assumptions.
- `P14-T3`: retained negatives have value when they improve held-out proposal or reopening decisions.
- `P14-T4`: a policy cannot score against a gold function that reuses its own specification.
- `P14-T5`: retrospective development incidents cannot establish controller superiority.
