Dear Editors of Artificial Intelligence,

Please consider the regular paper **“Selective Revalidation Without Authority Amplification Under Change.”**

The paper addresses a formal AI problem that arises when systems preserve certified state across changes while also carrying authorization, provenance and obligations. The surrounding mechanisms are deliberately credited to their parent literatures: truth maintenance and incremental recomputation, provenance, authorization and delegation logics, proof-carrying action, typed effects and capabilities, and assurance cases. The manuscript asks what remains when exact-change selective revalidation is composed with a non-amplifying authority boundary.

The bounded contribution is threefold. First, under support-sound dependencies, the affected certified set can be safely reopened while exact-change proofs preserve only claims whose validity is established for the declared change; directly changed certified roots require revalidation. Second, continuing authority is confined to its bound domain and epoch, so a repair with no fresh authority-bearing premise cannot increase authorization. Third, an information-equivalent typed donor receiving the same scientific coordinates is required to tie exactly, preventing an apparent advantage from being manufactured by hidden information asymmetry.

An implementation-independent finite checker reconstructs the primitive model without importing ORION transition functions or expected outputs. It covers 64 ordered four-node DAGs, 8,640 selective-revalidation states, 96 authority states and 48 information-equivalent donor states, with hostile countermodels for dependency omission, changed-root self-preservation, epoch/domain laundering and donor asymmetry. These checks are formal regression and countermodel evidence, not a claim of external empirical superiority.

A submission-date literature refresh further narrowed the novelty claim. Recent work already owns certificate-bearing action governance, scoped authority attenuation, revocation, temporal/non-expansive authority propagation and provenance-sensitive authorization. The paper therefore makes no first-of-kind claim for those mechanisms or for selective invalidation by itself. The surviving claim is the bounded composition and its exact donor-control boundary.

The proposed externally adjudicated authority/discharge study is explicitly successor science. This submission does not claim externally demonstrated reductions in false scientific promotion, real-world recheck savings or cross-domain empirical transfer.
