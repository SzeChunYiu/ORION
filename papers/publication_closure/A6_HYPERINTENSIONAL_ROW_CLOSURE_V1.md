## The load-bearing finding: one paper composes both halves

V5's row treated the two behaviors as coming from two literatures. The verification
found a single primary that asserts both in one abstract. Berto (2019), verbatim:

> (Revised) belief states are not closed under classical logical consequence; revising
> by inconsistent information does not perforce lead to trivialization; revision can be
> subject to "framing effects": logically or necessarily equivalent contents can lead
> to different revisions

— achieved "without resorting to non-classical logics" (S5 plus a mereology of
contents), with the book-length systematization in Berto, *Topics of Thought* (OUP
2022), ch. 6.

**Consequence for ORION-16:** any §2 claim to the *combined* behavior — tolerate
inconsistency without collapse AND revise differently on equivalent contents — is a
**specialization of Berto 2019**, not a union of two literatures, and must be
positioned as such. The claim gets narrower, not wider, when the row is closed.

**The one genuinely open-looking intersection** (recorded, not claimed): Berto reaches
inconsistency-tolerance *without* paraconsistent logic, and no paraconsistent-side work
checked here (Priest, Tanaka, Testa et al., Schwind et al.) claims
presentation-sensitivity — so a specifically *paraconsistency-based* hyperintensional
revision operator was not found. Scope: exact-phrase and combined-framework searches
plus the abstracts of every Half-A item. Each half separately, and Berto's
non-paraconsistent route to both, are all donor; the intersection is at most a residue
for a bounded paper to claim narrowly, never a top-tier frame.
