TARE block encoding exposes auxiliary anticommuting frames and non-unique Tag choices, but those donor-owned freedoms do not determine how much frame support an exact optimum can intrinsically require. We study a frozen three-block shared-Tag TARE-M2 grammar under a declared support-count objective. Exact counterexamples first show that natural weight-one joint families are incomplete: a split-anchor construction improves cost 9 to 8, and a frame-for-Tag borrow improves 6 to 5. The central all-`n` exchange theorem then proves that every admitted instance has an exact optimum with frame support at most two, while later counterexamples show that no small named support-two taxonomy should be promoted to a universal closed form. A prospectively frozen executable follow-up exploits this theorem to generate legal ordered support-two anticommuting pairs directly and validates a direct exact solver with `O(n^9)` search time after `O(n)` preprocessing for the frozen six-slot grammar. This algorithmic claim is independently checked on finite pair counts and exact panels, but it is not a production-runtime, hardware-resource, or generic TARE complexity claim. The result is a sharp expressivity and exact-algorithm study over donor-exposed compilation choices under one explicitly bounded objective.
