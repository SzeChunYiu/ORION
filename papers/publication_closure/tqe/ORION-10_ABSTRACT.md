Static quantum-cost analysis and resource estimation are established; this paper asks what authority a compiler forecast retains after an exact counterexample. In a frozen shared-Tag TARE grammar, an initial static forecaster evaluates three explicit feasible subfamilies without calling the unrestricted exact dynamic-programming referee. Across 9,546 registered DP-compared instances it agrees on 9,545 but fails on one prospectively generated `n=3` case, where the exact optimum is 10 and all three closed-form terms return 11. That single mismatch refutes universal closed-form exactness and is not diluted into an accuracy headline. The failure instead separates authority layers. The feasible construction still certifies the upper bound `10<=11`, an independent all-`n` theorem still places the exact optimum inside the full support-two family, and the exact witness localizes the missing mechanism. A separately frozen successor admits that mechanism, while the theorem-backed forecaster `F2` computes the exact support-two family minimum without the unrestricted DP. Later counterexamples refine the smaller taxonomy without weakening `F2`. We encode these distinctions in a ForecastCertificate reporting schema. The contribution is a bounded refutation-localization-repair study of feasible bounds, theorem-backed exactness, finite hypotheses, and forecast-only rows, not a new general quantum resource-estimation formalism.
