## Proposed title

## One-sentence result

A closed research programme can make negative, donor-absorbed, saturated, refuted, repaired and theorem-level terminals explicit and replayable, and the ORION-Q case study shows a complete sequence from repeated negative results to a sharp later theorem without rewriting earlier failures into successes.

## Significance statement

Autonomous-research systems are usually judged by positive end results, which makes negative paths easy to discard or post-hoc reinterpret. ORION-02 studies the transition layer after a hypothesis fails. The ORION-Q case provides a complete receipted trajectory in which strong donors absorb multiple apparent innovations, proxy wins disappear under stronger accounting, explicit counterexamples break successive closure hypotheses, and the resulting residual is eventually converted into a prospectively tested all-size theorem. The contribution is the executable recovery discipline and its full case-study record—not provenance by itself and not a claim of cross-domain superiority.

## Draft editor pitch

We present a complete, replayable case study of recursive negative-result recovery in a quantum-computing research programme. The programme treats negative, absorbed, mixed, saturated and cannot-check outcomes as first-class terminals, freezes successors prospectively, and gives the strongest known donor methods first right of refusal. This discipline prevents several initially promising method variants from being promoted as novelty. More importantly, the negative sequence becomes productive: explanatory analysis exposes a declared coupling gap, two successive closure conjectures are refuted by exact counterexamples, support-two finite closure is restored, a structural forecast is staged before ground truth, and the final residual is closed by an all-size theorem. We claim this as a methodology definition and complete case study, not as evidence that the process is universally superior across scientific domains.
