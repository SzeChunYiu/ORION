# From Negative Results to Theorem: A Receipted Recovery Protocol for AI-Assisted Science

**Recursively refined V3 — 2026-08-22**  
**Stretch target after broader validation:** Nature Computational Science  
**Current target:** npj Artificial Intelligence / comparable AI-for-science methods venue

## Abstract

AI-assisted research systems can generate hypotheses and execute experiments, but scientific value is not measured by the number of positive outputs. A research programme also needs explicit behavior after a candidate is negative, already owned by a stronger known method, saturated within a declared search class, or not yet checkable. We present a **negative-result recovery protocol** in which each research question is frozen before its result, receives a typed terminal, and may generate a new successor only after that terminal is recorded. Strong known methods receive first right of refusal, refuted hypotheses remain in the evidence graph, and saturation/lower-bound outcomes are valid stopping states rather than prompts to weaken the claim.

We document the protocol through the complete ORION-Q quantum-computing programme. The programme repeatedly records donor absorptions and negative resource outcomes rather than promoting them as discoveries. In its final compilation sequence, an exact donor saturation motivates an explanatory question; the first closure conjecture is refuted by a shared-Tag coupling mechanism; the repaired family is refuted by a converse frame-for-Tag trade; a support-two family then closes all registered finite instances; a structural prediction is frozen and confirmed on a previously unread public Hamiltonian; and a final analytic theorem proves the resulting support-two normal form for arbitrary system size. The parent failures remain unchanged throughout this sequence.

The contribution is the **recovery contract and its auditable case study**, not provenance itself and not a causal claim that the protocol outperforms ordinary science. The current evidence is one principal programme and therefore cannot establish cross-domain productivity superiority. Instead, it shows how negative, absorbed and saturated results can remain first-class scientific state while supporting a controlled transition from failed candidate methods to a different, sharper theorem. A separately frozen cross-domain protocol specifies the comparative study required for a broader claim.

## 1. The missing transition after a negative result

Research automation is increasingly capable of proposing ideas, implementing tests and producing polished reports. Provenance is therefore becoming a core requirement: without a replayable record of what was reasoned, executed and measured, later correction is difficult. Provenance, however, answers **what happened**. It does not decide **what research move is licensed next**.

A failed candidate can mean several different things.

- A stronger known method may already express the same mechanism.
- The benchmark class may have reached a structural ceiling.
- A proxy gain may disappear once a missing resource is charged.
- A negative may be valid only under a particular representation or information contract.
- A closure conjecture may fail at a small, identifiable mechanism.
- Available evidence may simply be insufficient for a verdict.

Collapsing these states into “try again” encourages post-outcome claim mutation. Collapsing them into “failure” discards information that can determine a more precise successor.

We therefore treat recovery as a typed transition system over research claims. The unit is not an endlessly edited hypothesis. It is a **claim episode** with a frozen question, donor/comparator, test, terminal and—only after that terminal—zero or more registered successors.

The resulting object is a research DAG whose negative nodes remain visible.

## 2. Recovery contract

### 2.1 Freeze the question before the answer

Each result-bearing lane fixes, before execution:

- the scientific question;
- admissible subject/data access;
- candidate and strongest registered donor;
- resource/accounting definition;
- validity and hostile controls;
- positive/refutation gates;
- stopping conditions;
- permitted terminal vocabulary.

A later result may motivate a new question. It does not retroactively alter the gate that judged the old one.

### 2.2 Give known work first right of refusal

A candidate receives incremental credit only after comparison with the strongest faithfully composed known mechanism under the same information and resource contract. When that donor ties or wins, the terminal is **ABSORBED**, not a weakened novelty claim.

This principle separates two questions that are often conflated:

1. did the mechanism help relative to a weak baseline?;
2. did anything remain after the strongest relevant existing method received the same opportunity?

Only the second question bears on incremental contribution.

### 2.3 Use terminals that preserve scientific meaning

The case study uses the following operational classes.

| Terminal | Operational meaning | Licensed next move |
|---|---|---|
| `POSITIVE` | frozen candidate gate passes against registered donor | validate/generalize without widening the parent claim |
| `NEGATIVE` | candidate fails while validity controls hold | diagnose the missing mechanism or stop |
| `ABSORBED` | donor explains/matches candidate value | subtract novelty; ask only a residual question if one exists |
| `MIXED` | value survives only in a declared regime | characterize the regime boundary |
| `SATURATED` | all registered successors in a declared class leave no residual | stop or change the scientific question/class |
| `LOWER_BOUND_CLOSED` | structural bound explains the ceiling | stop inside that class |
| `CANNOT_CHECK` | instrument/evidence cannot support a verdict | obtain missing evidence; never treat as negative or positive |

### 2.4 Define productive recovery

For this paper, a **productive recovery** requires all of the following:

1. a parent episode reaches `NEGATIVE`, `ABSORBED`, `MIXED`, `SATURATED` or `CANNOT_CHECK`;
2. the parent terminal identifies a specific unresolved obligation rather than merely low performance;
3. a successor question is registered after the parent terminal and changes that unresolved obligation without changing the parent's result;
4. the successor eventually produces a materially different validated object—such as an explanation, counterexample, boundary, lower bound, prospective prediction or theorem.

A later positive found by unconstrained retry does not satisfy this definition.

### 2.5 Receipts are infrastructure, not truth

Each protocol/result is content-addressed and replayable where computation is practical. This makes chronology and claim mutation auditable. Receipt integrity does not establish that the world, result or novelty judgment is correct; exact referees, independent implementations, external data and literature comparison remain separate evidence layers.

## 3. A complete recovery trace in ORION-Q

Rather than narrating every ORION-Q lane chronologically, we use one sequence to show each recovery operation.

### 3.1 Saturation changes the question from optimization to explanation

Exact successor grammars R6I, R6K and R6M repeatedly collapse onto registered donor envelopes on the open chemistry subjects. At this point the natural terminal is not “we need a more complicated optimizer.” The registered evidence says that the current optimization lane has no residual on its admitted subjects.

The successor therefore asks a different question:

> **Why does unrestricted exact optimization keep collapsing onto low-support donors?**

### 3.2 Explanation can succeed locally while the closure conjecture fails

R6N freezes a support-dominance conjecture together with an explicit unresolved shared-Tag repair obligation. The local support inequality survives 688,041,472 complete configurations with zero violation, explaining why spread support is usually expensive.

The proposed global donor closure nevertheless fails exactly at the declared gap. An exact instance uses different weight-one anchors and a weight-two shared Tag to achieve

`C_DP = 8 < 9 = C_R6L`.

The correct terminal is therefore **refutation plus identified mechanism**, not theorem.

### 3.3 Repair can expose the converse mechanism

R6O enlarges the donor to permit arbitrary support-one anchors and the cheapest compatible Tag. That family repairs the first counterexample but is itself refuted. A second exact mechanism spends support two on a cheap central frame branch to reduce Tag/Restore cost, with smallest witness

`C_DP = 5 < 6 = C_D+`.

The two failures reveal complementary coupling currencies rather than a sequence of increasingly permissive ad hoc models.

### 3.4 Finite closure is not promoted to an all-size theorem

R6P admits frame support at most two. It matches the unrestricted optimum on every registered finite domain and re-closes all 559 instances that violated the support-one family.

The protocol nevertheless records the all-size composition theorem as open. The finite result is terminal at exactly its earned scope.

### 3.5 Prediction is frozen before exact computation

R6Q derives a finite-domain structural predictor. R6R then selects a previously unread public benzene DUCC2 Hamiltonian by a rule fixed before coefficient access, commits the predicted regime before running the unrestricted referee, and confirms the prediction on all 15 matchings.

This supplies a prospective check that is distinct from fitting the earlier panel.

### 3.6 The final successor is a theorem, not a better candidate method

R6S returns to the precise open obligation: can support three or larger ever be necessary? An analytic `F_2^2` exchange proves that it cannot. Together with the exact support-one counterexample, the final scientific object is the sharp normal-form theorem

`kappa_R6M = 2`.

The sequence is therefore:

`donor saturation -> explanatory conjecture -> exact refutation -> repaired conjecture -> second exact refutation -> finite closure -> prospective prediction -> all-n theorem`.

Every failed parent remains failed. The scientific progress lies in changing the question under controlled evidence, not in retroactively relabeling the trajectory as a successful search for the theorem.

## 4. Other recovery outcomes in the closed programme

The same discipline also produces useful terminals that do **not** end in a theorem.

### Proxy gain removed by stronger resource accounting

Grouped sparse-QSVT is favorable under a low-overhead proxy, but realistic grouped-unitary synthesis erases that gain. The surviving result is a resource-model boundary, not a claimed compiler advantage.

### Hardware projection reverses the ranking

On N2, an internal representation coordinate improves while different outer compilation projections disagree on which method wins. The programme retains a Pareto/resource-dependent terminal rather than choosing the favorable scalarization.

### Strong donors absorb specialized policies

Several N-lane candidates outperform weak controls but are matched by ideal value-of-information planning, canonical transforms, library learning or stronger model selection once the donor receives the same state. These are useful negative controls for the methodology: “state matters” and “our policy is new” are treated as separate claims.

### Partial knowledge can reopen a full-knowledge negative

Other N-lane experiments show that an earlier negative can cease to license closure after the information or representation contract changes. The recovery rule therefore binds a negative to its applicability scope rather than treating it as permanent universal evidence.

## 5. What this case study establishes—and what it does not

### Observable properties established by the record

- Failed and absorbed hypotheses remain recoverable as distinct committed terminals.
- Successor protocols are separable from their parent claims in time and content.
- Strong donors repeatedly remove apparent incremental claims.
- The R6 recovery DAG contains two exact refutations before its final normal-form theorem.
- The programme can stop at negative, mixed and resource-dependent terminals rather than maximizing positive findings.

### Claims not established

The present study does **not** show that this protocol causally reduces false novelty, increases research productivity, outperforms human researchers, or generalizes across scientific domains. Those are comparative empirical hypotheses. A frozen successor protocol specifies matched RECOVERY, NAIVE_ITERATION and DONOR_STOP workflows across additional domains; no outcome from that study is reported here.

This distinction is important. A single deep case study can define and demonstrate a research-control mechanism. It cannot supply the counterfactual needed for a universal productivity claim.

## 6. Relation to neighboring work

### Provenance and experiment tracking

Complete provenance, versioning and replay are increasingly recognized as foundations for trustworthy autonomous science. ORION-02 uses those capabilities as prerequisites. Its residual object is the **transition policy after a terminal**, not the invention of provenance.

### Preregistration and registered reports

Pre-outcome freezing is conceptually aligned with preregistration: outcome-dependent rewriting weakens evidentiary interpretation. ORION-02 applies freezing at the granularity of iterative research successors and preserves parent terminals rather than claiming to introduce preregistration.

### Agent self-reflection and iterative improvement

Agent systems already critique and revise candidate outputs. ORION-02 differs in what receives authority: a revision cannot overwrite the evidence status of the parent research claim, and a strong donor can terminate a novelty direction even when an agent can continue generating alternatives.

### Negative-result publication and falsification

Science has long recognized the value of negative evidence. The present contribution is an executable state machine for **using** negative evidence inside an iterative research programme while preserving its original scope.

## 8. Limitations and next experiment

The main limitation is also the next empirical question: one programme cannot establish that recovery improves research trajectory quality in general. The preregistered successor study therefore requires materially different scientific programmes and matched workflows, measuring false-novelty rate, terminal calibration, productive-recovery rate, claim mutation and evidence cost.

A negative result in that study is valid. If ordinary iteration or donor-only stopping performs equally well, the additional recovery machinery is absorbed for that setting.
