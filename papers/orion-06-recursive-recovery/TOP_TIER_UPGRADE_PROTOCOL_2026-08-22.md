# ORION-02 top-tier upgrade protocol — cross-domain negative-result recovery

**Freeze date:** 2026-08-22
**Status:** prospective research protocol. The completed ORION-Q programme is one fixed case study; no cross-domain outcome is claimed yet.

## 1. Scientific question

Does an explicit negative-result recovery discipline improve the quality/calibration of scientific research trajectories relative to ordinary retry/agent-iteration baselines, **without increasing false novelty**, across materially different research domains?

The discipline under test consists of:

1. prospectively frozen questions/gates;
2. donor first right of refusal;
3. typed terminals (`POSITIVE`, `NEGATIVE`, `ABSORBED`, `MIXED`, `SATURATED`, `LOWER_BOUND_CLOSED`, `CANNOT_CHECK`);
4. successor registration after the terminal rather than post-outcome mutation of the parent claim;
5. explicit stop rules/lower-bound closure;
6. preserved/replayable evidence history.

Provenance/receipts are infrastructure, not the primary treatment.

## 2. Domains

The study requires at least three materially different programmes:

- **Domain A:** completed ORION-Q quantum-computing programme (fixed historical case, not randomized);
- **Domain B:** a prospectively launched non-quantum mathematical/formal/algorithmic research programme;
- **Domain C:** a prospectively launched computational/empirical science programme with externally checkable outputs where feasible.

For headline generalization, Domains B/C must be admitted after this protocol freeze and cannot be selected because their known outcomes favor recovery.

## 3. Unit of analysis

A unit is a **research claim episode**:

- a frozen scientific claim/hypothesis;
- the strongest registered donor/baseline;
- a result-bearing test;
- terminal disposition;
- zero or more successor episodes registered only after the terminal.

The trajectory is a DAG of episodes, not a mutable notebook claim.

## 4. Comparison workflows

Where resource constraints allow, each prospectively launched programme should run or simulate matched parallel workflows from the same initial evidence state.

### RECOVERY

Full ORION-style recovery contract.

### NAIVE_ITERATION

After a failed claim, the agent/researcher may revise/retry without a typed terminal or frozen successor boundary, under the same overall search/tool budget. Every revision must still be logged for audit.

### DONOR_STOP

Strongest donor receives first right of refusal; once the candidate is absorbed/refuted, the lane stops rather than recursively reopening explanatory/residual questions.

### RECOVERY_ABLATION

Where feasible, preserve provenance/frozen tests but remove one load-bearing recovery mechanism, e.g. typed responsibility/scope or explicit saturation stop rule.

No workflow may receive more external scientific information than another; compute/tool-use differences must be reported.

## 5. Primary outcomes

Do **not** use “number of positive findings” as the main metric.

Primary measures:

1. **false-novelty rate:** fraction of claimed incremental contributions later shown donor-equivalent/known/prior-art-owned;
2. **terminal calibration:** fraction of `SATURATED`, `LOWER_BOUND_CLOSED`, `CANNOT_CHECK` and strong-positive terminals supported by later independent evidence/review;
3. **productive recovery rate:** negative/absorbed episodes that lead to a later materially different validated theorem/positive/explanation rather than cosmetic claim narrowing;
4. **claim mutation rate:** post-outcome changes that would have altered the parent gate/claim if not frozen;
5. **evidence cost to validated terminal:** tool/compute/human-review cost;
6. **scientific terminal quality:** preregistered ordinal rubric scored blind by independent domain reviewers where possible (e.g. donor replication < bounded mechanism < exact domain result < theorem/generalization).

Secondary:

- time to terminal;
- number/depth of successor episodes;
- proportion of negative results preserved/published in the final record;
- rate of repeated rediscovery of the same donor mechanism.

## 6. Independent novelty adjudication

For any workflow output presented as novel:

1. freeze exact claim before novelty search;
2. run a hostile multi-route literature search by an adjudicator not involved in generating the claim where feasible;
3. label `NOT_FOUND_IN_SEARCH`, `ADJACENT`, `DONOR_OWNED`, or `DIRECT_PRIOR` rather than binary novelty where evidence is ambiguous;
4. preserve killed novelty claims in the dataset.

ORION-Q's own internal novelty authority remains false; the study measures process behavior, not self-certified novelty.

## 7. Independent scientific scoring

Whenever feasible, final/scientific terminals should be scored by at least one route not generated by the workflow itself:

- formal proof checker;
- exact independent implementation;
- external public data/experiment;
- blind expert review;
- later published result.

If no independent score can be obtained, record `UNRESOLVED` rather than treating the workflow's receipt as ground truth.

## 8. Adversarial controls

Include episodes intentionally designed so that:

- a plausible candidate is exactly donor-owned;
- an attractive proxy gain disappears under a stronger resource model;
- a negative is truly structural and should terminate;
- an initial negative is benchmark-limited and should reopen under a changed epistemic/representation contract;
- evidence is insufficient and `CANNOT_CHECK` is the only honest terminal;
- a later prior-art search kills an apparent theorem/method novelty.

The recovery workflow should not be rewarded for always reopening; correct stopping is part of the target.

## 9. Statistical structure

The programme is not naturally iid. Report results hierarchically:

- episode-level;
- trajectory/programme-level;
- domain-level.

Use paired comparisons where workflows share the same initial state. Treat the completed ORION-Q case as a fixed demonstration rather than pretending it is a randomized sample.

For prospective B/C programmes, predeclare tool/compute budgets and primary tests before outcomes.

## 10. Stop rules

- If RECOVERY lowers false novelty but increases cost without improving terminal quality, report the tradeoff rather than a victory.
- If DONOR_STOP performs equally on terminal quality, the recursive recovery layer is donor-absorbed for that setting.
- If NAIVE_ITERATION produces equally good science with no greater claim mutation/false novelty, the proposed recovery discipline is not supported.
- If cross-domain transfer fails, retain ORION-02 as a complete quantum case study.

## 11. Top-tier success criterion

A compelling general result would show, across genuinely different scientific programmes, that explicit negative-result recovery **changes research trajectory quality in a reproducible way**—for example by substantially reducing false novelty while converting a nontrivial fraction of strong negatives into independently validated explanations/theorems, without simply reopening indefinitely or spending unbounded resources.
