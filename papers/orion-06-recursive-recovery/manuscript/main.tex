\begin{abstract}
Negative results in computational research are often recorded only as prose, discarded after a method changes, or reopened without an explicit rule for what changed. We study an executable alternative in the ORION-Q programme: prospectively freeze the question, comparison set, gates, and honest terminals; give the strongest admissible donor method first right of refusal; retain nulls, refutations, and donor absorptions as first-class records; type the failure and authority state that permits reopening; and bind result-bearing computations to replayable receipts. A complementary claim-preserving recovery theorem formalizes a necessary condition for same-identity repair: every failed load-bearing predicate must have at least one admissible repair action on a declared causal ancestor. The same formalism yields no-repair certificates when an uncovered failed predicate exists, a minimum weighted causal-coverage lower bound on repair cost, and safe pruning of dominated actions; changing the question, population, estimand, primary metric, threshold, protected corpus or terminal semantics is classified as a new claim identity rather than a favorable repair of the failed one. The contribution is methodological rather than a new quantum algorithm. Across one end-to-end programme, the discipline produces donor closures, exact refutations, bounded positives, protected-data restraint and honest unresolved states. Independent replay of the registered headline generators reproduces their committed outputs under a two-sided harness that also detects perturbation and missing generators. These observations separate properties that are often conflated: a result can be reproducible without being scientifically valid, scientifically negative without being an execution failure, and methodologically informative without being a novel method. The evidence remains a one-programme case study, not a statistical evaluation of research-methodology effectiveness. A fresh literature closure remains a submission gate for any novelty statement about the methodology itself.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} negative results; research provenance; reproducibility; autonomous research; prospective protocols; donor comparison.
