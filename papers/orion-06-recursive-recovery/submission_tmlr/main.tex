\begin{abstract}
Negative results in computational research are often recorded only as prose, discarded after a method changes, or reopened without an explicit rule for what changed. We study an executable alternative: prospectively freeze the question, comparison set, gates, and honest terminals; give the strongest admissible donor method first right of refusal; retain nulls, refutations, and donor absorptions as first-class records; type the failure and authority state that permits reopening; and bind result-bearing computations to replayable receipts. Across one end-to-end programme, the discipline produces donor closures, exact refutations, bounded positives, protected-data stops, and cannot-check outcomes without rewriting earlier terminals. The evidence is a one-programme case study, not a statistical evaluation of research-methodology effectiveness. Submission-date literature subtraction narrows the positioning residual to the prospective typed-terminal/authority and receipt-binding contract; no exhaustive priority claim is made.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} negative results; research provenance; reproducibility; autonomous research; prospective protocols; donor comparison.
