# Venue decision — ORION-06

**Target: TMLR.** Artificial Intelligence (AIJ) was assessed and declined.

AIJ was the assigned primary. It is not defensible here, for reasons the paper
states about itself:

- The evidence is a single end-to-end programme. The manuscript says plainly
  that this is "a one-programme case study, not a statistical evaluation of
  research-methodology effectiveness."
- The paper's own revival audit records the cross-domain general-method
  question as undetermined: no prospectively admitted second programme with
  matched workflow budgets and independent scoring exists under the frozen
  protocol. Generality is therefore an open question, not a result.
- The central claim is explicitly descriptive: the workflow was instantiated
  and behaved in a non-monotone, outcome-sensitive way under prospectively
  frozen rules. There is no estimate of how often it improves anything.

AIJ selects for substantial, general AI contributions. Submitting a descriptive
single-case methodology paper there invites a desk rejection on scope, and the
only way to make the AIJ case would be to claim a generality the evidence does
not support.

TMLR is the right fit. Its criterion is whether the claims are supported by
convincing evidence, which is exactly the shape of this paper: a bounded
descriptive claim with a complete receipt trail behind it, including the
retained nulls and refutations.

**Article type.** Regular submission, 6 pages.

**Route back to AIJ.** If a second, content-disjoint programme is ever run
under the same frozen protocol with independent scoring, the cross-domain
question becomes answerable and an AIJ-scope paper becomes possible. That is a
new experiment, not a rewrite of this one.
