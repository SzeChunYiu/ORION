# Cover letter — ORION-06

To the Editors, Transactions on Machine Learning Research

**Manuscript:** Receipted Recursive Recovery of Negative Quantum-Method Results

Dear Editors,

We submit the manuscript above for consideration in TMLR.

Negative results in computational research are usually recorded as prose,
discarded when a method changes, or quietly reopened with no rule for what
changed. This paper studies an executable alternative and reports, in detail,
what happened when it was actually run.

The discipline has five constraints. Outcome rules are frozen before results
can be seen. The strongest admissible existing method gets first right of
refusal. Exact claims carry witnesses or independently checkable referees.
Failure and authority are typed, so a capability result cannot promote itself
into a scientific conclusion. And the record is replayable enough that a later
reader can reconstruct what was known, what was attempted, and why a line of
work stopped.

We instantiated this end-to-end across one computational research programme and
report the outcomes it produced: several candidate method families absorbed or
refuted before a bounded structural residual was isolated, recovery studies
containing both closures and exact positives, protected data that stayed sealed
because a frozen gate did not license access, and capability successes that were
not permitted to grant scientific authority. We use those cases to separate
three things that are routinely conflated. A result can be reproducible without
being scientifically valid. It can be negative without being an execution
failure. It can be methodologically informative without being a new method.

We want to be direct about the evidence class. This is one programme. The
manuscript states that it is a case study and not a statistical evaluation of
research-methodology effectiveness, and the paper's own coverage audit records
the cross-domain question as undetermined: no second prospectively admitted
programme with matched budgets and independent scoring exists to compare
against. We do not estimate how often this workflow improves science, and we do
not claim that receipt completeness substitutes for evidentiary quality.

One methodological point we would ask reviewers to weigh. The audit reports its
denominator. Of 51 eligible receipts, 23 enter the transition graph and 28 are
excluded with a stated reason; 10 nodes are negative or partial and 7 stand
alone with no invented successor. A coverage claim that reported only the
included nodes would look identical to a complete one, which is the specific
failure this construction is built to prevent.

We believe TMLR is the right venue because the claim is bounded and descriptive
and the question is whether the evidence supports it, which is TMLR's criterion
rather than a significance judgement.

The manuscript is original and is not under consideration elsewhere.

Thank you for your consideration.
