# D R9 Protocol: Blinded Real-Domain Validation of Typed Authority under Evidence Merge

## 1. Scientific question

On an independently maintained permission or evidence domain, does the frozen typed-authority system prevent operational authorization errors caused by coordinate erasure, evidence retraction, or cross-record proof splicing, without creating an unacceptable false-denial burden?

The protocol is designed to permit a negative answer. A synthetic corpus, a theorem replay, or a domain chosen after observing results cannot answer this question.

## 2. Frozen systems under comparison

### System T — typed authority

The evaluator uses the frozen positive-rule semantics, type coordinates, origin labels, retraction rules, merge rules, and authorized bridges bound before corpus unblinding.

### System U — coordinate-erased baseline

The baseline receives the same case information after the exact preregistered erasure map. It may not be intentionally weakened beyond that erasure. Any additional implementation difference must be separately ablated.

### Optional operational baseline O

A domain-native implementation or reviewer decision may be included as a third comparator. It does not replace the independently adjudicated gold decision.

The implementation teams must not tune rules, bridges, thresholds, or erasure after seeing gold labels.

## 3. Independent expert roles

### Domain curators

At least two reviewers with direct expertise in the selected permission or evidence system construct candidate cases from independently maintained materials. They must not implement System T or System U.

### Encoders

A separate team converts cases into the frozen typed and erased interchange formats while blinded to the gold authorization decision. Encoders may ask factual clarification questions through a logged adjudication channel.

### Evaluator custodians

Separate custodians execute the frozen systems. They receive case identifiers and encoded inputs, not gold labels or curator rationales.

### Gold adjudicators

At least two domain experts independently assign the operationally correct decision, applicable scope, confidence, and rationale. Disagreements are adjudicated by a third expert or preserved as `AMBIGUOUS_GOLD`.

### Statistical auditor

An independent auditor verifies preregistration, exclusions, paired analysis, and terminal selection before author identities are unblinded.

One person may not simultaneously own corpus curation, system encoding, evaluator implementation, and gold adjudication.

## 4. Domain eligibility

A domain is eligible only when all are true:

1. it is independently maintained outside the manuscript implementation;
2. it has explicit authorization, evidence, delegation, revocation, or merge semantics;
3. real or historically grounded cases can be cited and archived lawfully;
4. domain experts can adjudicate operational outcomes;
5. typed coordinates and an exact erasure map can be defined before evaluation;
6. the positive-rule semantics is not assumed correct by construction; and
7. both clean and hostile merge/retraction cases occur naturally or can be generated by a documented mutation protocol.

Ineligible domains include restatements of the synthetic OAuth corpus, cases authored from the theorem examples, and domains whose gold labels are produced by System T itself.

## 5. Case unit and corpus construction

A case contains:

- an immutable case identifier;
- source records and origin identifiers;
- typed facts available before merge or retraction;
- rule and bridge identifiers from the frozen registry;
- the exact operation under test—query, merge, retraction, delegation, or bridge activation;
- the requested authorization decision and scope;
- the domain-native context needed for adjudication;
- the gold decision: `ALLOW`, `DENY`, `ABSTAIN`, or `AMBIGUOUS_GOLD`;
- curator confidence and rationale;
- sensitivity classification;
- provenance and licensing information; and
- mutation ancestry, when derived from another case.

No case is discarded because one system fails. Exclusions are limited to preregistered data-integrity, legal, duplication, or `AMBIGUOUS_GOLD` rules.

## 6. Required strata

The final analyzable corpus must contain all of the following, with counts frozen before unblinding:

1. **clean allows** — valid single-record or authorized-bridge proofs;
2. **clean denies** — insufficient evidence without splicing;
3. **cross-record splice traps** — premises from different records jointly resemble a proof but no authorized bridge exists;
4. **authorized bridge cases** — cross-record reasoning that must remain allowed;
5. **retraction cases** — removal of one evidence item should retract exactly the dependent authority;
6. **stale or replayed evidence** — origin or epoch mismatch;
7. **coordinate collision cases** — erased representations coincide while typed decisions differ;
8. **benign merges** — merge should introduce no new authority;
9. **hybrid-only proofs** — any valid proof necessarily uses more than one source;
10. **out-of-model cases** — negation, defaults, inconsistency, probability, or other semantics the frozen positive system does not represent.

Out-of-model cases test abstention and scope discipline; they are not silently forced into positive Horn rules.

## 7. Mutation protocol

Hostile cases may be produced from independently grounded clean cases by preregistered mutations:

- swap one origin coordinate;
- delete one type coordinate;
- replay a stale record identifier;
- merge premises from two unrelated records;
- remove an authorized bridge;
- insert an unauthorized bridge;
- retract one necessary seed;
- duplicate a fact under a different origin; or
- change the requested scope while preserving the visible payload.

Each mutation must preserve a parent pointer and exact diff. Curators adjudicate the mutated case independently; the expected label is not assigned mechanically from the mutation name.

## 8. Blinding and freeze sequence

1. Select domain and curators.
2. Freeze inclusion/exclusion rules and strata targets.
3. Freeze System T, System U, rule registry, bridge registry, erasure map, and evaluator containers by digest.
4. Curators create cases and private gold labels.
5. Encoders receive cases without gold labels.
6. Custodians execute both systems and produce signed receipts.
7. Statistical auditor verifies completeness and digest bindings.
8. Gold labels are unblinded.
9. Primary analysis is run once.
10. Any rule repair or corpus correction creates a new version and is reported as post hoc.

The same cases may not be used to repair the system and then counted as held-out evidence.

## 9. Primary and secondary endpoints

### Primary endpoint

Paired false-authorization count on adjudicated `DENY` cases:

- `FA_T`: System T says `ALLOW` when gold is `DENY`;
- `FA_U`: System U says `ALLOW` when gold is `DENY`.

The primary discriminator is the discordant paired count:

- cases where U falsely allows and T does not;
- cases where T falsely allows and U does not.

The exact two-sided McNemar/binomial test is reported, together with the paired effect and exact confidence interval. Statistical significance is not sufficient without a material effect and clean error audit.

### Safety co-primary

Paired false-denial count on adjudicated `ALLOW` cases. A reduction in false authorization is not acceptable if it is achieved by indiscriminate denial.

### Secondary endpoints

- correct abstention on out-of-model cases;
- exact retraction behavior;
- explanation/proof-tree validity under domain review;
- origin-splicing detection rate;
- authorized-bridge preservation;
- decision latency and memory;
- rule/bridge count;
- minimal safe license basis size;
- inter-reviewer agreement; and
- performance by stratum.

## 10. Preregistered materiality thresholds

Before unblinding, the domain team must set:

- the minimum absolute reduction in false authorizations considered operationally material;
- the maximum tolerated increase in false denials;
- the minimum correct-abstention rate on out-of-model cases;
- the maximum unresolved encoding-disagreement rate;
- the minimum number of discordant pairs needed for an informative paired analysis; and
- the sample-size or sequential-stopping rule.

The protocol does not prescribe universal numerical thresholds because operational harms differ by domain. The selected values, rationale, and approver identities are immutable study inputs.

## 11. Analysis set and exact statistics

The primary analysis includes every case with:

- valid frozen encoding;
- complete paired system receipts; and
- adjudicated gold `ALLOW` or `DENY`.

`AMBIGUOUS_GOLD` and legally excluded cases are reported separately, not recoded.

For each endpoint report:

- the full paired 2x2 table;
- exact discordant-pair p-value;
- exact confidence interval for the paired difference;
- absolute counts and rates;
- stratum-specific results;
- every error case with blinded identifier and rationale; and
- sensitivity analyses treating abstention as error and as a separate action.

Multiplicity correction is required for any confirmatory secondary claim. Exploratory analyses are labeled as such.

## 12. Fail-closed terminals

### Positive but bounded

- `REAL_DOMAIN_PRIMARY_GATE_PASS` — material false-authorization reduction, false-denial bound met, blinding and receipts intact;
- `REAL_DOMAIN_TYPED_NONINFERIOR` — no safety regression but primary superiority not established;
- `REAL_DOMAIN_SCOPE_ABSTENTION_PASS` — out-of-model abstention succeeds, without a superiority claim.

### Negative or blocking

- `REAL_DOMAIN_NO_ADVANTAGE`;
- `REAL_DOMAIN_FALSE_DENIAL_HARM`;
- `REAL_DOMAIN_TYPED_FALSE_AUTHORIZATION`;
- `POSITIVE_HORN_SEMANTICS_REJECTED_BY_DOMAIN`;
- `ENCODING_DISAGREEMENT`;
- `GOLD_DISAGREEMENT_TOO_HIGH`;
- `BLINDING_BREACH`;
- `CORPUS_LEAKAGE`;
- `INSUFFICIENT_DISCORDANT_PAIRS`;
- `RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED`;
- `LEGAL_OR_PRIVACY_BLOCK`;
- `CANNOT_CHECK`.

A negative terminal is preserved as a scientific result and cannot be converted to PASS by narrowing the corpus after unblinding.

## 13. Required receipts

- preregistration digest and timestamp;
- curator, encoder, custodian, adjudicator, and auditor role declarations;
- domain-source manifest;
- case and mutation manifests;
- frozen rule, bridge, type, and erasure registries;
- evaluator container and source digests;
- per-case typed and erased input digests;
- per-case decisions, proof trees, timings, and error terminals;
- gold-label and adjudication manifest released only after execution freeze;
- exclusion log;
- paired-analysis script and output;
- disagreement log;
- environment and resource manifest; and
- independent reviewer report.

## 14. Promotion ceiling

Before a valid real-domain terminal, D may claim formal results and exact synthetic discrimination only. It may not claim operational safety, broad authorization improvement, real-world prevalence, or production readiness.

After a valid PASS, the claim remains domain- and corpus-specific unless a separate multi-domain study supports broader generalization. Journal-grade promotion additionally requires current primary-source positioning, independent semantic review, artifact replay, and a manuscript whose headline matches the weakest gate.
