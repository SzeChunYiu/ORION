## Second-review rationale

### ORION-01 — stable at Quantum
The full TARE paper was read. Donor ownership is now explicit, and the residual is a sharp exact support/closure theorem rather than generic compiler optimization. No new science is needed to fit Quantum.

### ORION-02 — AIJ remains a justified primary, with real fallback
The 51-receipt inclusion/exclusion denominator and validator remove the strongest methods-review concern. AIJ fit depends on presenting successor selection as an executable intelligent research-state decision mechanism. TMLR is the preferred fallback if editorial feedback treats the paper as evaluation/methodology rather than a broad AI advance.

### ORION-03 — no venue work can bypass science
TMLR remains the natural destination, but target formatting must not begin to imply readiness while the prospectively frozen additional frontier instances are unexecuted.

### ORION-04 — TMLR fit improved after donor subtraction
STALE, ContextNest and ORION-23 boundaries now make the contribution more credible: ORION-04 is not another memory/provenance architecture, but a matched-information mechanism study.

### ORION-09 — PRX stretch only
The post-cut R6I support-one theorem and QG16 certificate cone substantially strengthen the conceptual paper. Nevertheless, the evidence base is still a small number of exact compiler families. PRX is an honest stretch; Quantum is not a rejection fallback in scientific status, but a more natural technical venue if broad-impact framing remains fragile.

### ORION-10 — route changed
Second review found no technical gap but clear QST priority/breadth risk. Quantum's significance/verifiability criteria fit the exact counterexample/theorem story better. The recommended primary is therefore **Quantum**, not QST.
