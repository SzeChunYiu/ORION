\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Paper III establishes a positive systems result about scientific integration: \textbf{representational compatibility and scientific identity are different relations}. A source can be semantically alignable, schema-compatible or provenance-correct without thereby being authorized to denote the same scientific object for the target claim. ORION therefore makes identity itself explicit and claim-relative.

The architecture has three reusable parts. \emph{Projection before mapping} keeps source-local meaning and provenance intact before cross-source decisions. \emph{GLUE-or-obstruction} treats merge as a target-bound scientific transition rather than the default consequence of similarity: when the identity contract is not discharged, the system preserves a typed obstruction, contextual difference, plural portrait or unresolved state. \emph{Recoverability} records the source projections, mapping path, preservation conditions and non-preserved coordinates needed to audit any global statement back to its origins.

The empirical evidence supports both the safety and the stronger identity-authority interpretation. On the disjoint prospectively frozen 32-case public-reference confirmatory holdout, the typed mapping calculus produced zero false merges versus 0.1875 for flat predicate canonicalization, a paired difference of $-0.1875$ with 95\% bootstrap interval $[-0.34375,-0.0625]$, while matching the exact-coordinate conservative control on false splits ($0.000$ difference, interval $[0.000,0.000]$).

P3-X then raises the comparison standard by granting the strongest comparator structured construct, measurement, context, provenance and missingness information. Across 400 prospectively frozen heterogeneous exact contracts, P3-X reaches \textbf{400/400} exact scientific-integration decisions versus \textbf{250/400} for the strong semantic product and \textbf{50/400} for canonical matching. The P3-X-minus-strong-product effect is $+0.375$ with domain-stratified 95\% bootstrap interval $[0.3275,0.4225]$; P3-X makes zero false \glue{} decisions and preserves 1.0 clean-merge coverage.

The information-equivalent typed product also reaches 400/400. This is a positive portability theorem at the registered decision scope: the scientific-identity semantics are sufficient whether they are implemented as an ORION layer or in an equally informed integrated product. The contribution is therefore not centralized expressivity. It is the explicit target-bound identity relation that prevents a successful representation mapping from silently promoting itself into scientific sameness.

The strongest supported conclusion is consequently broader than a canonicalization benchmark result: \textbf{claim-relative scientific identity authority is a reusable interface between semantic interoperability and scientific integration}. In the registered families it is sufficient to retain clean integrations while eliminating the false integrations induced by weaker semantic products, and its decisions are representation-independent under an information-equivalent implementation.

Raw-text extraction, live ontology-engine transfer and downstream synthesis utility remain \conststatus{} as separate prospective questions. They do not weaken the proved structured-integration result; they test how far this identity-authority interface transfers when the upstream extraction and downstream task are no longer given.
