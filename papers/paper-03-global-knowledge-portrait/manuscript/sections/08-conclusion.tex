\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper asked whether a global knowledge portrait --- an integration of
scientific claims across disciplines and measurement systems --- can be built
without treating surface or representational similarity as scientific
identity. Three structural commitments answer that.

First, \emph{projection before mapping}. Each source retains a local
projection whose scientific meaning is represented by typed coordinates and
bound to exact provenance before any cross-source decision is made. This keeps
the question of what a source means separate from the question of which
sources may be combined.

Second, \emph{glue or obstruction}. Integration is decided over typed mapping
hypotheses: projections are merged only when the relevant compatibility
conditions hold; otherwise the record carries a typed obstruction, a
contextual difference, a plural portrait, or an unresolved relation rather
than silently flattening the distinction.

Third, \emph{recoverability}. The intended global portrait exposes its
construction --- source projections, mapping path, preservation conditions,
and non-preserved coordinates --- so that a downstream consumer can audit a
global statement back to its origins.

The formal projection and mapping semantics are verified by local exact
worlds. The prospectively frozen public-reference route provides a bounded
external check of the mapping layer without requiring a newly commissioned
annotation team. On the disjoint 32-case confirmatory holdout assembled from
pinned public human and expert resources, the typed comparison produced no
false merges, where flat predicate canonicalization false-merged 0.1875 of
cases; the paired difference was $-0.1875$ with a 95\% bootstrap interval of
$[-0.34375,-0.0625]$. Against the exact-coordinate conservative control, the
false-split difference was $0.000$ with interval $[0.000,0.000]$, so the rule
declared in advance passed.

The separate post-saturation battery tests a wider systems interpretation
after granting the comparator strong semantic integration and current
interoperability and provenance mechanisms. On 400 prospectively frozen exact
already-structured contracts, the claim-relative compatibility layer made
400/400 exact scientific-integration decisions against 250/400 for a strong
semantic product and 50/400 for canonical matching; the paired effect against
the strong product was $+0.375$ with a domain-stratified 95\% bootstrap
interval of $[0.3275,0.4225]$. It made no false merges and preserved 1.0
clean-merge coverage. An ideal product receiving exactly the same scientific
coordinates and rules tied at 400/400, which is an explicit negative boundary
against inherent-expressivity and centralization readings.

Together the two studies support a principle stronger than the original
flat-canonicalization result: \emph{representational compatibility is not
itself scientific identity authority}. In the tested contracts, an explicit
claim-relative compatibility layer can preserve obstruction, plurality and
unresolved state after structured semantic alignment, rather than letting a
technically valid mapping self-authorize a scientific merge.

The later protected zero-error regime-transition study provides a complementary ownership boundary. ORION-JUMP and the strongest verified representation-regime revision parent tie exactly on both disjoint frozen splits, including correspondence/preservation and protected-consequence outcomes with zero false jumps. The result therefore does not establish a distinct ORION representation-invention mechanism. It instead reinforces the role Paper III can legitimately own: representation changes must carry explicit correspondence, preservation, obstruction and reopening semantics, while the transformation mechanism itself remains parent-owned when the strongest registered parent is extensionally sufficient.
