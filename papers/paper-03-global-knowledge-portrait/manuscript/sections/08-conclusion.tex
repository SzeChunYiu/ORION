\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

ORION Paper III asks whether a global knowledge portrait --- an integration of scientific claims across disciplines and measurement systems --- can be built without treating surface similarity as scientific identity. The framework makes three structural commitments.

First, \emph{projection before mapping}. Each source retains a local projection whose scientific meaning is represented by typed coordinates and bound to exact provenance before any cross-source decision is made. This keeps the question of what a source means separate from the question of which sources may be combined.

Second, \emph{GLUE-or-obstruction}. Integration is decided over typed mapping hypotheses: projections are merged only when the relevant compatibility conditions are satisfied; otherwise the system reports a typed obstruction, contextual difference, or plural portrait rather than silently flattening the distinction.

Third, \emph{recoverability}. The intended global portrait exposes its construction --- source projections, mapping path, preservation conditions, and non-preserved coordinates --- so that a downstream consumer can audit a global statement back to its origins.

The formal projection/mapping semantics are verified by local exact worlds. The additional frozen public-reference experiment provides a first external check of the mapping layer without requiring a newly commissioned annotation team: on 32 already-structured cases assembled from pinned public human/expert resources, ORION's typed comparison produced no false merges, whereas flat predicate canonicalization false-merged 0.125 of cases; the paired difference was $-0.125$ with a 95\% bootstrap interval of $[-0.250,-0.03125]$. Forcing compatibility without the obstruction distinction produced the same $+0.125$ false-merge penalty.

That result is evidence for the mapping calculus, not for the full envisioned research system. The stronger questions remain open: whether a prospectively bound extractor can recover the required scientific projections from raw papers, whether the same advantage survives the full eight-family cross-domain study against resource-matched synthesis/schema baselines, whether generated global statements are practically recoverable, and whether obstruction improves downstream scientific decisions. Those claims remain \conststatus{} until their own frozen evidence exists.

The narrower result nevertheless falsifies one tempting assumption on externally grounded cases: equal or normalized predicates are not sufficient license to merge scientific representations. The broader ORION proposal will stand or fall on whether this structural advantage survives end-to-end scientific use rather than only the controlled mapping layer.
