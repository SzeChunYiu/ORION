% Method: formal definitions of source projection, scientific meaning
% projection, typed mappings, GLUE, obstruction, recoverability, W effect.
This section defines the ORION Paper III formal objects
(ScientificMeaningProjection.v1) precisely enough for reimplementation.
The design is registered as protocol \texttt{P3.cross-domain-atlas.v1} in
\texttt{protocol/}. All definitions below are data-level; they constrain
what any engine---local exact world, LLM orchestrator, or hybrid---must
compute to be evaluated against the protocol.

\subsection{Source and corpus}

Let $\mathcal{C} = \{d\}$ be a corpus of source documents. Each source
document $d$ is identified by a tuple
\begin{equation}
    d = \langle \mathrm{id}, v, \mathrm{span}, T, \mathrm{discipline},
    h \rangle
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{id}$ is a document identifier (DOI, PMC ID, or arXiv ID),
$v$ is the document version, $\mathrm{span} = [\mathrm{start}:
\mathrm{end}]$ locates the relevant passage in the released/authorized
text $T$, $\mathrm{discipline}$ is the scientific field, and $h$ is the
SHA-256 hash of the span text, fixing the exact bytes a judgment refers
to. No judgment is permitted to float free of its $\langle \mathrm{id},
v, \mathrm{span}, h \rangle$ address.

\subsection{Source-local scientific projection}

For each source $s = \langle d, \mathrm{span}, h \rangle$, ORION computes a
\emph{scientific meaning projection}
\begin{equation}
    P_s = \langle R_s, C_s, M_s, X_s, \rho_s, \mu_s, a_s, \delta_s, \Pi_s
    \rangle
\end{equation}
whose coordinates are:
\begin{description}
    \item[$R_s$ (referent):] the entity or class the claim is about;
    \item[$C_s$ (construct):] the theoretical construct invoked;
    \item[$M_s$ (measurement/operationalization):] the instrument,
        protocol, or operational definition;
    \item[$X_s$ (context):] temporal/state and other context;
    \item[$\rho_s$ (polarity):] positive/negative claim orientation;
    \item[$\mu_s$ (modality):] assertion strength or modal force;
    \item[$a_s$ (attribution):] speaker/source attribution;
    \item[$\delta_s$ (discourse):] discourse relation relative to the
        surrounding passage;
    \item[$\Pi_s$ (assumptions):] explicit assumptions and unresolved
        ambiguity.
\end{description}
Each coordinate is a \emph{typed hypothesis}, not a hallucinated ground
truth. The projection is source-local: it addresses the source's claim in
the source's own terms and is not contaminated by other sources or by an a
priori global schema. An extraction or normalization step is not permitted
to silently acquire scientific authority over the source view.

\subsection{Scientific meaning comparison}

A comparison between two projections $P_a, P_b$ is a vector of
\emph{coordinate-wise hypotheses}:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
    m(P_a, P_b) = \langle&\ \mathrm{ref}(R_a,R_b),\ \mathrm{const}(C_a,C_b),\\
    &\ \mathrm{meas}(M_a,M_b),\ \mathrm{ctx}(X_a,X_b),\\
    &\ \mathrm{pol}(\rho_a,\rho_b),\ \mathrm{mod}(\mu_a,\mu_b),\\
    &\ \mathrm{attr}(a_a,a_b),\ \mathrm{disc}(\delta_a,\delta_b) \rangle
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Each coordinate relation takes values in the frozen taxonomy of
\texttt{ANNOTATION\_SCHEMA\_V1.json}, e.g.:
\begin{align*}
    \mathrm{ref} &\in \{\mathrm{SAME}, \mathrm{DIFFERENT},
        \mathrm{PARTIAL\_OVERLAP}, \mathrm{UNRESOLVED}\},\\
    \mathrm{const} &\in \{\mathrm{SAME}, \mathrm{RELATED\_NOT\_SAME},\\
        &\qquad \mathrm{DIFFERENT}, \mathrm{UNRESOLVED}\},\\
    \mathrm{meas} &\in \{\mathrm{EQUIVALENT},
        \mathrm{TRANSFORMABLE\_WITH\_CONDITIONS},\\
        &\qquad \mathrm{NON\_EQUIVALENT}, \mathrm{NOT\_APPLICABLE},
        \mathrm{UNRESOLVED}\}.
\end{align*}
A mapping is \emph{proposal-level} until supported.

\subsection{Typed mapping}

A \emph{typed mapping} is a pair
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    m \in \{\ &\mathrm{IDENTITY},\ \mathrm{EQUIVALENCE},\\
    &\mathrm{CONDITIONAL\_TRANSFORM},\ \mathrm{SUBSUMPTION},\\
    &\mathrm{ASSOCIATION\_ONLY},\ \mathrm{NO\_LICENSED\_MAPPING},\\
    &\mathrm{UNRESOLVED}\ \}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
together with
\begin{itemize}
    \item a set of coordinate relations $\mathrm{comp}(m)$ it licenses
        (which coordinates it asserts to align);
    \item a set of \emph{preservation conditions} $\mathrm{pre}(m)$ that
        must hold for the mapping to be relied upon, e.g.\ ``$P_b$
        restricted to temperature range $[T_1,T_2]$''; and
    \item a set of \emph{non-preserved coordinates}
        $\overline{\mathrm{pre}}(m)$: information in $P_a$ or $P_b$ that
        the mapping does not carry, which must not be silently assumed to
        transfer.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{GLUE and obstruction}

A set of projections $\{P_{i}\}_{i}$ is \emph{composable} on a mapping set
$m$ if every licensed coordinate relation is satisfied and every
preservation condition $\mathrm{pre}(m)$ is verified. When composable, the
projections may be \textsc{glue}d into a joint chart
$\bigsqcup_i P_i$ with the mapping record attached. When any licensed
relation fails or any preservation condition is violated (and cannot be
repaired), the correct output is a \emph{typed obstruction}: the system
records \textsc{obstruction} with the failing coordinate(s) rather than
forcing a merge. When multiple internally valid but mutually incompatible
representations persist, the correct output is \textsc{plural\_view}.
Unresolvable cases take \textsc{unresolved}; the system is permitted (and
encouraged) to abstain rather than guess.

\subsection{Recoverable global portrait}

A \emph{global portrait} is the structure
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{G} = \langle \{P_i\}, \mathcal{M}, \sigma \rangle
\end{equation}
where $\{P_i\}$ retains every contributing source projection, $\mathcal{M}$
records every proposed and accepted mapping with its conditions, and
$\sigma$ is an \emph{assignment map} from each global statement back to its
source projections, mappings, and surviving deductions. Recoverability is
the requirement that for every global statement $g \in \mathcal{G}$ and
every source projection $P_i$, the reader (or automated evaluator) can
recover: (a) which source span(s) licensed $g$; (b) which mappings were
applied and under what conditions; and (c) which coordinates were
\emph{not} preserved --- i.e.\ what $g$ does \emph{not} assert. Recovery of
non-preserved coordinates is an evaluation target, not a display concern.

\subsection{The W effect}

Let $\Omega_0$ be the relevance/search-universe model of the integration
system before absorbing some reader-set $S$ of new sources, and let
$\Omega_1$ be the universe model after a projection $P_s$ for $s \in S$ is
absorbed. The \emph{W effect} is the claim that
$\Omega_1 \supsetneq \Omega_0$: the new representation does not merely add
facts to a static graph; it \emph{changes the search universe} --- new
relevance axes, new addressable referents, new mappings --- and thereby
reopens hypotheses. Formally, the effect is a feedback loop
\begin{equation}
    \Omega_{t+1} = \mathrm{Universe}(\mathcal{G}_t \cup
    \{P_s : s \in S_t\})
\end{equation}
so that integrating new literature can change which future sources are
retrieved and how they are posed. Ablation \textsc{ORION\_no\_W\_expansion}
removes this feedback to measure its marginal effect.

\subsection{What is not claimed}

ORION does not claim the first scientific knowledge graph, cross-domain
RAG system, schema matcher, literature-based discovery system, or
provenance graph. Its target is narrower: whether preserving source-local
projections and explicitly representing failed mappings reduces
scientifically damaging false integration while still enabling useful
synthesis. Local exact worlds \cite{orion2025paper1,orion2025paper2}
falsify the semantics of projection, mapping, and
\textsc{glue} in controlled settings; they do not by themselves establish
adequacy over the real literature, which is why the external gold study is
the decisive evaluation (Section~\ref{sec:eval}).
