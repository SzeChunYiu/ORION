\section{Successor interface: correspondence for representation candidates}
\label{sec:p3-successor-correspondence}

The later ORION discovery programme can propose changes of representation, but Paper III treats such proposals first as mapping problems. A representation candidate must name source and target objects, an explicit correspondence map, obligations intended to be preserved, and contracts the new representation is claimed to make available. These declarations are integration inputs, not evidence that the correspondence is correct or that the representation should be adopted.

Paper III's local projection discipline supplies the relevant outcomes. A correspondence that preserves registered source-local commitments can contribute \glue; a mapping that drops a load-bearing assumption or invariant is an \obstruction; multiple defensible target readings may remain a \pluralview; and missing or target-ambiguous reconstruction remains \unresolved. A visually or terminologically similar representation is therefore insufficient when its assumption structure changes the scientific object being transported.

The completed failure-staleness pilot sharpened this ownership boundary. Exact-context equality remains a safe conservative fast path, but semantics-preserving non-identity requires a complete P7-style transport witness; verified semantic change reopens the old negative conclusion, and incomplete or target-ambiguous transport stays unresolved. No separate ORION failure-staleness transport primitive was required. Likewise, the zero-error regime-invention programme required explicit old/new correspondence and protected consequences, yet the verified regime-revision parent matched the full ORION composition. Correspondence is therefore load-bearing for interpretability and preservation without becoming evidence of ORION-specific Jump novelty.

Paper III owns recoverable mapping, preservation, and obstruction for successor concept candidates. It does not decide novelty, protected scientific validity, or method adoption; a complete correspondence witness can make a later protected test interpretable, but it cannot substitute for that test.
