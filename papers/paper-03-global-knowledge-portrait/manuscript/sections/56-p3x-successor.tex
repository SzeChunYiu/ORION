\section{Post-saturation successor validation: scientific compatibility is not representational compatibility}
\label{sec:p3x}

After the scoped public-reference result was frozen, we opened a separate
successor study to test a wider architecture-level statement without rewriting
the original 32-case evidence.  The P3-X protocol was frozen before protected
outcomes and grants the comparator strong structured semantic information:
construct, measurement, context, provenance and explicit missingness.  It also
credits scientific-variable decomposition, ontology/schema alignment,
provenance-aware integration, plural/inconsistency representation and generic
consistency checking as prior work.  The only disputed interface is whether
those representational outputs themselves authorize scientific identity.

The protected battery contains 400 exact already-structured contracts: five
scientific domains, eight mapping archetypes and ten disjoint variants per
cell.  The gold terminal is one of \glue{}, \obstruction{}, \pluralview{} or
\unresolved{}.  A strong semantic-product baseline correctly handles direct
construct, measurement and context mismatches and missing load-bearing
coordinates, but treats provenance as trace rather than scientific stance,
forces a canonical choice for legitimate one-to-many mappings, and accepts
pairwise-compatible mappings without the P3-X global scientific-consistency
authority check.  A second canonical-matching baseline represents the usual
merge-on-high-local-match pressure.  An ideal fully typed product receives the
exact P3-X coordinates and rules and is required to tie.

P3-X achieved exact scientific-integration decisions on 400/400 protected
cases, versus 250/400 for the strong semantic product and 50/400 for the
canonical matcher.  The paired P3-X-minus-strong-product difference was
$+0.375$ with a domain-stratified 95\% bootstrap interval of
$[0.3275,0.4225]$; McNemar discordance was 150--0.  P3-X made zero false
\glue{} decisions, retained 1.0 clean-\glue{} accuracy, and the paired effect
was $+0.375$ in each of the five exact domains.  The ideal typed product was
400/400 with zero decision mismatches, explicitly ruling out an inherent
expressivity or centralization interpretation.  An independent implementation
reconstructed all four arms and the canonical protected-row digest exactly.

The result supports a wider but still bounded claim: in the tested exact
contracts, representational compatibility is weaker than scientific identity
authority.  A claim-relative scientific compatibility layer can add value over
a strong semantic integration product by preserving obstruction, plurality and
unresolved state where canonical identity is not licensed.  The study does
\emph{not} establish raw-text extraction, live ontology-engine superiority,
expert agreement on new corpora, or open-ended cross-domain integration; those
remain \conststatus{}.
