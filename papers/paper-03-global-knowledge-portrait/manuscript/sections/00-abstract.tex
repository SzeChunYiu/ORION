Cross-domain scientific synthesis fails when representational compatibility is mistaken for scientific identity: identical names can denote different referents, aligned schemas can preserve structure while changing scientific meaning, and technically valid mappings can erase measurement, context, or attribution differences that matter to the claim. We present a recoverable integration calculus that keeps each source as a provenance-bound scientific projection and makes global identity a claim-relative authority decision rather than a by-product of canonicalization. Proposed mappings distinguish referent identity, construct identity, measurement/operationalization, context, representation, and unresolved obstruction before any \glue{} operation is authorized.

Two prospectively frozen studies support this separation. In a disjoint 32-case confirmatory holdout assembled from pinned external human/expert resources, the ORION mapping calculus makes zero false merges versus 0.1875 for flat predicate canonicalization, a paired difference of $-0.1875$ with 95\% bootstrap CI $[-0.34375,-0.0625]$. Against an exact-coordinate conservative control, the false-split difference is $0.000$ with interval $[0.000,0.000]$, showing that the reduction in false integration is obtained without an added splitting penalty on the confirmatory set. A separate post-saturation successor grants a strong semantic product structured construct, measurement, context, provenance, and missingness information, then tests only whether representational alignment is sufficient authority for scientific identity. Across 400 prospectively frozen exact heterogeneous mapping contracts, P3-X attains 400/400 exact decisions versus 250/400 for that strong product, a paired advantage of $+0.375$ (95\% domain-stratified bootstrap CI $[0.3275,0.4225]$), with zero false \glue{} decisions and 1.0 clean-merge coverage. An information-equivalent typed product implementing the same scientific coordinates and authorization rules also attains 400/400, demonstrating that the result is an interface-level sufficiency claim rather than an architecture-specific expressivity effect.

Together, the studies establish the central systems result: semantic alignment becomes reliable scientific integration only when it is coupled to explicit claim-relative identity authority that can preserve obstruction, plurality, and unresolved state. The demonstrated scope is already-structured scientific projections and exact heterogeneous contracts; raw-text extraction, live ontology-engine generality, newly commissioned expert agreement, generated-portrait recoverability, and downstream utility require separate prospective evaluation.
