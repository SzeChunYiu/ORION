% Abstract (standalone fragment included by main.tex).
Scientific integration requires more than semantic alignment: two representations
can be compatible while still lacking authority to denote the same scientific
object for the claim being made. We formalize this missing relation as
\emph{claim-relative scientific identity authority}. Source-local scientific
projections preserve referent, construct, measurement/operationalization,
context, modality, attribution and provenance before any global merge;
compatible projections may be \glue{}d only when the target identity contract is
discharged, while incompatible or unresolved views remain typed
\obstruction{}, \pluralview{} or unresolved states.

Two prospectively frozen studies support the distinction. On a disjoint 32-case
public-reference confirmatory holdout assembled from pinned human/expert
resources, the typed mapping calculus produced zero false merges versus 0.1875
for flat predicate canonicalization, a paired difference of $-0.1875$ with 95\%
bootstrap CI $[-0.34375,-0.0625]$, while matching an exact-coordinate
conservative control on false splits ($0.000$ difference, interval
$[0.000,0.000]$). P3-X then grants a strong semantic product structured
construct, measurement, context, provenance and missingness information and
asks only whether representational alignment is sufficient authority for
scientific identity. Across 400 prospectively frozen heterogeneous exact
contracts, P3-X attains 400/400 exact decisions versus 250/400 for that strong
semantic product and 50/400 for canonical matching, with a P3-X-minus-strong
product effect of $+0.375$ (95\% domain-stratified bootstrap CI
$[0.3275,0.4225]$), zero false \glue{} decisions and 1.0 clean-merge coverage.
An information-equivalent typed product implementing the same identity relation
also attains 400/400, establishing implementation portability rather than a
centralized-expressivity advantage.

The strongest bounded conclusion is therefore positive and architectural:
\textbf{scientific identity is a target-bound authorization relation above
representational compatibility, and making that relation explicit prevents
false integration while preserving clean integration on the registered
contracts}. Raw-text extraction and downstream scientific utility remain
\conststatus{} as separate prospective transfer questions rather than premises
of this result.
