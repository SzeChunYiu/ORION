% Abstract (standalone fragment included by main.tex).
Scientific integration requires more than semantic alignment: two representations
can be compatible while still lacking authority to denote the same scientific
object for the claim being made. We formalize this missing relation as
\emph{claim-relative scientific identity authority}. Source-local scientific
projections preserve referent, construct, measurement/operationalization,
context, modality, attribution and provenance before any global merge;
compatible projections may be \glue{}d only when the target identity contract is
discharged, while incompatible or unresolved views remain typed
\obstruction{}, \pluralview{} or unresolved states.

Two prospectively frozen studies support the distinction. On a disjoint 32-case
public-reference confirmatory holdout assembled from pinned human/expert
resources, the typed mapping calculus produced zero false merges versus 0.1875
for flat predicate canonicalization, a paired difference of $-0.1875$ with 95\%
bootstrap CI $[-0.34375,-0.0625]$, while matching an exact-coordinate
conservative control on false splits ($0.000$ difference, interval
$[0.000,0.000]$). P3-X then grants a strong semantic product structured
construct, measurement, context, provenance and missingness information and
asks only whether representational alignment is sufficient authority for
scientific identity. Across 400 prospectively frozen heterogeneous exact
contracts, P3-X attains 400/400 exact decisions versus 250/400 for that strong
semantic product and 50/400 for canonical matching, with a P3-X-minus-strong
product effect of $+0.375$ (95\% domain-stratified bootstrap CI
$[0.3275,0.4225]$), zero false \glue{} decisions and 1.0 clean-merge coverage.
An information-equivalent typed product implementing the same identity relation
also attains 400/400, establishing implementation portability rather than a
centralized-expressivity advantage.

The strongest bounded conclusion is therefore positive and architectural:
\textbf{scientific identity is a target-bound authorization relation above
representational compatibility, and making that relation explicit prevents
false integration while preserving clean integration on the registered
contracts}. Raw-text extraction and downstream scientific utility remain
\conststatus{} as separate prospective transfer questions rather than premises
of this result.
Cross-domain scientific synthesis is vulnerable to false integration:
identical names may denote different referents, nominally similar
constructs may rest on incompatible operationalizations, and apparently
contradictory claims may differ only in context, modality or attribution.
This paper treats integration as a decision that has to be licensed rather
than as a similarity score to be thresholded. Each source is represented as
a source-local scientific projection bound to its provenance, and a proposed
cross-source mapping records referent identity, construct identity,
measurement and operationalization, context and representation equivalence as
separate coordinates. Compatible projections may be glued into one global
statement only under explicit preservation conditions; where a load-bearing
coordinate is incompatible or unresolved the mapping remains a typed
obstruction or a plural view rather than being forced into one canonical
summary. Local exact worlds falsify the mapping semantics. In a disjoint,
prospectively execution-frozen 32-case confirmatory holdout assembled from
pinned external human and expert resources, coordinate-governed mapping made
zero false merges against 0.1875 for flat predicate canonicalization; the
paired difference was $-0.1875$ (95\% bootstrap CI $[-0.34375,-0.0625]$).
Against an exact-coordinate conservative control, the false-split difference
was $0.000$ with interval $[0.000,0.000]$, satisfying the rule declared before
those outputs were seen. A separate post-saturation battery then granted its
comparator a strong semantic product carrying structured construct,
measurement, context, provenance and missingness information, and tested only
whether representational alignment is sufficient authority for scientific
identity. On 400 prospectively frozen exact heterogeneous mapping contracts,
the claim-relative compatibility layer decided 400/400 exactly against 250/400
for that strong product, a paired difference of $+0.375$ (95\%
domain-stratified bootstrap CI $[0.3275,0.4225]$), with no false merges and
complete clean-merge coverage. An ideal information-equivalent typed product
given the same coordinates and rules tied at 400/400, which rules out an
inherent-expressivity reading. These results concern already-structured
scientific projections and exact successor contracts only. Outcomes are
three-valued throughout: an outcome that could not be determined is reported
separately from an outcome determined to be false, and the two are never
merged. Raw-text extraction, live ontology-engine generality, newly
commissioned expert agreement, recoverability in generated portraits and
downstream utility all remain undetermined in that third sense.
