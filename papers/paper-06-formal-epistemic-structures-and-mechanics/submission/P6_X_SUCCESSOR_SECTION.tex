\section{Post-saturation successor: scientific-certificate semantics as a conservative enrichment}
\label{sec:p6x-successor}

The original V2.1 theory isolates a typed-erasure separation between bare
computation/dependency behavior and scientific admissibility.  A separate
post-saturation successor generalizes that boundary without rewriting the V2.1
evidence.  The successor grants mature donor mechanisms for dependency
maintenance, effectful computation, continuing authorization, provenance,
workflow reproducibility and execution attestation.  In particular, P6-X does
not claim novelty for certificates or provenance-bearing execution.

P6-X freezes three bounded donor semantic embeddings: dependency maintenance,
effectful computation, and continuing authorization plus execution provenance.
The scientific enrichment adds four certificate coordinates: current evidence
version, authorized scientific source, supported claim scope, and current
verification epoch.  For embedding $D$, scientific admissibility is donor-native
validity together with discharge of every scientific certificate obligation.

\begin{theorem}[Scientific non-reflection under non-inert erasure]
Let $U_D$ preserve the complete donor-visible state while erasing the four
scientific certificate coordinates.  If at least one erased scientific
coordinate is non-inert---that is, changing it while holding donor-visible state
and all other scientific coordinates fixed can change scientific
admissibility---then $U_D$ does not reflect scientific admissibility.  There
exist states $s,t$ with $U_D(s)=U_D(t)$ and equal donor-native validity but
different scientific-admissibility judgments.
\end{theorem}

The condition is explicit: P6-X does not assert that every donor formalism
necessarily erases scientifically relevant information.

\begin{theorem}[Conservative reduction]
If every scientific certificate obligation is discharged, scientific
admissibility reduces exactly to donor-native validity under the embedding.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Ideal-product equivalence]
An ideal donor product enriched with the exact P6-X scientific coordinates and
the same admissibility predicate is extensionally equivalent to P6-X.
\end{theorem}

This negative result rules out inherent expressivity or centralization as the
source of the contribution.

\begin{theorem}[Certificate continuity after change]
A donor-valid transition preserves an existing scientific certificate only when
every scientific certificate coordinate changed by the transition is either
preserved under the exact change or explicitly revalidated.  Donor-valid
recomputation, support, effect execution, or tool authorization alone cannot
preserve a certificate after a non-inert scientific coordinate becomes false.
\end{theorem}

An exhaustive finite model evaluates 512 Boolean states per embedding, 1,536
state evaluations total.  It records zero donor-preservation violations, 96
typed-erasure separation witnesses spanning all four scientific coordinates,
96 conservative-reduction cases with zero violations, zero ideal-product
mismatches, 96 certificate-revocation countermodels, and 24 donor-valid
no-alarm cases.  The canonical enumeration has SHA-256
\texttt{9a181cc61f1a1b56daa8f313d63acc3928f758b4150d242fe03c14e347ec511c}.
A second implementation independently reproduces all theorem counts.

The wider P6 claim is therefore bounded to this scientific-admissibility
separation and conservative-extension theorem family.  Generic certificates,
provenance, authorization, effects and dependency maintenance remain donor
territory; deployed-agent utility and universal scientific semantics are not
claimed.
