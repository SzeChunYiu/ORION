## One job

This paper defines and stress-tests **compilation regime geometry** as a way to study an exact optimization family: locate the donor-optimal region, identify exact trade configurations, prove a smallest sufficient search/normal-form bound where possible, derive a structural membership/cost rule when the family admits one, and prospectively test that rule. Refutations are part of the map rather than failed experiments.

The V1 manuscript is now materially stale. Wave 2 changed three headline conclusions:

- R6I's initial support-5 bound was tightened through the QG-9 ladder to the all-`n` intrinsic support number `κ_R6I = 1`;
- SixLCU's pair-derived P0 boundary was upgraded by QG-12 to an all-instance theorem;
- the candidate `boundary-is-low-order` motif was **refuted** by a third-family StabPrep transfer, and QG-15b showed that the failure is not merely insufficient predicate budget: the frozen natural feature vocabulary contains mixed cells with an irreducible error floor.

TARE's own map also evolved: theorem-backed support two remains exact under the unit objective, while successive closed forms were refuted by phantom and hybrid configurations. QG-8 supplies an all-`n` objective cone in which support two remains sufficient.

## Claim boundary

The contribution is the receipted mapping programme and the cross-family results actually established. The paper does not claim that every compiler family has a small trade basis, a low-order predicate, or a support bound inferable from one generic formula. StabPrep is the explicit counterexample to that universalization.
