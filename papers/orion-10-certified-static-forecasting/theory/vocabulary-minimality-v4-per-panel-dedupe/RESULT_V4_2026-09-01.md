## The pre-declared prediction cannot be scored from this run, and that is a defect

`PROTOCOL_V4.md` was committed before the run with three terminals —
`ENVELOPE_SURVIVES_20X_COVERAGE`, `CANNOT_CHECK_PREFIX_CONTROL_FAILED`,
`CANNOT_CHECK_WALL_CLOCK` — and this prediction:

> V3's nine violations all lie beyond V2's prefix, in `H2_n3` (5) and `H4_n3` (4) [...] If
> cross-panel skipping produced them, they should not survive. If they are genuine members
> of the frozen space, they should reappear.

`run_per_panel_v4.py`, the script that protocol names, **emits none of those three
terminals.** Its terminal vocabulary is `QG7_FOURTH_SUPPORT2_REGIME_FOUND`,
`QG7_CANNOT_CHECK`, `QG7_ENLARGED_BORROW_COMPLETENESS_ALL_N_MACHINE_CHECKED`,
`QG7_PARTIAL_NORMALIZATION__HYBRID_SHAPE_OPEN`. Nothing in the 1.8 MB receipt reports a
prefix control or an offset-3 envelope; the quantity it classifies is `gap4 = C_Dxx −
min(C_D+, f_B')`, which is a different quantity from "exceeds offset 3".

**Verdict on the prediction: `CANNOT_CHECK`.** Not refuted, not confirmed — measured by an
instrument that does not report it.

It is tempting to read the per-panel `fourth` counts as the answer, since H2_n3 shows 103
and H4_n3 shows 45 where V3 had 5 and 4. **That reading is not available**, because a
fourth-regime instance and an offset-3 envelope violation are not the same object, and no
mapping between them is established anywhere in the protocol or the runner. Recording the
numbers is legitimate; scoring the prediction on them is not.

The defect is the pre-registration's, not the run's: a protocol whose named script cannot
produce any of its declared outcomes is not a pre-registration. It was frozen without
checking that the runner and the protocol shared a terminal vocabulary.
