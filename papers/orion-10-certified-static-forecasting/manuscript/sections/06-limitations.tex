\section{Limitations}

\textbf{Grammar and objective.} Theorems~1 and Eq.~\eqref{eq:classification} apply only to the three-block shared-tag grammar and the unit structural support-count objective in Eq.~\eqref{eq:cost}. They do not transfer to different tag ranks, numbers of blocks, rotation trade-offs, or coefficient-weighted costs. Equation~\eqref{eq:reweighted} is an explicit adverse boundary.

\textbf{Resource meaning.} The predicted value is an abstract compilation cost. It is not a count of physical qubits, logical qubits, magic states, gates, energy, or elapsed time. It does not establish quantum advantage or deployment readiness.

\textbf{Finite panels.} The prospective, application-derived, seeded, and hostile panels validate executable behavior only on their stated rows. Application-derived higher-qubit equalities may use exact containment rather than direct restricted enumeration. The all-size claims come from the support and classification proofs, not from these panels.

\textbf{Machine-checked local obligations.} The support proof uses an exhaustive 18,432-case local inequality, and the explanation proof uses a complete $2^{24}$ target-letter domain for each of 378 geometries. The accompanying checker verifies the published aggregate arithmetic and selected witness relations; reproducing the complete searches requires the full scientific implementation and a suitable compute environment.

\textbf{Performance.} We omit historical timings and claim no asymptotic or empirical speedup. A canonical comparison should be performed only after the public implementation and execution environment are fixed.

\textbf{Literature and external authority.} The literature search was bounded rather than exhaustive. Neither internal exact checks nor a manuscript build substitutes for independent peer review. A persistent public archive, license choice, and final preprint deposit remain publication actions to be completed by the author.
