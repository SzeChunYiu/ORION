\begin{abstract}
A compilation-cost predictor can be numerically correct for three different reasons: theorem, benchmark agreement, or accidental coverage by a compact regime classifier. We separate these certificate layers for a frozen shared-Tag TARE grammar. The initial forecaster $F_1(t)=\min(C_{R6L},C_{D^+},f_B)$ passes 102/102 prospectively staged rows but is exactly refuted on a fresh $n=3$ instance: $C_{DP}=10$ while $F_1=11$. An existing all-$n$ support theorem instead proves $C_{DP}=C_{D^{++}}$ under the unit support-count objective, so $F_2(t)=C_{D^{++}}(t)$ uses an independent support-$\le2$ evaluator without an unrestricted-DP call. It has zero error on 9,547 compared instances; the theorem supplies exactness and the panel checks implementation. An enlarged-borrow explanation $B'$ repairs the original row, but a hostile search finds 64 hybrid configurations outside it. A subsequent full 740-instance fibre census sharpens that negative: six of seven $f_{B'}$ fibres contain mixed exact costs, so the named vocabulary cannot provide an exact $\Psi$-only explanation. The 64 selected hostile rows alone have a uniform offset only because selection conditioned on the gap. These explanation failures remain inside $D^{++}$ and do not affect F2 cost exactness. The contribution is certificate separation: theorem-backed cost, refutable explanation, prospective and hostile evidence, and row-specific verification authority. No full-circuit, runtime, transfer, or hardware claim follows.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} quantum resource estimation; certified forecasting; exact compilation; proof-carrying computation; regime classification.
