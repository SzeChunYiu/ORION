\title{Certified cost forecasts and refutable regime maps for exact quantum compilation}
\author{Sze Chun Yiu}
\email{sze-chun.yiu@fysik.su.se}
\affiliation{Stockholm University}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
A numerical compilation forecast may be supported by a theorem, agreement on tested instances, or a structural model that happens to cover those instances.  These warrants are not interchangeable.  We separate them for an exact shared-tag Pauli block-encoding model and then test whether the resulting regime description transfers to another compiler family.  An interpretable three-family formula first matches a prospectively staged panel, yet a frozen fresh instance refutes it: the formula returns 11 while the exact optimum is 10.  A support-two normalization theorem yields a stronger forecaster that equals the unrestricted optimum for every admitted size under the unit structural objective; all finite implementation comparisons agree.  Explanation remains separately falsifiable: a repaired borrow vocabulary fails on 64 hostile cases, and six of seven of its complete-census fibres contain mixed exact costs.  A later per-block-permutation argument closes the previously open commuting-support-two sector, while two alternative global proof links remain explicitly open.  In weighted Clifford state preparation, a four-feature separator is minimal on the complete small-size domain, but the frozen next-size rule makes 32 errors on 120 states and shows no improvement under the specified shuffle null ($p=0.51$).  Exact cost, explanation, finite verification and transfer therefore require distinct certificates.  The results concern frozen algebraic objectives and do not establish runtime, production-compiler, hardware or quantum-advantage claims.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} quantum compilation; exact optimization; resource forecasting; stabilizer circuits; algorithm selection; verified computation.
