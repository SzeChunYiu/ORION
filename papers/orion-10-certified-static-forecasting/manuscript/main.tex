\begin{abstract}
Static cost formulas are useful only if their authority is stated precisely: benchmark agreement, theorem-backed exactness, and a compact structural explanation are different claims. We make that separation explicit for a three-block shared-tag Pauli compilation grammar with a unit structural support-count objective. An interpretable three-family formula matched 9,545 of 9,546 distinct benchmark instances and all 102 predictions staged before exact evaluation, yet one three-qubit instance refuted its equality claim, with exact cost 10 and formula value 11. We retain that adverse row and replace the formula with an exact support-at-most-two evaluator. A support-reduction theorem proves that this evaluator equals the unrestricted optimum for every qubit count in scope; zero error over 9,547 comparison events then checks the implementation rather than serving as the proof. A first enlargement of the explanation family repaired the original row but failed on 64 of 740 hostile cases. A hybrid family covered 10,481 verified finite instances, and a complete local-domain argument subsequently closed the explanation classification for all sizes under the unit objective. Two failed proof routes are retained because they mark genuine limits of simpler arguments. Under a pre-specified reweighted objective the support theorem does not transfer: an exact witness has costs $11<13<23$ for the unrestricted, support-two, and weight-one families. The resulting certificate reports feasible bounds, theorem-backed cost, explanation, and row-level validation separately; it makes no claim about full-circuit, fault-tolerant, or hardware resource optimality.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} quantum compilation; Pauli operators; static resource analysis; exact optimization; machine-checked proof; falsification.
