\section{Grammar and objective}

\subsection{Pauli representation and block structure}
Global phases are irrelevant to support counting. At each qubit a Pauli letter belongs to $\{I,X,Y,Z\}$, and the binary symplectic product determines commutation. The six targets are supplied as three block pairs. Each block selects an ordered anticommuting frame pair $(A_0,A_1)$, assigns its two targets to the two branches, and marks one branch as central. A shared Pauli $S$ must realize the same two nonzero symplectic labels against every block's ordered frame pair. If six unpaired targets are supplied instead, the 15 possible pairings can be searched as a constant-size outer family.

For a target letter $p$ and assigned frame letter $f$, the local correction letter is the phase-ignored product $pf$. Corrections in corresponding branches of the three blocks are factored together. For local letters $a,b,c$, the factor cost is
\[
F(a,b,c)=w(a)+w(b)+w(c)-2\,\mathbf 1[a=b=c\ne I],
\]
where $w(I)=0$ and $w(X)=w(Y)=w(Z)=1$. Thus three identical nonidentity correction letters receive a two-unit sharing discount.

\subsection{Declared objective}
The total cost is coordinate-separable. Removing one nonidentity coordinate from a central frame refunds two units; removing one from a noncentral frame refunds four. The shared operator costs two units per supported qubit. The remaining term is the sum of $F$ over both branches and all coordinates. Within-block target order, central choices, and the two common-label orientations form constant-size families because the grammar always contains three two-target blocks.

The unrestricted exact referee searches the complete admitted grammar. The support-$k$ family restricts every frame Pauli to support at most $k$ while retaining all other choices. Family containment immediately makes the support restriction an upper-bound candidate; equality requires proof or exact comparison.

\subsection{Evidence boundary}
The mathematical proof uses the objective above, a parity-preserving exchange, and a well-founded support-reduction argument. Finite enumeration checks the local lemmas but is not the logical basis of the all-size theorem. The exact counterexample is a lower witness for support two. Implementation comparisons establish conformance only on their stated domains. Runtime measurements address one implementation and panel, not the theorem's correctness.
