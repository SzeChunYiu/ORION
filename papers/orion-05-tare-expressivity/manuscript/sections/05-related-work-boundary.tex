\section{Related work}

The studied grammar descends from version 4 of the Tag-and-Restore block-encoding preprint \cite{schillo2026blockencoding}. Its Section 4 introduces the primitive; Theorem 1 and Remark 1 leave the anticommuting frames, ancilla count, and controls open to user choice; and Theorem 2 and Remark 2 expose nonunique, optimizable label operators. Its numerical study fixes one canonical frame family and optimizes label strings independently. We inherit the primitive and those design freedoms. The present analysis fixes six target slots in three paired blocks, branchwise three-way factorization, one common binary syndrome, the two/four frame multipliers, and the normalized logical objective. The support restriction is an analysis family; the cost-nonincreasing transformation, sharpness instance, and induced direct bound concern this specialization only. This division identifies the scientific objects but makes no priority claim.

Anticommuting-unitary partitioning groups anticommuting Pauli terms for measurement, which is a different task and optimized quantity \cite{izmaylov2020unitary}. Pauli-cluster and Pauli-level compilers transform circuits or Hamiltonians in Pauli representations; they do not establish a same-object performance improvement for the grammar studied here \cite{vandenberg2020clusters,paykin2023pcoast,yang2025phoenix,yang2026symphony}.

Support reduction itself is not new as a general idea. Phase-gadget synthesis decomposes Pauli exponentials using one- and two-qubit components \cite{cowtan2020phasegadget}, and parity-network work exploits sharing across related terms \cite{amy2018cnotphase}. Two-qubit gates are universal for quantum computation \cite{divincenzo1995universal}, while stabilizer-state normal forms are classical tools of the Pauli formalism \cite{fattal2004entanglement}. These results remove any claim that support-two building blocks, parity sharing, or two-local universality originate here. They address different objects from the grammar-specific statement proved above: the present theorem transforms auxiliary frames while preserving one fixed shared-label objective and exact optimum. Hamiltonian weight reduction and perturbative gadgets likewise change the encoded Hamiltonian, often by adding degrees of freedom or approximation, rather than proving this cost-monotone frame exchange \cite{hastings2021weight,kempe2006local}.

Bounded web and arXiv searches were refreshed through 28 August 2026 using query families for Tag-and-Restore encoding, shared labels, Pauli frames, support-two normal forms, exact expressivity, binary-symplectic compilers, and sharp support thresholds. They also inspected exact and approximate synthesis, low-ancilla block encodings, fault-tolerant resource models, and compiler benchmarks. No exact equivalent of the full grammar-specific theorem was located, but this search cannot establish absence or priority. The residual contribution is therefore stated descriptively: a cost-monotone normal-form proof, its sharp obstruction, and the induced exact upper bound for this declared grammar. Novelty beyond that narrow formulation remains a matter for independent expert review.
