**Status:** `SCOPED_NON_SELF_PROMOTION_TRACK_SELECTED`  
**Date:** 2026-08-20

## Paper identity

The current P5 paper is **not** a performance paper claiming that Self-ORION already improves itself better than DGM-, ADAS-, AlphaEvolve- or other self-improving-agent baselines. That prospective campaign remains separately frozen and `CANNOT_CHECK`.

The scoped paper studies a different scientific object that is already implemented and auditable:

> **How can an autonomous scientific system diagnose its own failures and propose its own modifications without those fallible internal judgments becoming authority to adopt the modification?**

## Primary scoped result

Self-ORION separates three status transitions:

1. **diagnosis** — an internal system may infer or keep unresolved a failure cause;
2. **proposal** — it may nominate a candidate computation, repair or mechanic;
3. **adoption** — the candidate may change the scientific method only after the protected evidence/assurance/host boundary authorizes that transition.

The registered T1–T7 control path is structurally non-self-promoting. Responsibility reports, interface checks, computation choices, containment results, evidence-independence reports and the composed T7 recommendation may guide the next research action but do not grant revision, adoption, promotion, merge or global-task-stop authority. The controller exposes no self-merge primitive; final adoption remains host-owned.

## Non-vacuous error setting

The archived glm-5.2 hidden-cause diagnostic is **21/24**, with three incorrect attributions retained. Those failures remain failures. Their role in this scoped paper is to show that the authority architecture does not assume perfect self-knowledge: a wrong diagnosis is still not, by itself, an adoption certificate.

This is an architectural error-containment statement, not evidence that the three errors caused no downstream harm in a live self-improvement campaign.

## Explicit nonclaims

This scoped paper does not claim:

- superior fresh-task improvement versus self-edit/evolution baselines;
- successful protected transfer of a self-generated method change;
- that 21/24 attribution is sufficient causal accuracy for autonomous self-improvement;
- that the protected evaluator is immune to unknown gaming strategies;
- that P5 invented self-improving agents, evolutionary program search, issue-state memory, causal failure attribution or evaluator integrity.

## Strongest scoped conclusion

> **Fallible self-diagnosis and method proposal can be structurally non-self-authorizing: within the registered Self-ORION architecture, internal reasoning can determine what should be investigated or proposed next without obtaining authority to promote that proposal into the adopted scientific method.**

This is the P5 instance of ORION's broader scientific-status-transition programme: locally valid or fallible internal objects may guide research, but changing the standing of a different scientific object requires a separate protected transition.

## Follow-up performance science

The original H1–H4 campaign remains valuable as a separate empirical extension. A positive future campaign would show that the protected architecture also improves fresh development outcomes and reduces harmful transfer relative to strong self-improving baselines. A null or negative performance campaign would not retroactively erase the scoped non-self-promotion invariant; it would instead delimit the utility of the architecture.
