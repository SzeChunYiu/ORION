# Note to the TMLR Action Editor

**Manuscript:** *When Verification Axes Can Support Scientific Claims: Identifiability, Attainability, and Non-Compensatory Promotion*

**Submitted to:** Transactions on Machine Learning Research
**Review model:** double-blind; the manuscript carries no author, institution or repository identifier.

---

## What the paper claims

A benchmark score supports a competence claim only when the target is
identifiable, the compared interfaces can attain its terminal, and the panel
discriminates. The paper formalises those three requirements — through fibrewise
Bayes risk, a donor-factorization theorem, a terminal-adapter criterion, and
data-processing and total-variation bounds — and then measures a
non-compensatory promotion relation against them.

The central empirical result is a prospectively frozen, independently hosted
420-case protected campaign on a repaired subject: the governed pipeline made
0/360 false promotions against 180/360 for the strongest frozen mechanism proxy,
while both promoted 60/60 clean positives.

## What the paper does not claim, stated plainly

TMLR asks whether claims are supported, not whether results are exciting, so the
boundaries are drawn explicitly in the manuscript rather than left to inference:

- **The predeclared abstention-superiority hypothesis was null**, and is reported
  as null. It is not reinterpreted as a positive finding.
- Comparator arms are **protocol-matched reimplementations** under a common
  packet and budget. They are not executions of the cited authors' systems, and
  no claim is made about those systems in their native settings.
- Provenance, attribution, iterative verification, contamination detection and
  evaluator protection are **prior work** and are not claimed as novelties.
- The finite batteries support a factorization and attainability boundary, **not
  naturalistic superiority**. Several axes remain undetermined and are named as
  undetermined in the text rather than resolved by argument.
- The source programme authenticated 76/80 publication–archive–revision bridges
  but **zero eligible natural pairs**; the exact 106-class projection supplies no
  fresh compilation or signed build authority. Both are reported as shortfalls.

## Why TMLR

The contribution is a claim-support result: a formal criterion for when a
verification axis can carry a scientific claim, plus a protected measurement of
one relation against that criterion, with the negative and undetermined outcomes
retained. That fits TMLR's acceptance question directly, and does not depend on
a headline improvement.

## Reproducibility

Protected per-case labels remain in custody by design, because releasing them
would destroy the held-out property the campaign depends on. Everything that does
not compromise custody is released: safe aggregates, a post-run public slice,
code, hashes, the independent reproduction receipt, and the preregistration.
Section 10 lists every artifact with its path, and the tables and figures
regenerate from the released aggregates alone.

The repository is withheld during review and released, with a persistent
identifier, on publication.
