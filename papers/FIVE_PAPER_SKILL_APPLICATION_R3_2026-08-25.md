- iterate through research, review, revision, and editorial decision rather than treating prose polishing as the first step.

### 1.2 `nature-reviewer`

Applied functions:

- review independently of author intention;
- identify fatal, major, and minor concerns;
- require exact traceability from a concern to a theorem, counterexample, assumption, or missing evidence;
- separate mathematical validity from editorial priority.

Portfolio implementation:

- production realization, donor novelty, and exact-threshold claims were treated as major scope gates;
- the corrected finite-verifier ledger was treated as a reproducibility correction rather than hidden.

### 1.3 `atomic-claim-verification`

Applied functions:

- decompose broad contribution claims into atomic propositions;
- label every proposition by evidence type;
- distinguish written proof, finite replay, external donor theorem, unresolved claim, and deliberately excluded claim;
- block title/abstract promotion when a load-bearing atomic claim is unresolved.

Portfolio implementation:

- `FIVE_PAPER_ATOMIC_VERIFICATION_R8_2026-08-25.md` records the claim-level verdicts;
- Paper B's production factor-five claim and the non-quantum exact threshold remain explicitly unresolved;
- generic midpoint minimax and hitting-set mathematics are explicitly donor-owned.

### 1.4 `natural-scholarly-prose`

Applied functions:

- put the mathematical claim before its interpretation;
- define terms before compressing them into symbols;
- use paragraph-level logical progression rather than lists of disconnected advantages;
- calibrate verbs to evidence strength;
- state limitations where a reader needs them, not only at the end.

Portfolio implementation:

- every addendum begins with purpose and model scope;
- theorem, proof, application, and nonclaim are kept in that order;
- phrases implying generic speedup, physical advantage, truth, legal compliance, or exact generalized-Davenport resolution are excluded;
- application sections use “potential use,” “validation required,” and explicit nonclaims.

### 1.5 Deep-paper calibration and editor/reviewer decision architecture

Applied functions:

- identify the strongest defensible contribution, not the most marketable sentence;
- compare manuscript ambition with the maturity of its evidence package;
- return a paper-specific editorial disposition rather than one portfolio-wide label;
- preserve a visible route from reviewer concern to revision and final decision.

Portfolio implementation:

- Paper C advances as the top-tier theory candidate;
- Paper D advances after overlap and application gates;
- Paper A advances as a selective-specialist candidate;
- Paper B is held or merged until realization;
- the non-quantum paper advances as structural progress without an exact-value claim.

## 2. Writing refinements made during the pass

### 2.1 Titles

Titles are rewritten around proved mathematical objects and contrasts, avoiding production or exact-value implications not owned by the theorem.

### 2.2 Abstracts

Each proposed abstract follows a six-move structure:

1. concrete mathematical problem;
2. precise limitation of existing representation or proof language;
3. principal theorem;
4. strongest corollary or separation;
5. theorem-backed application implication;
6. explicit boundary where needed.

The polished candidates are stored in `FIVE_PAPER_POLISHED_TITLES_ABSTRACTS_R3_2026-08-25.md`.

### 2.3 Applications

Applications were rewritten from broad nouns into theorem-to-artifact contracts. For example:

- not “useful for AI,” but “identical frozen features impose an architecture-independent minimax radius on any model receiving only those features”;
- not “useful for regulation,” but “a per-license least Horn closure recomputes authority after direct refutation, while minimum seed intervention is a hitting-set optimization”;
- not “faster compiler,” but “a verified support-budget reduction changes the polynomial degree of one declared direct enumerator.”

### 2.4 Limitations

Limitations are located next to the affected theorem or application:

- axis separation beside direct-sum additivity;
- production realization beside certificate transfer;
- representation specificity beside minimax lower bounds;
- least-fixed-point positivity beside provenance applications;
- external Property C and unresolved `C_0(31)` beside the non-quantum boundary theorem.

## 3. Final prose guardrails

The final paper surfaces should retain these rules.

1. Use `prove`, `establish`, or `show` only for written theorem chains.
2. Use `verify` for executable finite checks and name the finite scope.
3. Use `suggest`, `motivate`, or `provide a route` for applications not empirically validated.
4. Do not use `intrinsic` for a certificate number lacking a production lower witness.
5. Do not call a representation globally sufficient or insufficient without naming the query.
6. Do not describe authority as truth.
7. Do not put the exact generalized-Davenport value in the non-quantum title or abstract.
8. Do not imply that classical donor theorems are original contributions.
