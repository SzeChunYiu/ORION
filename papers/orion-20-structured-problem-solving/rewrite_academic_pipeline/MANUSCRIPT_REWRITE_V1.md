# Closure-Relative Obstruction and Non-Unique Minimal Expansion in a Finite Method Grammar

## Abstract

Claims of “method invention” are easy to overstate when a solver is compared with search procedures that are definitionally restricted to a smaller method language. We isolate the formal object that survives such comparisons: **closure-relative obstruction**. For a registered language \(L\) with closure \(\mathrm{Cl}(L)\), a target outside that closure is unreachable by any procedure whose outputs are restricted to \(\mathrm{Cl}(L)\), regardless of search budget. Expanding the language can make the target reachable, but reachability alone does not identify a uniquely necessary primitive.

We study this distinction in a finite Boolean composition template. Exact enumeration shows that both AND and OR are admissible non-affine primitives and that the singleton bases \(\{\mathrm{AND}\}\) and \(\{\mathrm{OR}\}\) are both minimal expansions for the registered target family. No primitive is indispensable. The identity

\[
T_p(x,y,z)=p(x,y)\oplus p(x,z)\oplus p(y,z)
\]

cancels the affine difference between AND and OR across the three pairwise terms, so a complexity order may select AND while the closure structure itself does not. This exact alternative refutes a previous primitive-indispensability interpretation and terminates that promotion route.

A prospectively frozen multi-domain successor intended to test transferable method-space expansion executed zero scientific cases because its donor, evaluator, task and verifier inputs were absent. We therefore make no empirical invention, superiority or transfer claim. The contribution is a bounded formal separation between search inside a declared closure, expansion of that closure, minimal expansion under a fixed template, and unique necessity. Classical clone and algebraic-normal-form theory own the generic non-affinity facts; the present paper uses them to show why a closure certificate is not, by itself, evidence of invention or indispensable novelty.

## 1. Introduction

A system that solves a target by adding an operation unavailable to a baseline may appear to have invented a new method. That conclusion can be invalid for two different reasons. First, the baseline may have been denied the very candidate grammar used by the focal system, making the comparison a range restriction rather than a search-quality test. Second, more than one language expansion may solve the target, so one successful primitive is not uniquely necessary.

These two failure modes are conceptually distinct. **Closure-relative obstruction** says what cannot be reached without changing the language. **Primitive necessity** asks whether a particular change is required among all admissible changes. The first can hold while the second fails.

We analyze a finite method grammar in which these distinctions can be checked exactly. The resulting paper is deliberately narrower than the original “scientific invention” programme. The prospective empirical study designed for that broader claim did not execute, and an adversarial theorem audit found that several earlier headline formulations were stronger than the proofs in the repository. We therefore retain only the statements whose assumptions and proof obligations can be made explicit.

The central conclusions are:

1. a target outside a registered closure is unreachable by any procedure restricted to that closure;
2. adding an admissible primitive can make the target reachable;
3. in the studied majority-three Boolean template, both AND and OR are singleton minimal expansions;
4. therefore no one primitive is structurally indispensable under the registered grammar;
5. search “dominance” cannot be inferred when competing searches are not information-matched.

## 2. Registered method languages and closure

Let \(L\) be a finite set of primitives together with a fixed composition grammar. Its closure, \(\mathrm{Cl}(L)\), is the set of objects that can be generated by admissible compositions under that grammar.

The closure is always relative to the declared grammar and domain. This qualification matters. A target can be outside one registered closure and inside another after the domain, allowed composition depth, coefficient set or primitive vocabulary changes. “Outside closure” is therefore not an intrinsic statement about the target independent of representation.

For the finite setting used here, closure membership can be decided by exhaustive construction over the registered finite grammar. The paper does not generalize that decidability statement to unrestricted infinite grammars.

## 3. Obstruction is a range statement, not a search-quality theorem

**Proposition 1 (closure-relative unreachability).** If \(t\notin\mathrm{Cl}(L)\), then no procedure whose outputs are restricted to \(\mathrm{Cl}(L)\) can return \(t\).

The proof is immediate from the output-range assumption. Its value is not mathematical depth but scientific discipline: once an obstruction is established, increasing search time inside the same closure cannot solve the target.

The converse interpretation is unsafe. If a focal method receives an enlarged grammar \(L'\) and a comparator remains restricted to \(L\), then success under \(L'\) and failure under \(L\) do not demonstrate better search. They demonstrate different output ranges.

This distinction invalidates a broad “exhaustive-search dominance” reading of the earlier study. An information-matched enumerator supplied with the same candidate grammar can perform the same finite search. The correct conclusion is therefore closure-relative, not algorithmic superiority.

## 4. Certified expansion

Suppose \(t\notin\mathrm{Cl}(L)\) but \(t\in\mathrm{Cl}(L')\) for an enlarged language \(L'=L\cup E\). Then \(E\) witnesses an expansion that is sufficient for this target under the registered grammar.

That observation still does not establish that \(E\) is minimal, unique or scientifically novel. Three progressively stronger questions must be separated:

- **sufficiency:** does the enlarged language reach the target?
- **minimality:** does removing any element of the proposed expansion destroy reachability under the declared minimality order?
- **indispensability:** does every admissible minimal expansion contain a particular primitive?

Only the third licenses a statement that a specific primitive is necessary. A complexity-based tie-break may choose one minimal expansion without turning it into a structural necessity.

## 5. Exact Boolean template

The registered finite study uses a majority-three composition template built from a binary primitive \(p\):

\[
T_p(x,y,z)=p(x,y)\oplus p(x,z)\oplus p(y,z).
\]

Exact enumeration over the admissible two-input Boolean primitives identifies two non-affine candidates relevant to the target family: code 8 (AND) and code 14 (OR). Each singleton basis is sufficient under the frozen template.

The two primitives differ by affine terms. Under the three-pair XOR construction, those affine differences cancel. Consequently, the template is blind to the distinction that would be needed to establish unique primitive necessity.

The result is exact for the registered finite grammar. It does not claim that AND and OR are interchangeable in arbitrary circuits or arbitrary synthesis settings.

## 6. Minimality without indispensability

Exact enumeration gives two singleton minimal bases:

\[
\{\mathrm{AND}\},\qquad \{\mathrm{OR}\}.
\]

Each is minimal because the empty expansion cannot realize the target in the registered old closure, while the singleton expansion can. But the intersection of the minimal bases is empty. Therefore no primitive is indispensable.

A registered complexity order selects AND. That selection is an engineering or representational preference under the chosen order, not a theorem that OR is dispensable in every meaningful sense or that AND is uniquely necessary.

This distinction closes the earlier promotion attempt. The scientific statement that survives is “a non-affine expansion is required under this closure/template,” not “AND is the uniquely necessary invented primitive.” Classical clone and algebraic-normal-form theory already own the generic non-affinity content, so the donor-subtracted residual is the exact closure/minimality diagnosis for this registered grammar.

## 7. What the failed promotion teaches about method-invention benchmarks

A benchmark for method invention must prevent three substitutions.

First, a **restricted-range comparator** cannot stand in for an information-matched search baseline. If one arm receives the candidate grammar and another does not, the comparison tests language access.

Second, **one successful expansion** cannot stand in for unique necessity. Competing minimal expansions must be enumerated or otherwise ruled out under a declared order.

Third, **transfer intent** cannot stand in for executed transfer evidence. A target that becomes reachable in one finite template does not establish hidden, cross-domain or reusable method invention.

These requirements are stronger than checking whether a generated program is novel in surface form. The relevant object is the relation between old closure, expanded closure, donor-owned transformations and held-out consequences.

## 8. The unexecuted successor is not evidence

A broader successor was prospectively frozen over 480 planned cases in four domains, nine arms, three seeds and 12,960 planned run cells. Its purpose was to test donor-complete comparison, verifier-backed expansion, false-invention rate and held-out transfer.

The required frozen donor, evaluator, task and verifier inputs were absent. Zero scientific cells executed. All prospective hypotheses therefore remain untested rather than negative.

This zero-execution boundary is included because it prevents the formal paper from borrowing empirical authority from a protocol that never ran. The current manuscript makes no claim about verified solving superiority, transferable invention, false-invention reduction or cross-domain method discovery.

## 9. Relation to prior work

Program synthesis, CEGIS, theorem proving, planning, library learning and evolutionary search already study discovery within and beyond supplied grammars. Universal-algebra and Boolean clone theory characterize expressive closures of primitive sets. Algebraic normal form makes the AND/OR relationship in the present template unsurprising once exposed.

Accordingly, this paper does not claim a new general theory of expressive completeness or primitive bases. Its contribution is a bounded negative clarification for method-invention evaluation: closure obstruction is scientifically useful, but it must not be conflated with search superiority, and minimal expansion must not be conflated with unique primitive necessity.

## 10. Reproducibility

A reproducible package should expose the finite grammar, closure generator, target family, primitive coding, exact enumeration of minimal bases, and the algebraic identity relating the two surviving expansions. The older aspirational theorem labels should not be used as authority unless their statements are reconciled with the actual proofs.

The broader prospective protocol should remain archived with its zero-execution terminal so that later work cannot silently convert an acquisition failure into a scientific outcome.

## 11. Limitations

The formal result is grammar-relative and finite. Changing the composition template, primitive universe, domain or minimality order can change the closure and the set of minimal expansions. The paper does not prove a general decidability theorem for arbitrary method languages. It also does not establish that a system can autonomously identify scientifically useful expansions in open-ended tasks.

The AND/OR non-uniqueness result is a correction, not a universal claim that primitive necessity is impossible. Other grammars can have genuinely indispensable operations.

## 12. Conclusion

A target outside a registered method closure creates a real obstruction, but the obstruction says nothing about search quality when comparators have different output ranges. Expanding the language can remove that obstruction without making the chosen expansion unique. In the studied Boolean template, AND and OR form two alternative singleton minimal bases, so no primitive is indispensable even though a non-affine expansion is required. The result replaces an overbroad invention story with a narrower and more defensible formal lesson: reachability, minimality, indispensability and transfer are distinct claims and require distinct evidence.
