# Closure-Relative Obstruction and Non-Unique Minimal Expansion in a Finite Method Grammar

**ORION-20 — recursive academic-paper-pipeline final editorial master**  
**Paper type:** negative/formal correction  
**Primary route:** Theoretical Computer Science  
**Specialist fallback:** Journal of Automated Reasoning / synthesis-methods venue  
**Scientific authority:** proved closure obstruction and exact alternative minimal bases only

## Abstract

Claims of method invention are easy to overstate when a focal solver receives a richer output language than its baselines. We isolate the formal object that survives such comparisons: **closure-relative obstruction**. For a registered language `L` and composition grammar with closure `Cl(L)`, a target outside that closure is unreachable by every procedure whose outputs remain in `Cl(L)`, regardless of search budget. Enlarging the language may make the target reachable, but reachability alone does not identify a uniquely necessary primitive.

We study this distinction in a finite Boolean composition template. Exact enumeration finds two alternative singleton minimal expansions, `{AND}` and `{OR}`, for the registered target family. Neither primitive is indispensable. In the template

`T_p(x,y,z) = p(x,y) xor p(x,z) xor p(y,z)`,

the affine difference between AND and OR cancels across the three pairwise terms. A registered complexity order may prefer AND, but that preference is not structural necessity. The exact alternative refutes the earlier primitive-indispensability interpretation and terminates that promotion route.

A broader prospectively frozen successor planned 480 cases in four domains, nine arms, three seeds and 12,960 run cells. It executed zero scientific cells because its donor, evaluator, task and verifier inputs were absent. It therefore supplies no empirical invention, superiority or transfer evidence. The contribution is a bounded separation between search within a declared closure, expansion of that closure, minimal expansion under one template and unique necessity. Classical Boolean clone and algebraic-normal-form theory own the generic expressivity facts; this paper uses them to show why a closure certificate is not by itself evidence of invention.

## 1. Introduction

A system that solves a target after adding an operation unavailable to a baseline may appear to have invented a new method. Two distinct defects can invalidate that conclusion.

First, the comparison may be range-restricted. The focal system is allowed to emit objects from an enlarged grammar while the comparator is confined to the old closure. Failure of the comparator then demonstrates only that the target lies outside its allowed output set.

Second, the successful expansion may not be unique. Several alternative primitive sets can make the same target reachable. Selecting one by convenience or complexity does not prove that it is scientifically indispensable.

These defects correspond to separate questions:

- Is the target reachable in the old language?
- Which enlargements are sufficient?
- Which enlargements are minimal under a declared order?
- Is any particular primitive present in every admissible minimal enlargement?
- Does the enlargement transfer to held-out tasks?

The earlier ORION-20 framing collapsed these questions into a broad invention story. A theorem/proof audit found that several headline formulations were stronger than the repository's proofs, and a stronger exact alternative removed the claimed unique primitive. The present paper retains only the formal statements that survive those attacks.

## 2. Registered language and closure

Let `L` be a finite primitive set together with a fixed composition grammar. Its closure `Cl(L)` is the set of objects generated by admissible compositions. Every closure statement is relative to the domain, depth, coefficients and syntax declared by the grammar.

### Proposition 1 — closure-relative unreachability

If target `t` is not in `Cl(L)`, no procedure whose output range is contained in `Cl(L)` can return `t`.

The proposition is immediate from the output-range assumption. Its scientific importance is diagnostic. Increasing search time, heuristic sophistication or enumeration effort inside the same closure cannot reach a target outside it.

The proposition does **not** compare search quality between procedures with different output languages. If the focal arm receives `L'` and the comparator receives only `L`, success under `L'` and failure under `L` measure language access.

This observation removes the earlier “exhaustive-search dominance” claim. An information-matched enumerator given the same candidate grammar can perform the same finite search.

## 3. Expansion, minimality and indispensability

Suppose `t` is outside `Cl(L)` but inside `Cl(L union E)`. Then `E` is a sufficient expansion for that target under the registered grammar.

Three stronger claims require separate evidence.

1. **Minimality:** no proper sub-expansion of `E` remains sufficient under the chosen order.
2. **Uniqueness:** no other minimal expansion exists.
3. **Primitive indispensability:** every admissible minimal expansion contains a named primitive.

A single successful expansion establishes none of these automatically. A complexity tie-break can select one minimal solution without turning it into the only structurally valid solution.

## 4. Exact Boolean composition template

The registered finite study constructs a majority-three target from one binary primitive `p` through

`T_p(x,y,z) = p(x,y) xor p(x,z) xor p(y,z)`.

The admissible primitive universe is exhaustively enumerated. Two non-affine candidates reach the target family:

- Boolean code 8, AND;
- Boolean code 14, OR.

Each singleton basis is sufficient under the frozen template.

AND and OR differ by affine terms. Across the three pairwise XOR positions those affine differences cancel. The template therefore cannot distinguish them in the way needed by a unique-necessity claim.

The statement is exact for this grammar. It does not assert that AND and OR are interchangeable in arbitrary circuits or synthesis problems.

## 5. Minimal expansion is not unique

The empty expansion does not realize the target; either singleton expansion does. Hence both

`{AND}` and `{OR}`

are minimal. Their intersection is empty, so no primitive is indispensable.

A registered complexity order selects AND. That is an engineering preference within the declared order, not a theorem that AND is the uniquely invented operation or that OR lacks equivalent structural power in this template.

The strongest remaining scientific statement is:

> A non-affine expansion is required relative to the old registered closure, but the grammar does not identify one uniquely necessary primitive.

This is narrower than the original promotion claim and better supported.

## 6. What the correction teaches about invention benchmarks

A defensible method-invention benchmark must prevent four substitutions.

### 6.1 Restricted range for weak search

A baseline denied the candidate grammar cannot establish search superiority. The strongest baseline should receive the same information and admissible method language wherever the intended claim concerns search rather than expressivity.

### 6.2 One solution for unique necessity

Finding one successful method is insufficient. Competing minimal expansions must be enumerated or ruled out under a declared mathematical order.

### 6.3 Surface novelty for donor-subtracted novelty

A generated expression can look new while instantiating a donor-owned algebraic transformation. Classical clone and algebraic-normal-form theory receive first right of refusal.

### 6.4 Protocol intent for executed transfer

A frozen successor is not evidence until its cases execute, its outputs are independently checked and its terminal is bound to the actual result.

## 7. The zero-execution successor

The broad successor was frozen to test donor-complete comparison, verifier-backed expansion, false-invention rate and held-out transfer across four domains. Its design contained 480 cases, nine arms, three seeds and 12,960 planned cells.

The required donor, evaluator, task and verifier inputs were absent. Zero scientific cells executed. The correct disposition is not negative performance and not partial support. It is acquisition/execution failure with no scientific result.

The manuscript therefore makes no claim about:

- transferable method invention;
- verified solving superiority;
- false-invention reduction;
- cross-domain reuse;
- autonomous discovery of useful new primitives.

The archived protocol remains valuable as the next valid test, but it contributes no outcome authority to the current paper.

## 8. Relation to prior work

Program synthesis, CEGIS, theorem proving, planning, library learning and evolutionary search already study search over supplied grammars. Universal algebra and Boolean clone theory characterize expressive primitive closures. Algebraic normal form explains the AND/OR relation exposed here.

The paper does not claim a new general completeness or decidability theorem. Its residual is a bounded negative clarification for evaluating method invention: closure obstruction, search quality, minimality, indispensability and transfer are different claims and should have different comparators and evidence.

## 11. Limitations

The formal result is finite and grammar-relative. Changing the domain, composition template, primitive universe, depth or minimality order can change the closure and minimal bases. Other grammars can have a genuinely indispensable primitive. The present result does not show that autonomous systems cannot invent useful methods; it shows that this registered evidence did not establish that claim.

## 12. Conclusion

A target outside a registered method closure creates a real obstruction, but the obstruction is about output range rather than search quality. Expanding the language can remove that obstruction without making the chosen expansion unique. In the registered Boolean template, AND and OR are alternative singleton minimal bases, so no primitive is indispensable even though a non-affine expansion is required.

The corrected result replaces an overbroad invention story with a formal lesson that can improve future benchmarks: reachability, minimality, indispensability and transfer must be tested separately, and an unexecuted successor must remain evidence-free.
