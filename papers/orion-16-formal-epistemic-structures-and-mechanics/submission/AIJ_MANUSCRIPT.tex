\title{Scientific Admissibility Is Not Computational Correctness: A Contract Theory for Repair, Effects, and Authority}

\begin{abstract}
Autonomous scientific systems increasingly combine dependency repair, incremental recomputation, epistemic update, typed effects, authorization, and provenance. These mechanisms can determine whether a transition is computationally valid without deciding whether a scientific certificate is still entitled to stand. We formalize that missing layer as a history-aware scientific-admissibility contract with faithful semantic footprints, hard evidence and authority requirements, typed effects, invariants, provenance, residual obligations, and explicit failure terminals. The theory yields four positive results. First, root-inclusive affected-set reopening is safe under support-sound dependencies. Second, it is minimax inclusion-minimal for graph-only policies once the compatible semantics class satisfies an explicit affected-realizability condition; a spurious-edge construction proves that this premise is exactly what graph soundness alone lacks. Third, deterministic mechanics commute at the level of current scientific state under faithful full scientific separation, while audit history is preserved modulo swaps of independent commits; a hidden-read construction establishes the necessity of footprint fidelity. Fourth, typed erasure is not fully abstract for scientific admissibility: two contracts can have identical bare computational and dependency behavior yet differ in whether commit is scientifically authorized. Protected preservation/revalidation, hard-obligation persistence, and sequential authority non-escalation complete the contract. Standard-library checkers exhaust bounded DAG, changed-set, preservation, authority, obligation, footprint, and recursion cases and freeze the theorem premises against regression. The resulting contribution is a reusable interface above donor repair/effect/authorization mechanisms: computational preservation is necessary but not sufficient for scientific certification, and the contract states the exact additional conditions under which repair, reuse, and composition remain scientifically admissible.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} autonomous scientific agents; epistemic state; truth maintenance; scientific certification; effect systems; provenance; authorization

\section{Introduction}
A computation can be correct yet scientifically unauthorized. A dependency graph can identify which derived values might need recomputation without establishing which scientific certificates remain entitled to stand. A type-correct requested effect can lack the evidence or authority needed to mutate a protected scientific coordinate. Later computational success can coexist with an earlier verification obligation that remains open.

These distinctions matter for autonomous science because agent state is heterogeneous. Some objects are ordinary computational values; others are claims whose standing depends on evidence lineage, measurement semantics, independent verification, coverage, or protected evaluation. Treating both kinds of objects as interchangeable mutable state creates a failure mode in which computational continuity is silently converted into epistemic continuity.

The surrounding fields already solve substantial parts of this problem. Truth-maintenance systems (TMS/ATMS) maintain beliefs under changing justifications \cite{doyle1979,dekleer1986a,dekleer1986b}. Dynamic epistemic logic and belief revision formalize structured epistemic change \cite{agm1985,del2007}. Self-adjusting computation tracks dynamic dependencies and propagates change \cite{acar2002}. Contemporary agent systems add typed effects and residual obligations \cite{etas2026}, evidence-backed pre-effect authorization \cite{fava2026}, dependency-guided diagnosis and repair \cite{agenttether2026,rollback2026}, and typed decision graphs with preservation of unaffected artifacts \cite{boardroom2026}. P6 therefore treats these capabilities as donor substrate rather than rediscovering them.

The central question is what additional semantics make a computationally valid transition \emph{scientifically admissible}. The answer is a contract layer that keeps computation/support preservation distinct from certification authority. This paper contributes four theorem-level results. First, safe dependency repair is root-inclusive: a changed certified root must be reconsidered together with potentially affected descendants. Second, graph safety and graph minimality are different properties; affected realizability supplies the missing premise under which root-inclusive reopening becomes minimax inclusion-minimal for graph-only policies. Third, mechanic composition requires operationally faithful scientific footprints and then commutes on current scientific state modulo independent history order. Fourth, erasing scientific obligations, commit authority, and provenance is not fully abstract for admissibility, so computationally identical transitions can have different scientific standing.

The countermodels strengthen these results by making their premises sharp. Conservative/spurious edges explain exactly why support soundness does not imply minimality, and hidden ambient reads explain exactly why declared separation without footprint fidelity does not imply commutation. The resulting theory is not a monolithic replacement for donor systems: when the scientific dimensions are inert, donor-native judgments are recovered unchanged. Its purpose is to provide the common scientific contract needed when those systems are composed around certified state.

\section{Related work as donor structure}
\subsection{Truth maintenance and dependency repair}
Doyle's TMS and de Kleer's ATMS establish justification-based maintenance, dependency-directed revision, multiple support environments, and preservation under independent support \cite{doyle1979,dekleer1986a,dekleer1986b}. Recent memory-augmented-agent repair work likewise tracks typed dependencies from stored state to actions, invalidates downstream effects, preserves independent trusted support, and selectively replays affected computation \cite{rollback2026}. BoardroomAI similarly uses typed decision graphs and dependency-aware propagation after human interventions \cite{boardroom2026}. These works own selective invalidation and preservation. P6 uses them as the repair substrate.

\subsection{Epistemic update and incremental computation}
AGM-style belief revision and dynamic epistemic logic formalize rational and informational state change \cite{agm1985,del2007}. Adaptive and self-adjusting computation formalizes dependence-aware recomputation after input changes \cite{acar2002}. These traditions demonstrate why state change and recomputation should not be presented as new. P6 adds a separate certification\slash admissibility question to their outputs.

\subsection{Effects, authorization, and evidence}
ETAS makes requested\slash handled\slash denied\slash committed effects and residual obligations visible in an effect-typed agent language \cite{etas2026}. FAVA uses evidence-backed permission graphs and deterministic authorization before effectful execution \cite{fava2026}. Current action-evidence specifications also emphasize exact action binding, heterogeneous evidence roles, freshness, and a separate authorization decision \cite{aeb2026,aec2026}. Validation studies of repair agents show that a passing validation event can lack bug-discriminating information \cite{validation2026}. Accordingly, P6 claims neither effect typing nor evidence binding. The scientific layer asks whether the evidence/authority available actually discharges the certification obligations attached to the target scientific transition.

\section{Epistemic mechanic contracts}
Let a typed epistemic state be
\[
E=(\nu,s,D,P,O,A,H),
\]
where $\nu$ is the valuation of typed scientific coordinates, $s$ maps claims to scientific status, $D$ is a dependency abstraction, $P$ stores content/provenance identities, $O$ is the active obligation set, $A$ is authority state, and $H$ is immutable request\slash transition\slash failure\slash revocation history.

A mechanic is a contract
\[
m=(R,W,Pre,Req,Eff,\tau,Emit,Fail,Inv),
\]
with semantic read and write footprints $R,W$, a precondition, hard/soft requirements, typed requested effects, a transition relation, emitted obligations\slash provenance, failure terminals, and invariants. A commit is admissible only if the computational transition is valid, every hard requirement needed for commit is discharged, the write effect is within the faithful declared interface, commit authority is current and scope-correct, new scientific objects carry provenance, and any undischarged hard obligation remains visible.

This yields the basic non-implication
\[
\text{computationally executable}\not\Rightarrow\text{scientifically admissible}.
\]

\section{Safe root-inclusive repair}
Let $Q_{cert}(E)$ be the certified claims in $E$. For a changed set $X$, define
\[
Aff_D(E,X)=(X\cap Q_{cert}(E))\cup(Desc_D(X)\cap Q_{cert}(E)).
\]
The first term matters: a strict descendant operator omits a certified claim that is itself directly changed.

\begin{definition}[Support soundness]
A dependency abstraction $D$ is support-sound for a semantics class if every actual support whose mutation can invalidate a certified claim is represented by the changed claim itself or by an ancestor path to that claim.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Root-inclusive safety]
If only elements of $X$ change and $D$ is support-sound, invalidating or protectedly revalidating every claim in $Aff_D(E,X)$ leaves no certification standing whose validity may have been destroyed by the change.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A directly changed certified claim is included by the root term. For any other claim whose certification may be invalidated, support soundness supplies an ancestor path from some changed element, so it lies in the descendant term. Every potentially invalidated certificate is therefore reconsidered.
\end{proof}

The theorem is a safety result and permits conservative edges.

\section{Why graph soundness is not graph minimality}
Consider a graph containing $x\rightarrow q$ while every semantics compatible with the actual system says that $q$ depends only on independent coordinate $y$ and is invariant under $x$. The graph can still be support-sound because it omits no real support, yet reopening $q$ after changing $x$ is unnecessary. Thus support soundness alone does not imply that every graph descendant must be reopened.

\begin{definition}[Affected realizability]
For $(E,X)$, a compatible semantics class is affected-realizable if for every $q\in Aff_D(E,X)$ there exists a graph-indistinguishable admissible semantics in which the declared change invalidates the old certification of $q$ (directly for a changed root or through necessary support along a path for a descendant).
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Graph-only minimax minimality]
Among policies that observe only the graph-visible state and must be sound for every affected-realizable compatible semantics, root-inclusive affected-set reopening is inclusion-minimal unless a protected revalidation establishes invariance for the exact change.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Suppose a uniformly sound graph-only policy preserves an affected claim without protected revalidation. Affected realizability provides a compatible, graph-indistinguishable semantics in which that exact claim is invalidated by the change. The policy makes the same preservation decision and leaves a stale certificate, contradicting uniform soundness. The affected-set policy reconsiders all such claims and is therefore inclusion-minimal in this minimax information model.
\end{proof}

The additional premise is substantive. The theorem does not assert that every recorded dependency edge in a real agent is semantically necessary.

\section{Preservation versus revalidation}
Conservative dependency structure can reopen more certificates than a richer proof system needs to. A preservation certificate may keep a downstream certificate standing if it binds the exact changed set, content identity, scope, epoch, protected issuer, and a proof that the original derivation remains valid. A directly changed certified claim is different: its old standing cannot be preserved merely by continuity of identifier. It may end certified only through a new protected revalidation\slash re-certification derivation after the mutation.

This distinction prevents a changed claim from carrying its own previous authority through mutation while still permitting legitimate protected re-certification.

\section{Composition and history}
The commutation result below is bound as
\texttt{P6.COMMUTE.RW\_NONINTERFERENCE.V1}.
Declared footprints are useful only if enforced. A mechanic is read-footprint faithful when its effects, outputs, obligation emissions, and failure choices depend only on declared semantic inputs or explicitly registered external inputs. It is write-footprint faithful when every committed mutation lies within the declared write/effect interface.

A hidden ambient read defeats syntactic separation: if $m$ secretly reads mutable variable $z$ while $n$ writes $z$, order can change $m$ even though the declared footprints appear disjoint.

Let $\pi_{sci}(E)$ omit only ordered audit history. Two mechanics are fully scientifically separated when their faithful state footprints do not interfere and neither changes an obligation, authority fact, provenance object, dependency, resource, or declared external input consumed by the other.

\begin{theorem}[History-aware commutation]
For deterministic admissible footprint-faithful mechanics $m,n$ that are fully scientifically separated, whenever both sequential compositions are defined,
\[
\pi_{sci}(n(m(E)))=\pi_{sci}(m(n(E))).
\]
Their histories need not be equal as sequences; they are equivalent under the congruence generated by swapping adjacent independent commit events.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Footprint fidelity and full separation make each mechanic observe the same semantic inputs in either order and write disjoint scientific outputs. All non-history scientific components therefore agree. The audit log intentionally records a different event order, but the two events are independent and differ only by an allowed adjacent swap.
\end{proof}

This closes the other contract forms conservatively. Sequential composition is
defined only when postconditions establish the next preconditions and residual
obligations/authority restrictions cross the boundary. A conditional requires
an authorized footprint-faithful guard and a common declared join contract;
the selected branch's obligations and provenance remain visible. Independent
parallel composition denotes the equivalence class of the two sequential
interleavings under the theorem's full separation premises. Recursive
composition additionally requires the well-founded or cycle-rejection rule
below. These clauses specify well-formedness; they do not claim a new process
algebra.

\section{Obligation and authority conservation}
A non-escalating mechanic may retain authority, narrow it, or obtain new authority only from a protected root. By induction, a finite sequential composition of such mechanics is non-escalating.

Hard obligations obey a parallel conservation law: if an admissible step emits a hard scientific obligation, later computational success cannot delete it. It persists until an authorized discharge rule is applied with valid evidence and lineage. If the required evidence becomes unavailable, an explicit inability-to-check terminal is sounder than silent success.

These laws are not claims that effect systems or authorization are new. Their role is to ensure that adding repair/recomputation does not silently erase scientific obligations.

\section{Recursive audit and protected authority}
Recursive audit terminates if every recursive call decreases a well-founded rank or if a cycle detector terminates repeated audit states. Same-state unguarded self-audit immediately yields an infinite trace. Further, if a candidate mechanism can rewrite both its own admission predicate and all evidence consulted by that predicate, internal acceptance cannot imply an externally defined correctness property: a constant-accepting policy is reachable without satisfying the external property. The general authority problem belongs to authorization theory and ORION's dedicated authority paper; here it is a boundary condition for recursive mechanics.

\section{Typed-erasure separation}
Define $Erase(m)$ to remove hard scientific obligations, commit authority, and scientific provenance constraints while retaining the bare computational transition and ordinary dependency behavior.

\begin{theorem}[Erasure is not fully abstract for scientific admissibility]
There exist mechanics $m_1,m_2$ with identical erasures but different scientific-admissibility judgments.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Give both mechanics the same deterministic state transition and dependency behavior. Let $m_1$ have the required independent evidence and current in-scope commit authority, while $m_2$ lacks one mandatory scientific premise. Their erased computational semantics are identical. Under the full contract, only $m_1$ has an admissible commit.
\end{proof}

This is the principal cross-donor discriminator. It does not say that TMS, incremental computation, effect systems, or access-control systems fail at their own goals; it says that those goals do not automatically decide the standing of a scientific certificate.

\section{Conservative donor embeddings and falsifiers}
When scientific obligations, authority, and provenance are inert, ordinary transition systems are recovered. TMS/ATMS behavior is recovered when status follows justification\slash dependency maintenance. Self-adjusting computation is recovered when dependencies govern recomputation and all scientific commits are trivially admissible. Typed-effect systems embed their requested/committed effects and residual obligations. Evidence-backed authorization embeds permission/provenance dependencies as authority premises.

A purported P6 embedding is rejected if it changes a donor-native verdict without an explicitly stronger scientific premise. The separate-paper claim should also be rejected if a donor-complete formalism is shown to prove the same scientific-admissibility interface under equivalent semantics, if ORION embeddings require ad hoc exceptions that destroy the common contract, or if the added scientific dimensions never change a relevant decision.

\section{Deterministic verification}
The repository contains standard-library checkers that exhaust all forward dependency DAGs over four ordered nodes and all nonempty changed subsets, preservation\slash revalidation combinations, residual-obligation cases, authority compositions, history-aware commutation cases, recursive cycles, typed-erasure witnesses, spurious-edge countermodels, affected-realizability omissions, and hidden-read counterexamples. These checks support theorem boundaries and regression resistance. They are not measurements of deployed-agent performance.

The strongest future empirical comparator is an integrated donor product containing repair, incremental computation, typed effects, authorization, and provenance with correct interfaces. If that product implements the same scientific contract, behavioral equality is an expected and informative result: it would establish that the scientific-admissibility semantics are portable across implementation architectures.

\section{Discussion and implications for AI systems}
The practical implication is architectural: an autonomous research system should not reuse the same boolean notion of ``valid'' across computation, scientific certification, and permission. Dependency information can justify what to recompute; provenance can identify what evidence was used; authorization can control an effect; none alone proves that a scientific obligation remains discharged after a mutation. Exposing these interfaces creates more opportunities to fail closed without forcing full reset.

The framework also clarifies why repair systems can be both computationally conservative and scientifically unsafe. Preserving an unaffected computational node is desirable; preserving a scientific certificate attached to that node additionally requires that its scientific premises remain valid. Conversely, a conservative dependency graph may reopen too much, motivating protected preservation proofs rather than weakening the safety contract.

\section{Limitations}
The framework assumes that scientific obligation schemas, trusted roots, and preservation\slash revalidation rules are correctly specified. The bounded checkers establish the theorem premises on exhaustive finite families rather than claiming completeness for arbitrary infinite state spaces. A graph can be support-sound yet highly conservative, which is exactly why affected realizability is stated as an explicit modeling premise rather than smuggled into soundness. The paper establishes formal separation, safety, minimality conditions, and composition laws; deployed-agent performance is an empirical extension rather than a prerequisite for those theorem-level results. A donor-complete formalism proving the same scientific-admissibility interface under equivalent semantics would establish an alternative implementation of the same contract, not invalidate the mathematical separation proved here.

\section{Conclusion}
P6 establishes scientific admissibility as a semantic layer that survives maximal donor engulfing. Truth maintenance, incremental computation, epistemic update, typed effects, authorization, provenance, and dependency-guided repair can all be correct in their native senses while leaving unanswered whether a scientific certificate may remain standing after change. The proposed contract closes that interface with explicit evidence, obligation, provenance, authority, and footprint conditions.

The theorem set is correspondingly positive and sharp. Root-inclusive reopening is safe under support soundness and minimax inclusion-minimal under affected realizability. Protected preservation can avoid unnecessary invalidation without laundering a directly changed root's old certificate. Fully separated footprint-faithful mechanics commute on current scientific state while retaining auditable history. Hard obligations and non-escalating authority persist through later computation. Most importantly, typed erasure is not fully abstract: computationally identical transitions can have different scientific admissibility.

These results define a reusable contract for autonomous scientific systems whose state carries certification rather than merely values. The countermodels do not weaken that contract; they identify the exact assumptions that make its safety, minimality, and composition theorems true. When donor systems implement the same semantics, the expected outcome is architectural portability of the contract, not a need to manufacture a weaker or proprietary claim.
