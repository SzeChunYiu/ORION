## What the closure costs the paper

Two of the new dispositions are more than box-filling, and both bear on §3.

**Iterated revision is ORION-16's nearest parent, and it was entirely absent from
the A6 corpus.** Darwiche & Pearl's move from knowledge bases to **epistemic
states** — where the state carries everything needed for coherent reasoning,
*including the revision strategy itself*, so that revision can be iterated — is
not adjacent to "formal epistemic structures and mechanics"; it is the same
move. §3's residual-obligation preservation theorem and its recursion and
fixed-point claims must be stated as **satisfying, violating, or refining the DP
postulates**, not as new territory. One mitigating fact: those postulates are
contested, so a considered disagreement with them is a recognised position
rather than a defect.

**Dynamic epistemic logic already owns "an epistemic change is an object".**
Action models act on states and product update already handles who observes
what. Whatever ORION-16's mechanics do beyond a product update is where its
residual lives, and it should be claimed at that width.

A third is a narrow opening rather than a cost: CoALA is explicitly a
**taxonomy, not a formal semantics**, so the language-agent literature does not
own a formal mechanics. That is a real gap, and a small one.
