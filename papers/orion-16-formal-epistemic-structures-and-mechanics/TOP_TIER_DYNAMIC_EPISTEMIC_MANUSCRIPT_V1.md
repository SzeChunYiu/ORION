## Abstract

Scientific state changes invalidate some certificates and leave others untouched. ORION-16 represents certificates as typed hyperedges over dynamic scientific coordinates, with read/write effects and alternative support families. It absorbs incremental computation, build systems, proof maintenance, truth-maintenance systems, and certified transitions. We prove donor-native conservativity, the exact backward dependency slice requiring revalidation, read/write commutation, exact alternative-support revocation, and complexity boundaries for minimum repair. We then replayed 16 pre-existing content-bound transitions from four domains—ontology/regime change, proof-library change, scientific-rule-graph change, and scientific-workflow change—under four matched policies and 64 total policy decisions. Exact dynamic revalidation and the ideal support-hypergraph product matched every frozen gold disposition, retained five valid certificates, retained zero invalid certificates, performed four revalidation actions, and caused zero unnecessary reopening. Native dependency selection retained invalid certificates in all four domains, while full reset produced unnecessary reopening and lower exact accuracy. These results establish a bounded strict extension over native dependency selection and equivalence to the ideal product on the declared replay class; they do not establish prospective deployed-system superiority or universal minimality.
