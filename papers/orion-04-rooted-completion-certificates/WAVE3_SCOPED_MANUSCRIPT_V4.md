## Abstract

For a finite abelian group \(G\), the generalized Davenport constant \(D_k(G)\) is the least \(\ell\) such that every sequence over \(G\) of length at least \(\ell\) contains \(k\) pairwise disjoint nonempty zero-sum subsequences. We determine the fourth generalized Davenport constant of the elementary abelian group \(C_5^3\):
\[
D_4(C_5^3)=30,
\]
and consequently \(D_k(C_5^3)=5k+10\) for every \(k\ge2\). The lower bound \(D_k(C_5^3)\ge 5k+10\) is a direct instance of Theorem 4.1 of Freeze and Schmid (Discrete Math. 310 (2010) 3373–3389), so the contribution of this paper is the matching upper bound: the Freeze–Schmid bound is tight for \(C_5^3\) from \(k=2\) onward. The proof is finite and computer-assisted. The remaining case reduces to the nonexistence of a length-31 zero-sum sequence with no nonempty zero-sum subsequence of length at most five; a saturation argument restricts positive multiplicities to \(1,2,4\), the admissible support patterns form a 60-pattern family, and a rank/plane decomposition covers them by 78 exhaustive branches, each exhausted by two independently implemented exact searches with zero survivors in all 156 recorded runs. The entire cover was additionally regenerated by an independently written reconstruction that imports none of the original search code, and the acceptance pipeline rejects a battery of hostile mutations of the equations, the branch structure, and the result bindings. External human proof review, externally checked per-branch certificates, and final custody sign-off are deferred to journal review or later independent validation and are not represented as already performed.

## 1. Problem and theorem

Throughout, a sequence over \(C_5^3\) is a finite multiset of group elements; it is *zero-sum* (or *total-zero*) if its elements sum to zero, and *short-zero-free* if it contains no nonempty zero-sum subsequence of length at most five, the exponent of \(C_5^3\). For \(k\ge1\), \(D_k(G)\) is the least \(\ell\) such that every sequence of length at least \(\ell\) contains \(k\) pairwise disjoint nonempty zero-sum subsequences, and \(C_0(G)\) denotes the set of lengths \(\ell\) for which every total-zero sequence of length \(\ell\) contains a nonempty zero-sum subsequence of length at most \(\exp(G)\).

The lower-bound half of the picture is published prior work. Freeze and Schmid, Theorem 4.1 (Discrete Math. 310 (2010) 3373–3389), applied to \(G=C_5^3\) with \(s=3\), \(t=1\) (their hypothesis \(s(s-1)/2\le r-t+1\) holds with equality) and \(\delta=1\) since \(n_t=5\) is odd, gives
\[
D_k(C_5^3)\ \ge\ \mathsf D^*(C_5^3) + 3\lfloor 5/2\rfloor + 1 + (k-2)\cdot 5 \ =\ 5k+10\qquad(k\ge2).
\]
Earlier work in this programme established the width-one upper companion
\[
D_k(C_5^3)\le 5k+11\qquad(k\ge4),
\]
together with the implication, via the Freeze–Schmid recurrence in an archived instantiation,
\[
D_4(C_5^3)=30\Longrightarrow D_k(C_5^3)=5k+10\qquad(k\ge2).
\]
The one unresolved numerical question was therefore whether a length-31 total-zero sequence can be short-zero-free — equivalently, whether the Freeze–Schmid lower bound is attained.

> **Theorem 1.** No length-31 total-zero sequence over \(C_5^3\) is free of nonempty zero-sum subsequences of lengths one through five. Consequently \(31\in C_0(C_5^3)\), \(D_4(C_5^3)=30\), and \(D_k(C_5^3)=5k+10\) for every \(k\ge2\).

The proof is finite and computer-assisted. The generic recurrence, localization, saturation, and projective-space results used as premises are prior mathematics and are not claimed here.

## 2. Finite grammar and branch cover

Let \(S\) be a hypothetical obstruction: a length-31 total-zero short-zero-free sequence. A saturation argument restricts every positive multiplicity in \(S\) to \(1,2,4\). If \(a_1,b_2,c_4\) count the support points carrying those multiplicities, then
\[
a_1+b_2+c_4=s,\qquad a_1+2b_2+4c_4=31.
\]
These equations generate exactly 60 admissible multiplicity patterns. Prior exact work excludes support size at most 13. The remaining support sizes 14--31 are covered by a rank/plane decomposition with 51 lower-support branches and 27 upper-support branches.

For support sizes 14--22, the high-multiplicity part of \(S\) either forces rank three through the bound \(\eta(C_5^2)=13\), supplies a rank-three basis through four multiplicity-four points, or enters an explicit rank-two plane case when \(c_4=3\). For support sizes 23--31, the high-support set is partitioned by rank and multiplicity profile; normalized bases and the same \(\eta(C_5^2)\) restriction remove the impossible rank-two cases. The resulting cover contains exactly 78 branches.

## 3. Exact search

The archived computation attacks each branch with two exact search engines built on different state representations. Each engine tracks all group sums reachable at exact weights zero through five and rejects a partial sequence as soon as zero becomes reachable in a forbidden weight layer. Across the 78 branches and two engines, all 156 recorded runs complete, agree on the fixed branch fingerprints, and report zero solutions in every branch.

A replay on a second machine, using the same archived source but a different compiler major version and native instruction target, reproduces the exact result and cover digests. This replay is an independent execution of the same source; it is not counted as an independent implementation.

## 4. Independent reconstruction and adversarial verification

An independent reconstruction, written separately from the original search code, imports neither the original branch list, nor the C engines, nor their fingerprints or generator output. Starting only from the multiplicity equations and the rank/plane lemmas, it regenerates:

- the 60 admissible multiplicity patterns;
- the projective-line admissibility structure;
- the 51 lower-support branches;
- the 27 upper-support branches;
- the exact 78-branch partition.

Only after this reconstruction is complete is the regenerated branch set compared with the archived cover. The comparison is exact: no branch is missing and none is extra.

The reconstruction uses a materially different dynamic-programming representation in Python rather than a line-by-line translation of the original C search. In addition, an SMT-solver calibration exercises the primitive disjoint-zero-sum predicate on published small-group control values and agrees with a separately implemented brute-force occurrence assignment on exhaustive small domains. Where several verification routes exist, acceptance requires unanimity: a disagreeing or missing route terminates the check adversely or as unverifiable, never by majority vote.

Hostile controls confirm that acceptance depends on mathematical content rather than file identity: an omitted branch, an altered total-length equation, a deleted or modified rank split, an altered \(\eta\) threshold, a forged zero-solution digest, and a checksum-consistent forged nonzero-survivor result are all rejected.

## 5. Proof of Theorem 1

Assume a length-31 total-zero short-zero-free sequence exists. Saturation forces its positive multiplicities into \(\{1,2,4\}\), hence its support pattern belongs to the 60-pattern family generated by the equations of Section 2. Prior work excludes support size at most 13. The independently reconstructed rank/plane decomposition covers every remaining admissible support pattern by one of the 78 branches. The archived exact computation exhausts every branch under two representations and records zero survivors in all 156 runs. Therefore the assumed obstruction does not exist, and \(31\in C_0(C_5^3)\). Combined with the previously established corridor, this gives \(D_4(C_5^3)=30\); the Freeze–Schmid recurrence, in the archived instantiation, then yields \(D_k(C_5^3)=5k+10\) for every \(k\ge2\). \(\square\)

## 6. Priority and novelty scope

A dedicated priority audit, refreshed on 2026-09-02 with programme-internal vocabulary stripped from every query, found no retrieved source that already establishes the exact value \(D_4(C_5^3)=30\) or any exact \(D_k(C_5^3)\), and found that the lower bound \(D_k(C_5^3)\ge 5k+10\) \((k\ge2)\) is a direct instance of Freeze–Schmid Theorem 4.1, as stated in Section 1; the dated audit record accompanies the research archive. Two facts support the exact value being otherwise open: \(\eta(C_5^3)\) has no published exact value (Fan–Gao–Zhong, J. Number Theory 131 (2011), prove \(s(C_5^3)=\eta(C_5^3)+4\) without determining either constant), so no exact \(D_k(C_5^3)\) follows from the known upper-bound machinery; and the current state of the art (Zhong, Combinatorica 2025) records exact \(D_k\) values only for rank at most two, elementary 2-groups of rank at most five, and \(C_3^3\). MathSciNet and zbMATH were not accessible to this audit and remain explicitly unchecked; the audit closes the local literature task but is not an omniscient global priority certificate. The paper therefore states the exact theorem and its relation to the closest retrieved work while avoiding absolute “first”, “unique”, or equivalent language.

The nearest published exact rank-three companion sharpens what is new here. For \(C_3^3\) (Bhowmik–Schlage-Puchta 2007, as recorded by Freeze–Schmid Remark 5.3), the known values are \(D_1=7\), \(D_2=11\), and \(D_k=3k+6\) for \(k\ge3\): the Freeze–Schmid bound \(D_k(C_3^3)\ge 3k+5\) is attained at \(k=2\) but strictly exceeded from \(k=3\) onward. The present theorem shows the opposite regime for \(C_5^3\): the Freeze–Schmid bound is attained for every \(k\ge2\). Where the tightness threshold sits as a function of \(p\) for \(C_p^3\) is, to our retrieval, open; the \(p=3\) versus \(p=5\) contrast is stated as data rather than explained.

The paper does not claim novelty for the generic Davenport machinery, the saturation argument, the projective-space methods, the recurrence, or exact-search methodology in the abstract, and it does not claim the lower bound, which is Freeze–Schmid's. The contribution claimed here is the matching upper bound and hence exactness — the exact bounded theorem, its finite branch reduction, and the independently reconstructed verification path for this instance.
