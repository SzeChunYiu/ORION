**Purpose:** private foundation record for the canonical reader-facing TMLR manuscript.

## Research canon

- The inferential unit is one prospectively frozen unresolved scientific question.
- Exactly three valid questions are scored: V0, ORION-03-R1/QG-19 and ORION-03-R2/QG-20.
- Two earlier candidate questions are retained as contaminated protocol events and receive no retrospective score.
- Both instruments agreed on the primary diagnosis and primary next move in all three valid questions.
- The third valid question is an explicit agreement-with-misdiagnosis counterexample: both diagnoses are misaligned under the predeclared map, while both selected moves are aligned.
- The support-threshold stress test contains 53 exact rows and zero unrestricted-versus-support-two gaps. The theorem boundary remains open.
- The reweighted-objective census is exhaustive only for 729 ordered one-object instances and 38,760 reorder-quotiented two-object multisets, 39,489 cases total. It has zero original-predicate/label mismatches, no one-object positives and one two-object positive.
- D2 and D3 are unresolved limitations of the frozen instrument. They were not repaired between cases and did not trigger in any of the three valid cases.
- Current completion gates pass and bind the scientific results. They grant no separate scientific authority to this paper.

## Allowed claims

1. The prospective contract was executed on three valid unresolved questions.
2. Agreement was observed on both scored coordinates in each case, reported only row by row.
3. Case C is a prospectively scored counterexample to agreement as diagnostic validation.
4. A diagnosis can be misaligned while its selected discriminating experiment remains aligned in one observed case.
5. Contamination and malformed-success risks can be retained fail-closed rather than repaired into valid scores.
6. The 53-row panel and 39,489-case census establish only their declared finite scopes.

## Forbidden claims

- any agreement, diagnostic-accuracy or experiment-accuracy rate;
- reliability, calibration, predictive validity or population prevalence;
- statistical or causal independence of the instruments;
- cross-programme or real-world generalization;
- superiority, human equivalence, autonomy or governance authority;
- all-size extension of either finite scientific result;
- support-three necessity or theorem-cone extension from the 53-row null result;
- novelty language using “first” or unqualified “novel”;
- defect-free, secure or generally fault-tolerant instrumentation.
