# Editorial triage simulation

**Target:** Transactions on Machine Learning Research  
**Article type:** original research / evaluation methodology  
**Packet boundary:** canonical anonymous manuscript, bibliography and sanitized review record  
**Simulation label:** `send_to_review_but_positioning_risk`

## Target criteria

The paper states a narrow evaluation question that is in scope for readers interested in research agents and language-model evaluation. Its headline is not a performance claim. The claims/evidence chain is inspectable: three valid questions, one agreement-with-misdiagnosis counterexample, two retained contaminated candidates and two retained fail-closed defects. The manuscript uses a current TMLR format and separates successor reliability work.

## Send-to-review case

- The primary question and contribution are clear without internal programme context.
- All three valid questions are shown row by row.
- The finite scientific observations have explicit domains and exclusions.
- The strongest negative outcome is the headline rather than a buried limitation.
- The paper cedes broad scientific-agent benchmarking, delayed-outcome agreement gating and general judge-validation territory.
- No aggregate agreement, accuracy, reliability or calibration statistic is reported.

## Positioning risk

The evidence is a three-question case series from one domain. Some editors may regard the methodology as too programme-specific or too small for TMLR interest even though the paper does not overclaim. The minimum sufficient response is not a fabricated reliability study; it is a clear demonstration that the counterexample changes how future research-agent evaluations should separate agreement, diagnosis and experiment choice.

## Integrity and compliance screen

No central evidence blocker is visible before review. Exact author metadata, conflicts, funding, OpenReview profiles and permanent archival deposit remain human filing inputs. Those administrative inputs do not alter the blind scientific case.

This is a simulated triage posture, not a prediction of an actual editorial decision.
