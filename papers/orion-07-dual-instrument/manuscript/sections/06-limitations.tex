\section{Limitations}

The decisive limitation is still sample size, but it is no longer a missing-data blocker for the bounded paper: the current evidence is a three-question prospective case series. Three non-random questions do not authorize a reliability estimate, agreement rate, kappa coefficient, calibration claim, binomial success probability or population generalization. The stronger predictive-validity question remains a separately frozen successor requiring a larger multi-programme registry.

The lanes are not fully independent. They share the receipt corpus, originate in the same research programme, and Lane B's manifest is human/LLM-authored even though the native decision is not. Manifest construction can encode framing choices. Lane A's language-model-assisted synthesis may also share conceptual priors with the people who designed the typed hypotheses. Architectural heterogeneity is therefore not independent-investigator authority.

Deferred scoring is endogenous to the same programme rather than external ground truth. The third case shows why that distinction matters: both instruments agree on a diagnosis that later programme evidence scores as wrong while their selected move remains aligned. This supplies a prospective counterexample to agreement-as-validation, not an estimate of how often agreement is wrong.

The exact finite-panel results are also scoped. Zero gaps across 53 outside-cone rows do not prove an all-size sharpness theorem, and zero predicate mismatches across the complete declared one- and two-object domain do not establish an all-size weighted law.

Finally, reproducible receipts do not make a scientific decision correct. They make the evidence and decision path attributable. The benchmark's bounded contribution is temporal measurement with preserved contamination and deferred outcomes; predictive validity, cross-programme generality and external scientific adjudication remain open.
