\section{Discussion}

The three-case series changes the interpretation of the benchmark. The initial case showed that agreement between heterogeneous scientific decision instruments can be frozen and replayed before a frontier is resolved. The completed series adds the more important observation: agreement can be \emph{wrong at the diagnosis level} even when the agreed next move is useful. The benchmark should therefore be understood as a device for separating contemporaneous decision relations from later scientific scores, not as a consensus mechanism.

This separation matters because the instruments are heterogeneous but not causally independent. Lane A reaches decisions through language-model-assisted evidence synthesis; Lane B applies a typed deterministic controller. Yet both share upstream evidence, programme history and conceptual vocabulary. Their agreement can therefore reflect either convergent reasoning or shared framing. The objective-rescaling case exhibits exactly the failure mode that a consensus interpretation would miss: both lanes select the same diagnostic layer, later evidence supports a different responsibility interpretation, and the selected experiment still turns out to be the right discriminator.

The outside-cone case illustrates a different discipline. An exhaustive zero-gap result over the frozen panel is scientifically informative, but it leaves the all-size sharpness question open. Deferred scoring therefore needs typed scientific outcomes such as aligned, misaligned, unresolved and invalidated rather than a rule that turns every completed computation into validation.

Prospectivity is also part of the measurement rather than administrative metadata. Two candidate questions were retired when outcome-oriented work became visible before instrument freeze. Preserving those contaminated candidates in the audit record, while excluding them from the valid denominator, is preferable to silently substituting easier cases after outcome access.

A larger study could ask whether agreement, disagreement or cannot-check states have predictive value across research programmes. The present paper does not answer that population question. Its bounded result is the temporal evaluation contract and an explicit prospective counterexample showing that agreement is not correctness.
