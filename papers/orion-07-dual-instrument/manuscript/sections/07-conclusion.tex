\section{Conclusion}

Deferred-outcome scoring provides a measurable object for live scientific decisions that do not yet have a gold label. Across three prospectively valid questions, an LLM-host-driven research lane and a typed non-LLM controller record decisions before later scientific outcomes exist, and the frozen scoring maps preserve agreement, misalignment and unresolved scientific scope without rewriting the original questions.

The most important result is negative for consensus-based validation: in one complete finite-domain follow-up, the two instruments agree on the responsible layer and both diagnoses are later scored as wrong, even though their agreed next move remains aligned. Agreement is therefore an observation about the instruments, not scientific authority.

The bounded contribution is complete at the level claimed here: a prospective temporal benchmark contract, three scored cases, preserved contaminated candidates and an explicit agreement-not-correctness counterexample. A larger multi-programme successor is needed only for reliability, calibration or predictive-validity claims; it is not a prerequisite for publishing this bounded benchmark paper.
