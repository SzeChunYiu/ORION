\begin{abstract}
Scientific decision instruments often act before the relevant ground truth exists, so contemporaneous agreement cannot itself establish correctness. We define a deferred-outcome benchmark in which an unresolved frontier question, the exact evidence available at decision time, diagnosis and action coordinates, and a later scoring rule are frozen before resolution. Two heterogeneous instruments---an LLM-host-driven research lane and a typed non-LLM controller---then record decisions against the same frozen evidence, and later scientific results score each decision without rewriting the original question. The completed bounded series contains three prospectively valid questions. In all three, the instruments agree on the primary next move, and those moves are later aligned under the frozen scoring maps. Crucially, one case is an explicit counterexample to agreement as validation: both instruments agree on the responsible layer, yet the complete 39,489-instance follow-up supports a different responsibility interpretation while preserving the selected move. A second follow-up exhaustively checks 53 frozen rows outside a sufficient-cone boundary and finds no counterexample, leaving sharpness open rather than converting silence into a theorem. Two earlier candidate questions were retired before scoring when prospectivity became unsafe and remain visible in the audit record. With only three non-random questions from one research programme, we report a case series rather than an agreement rate, reliability coefficient, calibration estimate, or population success probability. The contribution is therefore a temporal evaluation contract and a concrete demonstration that agreement and correctness are distinct scientific objects; broader predictive validity requires a separately frozen larger multi-programme study.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} research agents; benchmark; deferred outcomes; epistemic control; provenance; scientific workflows.
