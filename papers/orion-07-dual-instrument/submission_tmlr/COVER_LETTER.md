Dear Editors,

Please consider our manuscript, “Prospective measurement of decisions on unresolved scientific questions: a three-question dual-instrument case series,” as an original research article.

The paper addresses an evaluation problem for research agents. A scientific decision may need to be made before the resolving evidence exists, so contemporaneous ground truth cannot score the decision. We prospectively freeze the evidence state and the outputs of a tool-capable host and a typed deterministic controller, then use later scientific evidence to score diagnosis and experiment choice separately. The complete evidence consists of three valid questions plus two retained contaminated candidates. The central result is an explicit case in which both instruments agreed on a diagnosis but both diagnoses were misaligned, while both selected experiments remained aligned.

We believe the work is relevant to TMLR readers interested in research-agent evaluation, language-model judging and evidence-grounded methodology. The paper deliberately does not report a reliability or accuracy rate, claim instrument independence, or generalize from the three non-random questions. Current nearest work on scientific-agent benchmarks, delayed-outcome agreement gates and language-model judge evaluation is cited and its broader territory is explicitly ceded.

The submission uses the official double-blind TMLR style. An anonymous supplementary archive provides sanitized records for every valid and retired question and an independent verifier. The manuscript also discloses language-model assistance and states the human authors' responsibility. No human participants, animals, clinical data or personal data are involved.

The manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere. Human filing metadata, declarations and the final archival deposit will be verified in the submission portal by the authors.

Thank you for your consideration.
