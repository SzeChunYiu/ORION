# Venue decision — ORION-12

**Target: Information Processing & Management.** Fallback JASIST.

IP&M is the assigned primary and it is right. The paper is an
information-retrieval and information-science contribution: it formalises
acquisition and stopping as one object, proves results about it, and reports a
controlled index study, an external screening campaign, and a set of external
probes. IP&M publishes exactly this combination of formal contribution and
empirical evaluation, and it is comfortable with substantial length.

**One thing the filer must know.** This paper's empirical terminal is narrowed
and partly adverse. Cross-arena superiority is untested, the external matched
campaign failed its own prospectively frozen provider-validity gate and is
therefore reported as undetermined rather than as a null, and the public
screening comparison went against the candidate on both endpoints in every
review. The paper's contribution is the framework plus an honest account of
where it did not win.

That is publishable and it is what the evidence supports, but it is not the
shape of paper an editor expects by default. The cover letter says so directly
in its second paragraph rather than leaving a reviewer to discover it. If IP&M
declines on the grounds that the empirical case is not positive, JASIST is the
fallback, and the manuscript should not be reshaped to manufacture a positive
result: the frozen stop rule that produced the narrowed terminal forbids
further tuning on the same tasks.

**Article type.** Full research paper, 47 pages at 11pt.
