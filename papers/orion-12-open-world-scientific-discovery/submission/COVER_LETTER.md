# Cover letter — ORION-12

To the Editors, Information Processing & Management

**Manuscript:** The Acquisition--Authority Envelope for Open-World Scientific
Discovery

Dear Editors,

We submit the manuscript above for consideration in Information Processing &
Management.

Systems that search scientific literature are usually evaluated as retrievers,
and their stopping decision is treated as a downstream detail. We argue that
this is the wrong unit. A system that retrieves well and stops wrongly closes a
question it should have left open, and no ranking metric will show it. The paper
defines an acquisition--authority envelope that compares controllers under
matched access and explicit budget contracts, so that acquisition and closure
are judged together.

We should be direct about the empirical outcome, because it is not the usual
one. The framework's theoretical results hold, but the framework did not win.
The controlled index study supports the mechanism and remains underpowered for
its own prefrozen superiority margin. The external matched campaign failed its
prospectively frozen provider-validity gate, so we report it as undetermined
rather than converting a non-transportable comparison into a null. In the public
screening comparison, across five reviews and 96,241 canonical rows, our
candidate lost to its own frozen donor on both endpoints in every review, and
the primary relative work-saving gates failed. We report those numbers, not a
subset of them.

What survives is worth publishing on its own terms. We prove an
acquisition-ceiling theorem that attributes an adverse outcome to route
generation whenever the required evidence never entered the candidate pool; a
necessary-and-sufficient state-factorization criterion for scientific closure;
an indistinguishable-world lower bound showing when new acquisition or new
assumptions are unavoidable; and a characterization of the maximally permissive
robust completion. The exact-contract battery then shows the predicted authority
distinction concretely: the authority rule obtains 400/400 exact closure
decisions against 250/400 for a donor product that drops unresolved material
routes, while an information-equivalent product ties at 400/400. That tie
matters: it shows the advantage comes from the missing coupling, not from
centralized expressivity.

The paper is therefore a framework and a boundary, not a demonstrated
improvement. It says when new state or new acquisition is necessary; it does not
show an open-world benefit, and it says so in the abstract. Every result-bearing
statement maps to an immutable archived artifact through the claim ledger, and
the checks that enforce that mapping are part of the submission.

We think this is the more useful contribution to make here. The alternative was
to keep tuning on the same development tasks, which the frozen stop rule
prohibits and which would have produced a positive number nobody should believe.

The manuscript is original and is not under consideration elsewhere.

Thank you for your consideration.
