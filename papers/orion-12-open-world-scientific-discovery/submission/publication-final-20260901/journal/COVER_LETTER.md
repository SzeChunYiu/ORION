Dear Editor,

I submit “Acquisition Is Not Closure: Fail-Closed Control for Open-World
Scientific-Literature Discovery” for consideration as a full-length research
article.

The manuscript studies a control problem at the boundary of information
retrieval and evidence synthesis: what may a discovery system infer about task
closure from local acquisition outcomes? The proposed fail-closed layer keeps
route stopping distinct from task closure, derives structural route distinctness
from provenance, separates content identity from question-conditioned processing,
and retains unavailable material routes as open obligations.

The evidence is deliberately bounded. A complete-gold controlled index provides
descriptive mechanism tests, and a 400-case exact-contract battery isolates the
closure rule. An information-equivalent comparator ties the controller on all
400 cases, so the paper does not claim inherent expressivity. The external
TREC-COVID comparison is reported as post-hoc and descriptive because no
separate pre-outcome record binds the exact decision rule. Recall@100 is lower
by 0.01769 with a 95% bootstrap interval of [-0.02729,-0.00906], and candidate
reads increase by 175.7%. The favorable nDCG@10 difference is retained, but the
opposing results do not establish overall superiority. Later ArguAna transformations leave all five
decisions unchanged. Provider-invalid and unaccessed campaigns remain
undetermined rather than being converted into null evidence.

The submission is therefore a methods and critical system-design contribution,
not a claim of external retrieval superiority or open-world completeness. Its
scope is relevant to IP&M readers working on retrieval, scientific-discovery
systems, systematic-review automation, and evidence-coverage semantics.

The manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere and has not been published
previously. I declare no competing interests and no funding for this work.
Generative-AI tools were used for literature-search support, source checking,
reproducibility review, editing, and package preparation; I verified the
result-bearing statements and remain responsible for the article.
