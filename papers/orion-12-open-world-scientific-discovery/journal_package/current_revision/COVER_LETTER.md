Dear Editor,

Please consider “Acquisition Is Not Closure: Fail-Closed Control for Open-World Scientific-Literature Discovery” as a full research article.

The paper studies a control problem at the intersection of information retrieval and information science: what may a system infer about task completeness from local acquisition outcomes? It separates route stopping from task closure, retains unresolved material routes as open obligations, and distinguishes content identity from question-conditioned processing state.

The evidence is deliberately bounded. A 390-task complete-gold study tests mechanism behavior, but its primary comparison is underpowered and is not presented as superiority. A 400-case exact-contract battery isolates the unresolved-route aggregation rule; an information-equivalent product ties the proposed control. Most importantly, the registered public TREC-COVID comparison is adverse: recall and cost criteria fail. A favorable nDCG result is reported as secondary evidence and does not replace those criteria.

We believe this combination of critical system design, explicit authority semantics, controlled mechanism evidence and retained external falsification is relevant to IP&M readers working on retrieval, scientific-discovery systems and evidence synthesis. The manuscript makes no claim of external retrieval superiority or open-world completeness.

The submission package contains an anonymized CAS single-column manuscript, editable blinded source, blinded review materials, a separate title page, glossary, figure captions, declarations, and exact file manifests. The intended publication sequence is arXiv first, followed by one journal submission at a time; the author will make the submission portal's originality and non-concurrent-submission attestations at filing.
