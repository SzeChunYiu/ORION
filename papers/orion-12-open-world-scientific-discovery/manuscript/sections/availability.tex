\section{Data and code availability}
\label{sec:availability}

The submission package contains the complete manuscript source, the controlled
index generator and evaluator, the exact-contract case definitions and protected
decision receipts, the 50 topic-level TREC-COVID result rows, and the frozen
BEIR protocol and result records. It also includes machine-readable claim and
result-retention ledgers that distinguish supported, adverse, null, and
undetermined outcomes. A manifest binds each distributed file by SHA-256.

The controlled index and exact-contract battery are project-authored and can be
replayed from the archived source. TREC-COVID and BEIR corpora are not
redistributed because their original providers control access and licensing.
The package instead supplies the pinned source identities, corpus digests where
available, analysis code, and derived per-topic or per-query records needed to
check the reported values after lawful retrieval from the original providers.

The journal reviewer package is anonymized and contains no author-identifying
repository link. An attributed public archive locator and immutable release
identifier will be supplied during filing or public release; these human- and
portal-controlled identifiers are not synthesized in the manuscript. The
OpenAIRE provider-invalid records and the unaccessed 48-task campaign remain in
the result-retention ledger and are not represented as successful, null, or
equivalent outcomes.

\section{Reproducibility}

Every quantitative statement in the filing manuscript is bound to a versioned
result record. The controlled and exact-contract studies support same-source
replay, not independent empirical replication. The original BEIR control was
replayed on two machines before the density-normalized and query-conditional
successors were interpreted; this is portability evidence
for the same implementation, not a second implementation or a new sample.
Third-party data access, provider availability, and future archive identifiers
remain explicit prerequisites rather than being inferred from local checks.
