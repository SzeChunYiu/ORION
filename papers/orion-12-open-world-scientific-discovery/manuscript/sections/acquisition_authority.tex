\section{Acquisition is not closure}
\label{sec:acquisition-authority}

The central design move in this paper is to separate two problems that are often
collapsed into one policy.  An \emph{acquisition policy} decides what to search,
retrieve, inspect, expand or reread next.  A \emph{closure authority} decides
whether the evidence state is allowed to support route-local stopping or
scientific task completion.  Better acquisition can improve recall and reduce
cost, but it does not automatically acquire authority to declare completeness.
This separation makes strong external retrieval and stopping mechanisms
composable rather than adversarial to the contribution of this paper.

Let $x_t$ denote the observable search state at step $t$.  We write an
acquisition mechanism as
\[
  a_t \sim A(x_t),
\]
where $a_t$ may be a lexical query, a reasoning-aware query, a fielded Boolean
filter, a graph-expansion action, a selective fetch, a reranking operation, or a
request for another retrieval round.  No novelty is claimed here for the
internal form of $A$.  Instead, acquired evidence updates both an evidence state
$E_t$ and a typed obligation state $O_t$.  Closure is a distinct map
\[
  C(E_t,O_t) \in \{\textsc{continue},\textsc{route-stop},
  \textsc{open},\textsc{cannot-check},\textsc{task-close}\}.
\]
A utility, relevance, confidence, sufficiency or coverage signal may influence
$A$ and may recommend a local stop, but it cannot by itself delete an unresolved
obligation in $O_t$.  This interface is where the residual contribution of this
paper sits.

\subsection{Absorbing strong acquisition mechanisms}

The acquisition layer intentionally composes with prior mechanisms. SAGE shows that
keyword-oriented agent queries can make strong lexical retrieval difficult to
beat and that corpus-side metadata/keyword augmentation can further improve
retrieval \cite{sage2026}.  AgentIR shows that an agent's reasoning trace can be
used jointly with its query as a retrieval signal \cite{agentir2026}.  SIEVE
shows that fielded Boolean admission, structure-rich inspection and selective
section fetching can improve the accuracy--context tradeoff
\cite{sieve2026}.  Breadth-first bibliography expansion can recover literature
missed by API-only retrieval, while also demonstrating why citation-list recall
cannot be the only evaluation axis \cite{rethinkinglitsearch2026}.  Lexical,
metadata-augmented, reasoning-aware, field-aware and citation-expansion
retrieval are therefore treated as candidate strengths to compose, not as
inventions of this work.

The same rule applies to continuation and stopping signals.  DeepControl uses
marginal information utility to regulate continued retrieval
\cite{deepcontrol2026}; decision-theoretic policies optimize screening stops
against explicit costs and payoffs \cite{decisionstop2026}; HALT uses
claim--evidence coverage to verify whether expected evidence has been obtained
\cite{halt2026}; MiCP applies conformal prediction to adaptive multi-turn
reasoning to maintain a target coverage guarantee while permitting early stopping
\cite{micp2026}; and structured Search-R1 stopping judgments explicitly encode
sufficiency and remaining gaps \cite{multiragstop2026}.  These signals and
guarantees can all be useful inputs to acquisition or route-local stopping.  The
question asked here is narrower: what are those signals permitted to certify
when routes are dependent, unavailable, censored or only locally exhausted?

\subsection{The authority residual}

The implementation composes four control elements: provenance-based route
distinctness, separation of route stopping from task closure, explicit open
states for unavailable or censored routes, and separation of content identity
from question-conditioned processing state. Prior work owns strong retrieval,
coverage, sufficiency, stopping, memory, and obligation mechanisms. The narrower
residual tested here is their task-level aggregation: a prospectively material
route that is unavailable, censored, or provider-invalid must not disappear
from the closure denominator. The exact-contract comparison changes this rule
while granting the comparator the other prior mechanisms.

The resulting design is deliberately asymmetric.  It is permissive about
\emph{how} evidence is acquired: a stronger retriever, query generator,
expansion rule or verification signal can replace a weaker one.  It is
conservative about \emph{what acquisition outcomes are allowed to mean}: local
flatness, high estimated coverage, high confidence, apparent sufficiency or a
provider failure cannot silently become proof of global completeness.  This
asymmetry is what permits the paper to absorb progress in retrieval without
making its claimed authority boundary obsolete whenever a stronger retriever is
published.

\subsection{Empirical consequence}

The decomposition also fixes the widening experiment.  Candidate-generation
methods should first be compared and composed under matched query/candidate
budgets.  Only after candidate recall is high enough to make stopping observable
should the closure layer be challenged with direct ablations and strong stopping
baselines.  A positive external result must therefore show both acquisition
quality and the incremental effect of typed closure authority; a cheaper stop
that hides reachable relevant evidence is not a positive result.  Conversely,
a stronger substituted retriever that improves acquisition while leaving the closure
constraints intact strengthens the system rather than weakening the claim.
