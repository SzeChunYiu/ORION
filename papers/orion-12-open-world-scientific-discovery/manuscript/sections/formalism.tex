\section{Formal mechanics}
\label{sec:formalism}

This section fixes the objects the evaluation measures. Every definition below is
implemented in the system under test rather than assumed: work identity and read
state, structural route distinctness and coverage, and route control each correspond to a
module of the implementation, named in Section~\ref{sec:availability}. Where a
definition is deliberately conservative we state the failure it is conservative
against, because the direction of the bias is what makes the refusal a safety
property rather than a preference.

\subsection{Work identity and content identity}

Let $R$ be the set of raw identifiers a route can emit (DOIs, arXiv ids,
OpenAlex ids, local ids, URLs). Canonicalization is a map
$\varphi: R \rightarrow (\mathcal{S} \times V) \cup \{\textsc{unknown}\}$ sending a raw identifier to a
scheme-qualified key and a rendition version, so that
$\varphi(\texttt{arXiv:2301.00001v1})$ and $\varphi(\texttt{arXiv:2301.00001v2})$
share a key and differ only in $V$: a version is a rendition of one work, not a
second work. A declared scheme is treated as a claim rather than a credential;
an identifier that does not match the shape of the scheme it announces resolves
to \textsc{unknown} instead of entering the graph as a key that can never be
fetched.

A \emph{work} is the alias-closure of canonical keys. Given identity records
with alias sets $A_1,\dots,A_n$, works are the connected components of the graph
on $\bigcup_i A_i$ that joins two keys sharing a record. Closure is computed as a
projection over an append-only ledger rather than at write time, because an alias
learned late can join two works recorded early; folding at write time would
freeze the wrong partition.

Separately from work identity, each retrieved rendition carries a content digest
$c$. Route overlap, deduplication and coverage are computed over content digests,
never over retrieval identifiers: a source that re-mints identifiers would
otherwise make two routes appear disjoint when they retrieved the same paper,
which inflates every coverage quantity built on their overlap.

\subsection{Question-conditioned read state}

A read receipt is a tuple $(w, c, \sigma, f)$: work, content digest, extraction
schema version, and frame $f$ --- the scientific question the extraction was
performed for. Given the receipt set $\mathcal{R}$, the read decision for a
candidate $(w, c, \sigma, f)$ is
\[
\mathrm{read}(w,c,\sigma,f) =
\begin{cases}
\textsc{unseen} & \nexists\, r \in \mathcal{R}: w_r = w\\
\textsc{content\_changed} & \exists\, r: w_r=w,\ \nexists\, r: (w_r,c_r)=(w,c)\\
\textsc{new\_schema} & \exists\, r: (w_r,c_r)=(w,c),\ \nexists\, r: (w_r,c_r,\sigma_r)=(w,c,\sigma)\\
\textsc{new\_frame} & \exists\, r: (w_r,c_r,\sigma_r)=(w,c,\sigma),\ \nexists\, r: r=(w,c,\sigma,f)\\
\textsc{already\_read} & \exists\, r \in \mathcal{R}: r = (w,c,\sigma,f).
\end{cases}
\]
\textsc{already\_read} therefore requires agreement on all four coordinates.
This yields the distinction the evaluation depends on: a repeat extraction with
all four coordinates equal is \emph{duplicate processing} and is waste, while a
repeat extraction under a changed content digest, schema or frame is a
\emph{legitimate reread} and is required work. Collapsing the two --- treating
``this paper has been seen'' as ``this paper has been read for this question''
--- is how a system reports coverage of material it only skimmed for a different
purpose.

\subsection{Structural route distinctness}

A route capture is a tuple $\rho = (k, b, d, C)$: route kind, backend identity,
query-derivation process, and the set $C$ of captured content digests. For two
captures $\rho_i, \rho_j$ the structural-distinctness predicate is
\[
\mathrm{distinct}(\rho_i,\rho_j) \iff k_i \neq k_j \ \wedge\ b_i \neq b_j \ \wedge\ d_i \neq d_j ,
\]
and the verdict otherwise names the shared cause
(\textsc{same\_route\_kind}, \textsc{shared\_backend},
\textsc{shared\_query\_derivation}). Independence is thus \emph{earned} from the
generation and capture process; a distinct route label carries no evidential
weight. This predicate is an eligibility screen for a coverage diagnostic. It
does not establish statistical independence between the returned rankings or
captures.

\begin{proposition}[Direction of the conservatism]
\label{prop:conservative}
Positive dependence between capture occasions can bias unseen-mass estimators
downward. Admitting a pair that shares known generation structure can therefore
produce false confidence that search is near exhaustion, whereas refusing a
structurally distinct pair only forfeits a diagnostic. The screen is accordingly
biased toward refusal.
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Coverage diagnostics are never authority}

For a structurally distinct pair whose captures additionally satisfy the
sampling assumptions required by the estimator, with sizes $n_i, n_j$ and
overlap $m_{ij}$,
the two-source estimate is $\hat{N} = n_i n_j / m_{ij}$, undefined at
$m_{ij}=0$. Zero overlap places no upper bound on the population, so the system
returns a refusal with reason rather than a large number that would read as a
measurement. When several independent pairs yield estimates, the reported value
is $\max_{ij} \hat{N}_{ij}$: the largest population implies the most still
unfound, which is the direction that fails safe. Every coverage estimate is
marked diagnostic-only, and adaptive search is explicitly not a sequence of
independent capture occasions --- following up on what was just found converts
singletons into doubletons and deflates the estimator toward ``nearly done''.

\subsection{Route control state machine}

Let a route hold attempts $a_1,\dots,a_t$, each with retrieved digests
$D_i$, novel digests $N_i \subseteq D_i$, cost, failure signatures and a
backend-exhaustion flag. For a nonempty return, marginal novelty is
$|N_t|/|D_t|$; a successful empty return has novelty zero. The
the flat streak $z$ is the number of trailing attempts that succeeded, reported
no exhaustion and returned no novel digest. Under a frozen policy with
thresholds $\theta_{\mathrm{ref}} \leq \theta_{\mathrm{sw}}$, the decision is
taken in strict priority order:
\[
\delta =
\begin{cases}
\textsc{stop}\ (\text{budget}) & \text{remaining budget} \leq 0\\
\textsc{stop}\ (\text{exhausted}) & a_t\ \text{reports backend exhaustion}\\
\textsc{suspend} & a_t\ \text{carries execution-failure signatures}\\
\textsc{switch} & z \geq \theta_{\mathrm{sw}}\ \wedge\ \text{alternatives exist}\\
\textsc{reformulate} & z \geq \theta_{\mathrm{ref}}\\
\textsc{continue} & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
\]
Two separations in this table are load-bearing. First, backend exhaustion and
transport failure are distinct states: the former is a statement about the
index, the latter about the channel, and a system that merges them converts an
outage into evidence of absence. The latter yields \textsc{suspend}, a
non-terminal state carrying an open obligation, not \textsc{stop}. Second, each
terminal decision carries typed reopen triggers (budget increase, backend
recovery or refresh, material frame change, material finding on an alternative
route); a route reopens exactly when an observed trigger intersects that set.

\begin{proposition}[Route-stop non-certification]
\label{prop:failclosed}
No individual route decision certifies task saturation. In the implementation
every route decision, \textsc{stop} included, has only route-local authority and
carries an explicit declaration that it does not certify task saturation.
Consequently, a route stop cannot be used by itself as a task-completeness
certificate. A composition of route outcomes may support task closure only
through a separately specified ensemble predicate whose route universe,
material-obligation registry, provider/observability/custody guards, resource
state and reopen triggers were fixed before the outcome.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The route decision alphabet contains no task-closure authority, so projection
of any one route output cannot emit a valid task certificate. The controller's
task decision is instead produced by the separately typed closure map over the
full ensemble state. If that map's registered completeness and validity guards
hold, a composition can certify closure; if they do not, composition of
route-local stops alone supplies no missing authority.
\end{proof}

Task closure is therefore a separate judgement over the route ensemble. It
requires that every route in the prospectively registered route universe is in
a terminal or explicitly reopenable state, that
no suspended or unavailable route leaves an unmet obligation, and --- by
Proposition~\ref{prop:failclosed} --- that no coverage diagnostic has been used
as its authority. It also requires the registered provider, observability,
custody and resource-validity guards to pass as one conjunction. An unavailable
route remains an open coverage obligation and
is reported as such; it is never converted into evidence that nothing further
exists.

\subsection{What the evaluation must therefore measure}

The definitions above fix four measurable error families, each with a
ground-truth condition that only a complete-gold world supplies: route-stop
false positives (a route stopped while it could still reach unfound relevant
items), route-stop false negatives (a route continued past its exhaustion
point), premature task closure (task closed while a gold item was still
reachable within remaining budget), and duplicate processing versus legitimate
reread as separated by the four read coordinates. Route overlap, per-route
marginal contribution and structural-distinctness status are computed over content
digests. Metrics whose denominator is the gold set are reported only where that
denominator is complete; the conditions are stated per metric in the frozen
measurement plan.
