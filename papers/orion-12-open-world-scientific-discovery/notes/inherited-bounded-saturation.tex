\section{Bounded saturation and search-basis challenge}

ORION does not represent unrestricted open-world completeness as a positive state. A scoped solve may reach bounded saturation only after repeated flatness in three different objects:
\[
\Delta K_t=0,\qquad \Delta W_t=0,\qquad \Delta F_t=0,
\]
where $F_t$ denotes the active formulation coordinates relevant to the question, representation, measurement and evaluator.

The bootstrap saturation basis requires at least two consecutive flat rounds and independent route families including current-vocabulary, function-only, parent-discipline, literature-bridge, adversarial-omission and freshness searches. A material residual, newly discovered domain, new representation, or formulation change resets the affected flatness suffix.

This rule is motivated by a practical failure of the predecessor RAKL project: a search can become quiet inside an incomplete conceptual basis. The repair is not to hard-code one newly remembered discipline, but to require explicit challenge of the basis used to decide relevance. The bounded certificate therefore means only that the registered search/formulation attacks were flat at the declared cutoff.

This position is consistent with literature-based discovery, where public but disconnected knowledge may remain undiscovered, and with empirical evidence-synthesis research showing that source/database and search-strategy choices materially affect recall.
