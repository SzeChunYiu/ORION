# Faithful Comparators Separate Search, Safety, and Cost in Recursive Scientific Repair

## Abstract

A strong result for a scientific-repair agent can disappear when a baseline receives the same opportunity to iterate. We report a falsification-driven reconstruction of a controlled recursive-repair study over 2,882 credential-free mechanical worlds, including 480 protected hidden-shift worlds and 2,402 controls. The original governed policy solved every hidden-shift case with no forbidden high-level mutation, whereas three registered parent mechanisms solved 49.375% of hidden shifts. The approximately 0.506 success margin was initially attributed to the governed responsibility mechanism.

A faithful-comparator study changes one operation in each parent: a single top-confidence choice becomes ordered search over the same public repair menu, under the same four-unit budget. All three repaired parents reach 100% hidden-shift success. One also reaches zero forbidden high-level mutation and therefore matches the governed policy on both primary gates. The original cross-policy necessity interpretation is retracted.

The repaired comparison exposes three distinct outcomes. Ordered search explains the old success margin. Intervention permissions explain why two repaired parents still make forbidden high-level mutations at rates 23.768% and 41.083%. Among the two policies that clear both gates, mean intervention cost is 1.8341 for the governed policy and 2.6676 for the faithful parent, but the mechanism behind this residual is unresolved. A donor-owned probability-to-cost ordering rule is a direct alternative, and the dedicated mechanism test has checker disagreement on 34 of 36 registered cells, so it supplies no comparative authority.

The durable contribution is not agent superiority. It is a corrected decomposition showing how comparator capability, scientific admissibility, and cost must be separated before credit is assigned to a focal mechanism.

## 1. Introduction

Comparative agent studies often identify the mechanism unique to the winning system and then treat that mechanism as the explanation for the margin. This inference is unsafe when the baselines lack an obvious operation that the winner is allowed to use.

Recursive repair creates exactly this risk. A policy that searches several candidate interventions can outperform a parent forced to stop after one top-confidence choice. If the parent receives ordered search and the margin vanishes, the former result measured iteration capability rather than the claimed responsibility mechanism.

The scientifically useful response is not to move the target. It is to preserve the falsifier, retract the unsupported mechanism claim, and ask which differences remain. In the present study, that reconstruction separates:

1. protected task success;
2. intervention safety under a declared mutation contract;
3. cost among policies that satisfy both gates.

This order is essential. A cheap failed policy is not efficient repair, and a successful policy that changes a protected level is not an admissible substitute for safe repair.

## 2. Evaluation contract

The frozen study contains 2,882 generated mechanical worlds. The protected set contains 480 hidden-shift worlds; the remaining 2,402 worlds are controls. Each policy has a four-unit intervention budget.

The primary endpoint is conjunctive. A policy must:

- solve the protected root task; and
- avoid every forbidden high-level mutation.

The governed policy carries protected-sibling, dependency-impact, and lower-level exclusion constraints. The original parent policies use related donor strategies but choose only one top-confidence repair from the public menu.

That difference is small in implementation and large in causal interpretation. It determines whether a failed first guess is terminal.

## 3. Original result and hidden comparator asymmetry

The governed policy achieves success 1.0 on the hidden-shift set and forbidden-mutation rate 0. Each registered parent achieves success 0.49375. The resulting success gap appears substantial.

The comparison, however, changes two things at once: responsibility governance and the ability to continue searching after a candidate fails. A claim that the governance mechanism is necessary must first rule out search as the simpler explanation.

The faithful comparator holds the world set, budget, public repair menu, seeds, and protected matrices fixed. It changes only the one-shot selection into ordered search.

## 4. Ordered search removes the success margin

All three repaired parents improve from 0.49375 to 1.0 hidden-shift success. The governed policy no longer has a success advantage.

The strongest repaired parent also produces no forbidden high-level mutation. It therefore matches the governed policy on the full primary endpoint. There are no paired disagreements on protected success, and neither policy violates the mutation gate.

This result falsifies the original cross-policy necessity interpretation. The old success margin cannot be used as evidence that the governed responsibility mechanism is required. It was absorbed by an obvious, capability-matched baseline repair.

The retraction is not a caveat added to a still-positive mechanism paper. It is the central result of the reconstructed paper.

## 5. Success and scientific restraint are separate

The remaining two repaired parents also solve every hidden-shift world, but their forbidden-mutation rates are 0.23768 and 0.41083. Ordered search is therefore sufficient for task completion but not for compliance with the protected intervention contract in those policy families.

This distinction is scientifically more informative than a scalar leaderboard. A repair can recover the root outcome by altering a level that the experiment declares out of bounds. Counting both cases as success erases the difference between admissible and inadmissible intervention.

The strongest repaired parent sets the boundary on the claim. Because one donor family reaches both success and zero forbidden mutation, the evidence does not show that the governed mechanism is uniquely necessary for safe repair. It shows only that the tested policy families encode different permissions and that some spend their recovered success by violating the registered constraint.

Under the common four-unit budget, the governed policy therefore uses less intervention resource on this frozen world family.

Several controls demonstrate why the conditioning matters. Some failed or unsafe parents spend less. Their lower resource use does not represent efficient admissible repair because they do not satisfy the scientific endpoint.

The supported statement is narrow: among gate-clearing policies in this generated environment, one governed policy has lower measured intervention cost than one faithful parent.

## 7. Why the cost mechanism remains open

The cost residual cannot be assigned automatically to typed responsibility governance. A simpler donor explanation is available: ordering candidates by probability-to-cost can reduce expected search cost without the responsibility hierarchy.

A dedicated successor was intended to distinguish the focal mechanism from this alternative. Its independent implementations disagree on 34 of 36 registered cells. That disagreement blocks a causal reading of the cost result. It does not make the observed 1.8341 versus 2.6676 difference disappear; it prevents the paper from saying why the difference occurred.

The correct disposition is therefore:

- measured cost residual: retained;
- mechanism attribution: unresolved;
- broad efficiency claim: not authorized.

## 8. Within-system evidence does not restore cross-policy necessity

Ablations of the governed policy show that removing protected-sibling, dependency-impact, lower-level exclusion, or ordering components changes its behavior in the registered direction. Those experiments concern internal dependence of one implementation.

They do not establish that another policy cannot achieve the same endpoint through a different mechanism. The faithful parent that matches both gates is the decisive counterexample to that broader claim.

This distinction between within-system necessity and cross-system necessity is easy to lose in revision. The refined paper keeps the two claim classes separate.

## 9. What the falsification establishes

The reconstructed evidence supports four conclusions.

First, ordered search fully explains the former protected-task success margin. Second, task success and intervention restraint are different endpoints. Third, at least one faithful parent matches the governed policy on both primary gates, so broad superiority and unique necessity are not supported. Fourth, a cost difference remains after gate matching, but its mechanism has not been identified.

The result is valuable because it diagnoses a comparative design error and replaces a monolithic claim with a testable causal decomposition.

## 10. Relation to repair and agent evaluation

Iterative repair, dependency-directed reasoning, value-of-information planning, counterfactual intervention, and cost-aware ordering are established donor areas. Ordered search itself is not the contribution.

The residual contribution is methodological and empirical: a faithful-comparator falsification under a protected intervention contract, preservation of the retracted claim, and separation of success, safety, and cost. The study provides a concrete example of why baseline capability must be repaired before a system-specific mechanism receives credit.

## 11. Limitations

The worlds are generated and credential-free. Their large case count does not make them a sample of natural scientific repair tasks. The faithful repair is one comparator improvement, and other capability-matched parents could alter the safety or cost picture further.

The replication world set remains non-adjudicative because its anchor gate was instrumented incorrectly. It is neither a replication success nor a replication failure. The cost mechanism is blocked by implementation disagreement in its successor test.

No claim is made about broad agent safety, scientific productivity, deployed robustness, or general resource superiority.

## 12. Conclusion

A comparator that cannot iterate can manufacture a mechanism story. Giving the parent policies the same ordered search opportunity removes the original success margin, and one repaired parent matches the governed policy on both task success and forbidden-mutation safety. Two other parents recover success while violating the scientific contract, which separates completion from admissibility. A lower cost remains for the governed policy among gate-matched arms, but the available evidence does not identify its cause. The refined result is therefore a falsification-driven decomposition, not a superiority claim.
