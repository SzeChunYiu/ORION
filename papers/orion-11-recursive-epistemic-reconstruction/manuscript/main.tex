\begin{abstract}
Evidence that a diagnosis or repair succeeds does not determine which scientific
layer may be changed. We formalize typed responsibility-to-authority licensing
through epistemic transition envelopes, exact interface factorization, and
legal-action deficiency. In archived primary and disjoint generator runs of
2,882 mechanical worlds each, the licensed-transition policy recorded
hidden-shift success 1.0000 versus 0.4938 and 0.4833 for three registered
non-iterating parents. A later registered challenge repaired one parent by
adding ordered candidate search. That comparator matched the policy on the
joint success and no-forbidden-mutation criterion in all 480 primary
hidden-shift worlds, with zero discordant pairs. We therefore withdraw the
comparative mechanism-necessity reading of the earlier margins. The challenge
replication remained undetermined after its anchor gate failed. On the primary
worlds, the remaining descriptive difference was lower mean intervention cost,
1.834 versus 2.668 units. On 400 authored exact contracts, the policy made 400
correct decisions versus 275 for a lossy donor-complete interface, while an
information-equivalent product tied at 400/400. A clean-source replay reproduced
the archived campaigns byte for byte, but cannot establish their original
chronology because three cited commits are unreachable. The broad 48-case
precursor also remains unsupported at 1/48 for both subject and baseline. These
results establish finite mechanical and reproducibility boundaries, not
naturalistic, model-general, comparative-necessity, or external authority.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} epistemic transitions; scientific reformulation; information sufficiency; belief revision; autonomous science; research agents.
