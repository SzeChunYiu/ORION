\begin{abstract}
Self-editing, evolutionary, experience-reusing, verifier-gated, issue-centric, and failure-learning agents increasingly improve themselves over repeated development cycles. ORION Paper V therefore targets the strongest upward claim: across multiple sequential generations and heterogeneous protected scientific/research tasks, can governed Self-ORION outperform the strongest donor-complete self-improvement systems in fresh-task improvement, harmful-transfer control, repair attribution, research-effort allocation, and the ability of later generations to produce still better improvements? The current 21/24 hidden-cause attribution result, including all three retained errors, is treated as immutable seed evidence rather than a ceiling. Each error becomes a causal-repair programme that must test whether better diagnosis changes repair selection and whether that change transfers to fresh protected tasks. Candidate systems operate under matched models, tools, search, compute, and evaluator budgets. Strong comparators receive direct self-edit, evolutionary/meta-agent search, issue-centric optimization, verifier-gated acceptance, retained experience, causal diagnosis, negative history, adaptive state/reasoning allocation, and recursive research-governance mechanics. Accepted changes require causal/pathway evidence, motivating replay, independent fresh transfer, protected assurance, preservation/reopening of old scientific state, and external adoption authority. Primary outcomes include fresh-task improvement, harmful regression, false upgrade, attribution and repair-selection utility, repeated-dead-end rate, cost, and generation-to-generation meta-improvement. The intended terminal is replicated recursive self-improvement superiority with a positive meta-improvement signal and at least one learned or invented mechanic that was not hand-written into the initial system. Null and harmful outcomes remain immutable and launch upward successor research rather than claim contraction.
\end{abstract}

\section{Largest scientific question}
P5-U asks whether a governed scientific system can become not only better at tasks, but better at \emph{improving itself}:
\[
ORION_0 < ORION_1 < ORION_2 < ORION_3
\]
on protected fresh scientific/research tasks, while later generations also become better at diagnosing, selecting, and validating the next improvement.

The comparison is not against weak fixed agents. All strong donor mechanics are admitted to the comparator. ORION earns superiority only by generalizing over them across generations and regimes.

\section{Upward-generalization principle}
Any self-improvement mechanism that solves a subclass becomes part of the donor-complete comparator: direct self-edit, evolutionary search, archive-based improvement, issue-centric optimization, causal diagnosis, verifier-gated acceptance, failure-memory retrieval, adaptive budget allocation, negative-history recurrence checks, and recursive research governance.

The strongest claim requires ORION to exceed that union through:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item larger protected fresh-task gains;
    \item lower harmful transfer and false upgrades;
    \item better causal repair selection;
    \item better research-effort allocation between diagnosis/state construction and additional edit/search attempts;
    \item preserved scientific validity across generations;
    \item positive meta-improvement, where later ORION generations improve the quality of future improvement proposals;
    \item at least one learned or invented mechanic that expands the pre-freeze improvement basis and transfers to new tasks.
\end{enumerate}
The intended terminal is \textbf{GENERAL_RECURSIVE_SELF_IMPROVEMENT_SUPERIORITY}.

\section{Immutable seed evidence}
The existing GLM-5.2 attribution result remains exactly 21/24 with three retained adjacent-level errors. No relabeling or deletion is permitted. Each error is a research seed:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item retrieval miss confused with representation gap;
    \item environment/tool failure confused with implementation bug;
    \item representation gap confused with method-basis gap.
\end{enumerate}
The first successor question is causal: which discriminating observation separates the competing causes? The second is consequential: does the corrected cause select a better repair? The third is general: does that repair transfer to fresh tasks and improve later improvement cycles?

\section{Multi-generation protocol}
A run consists of generations $g=0,\ldots,G$. Each generation receives a protected task stream containing motivating failures, replay cases, fresh-transfer cases, and protected regression cases. Candidate processes may search, diagnose, compile state, propose repairs, and test candidates, but cannot alter protected evaluators, hidden tasks, historical negative evidence, or adoption authority.

Every adopted change produces a responsibility-carrying improvement state declaring which prior evaluations, failure memories, and authority conclusions remain valid and what must reopen.

\section{Improvement-cycle stages}
Each candidate change follows
\[
\textsc{Observe}\to\textsc{Diagnose}\to\textsc{Discriminate}\to\textsc{Propose}\to\textsc{Replay}\to\textsc{Fresh}\to\textsc{Protected}\to\textsc{HostAdopt/Reject}.
\]
Missing protected evidence yields \textsc{CannotCheck/Block}, not improvement. Motivating replay alone never authorizes adoption.

\section{Primary hypotheses}
\paragraph{H1, repeated fresh-task superiority.}
Across multiple sequential generations, ORION achieves greater protected fresh-task improvement than the strongest donor-complete comparator by a prospectively frozen margin.

\paragraph{H2, harmful-transfer superiority.}
ORION reduces harmful transfer, regression, and false upgrades under frozen non-compensatory safety rules.

\paragraph{H3, causal improvement utility.}
Better responsibility diagnosis and frozen discriminators causally improve repair selection and intervention success, not merely attribution labels.

\paragraph{H4, research-allocation superiority.}
ORION allocates effort between state compilation/diagnosis, search, candidate generation, and evaluation more effectively than fixed or uncertainty-only policies under matched total budget.

\paragraph{H5, meta-improvement.}
Later generations have higher probability of producing a protected validated improvement per unit research budget than earlier generations, after controlling for task difficulty and accumulated resources.

\paragraph{H6, mechanic expansion.}
At least one learned or invented mechanic absent from the initial hand-written basis causes a replicated fresh-task gain and survives independent protected verification.

Report separately:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item protected fresh-task improvement by generation;
    \item fresh-transfer success rate;
    \item harmful-transfer/regression rate and worst-family regression;
    \item attribution accuracy and false broad-repair rate;

    \item negative-history completeness;

\end{enumerate}
A single average score cannot compensate catastrophic regression or evaluator compromise.

\section{Pathway evidence}
A claimed improvement must link the observed failure to the adopted mechanic through a frozen causal chain. At minimum retain diagnosis hypotheses, discriminator outcome, candidate patch/mechanic identity, replay result, fresh result, protected result, and later recurrence evidence. If the same gain appears without the proposed mechanic under a matched ablation, the causal claim fails even if total performance rises.

\section{Longitudinal state validity}
An improvement-state summary that was sufficient in generation $g$ may become insufficient after objective, representation, evaluator, or framework changes in generation $g+1$. The system must detect this and reopen the necessary raw evidence/history rather than silently carrying stale certificates forward.

\section{Adversarial controls}
Mandatory attacks include:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item replay improvement with fresh-task regression;
    \item correct diagnosis but wrong repair;
    \item wrong diagnosis with lucky improvement;
    \item evaluator or holdout modification counted as gain;
    \item deletion/truncation of negative history;
    \item compressed improvement state becoming invalid after framework change;
    \item more edit attempts masquerading as better self-improvement;
    \item recursive governance that becomes bureaucratic overhead under matched budget;
    \item self-certification or self-merge attempt;
    \item donor-complete dynamic routing among all registered improvement mechanics.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Statistics}
Freeze generation count, task sampling, fresh/protected split identities, model/provider/evaluator identities, effect-size and non-inferiority margins, multiplicity handling, catastrophic-harm rules, and longitudinal analysis before final protected outcomes. Repeated samples of one task are not independent tasks. Report generation-level and task-family uncertainty, not only pooled means.

\section{Negative-result recursion as upward search}
Every harmful transfer, failed repair, null generation, recurrence, or meta-improvement failure is preserved and classified by responsibility: diagnosis, discriminator, candidate generation, state representation, budget allocation, replay/fresh mismatch, evaluator/authority, negative-history use, or method-basis inadequacy.

The first discriminator localizes the cause. The programme then searches materially different successor mechanisms and parent disciplines for a rule that generalizes beyond the originating failure. Repeated failures of existing repair classes can justify new method-space invention, but only after lower-level causes are excluded. Successors must improve protected fresh outcomes and later improvement quality, not merely repair one benchmark item.

ORION issues \#653 and the existing causal-repair programme \#282 are the authority-bearing research lanes. New children are opened only for distinct newly observed responsibility/mechanic families with frozen discriminators. Margin relaxation, result deletion, baseline weakening, or self-certification are prohibited.
