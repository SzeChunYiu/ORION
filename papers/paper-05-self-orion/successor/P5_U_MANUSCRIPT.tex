\begin{abstract}
Recursive self-editing, evolutionary agent search, procedural-memory transfer, verifier-gated acceptance, intervention-backed attribution, scientific skill distillation, and even meta-skill evolution are now active donor mechanisms. ORION Paper V therefore targets a higher claim: across multiple sequential generations and heterogeneous protected scientific/research tasks, can governed Self-ORION outperform the strongest donor-complete self-improvement systems in fresh-task improvement, harmful-transfer control, causal repair selection, research-effort allocation, and the ability of later generations to produce future valid improvements? The immutable 21/24 hidden-cause result and its three adjacent-level errors are treated as the first causal research seeds, not a ceiling. Each seed must pass diagnosis, active discrimination, repair, replay, fresh transfer, protected assurance, and external host adoption. The intended terminal is \textbf{GENERAL\_RECURSIVE\_SELF\_IMPROVEMENT\_SUPERIORITY} with positive meta-improvement and at least one learned or invented mechanic absent from the initial hand-written basis.
\end{abstract}

\section{Largest scientific question}
P5-U asks whether a governed scientific system can become not only better at tasks, but better at improving itself:
\[
ORION_0 < ORION_1 < ORION_2 < ORION_3,
\]
with the inequality defined on prospectively protected fresh tasks and charged research budget, not replay score alone.

The donor frontier already includes self-referential code editing in G\"odel Agent \cite{godel2025}, archive-driven open-ended self-improvement in Darwin G\"odel Machine, continual agent evolution in AgentEvolver \cite{agentevolver2025}, recursive task/meta-skill evolution in MetaSkill-Evolve \cite{metaskill2026}, scientific-skill distillation in S1-NexusAgent \cite{nexus2026}, procedural-transfer evaluation in EvoAgentBench \cite{evoagentbench2026}, intervention-supported error attribution in REFLECT \cite{reflect2026}, and anytime-valid commit testing in PACE \cite{pace2026}. P5-U therefore cannot earn novelty merely by self-editing or gating edits.

\section{Upward-generalization principle}
Any mechanism that solves a self-improvement subclass is granted to the donor-complete comparator: direct self-edit, evolutionary/archive search, issue-centric optimization, experience/ability reuse, intervention-backed attribution, verifier-gated or anytime-valid acceptance, negative-history recurrence checks, adaptive allocation, scientific-skill distillation and recursive meta-skill evolution.

ORION earns the larger result only by producing greater fresh protected gain, less harmful transfer, better causal repair selection, more efficient valid-improvement discovery, preservation of scientific validity, and positive meta-improvement across generations.

\section{Immutable seed evidence}
The existing GLM-5.2 attribution result remains exactly 21/24 with the three observed errors:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item retrieval miss $\rightarrow$ representation gap;
    \item environment/tool failure $\rightarrow$ implementation bug;
    \item representation gap $\rightarrow$ method-basis gap.
\end{enumerate}
No relabeling or deletion is allowed. The scientific question is whether an intervention-backed discriminator separates each adjacent cause, whether that changes the chosen repair, and whether the resulting repair transfers to fresh protected tasks.

SEVA provides a direct warning that self-evolution can create benchmark specialists while degrading other benchmarks \cite{seva2026}; EvoAgentBench similarly reports that current automatic methods do not sustain positive transfer in all tested settings \cite{evoagentbench2026}. These are donor pressure for non-compensatory negative-transfer accounting.

\section{Multi-generation causal protocol}
A candidate change follows
\[
\textsc{Observe}\to\textsc{Diagnose}\to\textsc{Discriminate}\to\textsc{Propose}\to\textsc{Replay}\to\textsc{Fresh}\to\textsc{Protected}\to\textsc{HostAdopt/Reject}.
\]
Replay improvement is never sufficient for adoption. Missing protected evidence yields \textsc{CannotCheck/Block}. Every accepted or rejected candidate remains in the immutable improvement lineage.

Fresh-transfer sets change at least one independent axis relative to the motivating failure: task, domain, model, environment, representation or tool regime. Protected holdouts and evaluator artifacts are frozen outside candidate custody.

\section{Causal Repair Transfer and Meta-Improvement}
The first ChatGPT-led negative-to-positive successor is \textbf{Causal Repair Transfer and Meta-Improvement (CRTMI)}.

For the three historical errors CRTMI freezes direct discriminators before repair outcomes: retrieval expansion versus representation transformation; known-good environment/tool substitution versus unchanged-code failure; and representation repair versus persistence under an adequate representation. The discriminator does not itself authorize a method change.

For every later null/harmful result, CRTMI assigns a primary responsibility among diagnosis, discriminator, candidate generation, state/interface, budget allocation, replay/fresh mismatch, evaluator/authority, negative-history use or method-basis inadequacy. A broader method edit is admissible only after lower-level causes are prospectively excluded.

A successful repair becomes useful P5 evidence only if it changes intervention choice, improves fresh outcomes under matched cost, survives protected assurance, and later helps the system produce better valid improvements.

\section{Primary hypotheses}
\paragraph{H1, repeated fresh-task superiority.} Across multiple sequential generations, ORION achieves greater protected fresh-task improvement than the strongest runnable donor-complete comparator by a frozen margin.

\paragraph{H2, harmful-transfer superiority.} Harmful transfer, regression and false upgrade satisfy non-compensatory frozen guards.

\paragraph{H3, causal improvement utility.} Frozen discriminators improve repair selection and intervention success, not only attribution labels.

\paragraph{H4, research-allocation superiority.} ORION yields more protected validated improvement per charged research/evaluation budget than fixed or donor-complete allocation policies.

\paragraph{H5, meta-improvement.} Later generations have higher probability of producing a protected validated improvement per unit budget after prospectively frozen task-difficulty/resource controls.

\paragraph{H6, mechanic expansion.} At least one learned/invented mechanic absent from the initial hand-written basis causes replicated fresh-task gain under independent protected verification.

\section{Statistics and negative-history integrity}
The task or prospectively defined task cluster is the independent unit; generations form a longitudinal hierarchy. Repeated samples, patches and evaluator calls are technical repeats. Freeze generation count, sampling, split identities, margins, multiplicity, longitudinal model, catastrophic-harm rule and provider/evaluator identities before final protected outcomes.

Report fresh gain, harmful transfer, worst-family regression, attribution and repair-selection utility, false upgrade, replay/fresh gap, recurrence, negative-history completeness and validated improvements per research budget separately. Catastrophic regression cannot be averaged away.

PACE-style anytime-valid testing may strengthen acceptance uncertainty but cannot replace fresh transfer or external custody \cite{pace2026}.
