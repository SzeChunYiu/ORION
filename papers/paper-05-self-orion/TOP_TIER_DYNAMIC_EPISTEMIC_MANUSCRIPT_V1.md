## Abstract

A self-improving research system can generate useful changes while remaining unable to authorize its own adoption. P5 separates diagnosis, proposal origin, causal credit, transfer, negative history, evaluator custody, and adoption authority in a dynamic evolution state. It absorbs automated repair, self-improving agents, anytime-valid selection, causal intervention evaluation, and constitutional safety. We prove a reflexive-custody impossibility: if the candidate controls proposal, evaluator, threshold, and adoption, genuine improvement and evaluator gaming may be observationally indistinguishable. We give sufficient protected-adoption conditions for the declared workflow class and a well-founded evolution law. The frozen successor registered 2,228 cases, eight arms, three seeds, and 53,472 planned run cells, including SWE-bench Verified and fresh powered/sentinel strata. Zero cells were eligible because SWE-bench rights and protected fresh-adoption custody were unavailable. The paper therefore advances the self-certification impossibility and protected-adoption theory without claiming resolved-rate, transfer, causal-credit, or adoption superiority.
