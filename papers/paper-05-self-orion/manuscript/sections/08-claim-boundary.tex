\section{Conclusion}

Paper V separates three operations that self-improving systems often collapse: \emph{diagnose a problem}, \emph{propose a change}, and \emph{adopt the change}. Self-ORION allows the first two to be automated while keeping the third behind a protected status-transition boundary. Negative history remains immutable evidence; recurrence is not equated with cause; unresolved responsibility is allowed to remain unresolved; invention waits for ordinary explanations and transfer alternatives to be challenged; challengers run in isolation; replay and fresh transfer remain different evidentiary obligations; protected assurance is outside candidate write authority; and the final adoption decision remains in host custody.

The strongest supported result is therefore positive even though fresh-task self-improvement superiority is not yet measured: \textbf{within the registered architecture, fallible self-diagnosis and method proposal are structurally non-self-authorizing}. The controller exposes no self-merge route, and the T1--T7 successor mechanics culminate in a recommendation that deliberately lacks revision, adoption, promotion, merge and global-stop authority. Local hostile tests exercise the authority boundaries, and the retained 21/24 attribution archive demonstrates that the separation is relevant under actual diagnostic error rather than only under perfect self-knowledge.

This changes the role of the pending performance campaign. H1--H4 are no longer needed to justify the architectural contribution; they test the additional prospective question of whether the protected architecture also improves fresh development outcomes relative to strong self-improving baselines while reducing harmful repair, evaluator gaming and false broad revision. A positive future campaign would add performance authority to the already established governance result; a null campaign would leave the non-self-promotion invariant intact.
