\title{Protected Self-Improvement: Separating Diagnosis, Proposal, and Adoption Authority}
\author{Working framework draft}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
Self-improving scientific agents face a governance problem deeper than whether they can generate a useful patch: a system that diagnoses its own failure, proposes its own modification, and controls the evidence used to approve that modification can silently convert fallible self-assessment into adoption authority. Paper V separates these relations. Self-ORION retains failed, blocked, partial and cannot-check executions as persistent evidence; distinguishes recurrence from causal responsibility; keeps incompatible cause hypotheses unresolved until discriminating evidence exists; gates invention behind ordinary-cause and transfer challenges; executes challengers in isolated content-addressed environments; separates replay from fresh transfer; and places protected assurance and final adoption outside candidate write authority.

The strongest current result is an implemented \emph{non-self-promotion invariant}, not a claim of autonomous capability gain. The registered controller has no self-merge primitive: responsibility reports, interface checks, computation choices, uncertainty-containment reports, social-evidence reports and the composed T7 controller can nominate or recommend a next step but cannot grant scientific revision, adoption, promotion, merge or global-stop authority. Local hostile tests exercise recurrence-not-cause, negative-history retention, replay/fresh-transfer separation, invention readiness and reward-hacking paths. A separate diagnostic glm-5.2 hidden-cause archive scores 21/24 with three retained attribution errors. Those errors make the authority separation non-vacuous: an incorrect diagnosis is still not a valid self-promotion certificate.

Thus P5 establishes a bounded systems principle: \textbf{fallible self-diagnosis and method proposal can be made structurally non-self-authorizing by separating proposal evidence from protected evaluation and host adoption authority}. Whether this architecture improves fresh development tasks relative to strong self-improving baselines remains a prospectively frozen empirical successor rather than a premise of the governance result.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} self-improving agents; self-modification authority; failure attribution; fresh transfer; evaluator integrity; program evolution; AI governance.
