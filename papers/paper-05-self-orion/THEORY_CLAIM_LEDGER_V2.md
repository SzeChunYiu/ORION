## Novelty boundary

Blackwell comparison of experiments, Bayes decision identities, model-based
diagnosis, sequential experimental design, finite POMDP policy vectors,
multiple-prior rectangularity, robust control, and adaptive information
acquisition are parent theories and are cited. The state-indexed frontier is a
donor-derived synthesis and audit interface, not a distinct Bellman,
alpha-vector, or robust-POMDP novelty claim. P5's proposed contribution is the
composition around a typed minimal authorized revision front, the induced
cross-decision pair cover, and a protected non-self-promotion boundary. Whether
that composition improves real development outcomes is not settled by the
proofs.

The widest defensible framing is an identification-and-authority envelope:
decision-relative observational fibres determine what revision can be known;
legal costed acquisitions determine how a fibre may be refined; and protected
promotion custody determines who may authorize the resulting change. This
composition spans named controller implementations, but it is not a claim of a
new generic Bellman theory or of empirically superior self-improvement.

## Adverse-result rule

Every negative, null, harmful, interaction and `CANNOT_CHECK` outcome remains
attached to its original study identity. A materially new intervention family
receives a successor identity; it never rewrites the predecessor result.
