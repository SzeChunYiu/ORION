\begin{abstract}
Scientific workflows increasingly combine autonomous agents, retrieved evidence, analyses, simulations, state compilers, verifiers, human approvals, and cross-domain transformations. Every local action may be technically authorized while the final scientific conclusion is still unsupported. ORION Paper VIII targets the strongest general claim: across arbitrary typed scientific workflow graphs, can ORION prove and operationalize a non-amplification law under which local scientific authority is preserved or attenuated by composition, can never silently widen to a broader responsibility without an explicit evidence-bearing bridge, and is revoked exactly when load-bearing support changes while unaffected native facts remain valid? Existing P8 exhaustive evidence over 39,936 states and all 169 ordered donor pairs is preserved as a finite instance rather than the final theorem. P8-U extends authority from conclusions/actions to state construction, state-certificate scope, downstream use, and final scientific promotion; integrates minimal revalidation and transformation transport; treats cycles, aggregation, temporal expiry, representation change, objective change, evaluator change, and revocation as first-class; and compares against donor-complete capability/IAM, provenance, proof-carrying-action, workflow-governance, and responsibility-certificate systems. The intended terminal is a mechanized general scientific authority composition/revocation theorem plus prospective multi-domain evidence that ORION catches invalid scientific discharge missed by generic authorization/provenance systems without excessive blocking. Every theorem counterexample or empirical failure drives upward expansion of the authority primitives rather than local exception handling.
\end{abstract}

\section{Largest scientific question}
For a scientific workflow graph $G$ whose nodes include agents, evidence, tools, compiled states, analyses, claims, evaluators, and authorities, P8-U asks:
\begin{quote}
Can scientific authority be composed and revoked by a general law that prevents silent widening across arbitrary workflows while preserving every unaffected valid responsibility?
\end{quote}

The paper is not a generic software authorization paper. It asks whether technical permission, provenance, local proof, or agent capability actually discharges the scientific responsibility of the final target.

\section{Upward-generalization principle}
Capability systems, IAM, provenance, proof-carrying actions, workflow governance, and responsibility certificates are donor mechanisms. The strongest comparator receives all of them. ORION must generalize above them through a scientific non-amplification theorem plus practical detection of unauthorized conclusions in workflows where all software-level permissions and local checks pass.

The intended terminal is \textbf{GENERAL_SCIENTIFIC_AUTHORITY_COMPOSITION_AND_REVOCATION_THEOREM}.

\section{Primitive authority semantics}
\begin{definition}[Scientific authority token]
An authority token binds a subject, scientific target, responsibility, scope, evidence/support set, validity context, evaluator identity, authority owner, temporal validity, and revocation conditions.
\end{definition}

Responsibilities include at minimum:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item authority to construct a state;
    \item authority to certify the state's responsibility scope;
    \item authority to use the state for a downstream action;
    \item authority to verify a result;
    \item authority to promote a scientific conclusion;
    \item authority to repair or revise downstream state.
\end{enumerate}
No compiler/model may widen the responsibility set of its own output certificate by declaration.

\section{Target theorem family}
\begin{theorem}[No silent widening]
For an acyclic typed scientific workflow, composition of locally valid authority tokens cannot yield a final responsibility outside the closure of their explicitly bridged scientific scopes.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Bridge soundness]
A wider scientific responsibility may be discharged only when an explicit evidence-bearing bridge establishes the missing target relation and itself has adequate authority.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Attenuation]
Composition may preserve or narrow authority automatically under declared compatibility conditions, but may not broaden it without a bridge.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Exact revocation]
When a load-bearing source, compiler identity, representation mapping, objective, evaluator, or bridge changes, revocation propagates exactly through dependent authority while unaffected native facts remain valid.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[State-interface non-amplification]
Composing responsibility-scoped state interfaces cannot silently yield a broader scientific responsibility than any supported composition of their declared scopes.
\end{theorem}

P8-U also seeks a sharp characterization of cyclic workflows: safe fixed-point conditions or explicit rejection/\textsc{CannotCheck} when circular authority would be required.

\section{Primary hypotheses}
\paragraph{H1, invalid-discharge superiority.}
ORION reduces invalid scientific discharge/promotion versus the strongest donor-complete comparator by a prospectively frozen margin.

\paragraph{H2, unnecessary-blocking guard.}

\paragraph{H5, theorem transfer.}
The same law predicts authority across multiple scientific domains and independently implemented workflow families.

\paragraph{H6, authority-primitive expansion.}

\section{Adversarial controls}
Mandatory attacks include:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item every software permission passes but the scientific target scope is wider;
    \item every local state certificate is valid but their composition silently widens responsibility;
    \item compiler/model self-certifies a broader responsibility;
    \item stale state remains technically valid after source/objective/evaluator change;
    \item provenance is complete but scientific bridge is absent;
    \item valid conclusion is blocked by overconservative generic authorization;
    \item circular authority where evaluator and authority owner depend on each other;
    \item representation change preserves answer but invalidates promotion responsibility;
    \item donor-complete dynamic routing among IAM, provenance, proof, and certificate mechanisms.
\end{enumerate}
