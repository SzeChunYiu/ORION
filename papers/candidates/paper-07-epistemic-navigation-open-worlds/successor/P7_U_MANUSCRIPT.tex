\begin{abstract}
Modern scientific workflows transform evidence repeatedly through retrieval, filtering, extraction, normalization, mapping, compilation, aggregation, simulation, statistical analysis, and synthesis. Local correctness of every step does not imply that the final scientific conclusion remains closed. ORION Paper VII targets the strongest general claim: can a typed compositional calculus predict exactly which scientific closure obligations are preserved, weakened, invalidated, or require an explicit bridge under arbitrary admissible transformation chains, and can that calculus reduce false global closure more effectively than provenance-only, workflow, schema/type, and generic transformation systems without excessive recomputation? Existing P7 finite evidence of 155 restorations, 1,055 strict-subset failures, 25 successful heterogeneous compositions, and 25 matched missing-bridge cases is preserved as finite instance evidence. P7-U defines primitive transformation and responsibility semantics, seeks machine-checked composition laws, and adds state compilation, responsibility projection, caching/materialization, representation transport, stochastic transforms, lossy transforms, and cyclic workflows as first-class cases. The intended terminal is a general closure-preserving transformation calculus whose finite predecessor results follow as instances and whose prospective predictions outperform donor-complete workflow baselines on naturalistic multi-stage scientific pipelines. Every counterexample triggers upward expansion of the transform/bridge semantics rather than a local exception.
\end{abstract}

\section{Largest scientific question}
Given a workflow
\[
X_0 \xrightarrow{T_1} X_1 \xrightarrow{T_2} \cdots \xrightarrow{T_n} X_n,
\]
can ORION predict, before or during execution, which scientific responsibilities remain discharged at $X_n$, which have been weakened or invalidated, and where an additional evidence-bearing bridge is necessary?

The strongest target is not one navigation benchmark. It is a general scientific transformation calculus spanning data, representations, analyses, state compilation, and agentic workflow steps.

\section{Upward-generalization principle}
Provenance systems, workflow engines, schema/type systems, data-transformation calculi, proof/certificate transport, and state-compilation mechanisms are donor components. P7-U gives them to the strongest comparator. ORION earns superiority only by a higher-order composition law that generalizes over heterogeneous scientific transforms and predicts closure failures donor-complete systems miss.

The intended terminal is \textbf{GENERAL_CLOSURE_PRESERVING_SCIENTIFIC_TRANSFORMATION_CALCULUS}.

\section{Primitive transform semantics}
\begin{definition}[Scientific transform]
A transform $T$ maps an input scientific state to an output state while declaring its input contract, output contract, preserved responsibilities, weakened responsibilities, lost information, stochasticity, and required bridges.
\end{definition}

The transform vocabulary includes at least:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textsc{Retrieve};
    \item \textsc{Filter};
    \item \textsc{Extract};
    \item \textsc{Normalize};
    \item \textsc{Map/Align};
    \item \textsc{Aggregate};
    \item \textsc{Analyze};
    \item \textsc{Simulate};
    \item \textsc{Synthesize};
    \item \textsc{CompileState};
    \item \textsc{ProjectForResponsibility};
    \item \textsc{Cache/Materialize};
    \item \textsc{TransportVersion/Representation}.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Target composition laws}
P7-U seeks machine-checked results characterizing when:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item identity transforms preserve all declared responsibilities;
    \item sequential composition is associative at the scientific-contract level;
    \item local guarantees compose transitively;
    \item lossy transforms preserve a responsibility only relative to an explicit sufficiency contract;
    \item stochastic transforms require probabilistic/uncertainty obligations rather than deterministic closure;
    \item cycles admit a safe fixed point or must remain unresolved;
    \item representation changes require an explicit correspondence bridge;
    \item compilation/cache chains preserve current responsibility but may lose future optionality;
    \item a missing intermediate bridge invalidates global closure even when every local transform is technically valid.
\end{enumerate}

\section{State compilation as a scientific transformation}
A compiled state may intentionally omit information. P7-U distinguishes semantic preservation of the raw object from sufficiency of the compiled interface for a downstream responsibility. A chain
\[
\text{raw}\to\text{compiled}\to\text{cached/materialized}\to\text{transported/versioned}
\]
may be valid for \textsc{Predict} yet invalid for \textsc{Verify}, \textsc{Promote}, or \textsc{Repair}. Reuse for an unproven responsibility must produce \textsc{CannotCheck} or reopen the required state.

\section{Finite predecessor as instance}
The current 155/1,055 restoration law and 25/25 bridge-composition panel must be re-derived from the general transform semantics. They remain immutable. The new calculus may not define its primitives solely to restate those finite cases.

\section{Naturalistic workflow benchmark}
Prospective pipelines include:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item literature retrieval $\to$ filtering $\to$ extraction $\to$ synthesis;
    \item dataset normalization $\to$ model fit $\to$ statistical analysis $\to$ claim;
    \item simulation $\to$ surrogate model $\to$ uncertainty aggregation $\to$ decision;
    \item multi-source schema mapping $\to$ entity integration $\to$ scientific QA;
    \item raw evidence $\to$ compiled state $\to$ cached state $\to$ later repair;
    \item representation/version migration with preserved and invalidated certificates;
    \item stochastic/partial transforms with explicit unresolved states;
    \item cyclic workflows with iterative evidence/model updates.
\end{enumerate}
Each pipeline includes matched cases where every component is locally valid but global closure fails.

    \item generic type/schema checking;
    \item data-contract systems;

\section{Primary hypotheses}
\paragraph{H1, false-closure superiority.}
ORION reduces false global scientific closure versus the strongest donor-complete comparator by a prospectively frozen margin.

\paragraph{H2, recomputation efficiency.}
ORION reduces unnecessary bridge construction/recomputation while preserving closure safety.

\paragraph{H3, transform-family generality.}
The same calculus predicts closure behavior across multiple transform families, domains, and workflow lengths.

\paragraph{H4, compiled-state responsibility.}
ORION correctly distinguishes when lossy compilation preserves a requested responsibility and when reopening is required.

\paragraph{H5, calculus expansion.}
At least one prospective counterexample reveals a new transform class, bridge obligation, or composition law whose addition expands protected reach and transfers beyond the originating workflow.

\section{Outcomes}
Report false closure, missed bridge, unnecessary recomputation, correct unresolved/\textsc{CannotCheck}, valid closure coverage, resource cost, composition-prediction accuracy, and transfer by transform family. A single aggregate cannot compensate a false scientific closure with cost savings.

\section{Adversarial controls}
Mandatory attacks include:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item all local checks pass but a global intermediate contract is missing;
    \item pairwise mapping passes while a longer cycle fails;
    \item lossy compilation reused for an unsupported responsibility;
    \item cache/materialization becomes stale after representation/objective change;
    \item hidden stochasticity invalidates deterministic closure;
    \item provenance preserved but semantic correspondence fails;
    \item generic type compatibility without scientific target compatibility;
    \item overconservative bridge policy that recomputes everything;
    \item donor-complete dynamic routing among all registered transform/bridge mechanisms.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Negative-result recursion as upward calculus search}
Every composition failure is preserved and classified as missing transform semantics, missing bridge semantics, responsibility mismatch, stochasticity, cycle/fixed-point failure, representation transport failure, hidden dependency, or genuine non-composability. The response is to seek the more general law explaining the failure, then test it on fresh workflow families.

If a counterexample proves that a proposed associativity or preservation law cannot hold generally, P7-U seeks the sharpest necessary and sufficient conditions rather than hiding the failure. A materially new transform/bridge family may open a child issue under \#655.
