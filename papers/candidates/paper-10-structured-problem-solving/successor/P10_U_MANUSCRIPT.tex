\begin{abstract}
Structured state, proof search, program synthesis, theorem proving, tactic learning, evolutionary search, and tool-using language models can all improve difficult reasoning. ORION Paper X targets the strongest upward claim: across formal mathematics, algorithms/programs, scientific problem solving, and other verifier-backed domains, can a structured epistemic workspace outperform the strongest donor-complete problem-solving systems under matched information and compute, and, when the registered method basis is genuinely inadequate, can ORION diagnose that obstruction and propose a minimal semantics-preserving method, representation, operator, or interface edit that expands protected held-out reach without false escalation? Existing negative P9/P10 and ORION-Q results are preserved as research seeds, especially cases where exhaustive search or donor-complete synthesis already closes the registered space. P10-U generalizes upward by making those systems first-right-of-refusal baselines and entering method-space expansion only after a prospectively frozen obstruction certificate establishes that the current registered closure cannot solve the target under the matched bound. The study starts with native-state theorem proving and matched proof-search budgets, then crosses into algorithmic, scientific, and interface-synthesis domains. Primary outcomes include verified solve rate, verifier calls, branching/search depth, time-to-first-correct, invalid-action rate, repair success, false invention/escalation, held-out transfer, semantics preservation, and total resource cost. The intended terminal is replicated verified problem-solving superiority plus at least one independently verified method-space expansion absent from the registered basis, useful on held-out tasks, and not obtainable by stronger search, library learning, program synthesis, or donor-complete baselines at matched resources. Negative results recursively expand the search over representations, abstractions, and method languages rather than shrinking the ambition.
\end{abstract}

\section{Largest scientific question}
P10-U asks two nested questions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Does a structured epistemic workspace improve verified problem solving under matched information and total compute across heterogeneous domains?
    \item When the frozen method basis is inadequate, can ORION prove or independently certify that inadequacy and expand the method space with a minimal new operator/representation/interface that improves held-out reach?
\end{enumerate}

The second question is entered only after the first establishes non-vacuous real problem-solving value and after strong existing search/synthesis systems receive first right of refusal.

\section{Stage A: native-state verified problem solving}
Begin with formal theorem-proving tasks where the verifier is exact. Compare, under matched verifier-call and compute budgets:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item history/text only;
    \item native proof state;
    \item native state plus dependency structure;
    \item typed failure/repair state;
    \item strong current prover/tactic baselines;
    \item donor-complete state+search system;
    \item ORION structured workspace.
\end{enumerate}

Measure solve rate, verifier calls, branching factor, search depth, time-to-first-correct, invalid tactic rate, and repair success. Representation improvement without verified solve/search benefit is not sufficient.

\section{Stage B: action-abstraction phase diagram}
Freeze action abstractions from atomic through raw tactic, coarse family, effect-grounded action, macro/library action, and learned compositional action. The target is to identify when abstraction changes search efficiency or transfer, not merely prediction accuracy.

If a simpler action basis matches the richer system at matched resources, the richer mechanism is not credited.

\section{Stage C: method-basis obstruction certificate}
Method-space expansion can start only when all of the following are frozen and independently checked:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item exact current method basis and closure operation;
    \item computational/access/resource model;
    \item target task/family and verifier;
    \item exhaustive/search/program/library-learning baselines under the same bound;
    \item evidence that failure is not caused by representation, implementation, environment, insufficient search, or hidden tool access;
    \item a bounded obstruction witness showing the target lies outside registered reach or remains unreachable under the declared bound.
\end{enumerate}
A timeout alone is not an obstruction certificate.

\section{Stage D: candidate method-space edits}
Allowed edits include:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item new operator or macro with explicit semantics;
    \item representation/regime change with proved or verified correspondence;
    \item new decomposition or interface exposing previously inaccessible state;
    \item grammar/library extension;
    \item composition rule absent from the registered basis;
    \item method-selection or verification primitive that changes reachable solutions.
\end{enumerate}
The candidate is proposal-only until an independent verifier confirms semantics and held-out utility.

\section{Method-space expansion test}
A claimed expansion must satisfy:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item the new edit is outside the registered pre-freeze closure if claiming novelty of reach;
    \item the same semantics and access model are preserved or a valid correspondence is established;
    \item the edit solves or materially improves the registered obstruction;
    \item the improvement transfers to held-out tasks/families;
    \item known-method controls show low false-invention/escalation;
    \item strong search/program-synthesis/library-learning baselines do not obtain the same result at matched budget;
    \item independent verification reproduces the reach expansion.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Primary hypotheses}
\paragraph{H1, verified problem-solving superiority.}
Across heterogeneous verifier-backed tasks, ORION achieves higher solve/success utility than the strongest runnable donor-complete comparator under matched information and total compute.

\paragraph{H2, search-efficiency superiority.}
ORION reduces verifier calls, search depth, branching, or time-to-first-correct at matched verified quality.

\paragraph{H3, obstruction-diagnosis validity.}
ORION distinguishes true method-basis inadequacy from representation, search, implementation, and environment failures with lower false escalation than donor-complete diagnosis systems.

\paragraph{H4, method-space expansion.}
At least one protected obstruction is solved by a minimal new method/representation/operator edit outside the registered closure, with positive held-out transfer and independent verification.

\paragraph{H5, low false invention.}
On known-method controls, ORION rarely declares invention necessary when the registered basis suffices.

\paragraph{H6, cross-domain transfer.}
The problem-solving and expansion mechanism transfers prospectively to multiple domains without target-specific refitting.

\section{Adversarial controls}
Mandatory attacks include:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item search timeout mislabeled as language/method inadequacy;
    \item generated edit merely renames a known composition;
    \item compiler/verifier feedback leaks the target action;
    \item stronger oracle/tool/access model is silently introduced;
    \item edit wins only on originating instances;
    \item method invention fires when an existing method suffices;
    \item native state improves prediction but not verified solve/search;
    \item larger search budget closes the claimed ORION advantage;
    \item representation change alone closes the obstruction;
    \item donor-complete program synthesis finds the same edit at matched cost.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Negative-result recursion as upward method search}
Every negative is first localized to search complexity, representation accessibility, action abstraction, implementation/environment, verifier limitation, method-basis inadequacy, or donor domination. The programme then explores materially different successor families rather than retuning one failed candidate.

A former negative such as exhaustive-search dominance becomes a challenge to move into a regime where enumeration is provably or empirically intractable under the matched bound. If new operators remain donor-owned, they are absorbed and the method language is generalized further. If all materially different successor families collapse, the programme seeks a lower bound or impossibility statement rather than manufacturing novelty.

Existing programmes \#586 and \#633 remain the negative-result research authorities for their covered P9/P10 and ORION-Q failure families. New P10 children are opened only for distinct obstruction or method-language families with prospectively frozen discriminators.
