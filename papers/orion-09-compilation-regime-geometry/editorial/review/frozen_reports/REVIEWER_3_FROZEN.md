# Reviewer 3 frozen report

## 1. Overall assessment

The manuscript presents a useful distinction between exactness regions, constructive mechanisms, intrinsic support, proof-validity regions, feature determination and prospective transfer. Its strongest reporting feature is disciplined boundary control. Finite censuses, all-size theorems and one frozen transfer panel are not conflated. Adverse results remain prominent in the abstract, Results, tables, Supplementary Information, Limitations and Conclusion.

The packet manifest was successfully verified. All 24 bound files matched their declared byte counts and SHA-256 digests, with no unlisted packet files other than the manifest itself.

At a conceptual level, a technically capable *Quantum* reader can identify the intended estimands and evidence classes. The reader cannot yet reconstruct the concrete model grammars, several theorem conditions, the transfer procedure, the null calibration or the stated reproduction workflow. These are scientific reporting gaps rather than requests for additional positive results.

The manuscript correctly states that the numerical and verification checks remain within one project and custody chain. It does not present them as external replication or independent proof review.

## 2. Central claim and evidence readout

The central claim is that an exact compiler regime map should keep several logically different objects separate. The four supplied examples support that claim through the following evidence.

1. The shared-tag Pauli model has an all-size support-two normalization statement, supported by an analytic deletion argument and finite local obligations.
2. The rank-two dependent-triple model has a tight support-one normalization under the unit objective and a sufficient objective-weight region for the same proof.
3. The six-term linear-combination model is claimed to have an exact reference-compiler boundary given by a pair-gain condition.
4. Weighted Clifford state preparation supplies complete finite domains through \(n\leq3\), followed by adverse transfer results on one frozen 120-state \(n=4\) panel.

The abstract definitions of \(C^\star\), \(\mathcal R_D\), \(\kappa\) and \(E_{\min}\) make the intended quantities clear. Table 1 distinguishes all-size, complete-domain and frozen-panel authority. Table 2 and Supplementary Tables 2 and 3 expose finite-domain denominators, near-injectivity, negligible transfer coverage, the shuffle-null result and the earlier failed forecast.

The numerical bindings are supported only by author-controlled resources. `SELECTED_REPLAY_RECEIPT.json` records three same-repository reruns. `INDEPENDENT_PAPER_VERIFICATION.json` records 29 passing binding checks but explicitly marks both external replication and independent custody as false. These are consistency receipts, not external scientific authority.

## 3. Major strengths

1. **Generalization boundaries are unusually visible.** The manuscript repeatedly limits the all-size results to their declared algebraic grammars and structural objectives. It limits the state-preparation conclusion to complete graphs through \(n=3\) and one 120-state \(n=4\) panel.

2. **Adverse results are not concealed.** The failed prospective forecast, low transfer coverage, 32 transfer errors, \(p=0.51\), corrected zero-witness search and rewrite-dependent two-point observation remain visible in both the main article and Supplementary Information.

3. **The distinction between finite determination and transfer is clear.** Reporting 1,109 cells and 1,072 singletons for 1,146 states prevents the zero mixed-cell result from being mistaken for compact explanatory structure.

4. **Same-custody verification is described honestly.** Methods, Limitations and the Supplementary reproduction section all disclaim external replication and external proof review.

5. **The existing tables serve the main comprehension needs.** They expose evidence class, domain size, coverage, error counts and adverse boundaries. The present gaps are better closed by formal definitions, protocol reporting and executable resources than by an additional figure.

## 4. Major Concerns

### R3-M1 [methods_and_analysis]

**Severity** Major  
**Blocking** Yes  
**Target criterion** Correctness and clarity  
**Claim pointer** The two normalization theorems and the six-term reference-exactness theorem hold for every instance admitted by their respective models.  
**Evidence pointer** Main article, Sections 2.1 to 2.4 and 3.1 to 3.4; Supplementary Information, Section 1 and Table 1.  
**Concern** The manuscript gives generic estimands and short prose model registers, but it does not provide sufficiently complete definitions of the concrete feasible configuration spaces, target constraints, structural objective functions or admitted size domains. Most seriously, neither the six-term pair-gain condition nor the definition of an admitted six-term batch is stated. The local proof-obligation totals are reported without the underlying case definitions or tables needed to inspect completeness. The support-zero infeasibility argument is asserted but not developed. Consequently, a technically capable reader cannot reconstruct or independently evaluate the central mathematical claims from the manuscript and supplement.  
**Alternative interpretation** The unavailable project records may contain complete formal specifications and successful computations. The same-project receipts support consistency with those records, but they do not replace a reader-visible mathematical definition or proof.  
**Why it matters** These are central theorem claims. Without self-contained model and boundary definitions, correctness cannot be evaluated by technical peer review, and “all sizes admitted by the model” is not operationally determinate.  
**Resolution test** Supply complete formal definitions for each model, including feasible objects, constraints, objectives, support coordinates and admitted domains. State the six-term pair-gain condition explicitly. Provide enough proof and finite-obligation detail, either in the supplement or a permanently archived technical appendix, for a reader to verify how the local cases close each analytic composition and how tightness is established. Closure is achieved when the theorem statements and their domains can be reconstructed without relying on undisclosed repository objects.

### R3-M2 [reproducibility_or_auditability]

**Severity** Major  
**Blocking** Yes  
**Target criterion** Correctness, reproducibility and clarity  
**Claim pointer** The 127-feature representation determines the complete \(n\leq3\) labels but fails prospective transfer on the frozen \(n=4\) panel, with 32 errors and shuffle-null \(p=0.51\). The review package is also claimed to contain the resources needed to check every headline number.  
**Evidence pointer** Main article, Sections 2.4, 2.5, 3.5 and Data and code availability; Table 2; Supplementary Information, Sections 2 to 4 and Tables 2 and 3; `PACKET_MANIFEST.json`; `SELECTED_REPLAY_RECEIPT.json`; `INDEPENDENT_PAPER_VERIFICATION.json`.  
**Concern** The transfer and reproduction procedures are not reconstructable. The supplement gives feature groups but not the exact 127 coordinates. It does not define the panel-selection procedure or panel keys, the parity split, lookup construction, uncovered-state default, earlier forecast rule or pre-specified success criteria. The null report gives 200 shuffled controls, a mean and a tail probability, but does not specify what was shuffled, the exchangeability unit, randomization procedure, seed or empirical \(p\)-value convention. The manuscript’s Data and code availability statement says that the review package contains exact result records, a table generator and the binding verifier. The verified manifest contains none of those executable or primary result objects. The two JSON receipts report successful same-project checks but provide only paths and outcomes for resources outside the frozen packet. The four-layer reproduction order is therefore descriptive rather than executable from the supplied package.  
**Alternative interpretation** The visible counts support a conservative finite-panel conclusion, and the 32 errors are transparently connected to the 32 reference-exact panel states left uncovered by the effective default. This does not establish the unreported rule, panel construction or null-generating process.  
**Why it matters** The failed transfer is presented as the decisive adverse result. Its interpretation depends on the exact frozen rule, panel, default and null calibration. The current package also cannot perform the reproduction sequence that the manuscript says it supports.  
**Resolution test** Report the complete feature map, reference-synthesis algorithm, panel construction and identifiers, transfer rules, defaults, success criteria and shuffle-null procedure. Bind the exact result records, analyzers or sufficient executable equivalents, table generator, verifier and environment instructions into the permanent archive referenced by the manuscript. Reproduction should execute the four stated layers in order and recover the theorem fields, tables, transfer counts and null result. No new figure is required.

## 6. Blocking technical failings

The blocking technical failings are R3-M1 and R3-M2.

The missing arXiv record, archive identifier and licence, affiliation, ORCID disposition, funding, conflicts, acknowledgements, final contribution statement, generative-AI disclosure approval and final author approval are author-controlled filing inputs. I do not treat their current absence as evidence against the mathematical results. A permanent archive can help close R3-M2, but the scientific requirement is that the archive actually contain the reported definitions, data and executable verification resources.

Lack of external replication or external proof review is not itself listed as a blocking failing. The manuscript handles that boundary correctly. Same-custody verification can support internal consistency, but it cannot substitute for reader-visible methods, proofs or reproducible artifacts.

## 7. Assessment against *Quantum* criteria

**Correctness**  
The claims are carefully bounded and numerically coherent within the supplied summaries. Full correctness is not presently assessable because central model definitions, one theorem condition, detailed proof obligations and the transfer protocol are incomplete.

**Significance and meaningful advance**  
Separating intrinsic structure, proof scope, representation limits and prospective failure could provide a meaningful reporting framework for exact quantum-compilation studies. The heterogeneous examples make the distinction concrete. The contribution remains appropriately bounded as a typed combination rather than a universal compiler theory. Its advance cannot be evaluated confidently until the mathematical objects are fully reader-evaluable.

**Clarity**  
The high-level argument is clear. The reader can follow the sequence from exactness to support, proof validity, feature determination and transfer. Technical clarity is insufficient at the points where the prose substitutes names and case totals for formal definitions.

**Reproducibility and reporting completeness**  
Finite-domain denominators and adverse outcomes are reported well. The transfer rule, panel construction, null calibration and executable reproduction materials are incomplete or absent. The current frozen packet does not contain several resources that the Data and code availability statement says it contains.

**Generalization boundaries**  
This is a major strength. The paper avoids physical-runtime, hardware-advantage, production-transfer and universal-compiler claims. It also distinguishes one finite panel from a population-level result and a sufficient proof region from a necessity theorem.

**Reader evaluability**  
A capable reader can understand what each evidence class is intended to show, but cannot yet reconstruct how several central results were obtained or verify their exact scope. This is the principal obstacle under the supplied *Quantum* criteria.

## 8. Recommendation posture

In this simulated pre-submission review, the manuscript requires major revision before its central technical case is fully evaluable. The necessary closure is primarily formal specification, protocol completion and artifact binding. It does not require manufacturing a favorable experiment, hiding the failed transfer result or adding a cosmetic figure.

The adverse findings should remain as prominent as they are now. If the model definitions, theorem conditions, transfer protocol, null procedure and executable archive are supplied, the paper’s conservative claim structure could become a strong feature rather than a liability.
