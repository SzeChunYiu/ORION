## 1. Overall assessment

The frozen packet passed manifest verification for all 24 listed objects by byte count and SHA-256 digest.

The manuscript offers a useful distinction among intrinsic support, proof-derived ceilings, objective-conditioned certificates, finite feature determination and prospective transfer. It preserves adverse results and generally limits its claims appropriately.

The central mathematical results are nevertheless not assessable at theorem level from the supplied article. The two all-size normalizations depend on model definitions, global composition arguments and finite local obligations that are not exposed in sufficient detail. The six-term equivalence does not state the pair-gain condition to which the theorem refers. There is also an unresolved ambiguity between the stated objective inequalities and the claim that the unit objective lies on their boundary.

The state-preparation findings are appropriately finite and adverse, but the prospective protocol, lookup rule and shuffle calibration are not specified sufficiently for independent scrutiny. These are blocking technical deficiencies rather than requests for external replication as such.

## 2. Central claim and evidence readout

- **Shared-tag support-two normalization.** The article presents a plausible parity-deletion argument. The required connection from the two-bit classes to every feasibility constraint is not formally defined, and the restoration-cost step is supported only by a reported 18,432-case outcome.
- **Rank-two support-one normalization.** Deletion credits, alignment costs and reconstructed-tag bounds are reported. The complete case split and its all-size composition are not shown, and support-zero infeasibility is asserted rather than demonstrated.
- **Objective-conditioned proof region.** Four halfspace inequalities are visible, and the manuscript correctly treats them as sufficient for one certificate rather than necessary. The parameter domain, the exact unit-objective tuple and the derivation from the support-one proof are missing.
- **Six-term equivalence.** The manuscript reports 203 partitions, 11 shapes and finite regressions. It never states the actual pair-gain condition or the affine inequalities, so the claimed if-and-only-if boundary cannot be evaluated.
- **Finite enumerations.** Counts and outcomes are supplied, but not the case definitions, records, certificates or executable checkers. Counts alone cannot demonstrate exhaustive coverage.
- **Prospective state-preparation tests.** The adverse results are retained and restricted to a 120-state panel. The frozen rule, panel construction, deterministic reference compiler and prediction records are not exposed.
- **Null calibration.** The article gives 200 shuffled controls, mean error 32.41 and \(p=0.51\). It does not define what was shuffled, what constraints were preserved, how the empirical probability was calculated or how the lookup default entered the null.
- **Verification authority.** The manuscript correctly states that digest checks, fresh same-project replays and a structurally separate verifier are not external replication or independent proof review. These checks establish custody and consistency, not theorem validity.

## 3. Major strengths

1. The manuscript keeps all-size theorems, finite censuses and frozen-panel evidence as distinct evidence classes.
2. It explicitly states that the objective region is sufficient for one proof and is silent outside that region. The zero-witness search is not promoted into a necessity theorem.
3. The near-injective feature representation and both failed prospective tests remain visible. The manuscript does not convert finite-domain separation into a compact predictor or universal compiler law.
4. The finite-domain error-floor definition is mathematically clear and correctly distinguishes representation-level impossibility from classifier performance.
5. The limitations exclude physical-runtime, hardware-advantage, production-transfer and universal-compiler claims.
6. The distinction between same-project verification and external replication is unusually explicit and should be preserved.

## 4. Major Concerns

### R1-M1 [claim_evidence_validity]

**Severity** Major  
**Blocking** Yes  
**Target criterion** Correctness and clarity  
**Claim pointer** The two all-size normalization theorems, the objective-conditioned support-one certificate and the six-term if-and-only-if boundary.  
**Evidence pointer** Main article Sections 2.4 and 3.1 through 3.4. Supplementary Information Section 1 and Table 1. `SELECTED_REPLAY_RECEIPT.json`, `INDEPENDENT_PAPER_VERIFICATION.json` and `ATOMIC_CLAIM_LEDGER.json`.

**Concern**  
The central theorem claims are not supported by an inspectable proof in the supplied article. The model register does not formally define the admitted instances, feasible configurations, tag and restoration constraints, or the complete structural objectives. The all-size arguments refer to finite local checks, but the packet supplies only counts and reported extrema. It does not expose the enumerated cases, the predicates checked or certificates connecting those cases to the analytic composition.

The six-term theorem is especially underdetermined because the “proved pair-gain condition” is never stated. A reader cannot apply, falsify or verify the claimed equivalence.

The objective-region statement also needs correction or definition. If “unit objective” means
\[
t_c=t_{nc}=t_{\mathrm{tag}}=t_r=1,
\]
then it satisfies none of the four displayed inequalities, rather than lying on their boundary. If “unit objective” denotes another tuple after hidden base coefficients are applied, that tuple and parameterization must be stated.

**Alternative interpretation**  
The omitted project records may contain valid definitions, proofs and exhaustive checks. The receipts demonstrate that some same-project generators reproduced committed digests. They do not allow a reader to determine whether those generators encode the intended theorem or whether the all-size compositions are complete.

**Why it matters**  
These are the principal mathematical advances claimed by the article. A local verifier passing author-defined fields cannot substitute for an exposed proof, and finite regression cannot establish an unstated if-and-only-if condition.

**Resolution test**  
To preserve the theorem-level claims, provide the following in the article or Supplementary Information.

1. Formal definitions of each admitted model, feasible family, objective and support coordinate.
2. Complete theorem hypotheses and all-size composition proofs.
3. The exact unit-objective tuple and a direct verification of its relation to the displayed halfspaces.
4. The explicit six-term pair-gain condition and all affine shape inequalities required for both directions.
5. Auditable local-obligation definitions, complete result records and checker or certificate material bound to the submitted source.

An external proof review would increase confidence but is not a substitute for exposing the proof. If this material cannot be supplied, the valid closure route is to narrow or remove the all-size and equivalence claims and report only the bounded computational statements actually available for inspection.

### R1-M2 [methods_and_analysis]

**Severity** Major  
**Blocking** Yes  
**Target criterion** Correctness, clarity and reproducibility  
**Claim pointer** The prospectively frozen four-qubit forecast, the 127-feature parity-split transfer test and the shuffle-null interpretation.  
**Evidence pointer** Main article Sections 2.4, 2.5 and 3.5, Table 2, and the abstract. Supplementary Information Sections 2 through 4. `SELECTED_REPLAY_RECEIPT.json` and `INDEPENDENT_PAPER_VERIFICATION.json`.

**Concern**  
The packet does not provide enough information to reconstruct the prospective analyses. The deterministic reference compiler is described only as Gaussian-elimination-style. The 127 features are described by families rather than exact formulas. The panel-selection procedure, frozen keys, prediction rule, parity split, coverage definition, default prediction and temporal separation from label opening are not shown.

The shuffle procedure is also undefined. The manuscript does not state the shuffled unit, preserved margins, randomization method, seed, empirical \(p\)-value convention or complete null distribution. With 32 reference-exact panel states and a negative default on uncovered states, the observed 32 errors can be understood primarily as a consequence of almost zero lookup coverage. The result establishes failure of this declared rule on this panel if the counts are correct. It does not by itself establish statistical equivalence to the null or a broader absence of transferable information.

The Data and Code Availability section says the review package contains exact result records, the table generator and the verifier. The frozen manifest instead contains summaries and verification receipts, not those executable or machine-readable objects.

**Alternative interpretation**  
The evaluation may have been genuinely frozen and the null may have been correctly implemented within the project. The supplied packet establishes only the resulting assertions and digests, not the protocol needed to audit those assertions.

**Why it matters**  
Prospective falsification is part of the paper’s central contribution. Without the exact frozen protocol, the reader cannot distinguish a valid held-out failure from an analysis whose panel, representation or default rule was fixed with partial outcome knowledge.

**Resolution test**  
Supply a bound prospective-analysis package containing:

1. The complete reference-compiler specification.
2. The panel manifest and deterministic selection procedure.
3. The freeze record showing when panel keys, rules and criteria were fixed relative to label opening.
4. Per-state predictions, exact labels, exact costs, feature vectors and coverage status.
5. The complete feature construction, parity split, lookup matching and uncovered-state default.
6. The exact shuffle-null algorithm, number of randomizations, random seed, tail convention, empirical \(p\)-value formula and null distribution.

Replace “indistinguishable from a shuffle null” with the narrower conclusion that the test found no improvement under the specified null unless an equivalence analysis is actually supplied. If prospective provenance cannot be demonstrated, describe the result as a same-project held-out panel evaluation rather than a prospectively frozen test.

## 5. Minor Comments

### R1-m1 [data_and_reporting_integrity]

**Severity** Minor  
**Target criterion** Correctness and clarity  
**Affected element** The frozen-panel feature-cell entries in Table 2.  
**Evidence pointer** Main article Table 2 and `source/generated_results_tables.tex`.

**Issue**  
The table reports 120 states, 120 feature cells and 118 singleton cells. Under the manuscript’s definition of a feature cell, 120 distinct cells for 120 states implies that all 120 cells are singletons. Conversely, 118 singleton cells plus any non-singleton cells would require more than 120 states. The value 118 appears more consistent with the separately reported number of uncovered states.

**Required correction**  
Correct either the feature-cell or singleton-cell entry, regenerate the table and re-run an explicit partition-arithmetic invariant. Keep the retrospective in-panel cell statistics visually separate from the two-state prospective lookup coverage.

## 6. Blocking technical failings

- **R1-M1.** The central all-size and equivalence claims lack sufficiently exposed definitions, proof and auditable computational obligations. The objective-region connection to the unit objective is unresolved.
- **R1-M2.** The prospective state-preparation protocol and null calibration cannot be reconstructed from the packet.

The absence of external replication is not itself the blocking failing. The immediate requirement is a self-contained and independently auditable evidence chain. Same-project digest agreement cannot close either concern.

## 7. Assessment against *Quantum* criteria

**Correctness**  
Not established for the central theorem and prospective-transfer claims from the supplied material. The manuscript contains plausible proof ideas and appropriately bounded negative results, but the decisive steps are not inspectable.

**Significance**  
The typed separation of intrinsic structure, proof scope, representation scope and prospective failure could constitute a meaningful methodological contribution. Its significance depends on the validity of the mathematical examples used to establish that separation.

**Clarity**  
The conceptual distinctions and limitations are clear. The mathematical models, certificate derivations, six-term boundary and prospective protocol are too compressed for technical evaluation.

**Meaningful advance beyond the state of the art**  
The supplied author-side literature audit supports a bounded contribution in the combined reporting framework. The present review cannot determine whether that advance is established until the central technical claims are closed.

**Reproducibility and auditability**  
Same-project replay and binding checks are reported honestly. The frozen packet does not contain the exact records, generators, checker implementation or prospective data needed for independent scrutiny. The planned archive and licence remain filing requirements rather than evidence against the mathematical results.

**Criteria not invoked**  
No physical-runtime, hardware-advantage, production-transfer or universal-compiler claim is assessed because the manuscript explicitly excludes them.
