\title{Regime maps for exact quantum compilation separate expressivity, certificates and feature transfer}
\author{Sze Chun Yiu}
\email{sze-chun.yiu@fysik.su.se}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Quantum compilation is instance dependent, but a performance footprint alone does not distinguish an intrinsic compiler property from a proof limitation or a failed representation.  We define an exact regime map that records reference-compiler exactness, constructive improvement mechanisms, intrinsic support, proof-validity regions, feature determination and prospective transfer as separate objects.  Four frozen models show why the distinctions matter.  A shared-tag Pauli block-encoding model has an all-size support-two ceiling, whereas a rank-two dependent-triple model has intrinsic support number one and a nontrivial objective region where the support-one proof applies.  A six-term linear-combination model admits an exact structural boundary.  Weighted Clifford state preparation behaves differently.  Its complete $n\leq3$ domain contains 1,146 states, and a sign-aware 127-feature representation separates all labels into 1,109 cells.  This representation is nearly injective rather than compact, and it does not transfer: parity-split lookup makes 32 errors on a frozen 120-state $n=4$ panel, covers only two states, and is indistinguishable from a shuffle null ($p=0.51$).  An earlier prospective forecast also failed, matching 100 of 120 regime labels and 67 of 120 exact costs.  These results do not supply a universal compiler law or a physical advantage claim.  They show that exact compiler studies should report what is intrinsic, what is certified, what is representation dependent and what survives prospective falsification.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} quantum compilation; exact optimization; stabilizer circuits; block encoding; algorithm selection; instance-space analysis.
