\section{Conclusion}

Exact quantum-compilation studies can report more than a winner map, but the additional objects must remain distinct.  Three model-specific exact results and one adverse transfer study show why: an intrinsic support result separates from a named mechanism vocabulary, a proof ceiling separates from a tight normal form, a certificate region separates from necessity, and in-domain feature determination separates from transfer.

The decisive adverse result is the state-preparation test.  A rich feature map removes all mixed cells on the complete $n\leq3$ domain but is nearly injective and fails on the frozen $n=4$ panel.  The appropriate conclusion is not that every compiler has a simple regime geometry.  It is that exact witnesses, proof scope, representation scope and prospective failures should be recorded separately before a compiler map is generalized.
