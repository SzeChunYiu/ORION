\title{Regime maps for exact quantum compilation separate expressivity, certificates and feature transfer}
\author{Sze Chun Yiu}
\email{sze-chun.yiu@fysik.su.se}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Quantum compilation is instance dependent, but a performance footprint alone does not distinguish an intrinsic compiler property from a proof limitation or a failed representation.  We prove three model-specific results and organize them with a typed exact-regime record: a shared-tag Pauli block-encoding model has an all-size support-two ceiling; a rank-two dependent-triple model has intrinsic support number one and an explicit objective region where the proof applies; and a six-term linear-combination model has an exact pair-gain boundary.  Weighted Clifford state preparation supplies an adverse transfer test.  Its complete $n\leq3$ domain contains 1,146 states, and a sign-aware 127-feature representation separates all labels into 1,109 cells.  The representation is nearly injective rather than compact and does not transfer: parity-split lookup makes 32 errors on a frozen 120-state $n=4$ panel and covers only two states.  It shows no improvement under the specified 200-permutation shuffle null on this panel (mean 32.41 errors; empirical $p=0.51$).  An earlier prospective forecast also failed, matching 100 of 120 regime labels and 67 of 120 exact costs.  The combined record rules out three otherwise tempting inferences: a proof ceiling need not be intrinsic support, certificate silence need not be necessity, and finite feature determination need not imply transfer.  No universal compiler law or physical advantage is claimed.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} quantum compilation; exact optimization; stabilizer circuits; block encoding; algorithm selection; instance-space analysis.
