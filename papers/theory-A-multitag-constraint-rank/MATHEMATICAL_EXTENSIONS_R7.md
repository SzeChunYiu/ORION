## 1. Scientific question

The earlier layers identify the exact alphabet-restricted zero-sum threshold, prove direct-sum and quotient bounds, and replace the full kernel by the smaller alphabet of residues actually generated by lifted image atoms. Two questions remain: can several quotients be composed into one auditable certificate, and can the exact support theorem be recovered when the unregularized objective is slightly outside the deletion-dominance cone?

## 2. Iterated quotient data

Let `G_0=H` and `A_0=A`. For `i=0,...,r-1`, choose a homomorphism `phi_i:G_i -> Q_i` with kernel `G_{i+1}`. Put `B_i=phi_i(A_i)`. Let

`a_i=max{|U|: U is an inclusion-minimal nonempty zero-sum word over B_i that has a lift over A_i}`.

Let `A_{i+1}` be the set of all nonzero kernel sums of such lifted image atoms. Zero residues are omitted because a zero residue would already be a zero-sum subsequence in the source word. Write `q_i=zsf(Q_i;B_i)`.

The R6 lift-alphabet theorem gives

`zsf(G_i;A_i) <= q_i + a_i zsf(G_{i+1};A_{i+1})`.

## 3. Quotient-chain theorem

**Theorem A12 (quotient-chain certificate).** With the data above,

`zsf(H;A) <= q_0 + a_0 q_1 + a_0 a_1 q_2 + ...`

`+ (product_{j=0}^{r-2} a_j) q_{r-1}`

`+ (product_{j=0}^{r-1} a_j) zsf(G_r;A_r)`.

If `A_r` is empty, the final term is zero.

**Proof.** Apply the one-step lift-alphabet bound at level zero. Substitute the corresponding bound at level one into its kernel term and continue. Every multiplier records the maximum number of source positions consumed by one atom at each preceding quotient level. No unrestricted kernel alphabet is introduced. ∎

**Corollary A13 (compiler support certificate).** In any finite grammar satisfying persistent zero-sum deletion soundness and objective dominance, every exact optimum has support at most the right-hand side of Theorem A12.

This is an upper certificate, not an intrinsic lower bound. Exactness for a compiler still requires a realizable terminal witness that survives every production rule.

## 4. Why the chain can be much sharper

The classical subgroup estimate repeatedly substitutes an unrestricted Davenport constant for every kernel. Theorem A12 instead propagates only the residues generated by image atoms. A quotient can therefore delete an entire semantic kernel from later analysis when no lifted image atom reaches it.

For the explicit `C_2 direct_sum C_4` alphabet used in R6, the lift alphabet is a proper subset of the kernel and the one-step certificate is exact at three, while the unrestricted product-style estimate is seven. A longer chain preserves this alphabet sensitivity at every level.

## 5. Exact regularization theorem

Let `C(x)` be the unregularized objective and `s(x)` the support. Suppose every admitted zero-sum deletion of `d` coordinates preserves feasibility and satisfies

`C(after)-C(before) <= gamma d`

for a known `gamma>=0`. For `lambda>=0`, define

`J_lambda(x)=C(x)+lambda s(x)`.

**Theorem A14 (regularization restores exact deletion dominance).** If `lambda>=gamma`, every finite admitted instance has a `J_lambda`-optimal representative whose support is bounded by `zsf(H;A)`, and hence by every quotient-chain certificate from Theorem A12.

**Proof.** A deletion of `d` positions changes the regularized objective by at most `gamma d-lambda d <= 0`. The semantic hypotheses and nonzero-total condition are unchanged, so the global support descent from the base normal-form theorem applies. ∎

**Sharpness.** If a legal one-coordinate deletion increases `C` by exactly `gamma`, then any `lambda<gamma` makes that deletion strictly increase `J_lambda`. Thus the threshold cannot be lowered from the stated local information alone.

## 6. Application consequences

The quotient-chain theorem gives an auditable hierarchy for exact search. A cheap quotient supplies the first interval; only its realized lift residues are passed to the next layer. The process can stop as soon as the certified support is computationally acceptable.

The regularization theorem gives a model-design rule. When a useful semantic deletion is almost, but not quite, cost-nonincreasing, a support penalty at the exact local sensitivity restores a rigorous finite normal form. Potential uses include modular compiler search, proof-carrying configuration systems, sparse exact synthesis, and other finite optimization grammars with additive signatures.

## 7. Prior-art and claim boundary

Subgroup recursions, Davenport constants, restricted zero-sum problems, sparse optimization, and Lagrangian regularization are donor mathematics. The paper-specific contribution is their exact composition in the declared deletion grammar: image atoms determine the only kernel alphabet that remains relevant; the bound composes through a quotient chain without reverting to unrestricted kernels; and the same semantic theorem becomes exact for a support-regularized objective at the local deletion-sensitivity threshold.

No physical T-count, depth, runtime, qubit, or quantum-advantage claim is made.
