# Mathematical Extensions R6 — Transfer Alphabets and Exact Quotient-Block Budgets

Date: 2026-08-26

Canonical predecessors: `MANUSCRIPT_V3_PIPELINE.md`, `MATHEMATICAL_EXTENSIONS_R4.md`, and `MATHEMATICAL_EXTENSIONS_R5.md`

Status: rigorous theorem addendum with an independently executable finite fixture. The statements concern the declared alphabet-restricted zero-sum invariant. Compiler consequences remain conditional on the semantic deletion hypotheses already isolated in V3.

## 1. Argument and boundary

In an alphabet-restricted zero-sum system, a quotient does not send arbitrary kernel elements back to the source problem. It sends only sums of source blocks whose images are minimal zero sums. R5 bounded those block sums by the ordinary Davenport constant of the whole kernel. This addendum replaces that coarse step by the exact *transfer alphabet* generated by the quotient blocks that can actually occur.

The result is a strictly sharper quotient theorem. On a standard-basis fixture in `C_2^3`, the R5 ordinary-kernel estimate is five, whereas the transfer-alphabet estimate is three, equal to the exact restricted invariant. This is an abstract algebraic closure, not evidence that a production compiler realizes the same deletion grammar.

## 2. Transfer data of a quotient

Let `H` and `K` be finite abelian groups, let

`phi:H -> K`

be a homomorphism with kernel `N`, and let `A` be a finite source alphabet. Put

`B=phi(A)`.

A *minimal image block* is a nonempty word over `B` whose sum is zero and whose every proper nonempty subword has nonzero sum. Let `M(K;B)` denote the finite set of its multiplicity types.

For a source word `U` over `A` such that `phi(U)` is a minimal image block, its source sum belongs to the kernel:

`tau(U)=sigma(U) in N`.

Define the nonzero transfer alphabet

`T_phi(A)={tau(U) != 0 : phi(U) is a minimal image block}`.

For `n in T_phi(A)`, define its transfer weight

`ell_phi(n)=max{|U| : phi(U) is a minimal image block and tau(U)=n}`.

The maximum exists because every minimal image block has length at most `D(K)` and `A` is finite.

For a finite weighted alphabet `(T,ell)` in a finite abelian group `N`, define

`wzsf(N;T,ell)`

as the maximum of

`sum_i ell(t_i)`

over all zero-sum-free words `t_1...t_q` over `T`. If `T` is empty, set the value to zero.

## 3. Transfer-alphabet quotient theorem

**Theorem A12 (weighted transfer-alphabet upper bound).**

With the notation above,

`zsf(H;A) <= zsf(K;B) + wzsf(N;T_phi(A),ell_phi)`.

**Proof.** Let `W` be a zero-sum-free source word over `A`. In the image word `phi(W)`, repeatedly remove an inclusion-minimal nonempty zero-sum block until the remaining image word is zero-sum-free. Let the lifted source blocks be `U_1,...,U_q`, and let the source positions left over be `V`.

The image of `V` is zero-sum-free, so

`|V| <= zsf(K;B)`.

For every removed block, `n_i=sigma(U_i)` lies in `N`. It cannot be zero, because then `U_i` itself would be a nonempty zero-sum subword of `W`. Moreover, no nonempty subword of `n_1...n_q` can sum to zero: the union of the associated disjoint source blocks would be a nonempty zero-sum subword of `W`. Hence `n_1...n_q` is a zero-sum-free word over `T_phi(A)`.

By definition of the transfer weights,

`|U_i| <= ell_phi(n_i)`.

Therefore

`|W| = |V| + sum_i |U_i|`

`<= zsf(K;B) + sum_i ell_phi(n_i)`

`<= zsf(K;B) + wzsf(N;T_phi(A),ell_phi)`.

Taking the maximum over `W` proves the theorem. ∎

## 4. Relation to the R5 bound

Let

`atom(K;B)=max{|U| : U is a minimal nonempty zero-sum word over B}`.

**Corollary A13 (successive relaxations).**

`zsf(H;A)`

`<= zsf(K;B) + wzsf(N;T_phi(A),ell_phi)`

`<= zsf(K;B) + zsf(N;T_phi(A)) atom(K;B)`

`<= zsf(K;B) + (D(N)-1) atom(K;B)`.

**Proof.** Every transfer weight is at most `atom(K;B)`. The transfer alphabet is contained in `N\{0}`, so its unweighted zero-sum-free budget is at most `D(N)-1`. ∎

Theorem A10 is thus the final relaxation in a chain of sound bounds. It is exact only when the quotient can transfer the full zero-sum complexity of the kernel at the maximal image-block length.

## 5. Recursive quotient chains

Consider a chain

`H_0 -> H_1 -> ... -> H_m`

with alphabets `A_{i+1}=phi_i(A_i)`. Let `W_i` denote the weighted transfer budget of the `i`th map.

**Corollary A14 (recursive transfer certificate).**

`zsf(H_m;A_m) <= zsf(H_0;A_0)`

and

`zsf(H_0;A_0) <= zsf(H_m;A_m) + sum_{i=0}^{m-1} W_i`.

**Proof.** The lower bound follows by lifting an image zero-sum-free word at every quotient step. The upper bound follows by applying Theorem A12 successively and telescoping. ∎

A finite family of quotient chains therefore supplies a certified interval by taking the largest lower endpoint and the smallest upper endpoint. No claim is made that inserting an arbitrary intermediate quotient always improves the interval; the transfer data decide that question.

## 6. Exact fixture: the standard basis of `C_2^3`

Let

`H=C_2^3`, `A={e_1,e_2,e_3}`,

and let `phi` project onto the first coordinate. Then

`K=C_2`, `N=C_2^2`, and `B={0,1}`.

The exact image budget is one. The minimal image blocks are the one-letter zero block and the two-letter word `1,1`.

- Lifting the one-letter zero block gives either `e_2` or `e_3`, both with transfer weight one.
- Lifting `1,1` can use only two copies of `e_1`; its source sum is zero, so it does not enter the nonzero transfer alphabet.

Thus

`T_phi(A)={e_2,e_3}`

and

`wzsf(N;T_phi(A),ell_phi)=2`.

Theorem A12 gives

`zsf(C_2^3;A) <= 1+2=3`.

The word `e_1 e_2 e_3` is zero-sum-free, so equality holds. By contrast, the R5 ordinary-kernel relaxation gives

`1+(D(C_2^2)-1) atom(C_2;{0,1}) = 1+2*2 = 5`.

The executable R6 verifier independently enumerates the source words, image atoms, transfer alphabet, weighted kernel words, and both bounds.

## 7. Compiler interpretation

Under the V3 persistent-deletion hypotheses, a quotient block represents an image-visible deletion certificate. The transfer alphabet records the semantic residue that such a block can leave in the hidden kernel. Theorem A12 therefore separates two questions that the ordinary-kernel bound conflates:

1. which kernel residues are reachable by valid image certificates; and
2. how many reachable residues can coexist without creating a source zero sum.

This can reduce an exact support cap before a full multiplicity search. It does not prove that the abstract alphabet is complete for a production compiler, nor that the resulting cap is intrinsic.

## 8. Prior-art calibration

Subgroup and quotient arguments for Davenport-type constants are classical, as are restricted and weighted zero-sum invariants. The paper does not claim novelty for quotient decomposition in general. The paper-specific contribution is the exact transfer object required by the declared normal-form pipeline: minimal image blocks induce a weighted restricted alphabet in the kernel, and that alphabet—not the whole kernel—controls the hidden budget.
